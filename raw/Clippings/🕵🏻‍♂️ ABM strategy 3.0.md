## What has changed in our full-funnel ABM strategy framework.

First, let’s start with the core changes we’ve made ([read ABM strategy 2.0 here](https://fullfunnel.io/abm-strategy/).)

The primary change we made was in how we select and target the accounts. We made the **shift from vertical and firmographic targeting to [cluster-based targeting](https://fullfunnel.substack.com/p/how-to-target-accounts-in-2026)**.

Like any other company, we used to adjust our ABM strategy to conventional industry targeting and tiers. The companies built account lists based on industry, territory, and revenue tiers (Tier 1 for 1:1, Tier 2 for 1:few, Tier 3 for 1:many). But this often led to targeting accounts with entirely different use cases. This approach forced marketing and sales to create generic messaging that failed to resonate with anyone.

We made workarounds inside that strategy, but it was time-consuming and not scalable. Until we completely revamped the approach and started with cluster-based targeting (our first case study was with Cardata, [read here](https://fullfunnel.substack.com/p/case-study-breaking-marketing-and)).

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cPdjWYnK_g4)

---

Here is exactly what has changed.

### 1\. Targeting by shared challenges instead of industry

A “cluster” groups accounts that share the exact same strategic challenge and use case (job-to-be-done), regardless of their industry or tier. This allows teams to create highly relevant messaging and continuous nurturing programs tailored to specific pain points.

It also enables to unite new logo, deal acceleration, and expansion accounts into one program, instead of running 3 separate programs.

### 2\. Deal-led account qualification criteria

Instead of starting with sales wishlists, intent data, or broad firmographics, we start by exporting best and fastest deals from the last 2 years for the specific use case. We then analyze the patterns between these accounts to develop account qualification criteria.

### 3\. Account prioritization based on awareness and intent

Instead of static revenue tiers, we dynamically prioritize accounts within a cluster into three lists based on their **level of awareness, engagement, and relationship** with us.

- **Cluster ICP:** Accounts that fit the qualification criteria but are unaware of the vendor, requiring brand awareness and demand generation.
- **Future Pipeline:** Accounts that are aware of the vendor and have engaged with content, but require further relationship-building to validate their exact needs.
- **Active Focus:** Accounts that demonstrate strong engagement, an established relationship, and strong evidence of product need. These accounts receive 1:1 attention to develop sales opportunities.

### 4\. Compounding playbooks

With industry targeting strategy we needed to create a new ABM program every quarter to cover a new vertical. There was no compounding impact. If an account demonstrated an engagement but wasn’t converted into a sales opportunity, rarely somebody touched it later.

By switching to clusters, we started planning with the clients a year-long ABM program where we build playbooks on top of one another, utilizing the same narrative and messaging across the entire long-term buyer journey.

It enabled us to have a consistent flow of touchpoints with the strategic accounts and nurture them across long sales cycles.

### 3\. 6A framework to design an ABM program

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!iSpb!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F0430af10-8eb5-404a-bf2a-5a7e6c7a1424_800x644.png)

---

We use the 6A framework to orchestrate personalized, continuous nurturing for strategic accounts across the entire buyer journey. It consists of:

- **Account Qualification:** Defining specific criteria to determine what makes a company a good or bad fit, moving beyond basic firmographics to include product-need signals, disqualification criteria (red flags), and a detailed breakdown of the buying committee.
- **Account Segmentation and Prioritization:** Dynamically dividing qualified accounts into 3 lists based on their revenue potential, vendor awareness, and evidence of product need: Cluster ICP (unaware), Future Pipeline (engaged but need is unknown), and Active Focus (high intent).
- **Account Research:** Gather publicly available information and mapping out the buying committee to uncover their specific strategic initiatives, “Jobs-to-be-Done” (JTBD), KPIs, and challenges to develop our Point of View, account narrative, and engagement plan before any outreach or engagement.
- **Account Awareness:** Defining targeted activities (e.g. thought leadership, content collaborations, social selling, etc) to create awareness among the buying committee of target accounts.
- **Account Development (Nurturing):** Engaging the buying committee through continuous, meaningful non-sales touchpoints. This includes a mix of time-based playbooks (planned events/content) and signal-based playbooks (reacting to high-intent actions or hiring triggers). I’ll cover it in more detail below.
- **Account Activation:** Co-creating highly personalized solutions and leveraging “bridge activities” (strategy sessions, custom content hubs, or audits) to convert engaged accounts into discovery calls without pushy sales pitches.

What makes the 6A framework efficient is that it focuses both marketing and sales on dynamic lists of accounts that show high revenue potential, awareness,l and strong product-need signals.

It prevents pouring marketing budget into a “leaky bucket” of broad, unaware audiences. Furthermore, this framework replaces generic messaging with **1:1 personalized solutions** directly mapped to the KPIs and pain points of individual decision-makers of strategic accounts. Both marketing and sales are involved in planning which creates a complete alignment, turning them into a unified, cross-functional revenue team with shared goal.

**With every client who applied the 6A framework we have seen higher win rates, increased ACV, and significantly shorter sales cycles.**

The core differentiation from the “conventional ABM”:

- **Targeting:** The old-school ABM relies on targeting static “wish lists” of Fortune 500 companies or broad firmographics. 6A framework builds dynamic target lists using first-party and third-party intent data, engagement signals, and strict qualification criteria.
- **Engagement strategy:** The old playbook relies heavily on programmatic display ads for “air cover” and passing contacts into automated, 21-step cold outbound cadences. The new approach focuses on multithreaded relationship building, 1:1 events, content co-creation, and personalized content hubs.
- **Alignment & Handoffs:** In the old model, marketing works in silos, generating MQLs or ad clicks, and transferring them to sales for activation. The new model completely eliminates siloed handoffs by establishing joint marketing and sales playbooks, weekly pipeline review meetings, and shared accountability for target accounts.
- **Success Metrics:** The old way measures success by ad clicks (aka account engagement) and MQLs. The new framework measures success through leading indicators (like account penetration), Account-to-Pipeline ratio, sales pipeline velocity, and allbound-sourced revenue.

### 4\. ABM playbooks optimized for long sales cycles

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!2bcw!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F2c7972dc-04d2-4cab-879b-1bfa80c6f27b_1456x1559.png)

On a recent client call, I’ve asked an enterprise sales rep what he does with an account if they don’t reply after ten touches. He said:

> “It means to me that they are not in the market, so I will just move forward”.

And this is the problem: we assume that if we are selling to them, and they have a challenge that our product helps with, they’re going to buy.

But there are multiple studies that prove that there are more than [400 touch points](https://fullfunnel.substack.com/p/how-to-create-playbooks-for-long) required to generate an enterprise deal. That’s why enterprise sales cycles are long. The problem is that in many B2B companies the marketing and sales playbooks are not aligned with it.

The goal of ABM playbook is to match the reality of enterprise B2B buyer journey. The goal is to build lasting relationships, establish trust, and influence the entire buying committee long before they are actively looking to purchase (aka getting into a consideration set).

To successfully orchestrate ABM for long sales cycles, we mix Always On, Time-Based and Signal-Based Playbook. We run them in parallel:

- **Time-based touchpoints:** These are planned activities mapped out over a quarter and synced with Always On programs: expert roundtables, market research, writing personalized LinkedIn “account love letters:, etc. It helps to stay top-of-mind and delivers value regardless of where the account is in their buyer journey.
- **Signal-based touchpoints:** These are reactive plays triggered by specific buying signals: new strategic initiatives, hiring for specific roles, visiting high-intent pricing pages, etc. Signals allow your team to spark highly relevant conversations at exactly the right moment (learn more here: [https://fullfunnel.substack.com/p/signal-based-strategy](https://fullfunnel.substack.com/p/signal-based-strategy) )

**Here are 8 principles we follow when creating playbooks for ourselves and clients:**

1\. Optimize for the length of the sales cycle (not just send a cadence and forget).

2\. Multithread the buying committee and people influencing your buyers.

3\. Create a constant flow of touchpoints using time-based and signal based playbooks.

4\. Align playbooks with the buyer journey stage

5\. Build one playbook on another for continuous nurturing and compounding impact.

6\. Co-create playbooks with sales.

7\. Design clear steps in the playbook, including both sales and marketing activities.

8\. Define clear metrics and set expectations

### 5\. Create Center Of Excellence (COE)

Scaling a successful ABM across an entire organization is where most marketing teams fail. They simply hand a “playbook” of assets and templates to other teams and ask them to replicate it. Which always fails.

To scale successfully, with every client we establish an internal Center of Excellence (COE). It is a dedicated team (ideally formed from the members of the successful pilot ABM program) that owns the ABM strategy, execution quality, and scaling of the ABM program

A standard COE typically consists of an ABM Lead, a Content & Messaging Strategist, an Operations Manager, and a Sales Representative.

COE operates on a 80/20 model:

**80% of the time: operationalization, documentation, scaling, and training.**

Instead of dictating how other teams should execute, the COE acts as a coach, helping new teams adapt the central ABM framework to their specific market challenges, cultural nuances, and buyer needs

**Operationalization & documentation.**

The COE documents the core principles, step-by-step program planning, and quality benchmarks so that any new team understands exactly what to do, why, how, and in what order.

Scaling & training.

The COE plans gradual ABM deployments (e.g., onboarding 1-2 new teams per quarter). They:

- train other teams on the strategy
- guide them through their first pilot
- help them adjust central framework and messaging to local market
- run weekly pipeline review meetings to support them through their initial launch

The COE is in charge of execution quality control. They do regular audits of the ABM program execution of other teams. It prevents regional teams from cherrypicking different tactics, creating their own version of ABM, and complaining that it doesn’t work.

We developed an ABM audit framework which you can access here:[https://fullfunnel.substack.com/p/how-to-audit-your-abm-program](https://fullfunnel.substack.com/p/how-to-audit-your-abm-program)

**20% of the Time: Innovation and experimentation.**

The COE dedicates the remaining 20% of their time to running new experiments. They test new channels (e.g. communities), evaluate new technology, develop new AI-enabled processes, try new content formats.

When an experiment meets success criteria, we operationalize it and add to the central documentation. Then, roll out to the rest of the organization

By owning documentation, coaching, auditing, and innovation, the COE ensures that ABM scales as a repeatable, revenue-generating engine rather than fragmenting into a dozen broken, siloed campaigns.

## We see a lot of B2B marketing (and sales) teams often fall into the “technology trap,” deploying AI to automate processes that haven’t been properly defined

If your team doesn’t have a robust, proven process, AI will simply scale your broken processes.

An AI-enabled (not AI-created and executed) ABM program relies on the 80/20 rule: AI handles 80% of the legwork (like data collection and signal capturing), while humans add the crucial 20% of strategic insight and relationship building.

Here are the core rules we use to integrate AI in our ABM programs.

#### 1\. Establish quality standards and “What good looks like”

Before you can outsource a process to AI, you must know exactly what a high-quality output looks like. Which you must do manually first 🙂. You must clearly define:

- The required inputs and context needed for the task.
- The specific judgments that need to be made (e.g., specific evaluation criteria for qualifying an account).
- What “good” looks like by setting strict quality benchmarks and defining the exact output format you expect.

#### 2\. Develop detailed SOPs (Standard Operating Procedures)

Every AI prompt must be built on a detailed SOP. For example, for account research, your template should clearly outline:

- The specific research question (e.g., “acquisitions in the last 12 months”).
- What this insight tells you and how to use it.
- Exactly where to find this information.
- Where and in what format this insight should be saved in your CRM.

You then test the AI’s output against your manual SOP and use a “human-in-the-loop” verification checklist to catch hallucinations, missing context, and source constraint violations.

#### 3\. Define what to automate vs. what to keep human

AI should only be applied to repetitive, data-heavy tasks.

- **Automate:** Collecting engagement signals, scoring accounts, running initial account research, and extracting customer challenges from call transcripts.
- **Keep human:** Strategic account prioritization, relationship building with target buyers, subject-matter interviews, and content ideation910.

#### 4\. Create a vault library

As you document these processes, you will generate a massive amount of files, templates, and scripts. If your team cannot easily find this documentation, it becomes useless.

You must create a centralized vault library with:

- Step-by-step playbooks and SOPs.
- Quality benchmarks and examples of successful execution/output (how “good looks like”)
- Shared AI prompts and technology training videos.
- Templates, scripts, and reporting dashboards.

By creating a centralized knowledge with clear guidance on how and where to use AI, you simply allow all your teams to follow proven, AI-accelerated internal best practices.

### 8\. Coordination: quarterly - weekly - daily sync meetings

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!eVDr!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fafad30e9-f5dd-488e-a092-89737a9c5cd6_1456x1015.png)

To avoid the common trap of being “aligned in theory but siloed in practice,” we establish a strict operational rhythm with quarterly planning sessions & retrospective, weekly planning & sync meetings, and daily standups.

Here is the breakdown.

#### 1\. Quarterly planning

It includes:

- **Strategic levers:** We define 1-3 strategic levers (e.g., expanding enterprise accounts or accelerating stalled deals) to ensure everybody runs in the same direction.
- **Define new cluster and target accounts:** we’ve already covered it early.
- **Syncing always-on programs with ABM:** The goal is to ensure that Always On programs are relevant to the selected cluster of accounts. The worst thing you can do is always running in silos.

#### 2\. Quarterly retrospective

At the end of each quarter, we host a combined review and strategy session to evaluate what worked, kill what didn’t, and plan the next 90 days together.

We review all past activities and decide what to

- Operationalize and make a standard/always-on process
- Scale and expand to new segments
- Refine promising but underperforming tactics, or
- *S* top resource-draining tactics with zero pipeline ROI (e.g. generic email nurtures or gated ebooks).

#### 3\. Weekly planning & review meetings

This is the core execution engine of the ABM program. The agenda covers:

- **Account insights & engagement.** Reviewing target account activity: high-intent website visits, social engagement, and conversations started.
- **1:1 account planning.** Deciding the exact next steps to progress specific accounts. For example, marketing might agree to prepare a personalized content hub, while sales agrees to use it to follow up with an internal Champion.
- **Leading indicators & bottlenecks.** Reviewing if the team hit their weekly KPIs (e.g., personalized connection requests sent, non-sales touchpoints completed), identifying what is holding them back, and removing friction.
- **Pipeline updates.** Dynamically moving accounts between lists (e.g., from Future Pipeline to Active Focus), adding newly engaged accounts, and removing unresponsive ones.

#### 4\. Daily standups in micro teams (aka accountability calls)

Daily standups (10-15 mins max) help to maintain execution of the new tactics like social selling and continuous engagement with target accounts.

The meeting starts with everyone quickly sharing what they have done yesterday, what bottlenecks they faced, and their exact plan for that day (e.g., “I’ll share a new post, connect to 5 new buyers, and send 20 invites for our webinar”).

Daily standups help to uncover and to fix bottlenecks earlier in the week instead of waiting until the next sync meeting.
