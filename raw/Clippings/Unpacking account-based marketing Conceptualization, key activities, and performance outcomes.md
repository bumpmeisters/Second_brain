## Introduction

Managerial literature portrays Account-Based Marketing (ABM) as a strategic growth approach in which marketing and sales collaborate to engage a select set of key accounts with substantial revenue potential by delivering highly personalized content to the specific needs and priorities of the accounts (Gartner, 2023; Van Rensburg, 2024). Over the past few years, ABM has gained prominence as a rapidly growing business-to-business (B2B) marketing approach across various sectors (Hollier, 2024). Recent evidence shows that B2B companies allocate 30% of their total marketing budgets to ABM, and 66% of marketers plan to increase these investments (Leavitt, 2023). This growing interest is largely driven by its perceived effectiveness; whitepapers indicate that ABM can increase customer engagement (Brown, 2021), improve retention (Kelman, 2024) and generate higher ROI compared to traditional marketing approaches (Leavitt, 2023). Complementing these performance claims, company-reported cases from industry offer illustrative examples of ABM's potential in practice. The technology firm LiveRamp, for instance, concentrated its ABM program on a small set of 15 Fortune 400 companies and reported a 33% conversion rate that translated into 50 million dollars in annual revenue from these accounts (Metadata.io, 2025). Similarly, the cybersecurity company Mimecast reports that a focused account-based approach led to a 170% year-to-date pipeline uplift and a 60% increase in average deal value for marketing-sourced ABM opportunities, indicating improved mid- and late-funnel sales performance (Haussegger, 2025).

Despite the high interest in and widespread practitioner adoption of ABM, academic research on the topic remains scarce. While some studies have acknowledged ABM as an important emerging area in B2B marketing, they are largely conceptual and lack empirical grounding (see Tanner, 2021). Consequently, most existing insights into ABM stem from professional sources such as management books and web articles (e.g., Lyndon & Peppers, 2023). Thus, the extant views on ABM are heavily shaped by individual consultancies and thought leaders rather than supported by a broader empirical base, and they fail to establish clear connections to established marketing theories or related constructs, resulting in significant conceptual ambiguity in this area. Furthermore, firms seem to implement ABM in diverse ways, from lead generation (Penfold, 2024) to long-term strategic account development (Tanner, 2021), complicating consensus on its scope, core activities, and boundaries. This lack of conceptual clarity hinders both scholarly progress and managerial practice, making it difficult to evaluate ABM's effectiveness, design programs, or develop organizational capabilities. As leading marketing research underscores the importance of identifying and defining emerging constructs to advance theory and reinforce the discipline's relevance (Zeithaml et al., 2020), we contend that there is a critical need to position ABM within established literature and to develop a theoretically grounded definition and comprehensive framework that elucidates how ABM functions and why it holds strategic significance.

To address this knowledge gap, this study aims to conceptualize ABM in business markets by defining the construct, delineating its key activities, and examining its perceived outcomes for organizational performance. The study begins with a conceptual section that reviews practitioner literature of ABM to build an initial understanding of the concept and to position the concept within three academic literature streams of digital marketing, key account management (KAM), and customer prioritization. Following this, we adopt a Theories-in-Use (TIU) approach to explore the managerial view of ABM by engaging with leaders responsible for ABM in 22 firms across multiple countries and B2B industries.

Through rigorous data analysis, we develop a theoretically grounded conceptualization of the ABM process, including a definition supported by evidence and a detailed examination of its three core activities. Additionally, we introduce a comprehensive framework that demonstrates how the ABM process evolves across distinct stages of the sales cycle, encompassing prospecting, sales negotiations, and relationship expansion, while showing how prioritization varies in granularity according to opportunity complexity and value. The framework further highlights the key benefits of ABM, namely its perceived value for KAM sales and organizational outcomes. We conclude the paper by discussing the theoretical and managerial implications of the study, along with its limitations and directions for future research.
