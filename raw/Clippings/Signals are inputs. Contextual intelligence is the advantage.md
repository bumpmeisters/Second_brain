## What signals can and can’t tell you

A signal is an observable event or behavior — a page visit, a job change, a funding announcement — that suggests something may be happening with an account. *Something* is a very important word.

What signals alone can’t tell you is whether that event is meaningful, who in the buying group it came from, or where the account is in its buying journey.

Unfortunately, a lot of go-to-market plans use individual signals as a green light for the wrong actions like blasting buyers with irrelevant outreach or alerting reps to chase accounts that aren’t ready.

### Predictive buying stage modeling

Predictive buying stage modeling helps prioritize leads, accounts, and opportunities by indicating the likelihood of an account opening or progressing within the next 90 days. It is updated daily. The 6sense predictive model categorizes accounts into distinct buying stages based on scores ranging from 0 to 100:

- **Target** (0-19): Accounts showing little to no activity, suitable for broad awareness messaging
- **Awareness** (20-49): Accounts with some activity, considered early stage; targeted for broad-based engagements and mid-funnel activities above certain thresholds
- **Consideration** (50-69): Accounts with significant activity and longer engagement history; targeted with banners, content syndication, and buyer’s guide content
- **Decision** (70-85): Accounts showing significant digital research activity; treated as mid- to low-funnel with marketing “air cover” content and sales outbound prospecting
- **Purchase** (86-100): Accounts at the bottom of the funnel, most likely to open an opportunity soon; targeted for high-value engagements like telephone prospecting and sales touches

This modeling unifies sales and marketing teams by providing analytics that identify high-priority accounts and guide targeted engagement strategies throughout the buyer’s journey.
