## The challenge

Enterprise organizations are in the middle of massive GTM transformations—but not everyone makes it through successfully. Teams get stuck with messy data, overwhelmed sellers, siloed campaigns, and stale target account lists. This leads to wasted ad spend, disjointed buyer experiences, and misalignment between sales and marketing that leaves pipeline on the table.

## The goal

Accelerate your ABX maturity by adopting the tactics used by leading enterprise GTM teams. Whether you’re just getting started or looking to level up an established program, these 10 hacks will help you build cleaner data foundations, deliver more relevant experiences, and finally get sales and marketing rowing in the same direction.

## The 10 hacks

### 1\. Uplevel target account selection

Treat target account selection as an ongoing, dynamic process.

Start by aligning on a single data provider as your source of truth. Then QA all firmographics and domains using a combination of manual checks and AI validation. One client discovered that less than 50% of their domains were currently accurate due to acquisitions, rebrands, and mergers over the years. Another found wildly inconsistent employee counts across providers—50, 500, and 5,000 for the same company.

For enterprise lists, map accounts to their exact LinkedIn company page (name, profile ID, and URL). When LinkedIn commands the majority of your ABM budget, this dramatically improves match rates.

Segment with custom tags and recalibrate at least quarterly to surface emerging demand. Accounts are merging, startups are launching, and well-funded companies you didn’t know existed are suddenly a perfect fit. A dynamic list keeps your 6sense segments, CRM, and campaigns firing on all cylinders.

### 2\. Customize signal aggregation

Your sellers are drowning in intent signals. As marketers, we think we’re doing them a favor by providing more insight, but too much data creates complexity, not clarity.

Align on the top signals at both the account and person levels, then create custom weightings because not every signal carries equal strength. A website visit to your demo page? Strong signal. A random third-party intent hit? Could go either way. Engagement with your ads? Worth tracking, but weight it accordingly.

Identify three to five custom signals unique to your business. For example, a change in CMO at a prospect account is a huge opportunity signal, while the same change at a customer account could signal churn risk.

Get creative with signals your competitors might miss. Google search intent is a great example: don’t just track who converted from a Google ad. Track who clicked on your paid search. Only 3–5% of clicks convert to demos, but 100% of those clicks represent real interest. Push that data into your CRM, edit the 6sense taxonomy to score these custom insights, and build custom 6QAs per region and product.

### 3\. Proactive buying group mapping

Here’s something marketers can learn from SDRs: They start each day by going into LinkedIn Sales Navigator, plugging in an account, and finding the exact people they need to reach. Marketers tend to target job titles and “see what happens.”

Flip the script. Map out the buying committee contacts for each target account at scale.

Start in LinkedIn Sales Navigator, manually identifying contacts within a sample of accounts to build a model of what your ideal contacts look like. Then apply job title keywords and exclusion criteria into 6sense (or another data provider) to map ideal contacts across your entire TAM. Enrich those contacts and load full buying committees into the CRM.

The math is math-ing: If you’ve already mapped out every person you want to target and loaded only those people into a campaign, every lead is already qualified. Your conversion rates, cost per lead, and click-through rates all improve because you’ve essentially guaranteed success.

### 4\. Make contact data a superpower

The only clean CRM is one you just bought. After day one, it’s progressively messier.

Load contacts into the CRM and CDP at the beginning of each campaign, then refresh through automated 6sense workflows. This creates a known audience for granular tracking and targeting and keeps your data from decaying.

It also gives sellers time back. When you’ve done the heavy lifting of finding, enriching, and loading contacts, reps can use the “exclude people in CRM” filter in LinkedIn Sales Navigator to only find net-new contacts. You’ve just handed them 20% of their day back by eliminating admin work.

Use 6sense to sync target contacts to Google Search, Display, and YouTube. When you have first-party data loaded and maintained, your campaign effectiveness compounds across channels.

### 5\. Use an identity graph

Business email alone won’t cut it for targeting, especially on channels that aren’t B2B-friendly by design. If your personas live on Meta or Reddit, you need to meet them where they are.

Enrich contacts with mobile IDs, personal email, personal phone numbers, and addresses to increase ad targeting match rates. This expands your reach beyond LinkedIn and Display into Meta, Reddit, and other channels where business identifiers don’t match well.

Add person-level web scripts to identify contacts visiting your website. This creates inferred engagement data at the account and buying group level — combining your opt-in data with behavioral signals for a fuller picture of who’s actually engaging.

### 6\. Micro-segmented personalization

The old “TOFU-MOFU-BOFU” framework was good enough once. Not anymore. There’s so much marketing noise that only highly relevant content cuts through, and “relevant” now means far more than just buying stage.

Identify every micro-segment of relevant messaging by combining persona, industry, solution fit, journey stage, and account size. That’s not three campaigns. It could be 20, 50, or 100 micro-experiences across landing pages, ads, email, and video.

Scale this with technology. Tools like PathFactory, Folloze, Mutiny, Webflow, and Hushly enable programmatic personalization so you’re not manually building every variation.

Remove friction by building ungated experiences. Buyers complete 80%+ of their journey anonymously. Making content accessible isn’t just nice; it’s how you actually get engagement.

### 7\. Orchestrate audience activation

Once you’ve mapped your buying groups and built contact-level audiences, orchestrate those audiences across every relevant channel:

- LinkedIn
- Native Ads
- Display
- Meta
- Google Search
- YouTube
- Reddit
- CTV

Standardize on one audience and activate it everywhere.

When you know it’s the right buyer, your job becomes hitting them wherever they are. You don’t know if they’re spending time on LinkedIn or YouTube or Reddit, but if you’ve covered all the bases, they won’t miss you.

One of the best things a seller can hear from a prospect is “I’ve seen your ads everywhere.” That kind of brand recall is hard to quantify, but you know it when you’ve hit it, and it makes every sales conversation easier.

Use custom audience segmentation with optimal bid structures depending on targeting accuracy and channel. The tighter your audience, the more you can afford to bid.

### 8\. Actually align with sales

Real alignment starts early, and it goes deeper than agreeing on a slide deck.

Interview your sellers before building campaigns. Talk to your best AE to understand how they actually sell. Talk to your best SDR to learn the pain points that earn first meetings. If you can message around what it takes to get a first meeting, you know how to get a demo request.

Then trust but verify. Audit the CRM to see what messaging is actually going out from the sales team.

Activate 6sense data and custom signals within sales workflow tools like Outreach, Salesloft, Gong, Clari, and LinkedIn Sales Navigator. When insights live where sellers already work, they actually get used.

### 9\. Support sales efforts with AI agents

Expecting your sales team to follow up on every lead and signal is a recipe for inconsistency. The best teams use AI agents to make “sales” touches programmatically, ensuring timely, on-message follow-up that doesn’t depend on seller bandwidth.

Assign clear swim lanes for what AI handles vs. SDRs:

- Inbound vs. outbound
- SMB vs. mid-market vs. enterprise
- Consideration Stage vs. 6QA

This isn’t about replacing sellers. It’s about handling the motions that marketing can now own as part of a unified GTM function.

Track AI agent performance independently using unique SFDC campaigns and UTM source/medium values. You need clean measurement to know what’s actually working.

Empower sales reps with AI to draft 1:1 personalized emails, social touches, and talking points. Give them a head start on personalization instead of expecting them to start from scratch every time.

### 10\. Custom BI reporting

Native reporting within your existing tools will only take you so far. You need dashboards that fit the way your business analyzes data, makes decisions, and informs stakeholders.

Use 6sense Data Packs and pull through APIs from various channels to build custom dashboards that correlate all media spend and tie it to pipeline. This creates a single source of truth across the enterprise.

Build dashboards for specific personas.

- Marketing leaders need to see the full picture
- Sales leaders need a streamlined view focused on prioritization and outreach
- Marketing practitioners need campaign-level details
- Sales practitioners need account-level actions.

When reporting fits how each stakeholder actually works, it actually gets used.
