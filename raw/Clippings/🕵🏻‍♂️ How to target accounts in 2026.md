## Why vertical targeting fails in enterprise B2B if you don’t have a niche vertical solution

Remember the numbers I shared in the beginning of this newsletter? Here is the story behind them.

An enterprise fintech company targeted “banks” as their vertical. They built an entire ABM program around it—selected the biggest banks, created industry-specific content, launched campaigns.

Six months later: 178 MQLs, 2 sales-accepted opportunities, 0 closed wins.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!TyE6!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd58a5e15-ad23-430c-90e3-4d5babeec801_1036x490.png)

Why did it fail?

**Accounts in the same vertical had multiple lines of business and different use cases.**

Retail banks needed onboarding solutions. Investment banks needed advisor retention tools. Commercial banks needed fraud prevention. Different buying committees, different challenges, different business units.

When you pick accounts by industry alone, you have no idea what your messaging should be about. You end up with two bad options:

1. Create generic “we help companies like yours” messaging that resonates with nobody
2. Build hyper-personalized 1:1 programs for every buying committee (which nobody has resources for)

Both approaches fail. There’s a better way.

## What is cluster-based ABM

A cluster is a set of ICP accounts that share the same use case and challenges – regardless of their industry or tier. Cluster-based ABM helps to:

- Nurture the entire cluster of ICP accounts despite of the tier
- Create a relevant message and content
- Focus on accounts with a high-probability of becoming a sales opportunity

The visual below shares the difference between cluster and vertical targeting.

## How to select the target cluster of accounts: 6-step framework.

Here’s exactly how to select your target cluster and build your program around it.

### Step 1: Export deals and categorize them by use case.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!vajZ!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fbe425f10-c205-414f-ac0c-a92cd3482785_1600x795.png)

Pull all your qualified deals from the past 12-24 months (adjust based on your sales cycle length).

For each deal, document:

- Industry/vertical
- Business challenge they mentioned (from inquiry forms, discovery transcripts, CRM notes)
- Use case (the specific job-to-be-done they wanted to accomplish)

**Pro tip:** Use AI to extract challenges from call transcripts and inquiry forms, but manually map deals to use cases.

The best use case definition comes from our friends from [FletchPMM](https://www.fletchpmm.com/):

> *Use case is a specific job-to-be-done of a specific buyer persona, with a clear process and desired outcome.*

Examples: “Develop account-based marketing strategy,” “Create expansion program for existing enterprise accounts.”

### Step 2: Analyze revenue metrics to select the right cluster

Don’t just pick the biggest deal cluster. Look at the full picture.

For each use case, calculate:

- Average Contract Value (ACV)
- Lifetime Value (LTV)
- Win rate
- Sales cycle length
- Number of successful case studies

**Example from our analysis:**

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!PKEh!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe576842a-72ea-48b9-994e-97f08133f9d0_1600x348.png)

Which would you choose?

Most teams pick **Use Case 4** (biggest deals). But look at the win rate (17%) and sales cycle (309 days). Starting here means you won’t show results for a year.

**Use Case 2** has lower ACV but **42% win rate and 97-day sales cycle**. If your goal is proving the program works THIS quarter, this is your best bet.

The cluster selection depends on your goals. Need to show traction faster? Go for shorter cycles and higher win rates. Building long-term motion? Focus on highest LTV and retention.

Make this decision WITH sales leadership, not alone.

## How to start with cluster ABM

Don’t start with tactics. Start with fundamentals.

If you’re planning Q1 2026 programs right now, this framework decision—cluster vs. vertical—will determine whether you’re presenting pipeline results or explaining why you need “more time” in your April board meeting.

Before you launch your next ABM program or finalize 2026 campaigns:

1. **Export your deals** from the past 12-24 months
2. **Categorize them by use case**, not just industry
3. **Run the revenue analysis** to see which clusters perform best
4. **Make the cluster selection** with sales leadership (not alone)
5. **Build your qualification criteria** based on real win-loss patterns

This takes 2-3 weeks of focused work. It’s not exciting. It doesn’t involve new AI tools or fancy martech.

But it’s what separates programs that generate pipeline from programs that generate 178 MQLs and zero revenue.

The cluster framework isn’t a new tactic. It’s a different way of thinking about your market—starting with challenges and use cases, not industries and firmographics.

Once you see it, you can’t unseen it. And you won’t target accounts the same way again.
