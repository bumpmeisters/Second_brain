## 3 sales reports in ABM

**Sales Rep to Marketing:** *"Do you want me to track the account engagement because you don't trust me?"*

The trust is not the problem; sales productivity is.  
  
When we launch ABM programs, sales engagement with the target accounts looks like a black box:  
  
*You know that something is happening, but you don't know exactly what. Some emails and messages in -> some replies (in the best case scenario) out.*  
  
But **ABM is not a gumball machin** e.  
  
The average number of value-added touchpoints to generate an enterprise deal is above 250. They must be split between marketing and sales.  
  
Hence, the account engagement process must be streamlined and tracked.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!ExVK!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb73897db-ab02-49a5-b5a5-530d5bd098dc_1329x1200.png)

Here are three areas we ask sales to track on a daily and weekly basis.

#### 1\. ACCOUNT RESEARCH, BUYING COMMITTEE MAPPING, AND WELCOME ENGAGEMENT CADENCE.

Most sales reps stop their engagement at finding a VP of a target department and sending a discovery-pitching connection request on LinkedIn + email.

They almost never connect and engage with the buying committee, and rarely fully research the account. Not to say the insights from the research and engagement are never added to the CRM.

To progress with ABM, you need to define a bare minimum of the buying committee members engaged and # of new accounts (or business units if you do expansion) the sales rep must work on.

#### 2\. PLAYBOOK EXECUTION METRICS.

Most playbooks for sales end up with automated email cadences to senior decision-makers (see p. 1).

But if getting a reply from Enterprise VP / signing him up for the webinar or executive dinner/ commenting on content was that easy, building a pipeline would never be a problem.

You need to make sure that sales executes multi-channel follow-ups, including progressive profiling.

#### 3\. WEEKLY ENGAGEMENT PILLARS.

Everybody talks about the importance of 1st-party and 3rd-party pillars + value-added touchpoints, but I rarely see a system that incentivizes sales reps to do it.

Make sure you track on a weekly basis:

- \# of comments
- \# of conversations started / questions asked
- \# of pieces of content/content hubs distributed
- \# of engagements based on captured signals

The best way to stay top of mind with target accounts is not by setting up quarterly automated emails:

*"I just wanted to check in...".*

The best way is to constantly remind yourself through non-sales touchpoints and follow-ups that add value to the existing thread with the target account.

Peter Drucker once said: *"What gets measured gets managed.”*

It's not about not trusting the sales reps. It's about making your ABM program efficient.

But as you guessed, it requires a significant change of KPIs. I’ll cover them next.

## ABM KPIs for Sales.

First, let’s start with what happens if KPIs for sales are not adjusted.

You don’t need to be Einstein to guess that sales would focus on the primary KPI and execute on what drives the results. ABM goes to the second plan.

To make it visible, here is a real example I’ve witnessed.

The company wants to involve sales in thought leadership to create awareness among target accounts.

Sales are supposed to share and distribute content, engage with the target buyers and run account research.

But their KPIs remain the same: dials, InMails, emails.

Guess what happens next?

Instead of educating, nurturing, engaging and building relationship with the target buyers, sales:

- Publish self-promotion content linking to the website
- Do pitch-slapping and present it as the engagement
- Focus on volume of the activities instead of quality.

The program fails fast because wrong KPIs always lead to the wrong outcomes.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!ut1U!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa27c7ddf-c56f-4b7b-93da-d461fa5693e5_1580x1600.jpeg)

To run ABM properly I adjust KPIs for ABM team (both for marketing and sales) to incentivize the behaviour that is aligned with the current buyer journey stage.

#### CLUSTER ICP ACCOUNTS (not aware of us & product need is unknown).

**Goal**: Creating awareness.

**Leading indicators:**

- Personalized connection requests
- Thoughtful comments
- #of posts produced and published
- Non-sales touchpoints to start a conversation

#### FUTURE PIPELINE ACCOUNTS (Aware of us & product need is unknown).

**Goal**: Engaged buying committee + fully researched account.

**Leading indicators:**

- Fully researched accounts
- Buying committee members enrolled in the playbooks
- Insights collected from 1-1 conversations

#### ACTIVE FOCUS ACCOUNTS (Aware of us & product need is known).

**Goal**: Generating discovery calls.

**Leading indicators:**

- Fully personalized solution offerings
- Personalized account and buyer-persona content
- Signal-based personalized engagement
- Booked "bridge activities": strategy sessions, audits, etc.

> ***Learn more about measuring ABM here:***
> 
> ***[https://fullfunnel.substack.com/p/how-to-measure-abm-w-report-templates](https://fullfunnel.substack.com/p/how-to-measure-abm-w-report-templates)***

You can't make ABM successful if your team still reports on the old KPIs. Make sure that ABM KPIs are aligned with the buyer journey of your target accounts. Otherwise, the program is doomed.

## Real results for account-based SDR: From 0 replies to 41 engaged accounts.

While everything that we share makes sense to sales leaders, they always ask for a proof.

Here are the results of the 90-days pilot ABM program for SDR who moved to account-based marketing team.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!prxO!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4094e8e9-2d34-445d-834b-9389f3f1c3d1_800x438.jpeg)

**Vertical:** Enterprise SaaS (US & Canada) **Buyer persona:** Senior HR leaders

**Before pilot:**

- Motion: Standard cold outreach (asking directly for demo)
- Results: 0 replies and engagement (90 days before the pilot)
- Time allocation: outbound 20% of time, 80% inbound sales

**After pilot:**

- Motion: Account-based marketing
- Results: 41 engaged and researched accounts (vendor aware + some level of relationship with a member of the buying committee)
- Time allocation: outbound 0% of time, ABM 20%, 80% inbound sales

**What made the difference?**

Our Account-Based Sales Rep worked closely with marketing and subject matter experts on account list building, qualification, scoring and prioritization.

Instead of spray-and-pray outreach, every touchpoint was researched, personalized, and aligned with the buyer journey stage. The focus shifted from pitching to relationship building and value creation.

**TLDR;**

Enterprise opportunities require 250+ touchpoints before engagement. If you just rely on “trust me” conversations instead of looking at account engagement numbers, you’ll fail fast.

It’s not about lack of trust. It’s about making visible your input and consider ROI based on data, not the gut feeling.
