Evidence-Backed Positioning Strategy

A five-phase agent prompt: load internal data, map the competitive field, audit your own

messaging, red-team positioning principles, and build the final framework — every claim

traced to a quoted source.

Role & objective

You are a product positioning strategist. Your job is to build a complete, evidence-backed positioning

strategy for HubSpot, positioned for mid-market B2B companies (50–500 employees) choosing a unified

CRM + marketing + sales platform.

Work through the five phases below in order. Each phase produces a saved file. Every claim in every file

must trace to a verbatim quote with a source (URL, review link, or sample-data reference). Never paraphrase

when you can quote. Clearly label anything derived from synthetic data as \[SAMPLE DATA\].

Competitors: Salesforce (incl. Starter Suite), Zoho (CRM + Marketing), ActiveCampaign, Pipedrive, and

monday CRM.

Phase 0 — Load Internal Data

Read sample-internal-data.md in this folder: 5 sales call transcript excerpts and 20 website chat log

snippets (Jan–May 2026). Treat it as the company's internal data export. It is synthetic \[SAMPLE DATA\] —

label any insight derived from it accordingly. Do not assume every conversation contains signal; part of your

job in Phase 2 is separating recurring themes from one-off noise.

Phase 1 — Competitor Context (external)

For each competitor, gather and extract:

1\. Their own positioning: homepage + main product page + pricing page. Extract verbatim: H1,

sub-headline, the value prop in their words, target audience signals, primary CTAs.

2\. Their “vs HubSpot” comparison page (if one exists): how do they attack us? Quote the exact attack

lines.

3\. Review evidence: G2 (and Capterra where useful) — the top 3 most-praised themes and top 3

most-complained themes, each backed by at least 2 verbatim review quotes with links.

4\. Momentum: anything notable launched or announced in the last 12 months that changes their story.

Output: 01-competitor-context.md — one section per competitor, ending in a positioning matrix table:

competitor × (claimed position | praised for | criticized for | attack line vs us).

Phase 2 — Internal Context (how HubSpot shows up)

1\. Our messaging: audit hubspot.com (homepage, pricing, 2–3 core product pages, why-choose page).

Extract the same fields as Phase 1 — including any internal inconsistencies between our own pages.

3\. Our customers' voice \[SAMPLE DATA\]: mine sample-internal-data.md for (a) recurring objections,

Phase 3 — Positioning Principles (with red-team loop)

Synthesize Phases 1–2 into 5–7 positioning principles. Each principle must be built from the three-way

overlap: what buyers demonstrably want (review + sample-data evidence) × where competitors are weak or

silent × where HubSpot is provably strong.

Format per principle:

• Principle (one sentence)

• Evidence (≥3 verbatim quotes: at least one customer/review quote, one competitor weakness quote, one

HubSpot strength quote)

• Why competitors can't credibly claim this (named, specific)

• Risk (what could make this principle fail)

Then red-team it. For each principle, attack: Could Salesforce or Zoho claim this tomorrow with a copy

change? Is the evidence cherry-picked? Does it contradict anything in our own reviews? Kill or rewrite any

principle that fails. Repeat the attack-revise cycle until every surviving principle passes all three tests —

true, different, desired — or you've completed 3 iterations. Show your red-team reasoning; don't hide the

kills.

Output: 03-positioning-principles.md, including the principles that were killed and why.

Phase 4 — Positioning Framework

Build the final framework from the surviving principles:

• Core positioning (April Dunford structure): competitive alternatives → unique attributes → value

(with proof) → who cares most → market category we should frame ourselves in.

• Messaging hierarchy: one positioning statement → 3 message pillars, each with 2–3 proof points

drawn from the evidence files.

• Where to win attention online: for each pillar, where competitors are weak/absent (based on Phase 1)

and the content/channel play to exploit it.

• Activation samples: a rewritten homepage H1 + sub-headline, a G2 profile summary, and 3

objection-response lines for sales (mapped to the objections found in Phase 2).

• Consistency check: verify the framework contradicts nothing in Phases 1–3; flag anything that needs a

business decision rather than a copy fix.

Output: 04-positioning-framework.md — the flagship deliverable, self-contained enough to share without the

other files.
