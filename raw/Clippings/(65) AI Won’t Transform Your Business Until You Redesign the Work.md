# (65) AI Won’t Transform Your Business Until You Redesign the Work

> Quelle: https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ai-wont-transform-your-business-until-you-redesign-work-tiffani-bova-zozdc/
> Veröffentlicht: 
> Geclippt am: 2026-08-21

Most organizations are approaching AI as a layer added onto existing work rather than an opportunity to rethink how work actually happens. They are experimenting with tools that can summarize meetings, draft emails, generate reports, or automate isolated tasks. Those improvements may create pockets of efficiency, but many companies are discovering that adding AI on top of existing systems does not automatically make the business operate better.

A recent MIT Sloan *Ideas Made to Matter* article explores research showing that the greatest value from AI often comes from redesigning workflows entirely. The opportunity lies in examining how work moves across teams, where bottlenecks slow progress, which handoffs create unnecessary friction, and how people and technology can work together more effectively.

When workflows remain fragmented, employees often become the bridge holding disconnected systems and processes together. That creates a problem that many organizations underestimate.

### Employees Experience the Friction First

Customers may eventually see faster service or quicker responses. Employees experience the operational reality immediately. They experience whether workflows have become simpler or harder to navigate. They experience whether technology reduces unnecessary work or simply redistributes it into new forms. They also experience whether leadership took the time to redesign the process itself or simply layered AI onto systems that were already difficult to manage.

This influences adoption more than many organizations realize. When AI is introduced into workflows that already involve too many approvals and disconnected systems, employees often spend significant time correcting errors and managing additional coordination. At that point, the organization may appear more efficient externally while becoming more operationally strained internally.

### Workflow Design Matters More Than Task Automation

AI does not need to outperform humans at every task to create meaningful business value. In many cases, the larger gains come from improving how work moves across the organization. Reducing operational bottlenecks can create far more impact than automating one isolated activity at a time.

Organizations seeing stronger long-term results are asking different questions:

🔶 Where does work repeatedly stall?

🔶 Which handoffs create unnecessary friction?

🔶 Which workflows frustrate employees and customers alike?

🔶 Where are teams spending too much time coordinating instead of contributing value?

Those questions tend to produce better transformation outcomes because they focus on the structure of the work itself.

### Better Workflows Create Space for Better Work

When workflows improve, employees spend less time buried in repetitive administrative tasks and operational friction. That creates more capacity for the kinds of contributions that still rely heavily on human judgment. Teams can spend more time helping customers navigate complex situations, solving problems collaboratively, and making thoughtful decisions that require experience and context.

Technology alone rarely creates lasting differentiation. Most AI tools will eventually become widely accessible. What separates organizations over time is how effectively they integrate those tools into the way people work together.

The companies seeing the strongest results from AI are taking the time to rethink how work flows across the organization and where employees create the greatest value. That work is slower than simply adopting new software. It is also where the larger opportunity exists.

AI can alter the way work is performed, but it is leadership that decides whether the work experience genuinely improves.
