## The problem behind ABM: what we consider ABM vs what it actually is.

Jaleh hit the nail on the head describing the real problem with ABM.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!mr64!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4c361c7a-9481-4a49-afa8-8b43fc068dc4_1010x1060.png)

There's no unified definition of what ABM actually is. Some think it's just targeting accounts with AI-personalized outbound and ads. Others believe it's a complete overhaul of your go-to-market strategy.

This confusion leads to subpar implementation and disappointing results.

The truth is, ABM isn't programmatic ads + automated outbound sequences enriched by AI sales tools to a wish list of 1000+ accounts.

It's not a substitution for demand generation and awareness.

And it’s not a marketing-only owned initiative where sales only show up for the qualified discovery calls.

To change the game (quoting Jaleh), we MUST:

1. **Give every account 1:1 marketing:**

“Insert company name” doesn't work for enterprise accounts. You have to pull in sales’ account knowledge and do research to customize your ads, website, and emails for each account.

2. **Unify sales and marketing:**

Talk accounts, not funnels with sales. The old head of ABM at Zoom sent weekly emails to each rep explaining what ABM did for their accounts that week. Identify and follow up on engaged contacts. Involve sales in messaging. Support their multithreading.

Below I’ll share the key insights from our interview with CMOs on how to achieve this.

## 7 Core Pillars for ABM Success

Based on real experiences from our panel, here are the five essential components every effective ABM program needs:

### 1\. Define Your ABM Strategy

A well-defined ABM strategy is the foundation upon which everything else is built. Here are the 8 essential pillars that must be in place *(we reviewed them in more detail in different posts, I’ll add link).*

**[1\. ABM Goals:](https://fullfunnel.io/abm-strategy/)**

There are 4 distinct goals ABM helps to achieve:

- Net new revenue - generate sales opportunities
- Pipeline acceleration - win stalled opportunities
- Expansion - upselling or cross-selling to existing customers
- Renewal/Churn prevention - renewing contracts with key customers

Make sure you are aligned on the priority goal, especially for the pilot program.

**[2\. Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)](https://fullfunnel.substack.com/p/ideal-customer-profile-for-abm)** [including](https://fullfunnel.substack.com/p/ideal-customer-profile-for-abm)

- Firmographics
- Buying committee
- Account qualification criteria
- Account segmentation approach
- Account enrichment process

**[3\. Account List Building](https://fullfunnel.substack.com/p/selecting-accounts-for-the-abm-program)**[.](https://fullfunnel.substack.com/p/selecting-accounts-for-the-abm-program)

Make sure all accounts are qualified and fit your ICP. Avoid broad targeting or creating a wishlist.

**[4\. Account Development Playbook](https://fullfunnel.io/account-based-marketing-playbook/)**

**6\. Team and Skillset.**

A lean ABM team setup includes 5 core functions:

- Senior ABM manager
- Researcher
- Copywriter
- Designer
- SDR

**6\. Technology Stack.**

If you're building a pilot ABM motion, don't buy expensive stack. Start with Sales Navigator and website traffic reveal software.

If you have robust processes and are scaling ABM, consider more expensive software to enhance your processes.

**[7\. Reporting and Tracking](https://fullfunnel.substack.com/p/how-to-measure-abm-w-report-templates)**

- Define key metrics and leading indicators for your program.
- Create simple dashboards to track campaign performance.
- Align everybody on reporting.

> *"You have to start with the strategy piece. First, clearly defining your ICPs, your right-fit clients, and making sure that marketing and sales are aligned on who they are and how you're qualifying them. Then really understand what their needs and challenges are, their own buyer journey."* – **Amy Loesch**

### 2\. Source Engaged Accounts from Demand Generation

Forget the wishlist of accounts you'd like to work with. Focus on accounts already showing engagement signals.

**What to do:**

- Identify accounts that hit a specific engagement threshold
- Look for accounts that spent significant time on your website
- Track accounts checking pricing or product pages
- Monitor webinar registrations and attendance from target companies

> *"It's really stopped that spraying of contacting people and just seeing what comes back. We're a lot more focused in terms of the companies and people we're reaching out to. It's given the SDR much clearer direction about who to target and what to do with those people."* – **Joseph Threlfall**

### 3\. Conduct in-depth account research.

- For each target account, understand:
	- Account key initiatives, strategy, and goals
		- The role of every buying committee member in strategic initiatives, their KPIs, needs, and challenges
		- Value proposition mapping: how exactly your product fits their needs and what value it brings
	> *"You need to have a trigger to really prove that everything you did as homework at the beginning makes sense and is interesting for your target audience. These ideas must stand out. They must be different, surprising. They need to provide a sense of personalized approach."* – **Tomasz Brzakala**

### 4\. Implement Strategic Account Warm-Up

Forget the old way of uploading accounts to LinkedIn ABM software, running display ads, tracking engagement, and reaching out. This rarely works in 2025.

Create a clear plan for:

- Warm-up: how marketing and sales will create awareness inside buying committee, connect and engage with them
- Activation: how sales should activate accounts
- Nurturing: how marketing and sales will stay in touch with target accounts

**What to do:**

- **Content collaboration**
	- Invite target accounts to your podcast
		- Feature them or get quotes for content
		- Run market research with them
		- Host educational webinars together
- **Diversified event strategy**
	- 1:few educational webinars for specific verticals or roles
		- 1: many cluster webinars to educate on solving typical challenges
		- Micro-events with thought leaders to connect with target accounts
- **Social engagement**
	- Leave meaningful comments to attract attention
		- Connect and move conversations to DMs
		- Tag or feature target accounts in your posts

> *"One thing that's been really helpful is content repurposing. Our SDRs have been reaching out to key contacts at our target accounts and asking them for input on our content. People really enjoy being asked those types of questions to showcase their expertise. And then it also fuels our content as well. It's a win-win on both sides."* – **Briana Manrique**

### 5\. Create Personalized Account Activation

Once you've built awareness and relationships, it's time for activation that converts interest into opportunities.

**What to do:**

- Match insights from account research with personalized proposals
- Create specific messaging for each buying committee member
- Develop business cases that help champions sell internally
- Provide content that addresses specific concerns for each stakeholder

### 6\. Create for sales account-based content.

The best marketing teams:

- Create content frameworks sales can personalize
- Focus on business problems rather than product features
- Adapt content based on which accounts are currently engaging
- Provide high-value, educational material sales can share
- Create account-specific one-sheeters and relevant case studies

As Customs4Trade Enterprise AE Ra noted: *"The team responsible for ABM helped us massively to start building a content library with good quality content, be more flexible on it, and helped everybody join in doing what we are doing."*

**Check sales perspective on ABM here:**

### 7\. Set up weekly meetings.

We set a weekly cadence of 60-mins Monday meetings.

Agenda:

- SDR shares the insights from the conversations with the target accounts and account research. Next, share what he/she might need for particular accounts to proceed with nurturing or activation.
- AE shares the insights from pipeline deals or conversations with new buying committee groups of the existing accounts. Same, next, he/she shares the needs and ideas to influence the deals.
- RevOps share the account engagement insights from the past week (high-intent pages visits, event sign-ups, new high-engaged accounts, etc.).
- ABM lead structures and prioritizes with the team all collected insights and creates a sprint for the next week.
- The team reviews the action plan from the past week, KPIs and weekly report, defines the bottlenecks and small wins, and plans the next week.

Outcomes:

- Exact content the content team should create to drive new pipeline, nurture accounts or accelerate existing deals
- Accounts & buyers SDR should engage with the particular actions, not "I wanted to check in"
- Actions for AE

> *"We pull together a weekly report. We present both the qualitative and quantitative results. We track the replies we've had from people on LinkedIn, the amount of LinkedIn followers and responses from the SDR. That's fortunately going up every week, so those are nice indications that things are working."* – **Joseph Threlfall**

## How to Get Buy-In from Leadership and Sales

Securing buy-in from leadership and sales teams is often the biggest challenge when implementing ABM. Here's how to get it right:

### Start with a focused pilot

Start small to prove the concept before scaling. Create a business case with clear objectives and timelines.

> *"What helps is a small steps strategy, meaning to propose testing a new approach with a small team or a specific period of time—call it a pilot campaign. It's crucial to create a strong presentation of this proposed approach upfront. Essentially, build a business case for the pilot."* – **Tomasz Brzakala**

### Clarify what ABM is (and isn't)

Many teams misunderstand what ABM actually entails. Take time to educate stakeholders.

> *"What was important for us was to actually help everyone understand what ABM is not. There was a perception that it was all marketing-led, marketing-owned, marketing-executed, and then sales would be on the receiving end. We spent a lot of time upfront explaining that it's a collaborative effort."* – **Briana Manrique**

### Show it's complementary, not additional work

Frame ABM as a more effective way of doing existing work, not an added burden.

> *"The one change in perception was that ABM was complementary. It was not additional. Management thought it would be taking people away from doing their daily work in addition to having to do ABM, when really it isn't. It's just changing the process of what they should be doing daily anyway."* – **Joseph Threlfall**

### Highlight small wins consistently

Build momentum by showcasing every positive interaction and result.

> *"I think what was helpful was showing those little wins every single day so that it kind of built up like, 'Oh, this is working.' It was definitely uncomfortable at times. Some of the tactics that we tried was definitely a new muscle for us. But the more wins that we could add up every single day, the more buy-in we began to have into the strategy."* – **Briana Manrique**

## Don't Fall for "Light ABM" - Go All In

**When we asked if companies should consider a "light" or partial ABM approach, every CMO replied: go all in or don't bother.**

> *"You have to go with the fully structured ABM program. It's your new way of working together. It all works together, so there aren't any shortcuts."* – **Amy Loesch**
> 
> *"I've tried it both ways and did not see success from a more light approach. We've seen the most results from a fully structured campaign. When we lean out of it due to bandwidth or other priorities, we don't see results. When we lean back in, we do."* – **Briana Manrique**

## Actionable Takeaways

1. **Start small and focused** - Begin with a pilot program with a cross-functional team.
2. **Secure proper alignment** - Ensure sales and marketing agree on targets, approach, and expectations.
3. **Transform your SDR function** - Move from volume to quality engagement with the target accounts.
4. **Create content that enables buyers** - Help them sell your solution internally.
5. **Measure and celebrate incremental wins** - Track both engagement and pipeline metrics.
6. **Go all in** - Half-measures don't work in ABM.

Remember: ABM is not a silver bullet but a strategic motion requiring complete alignment between marketing and sales. What you put in is what you'll get out.

The success of ABM lies not in your stack or budget, but in strong fundamentals, strategic and executional alignment with sales, subject-matter experts, client success and product, and leadership buy-in.
