## How to change sellers’ mindset from “always be pitching” to “always be helping”.

Before we get into tactics, let me address the elephant in the room. None of them work if your sales team treats every buyer interaction as an opportunity to pitch.

The mindset shift we ask about isn’t “stop selling.” It’s “stop pitching and start helping.” Every touchpoint should add value — share a relevant insight, ask about their priorities, offer a useful resource. Not “we help companies like yours with X, want a demo?”

We, B2B marketers, know this well. But how can we make this shift happen?

Obviously, not just by telling sales to “be more human and adding value.” This is useless advice.

Instead, we need to make it easier for them to be helpful than to pitch. Give them content worth sharing. Give them research worth referencing. Give them signals that tell them when and why to reach out.

When a seller has a real POV on the buyer’s challenges, a relevant case study to share, and a legitimate reason to reach out — they naturally stop pitching. They have something better to say.

Let me share 5 ways we enable social selling at [Fullfunnel.io](http://fullfunnel.io/) with our clients.

## 2\. Build the buyer research and POV that sellers don’t have time to create.

I recently had a chat with Lauren Goodell, a former enterprise AE at Microsoft and Salesforce and founder [Zinnia](https://www.getzinnia.com/) (the recording is below). She made a point that stuck with me:

> *“The biggest deals I won — I had done the research. I knew the name of my buyer’s dogs. I knew how the CFO was compensated. I knew the company’s biggest initiatives and how we could help.”*

Every sales rep knows they need to do this granular research, but they don’t have time to do this for every account. I firmly believe this is where marketing can help a lot.  
  
Here is what works for us.  
  
**1\. Standardize account research and the insights we need to collect.**  
  
Next, we define where to store the information and what insights should marketing bring:  
  
\-historical interactions,  
\- intent data,  
\- social engagement  
  
**2\. Prepare account briefs that include strategic initiatives and priorities from press releases, earning calls, and corporate reports.**  
  
What are the key initiatives?  
What’s the org structure?  
What technology gaps exist?  
  
**3\. We ask sales reps to focus on buying committee mapping.**  
  
They must identify:  
\- champions  
\- decision-makers  
\- power users  
\- influencers  
  
with their individual KPIs, success metrics, and potential challenges.  
  
Not just find “VP of IT”, but define a concrete VP of IT at this specific company who might be the right buyer.  
  
**4\. We prepare together POV (point of view) documents for each target account that outline:**  
  
\- what buyers are trying to achieve  
\- what’s blocking them,  
\- what alternatives they might consider  
\- how we can help them get there faster.

Here is an example.

When this planning happens together, we observed drastically better alignment and willingness to collaborate with marketing.  
  
Sales reps clearly see the solution they can provide to a target account. They see how marketing can support in creating awareness and nurturing the buying committee group.  
  
They’re no longer “another vendor asking for 15 minutes.” They’re someone who understands the buyer’s world and has a solution.

**3\. Set up weekly sync meetings and bring first-party signals.**

Most GTM teams are dealing with **[account blindness problems.](https://fullfunnel.substack.com/p/account-blindness)**

Sales are often unaware of marketing touchpoints with the target accounts. Did they engage with a specific ad? Was there a spike in engagement from a specific account? Did a buying committee member attend a webinar?

> One enterprise SDR described it perfectly to me: *“Marketing assigns me contacts without any context. I don’t know what happened before and why I’d follow up. I tried following up with some and they didn’t respond. So I stopped.”*

> Meanwhile, marketing is saying: *“We’re warming up all these accounts and passing leads to sales, but they ignore them.”*

To fix it we host with our clients **weekly ABM sync meetings** where both teams review the same accounts, share signals, and decide together what’s next.

Here is a great anecdote from one of our clients: [Tyk.io](http://tyk.io/).

Marketing prepares a list of accounts that visited high-intent pages on the website: pricing and book a demo. When the team was reviewing an account, a product marketer recognized the account and mentioned, “I know a person from that company — he’s in our Discord community. Let me message him there and ask about their initiatives.”

Within two hours, sales received a personalized intro to the champion. This insight would’ve stayed buried if they’d worked in silos.

## 4\. Enable multi-threading across the buying committee and the buyer ecosystem.

In enterprise deals, you’re not selling to one person. You’re engaging a buying committee of 7+ people with different priorities. The CMO cares about brand, the CFO cares about ROI, procurement cares about risk mitigation, the end user cares about workflows.

What most marketing teams miss: **multi-threading is not just a sales activity. Marketing should be enabling it — both inside the account and through the buyer ecosystem around it.**

Here is how.

**Marketer-to-marketer engagement opens doors sales can’t open alone.** When we feature an executive from a target account in our content — a podcast, a market research report, a newsletter quote — we don’t stop with that one person.

> We reach out to their marketing colleagues: *“Hey, we featured your VP of Marketing in our research on AI best practices. Thought you might enjoy it. If you want, we can do co-promotion, or here’s a quote you can share on your company’s LinkedIn page.”*

One of our former clients, Renan Oliveira from CrossKnowledge, uses this approach systematically with their podcast.

Every time they feature an executive from a large European corporation, he reaches out to their marketing team with shareable assets. Several of these companies shared the podcast from their corporate pages — some with 500k+ followers — creating massive engagement and visibility CrossKnowledge couldn’t have reached on their own.

**Read our full case study here: [https://fullfunnel.io/abm-pilot-program/](https://fullfunnel.io/abm-pilot-program/)**

**But don’t stop at direct account engagement. Map and engage the buyer ecosystem.**

The B2B buyer ecosystem includes communities and associations where your buyers learn, industry media and thought leaders they trust, partners they work with, and peer networks they rely on.

When we were writing our book about full-funnel marketing, we interviewed VPs of Marketing at enterprise B2B SaaS companies about their AI challenges.

But we didn’t stop with direct interviews. We invited industry thought leaders to participate. We created multiple assets featuring all participants:

- a report
- series of LinkedIn post
- [a newsletter](https://fullfunnel.substack.com/p/ai-in-enterprise-b2b-marketing)

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!yjsq!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdef20abb-ef57-44b1-916f-42fee7d5cf2f_1022x1378.png)

The participants engaged by commenting, sharing from their profiles, and sharing with colleagues. This gave us exposure among strategic buyers through channels we couldn’t have accessed directly.

**Partners can amplify the ecosystem playbook even further.**

If you have a channel or partner ecosystem, your partners already have relationships with target accounts. Joint events, co-created content, and shared distribution multiply your reach.

Instead of only direct outreach to a buying committee member, you can reach them through a trusted partner who already has a relationship with them.

## But what if our buyers aren’t active on LinkedIn?

Of course, not every industry has buyers scrolling LinkedIn. Running ads and trying social selling there is useless.

It doesn’t mean that the tactics we’ve shared don’t work. You just need to adjust them to the channels where your buyers us

Take our client TestRail (Gurock Software). Their target audience was QA engineers. These people don’t hang out on LinkedIn as actively as sales and marketing.

Instead of LinkedIn, TestRail focused on the Ministry of Testing community, QA-focused Reddit threads, and technical newsletters. They used educational technical content instead to create awareness. They ran an expert-led webinar focused on best practices for test automation attracting 500+ sign ups including 72 strategic enterprise accounts. and driving 22 discovery calls afterwards.

> As João Crisóstomo, their Enterprise Product Marketing Manager, says: *“We found QA teams in places where they actually engage — Slack, Reddit, and industry newsletters.”*
