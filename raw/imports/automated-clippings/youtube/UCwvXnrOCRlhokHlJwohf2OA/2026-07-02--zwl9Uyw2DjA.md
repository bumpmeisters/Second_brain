A few weeks ago Google Cloud announced the Open Knowledge Format, or OKF — a vendor-neutral open standard that, in their words, "formalizes the LLM-wiki pattern" into a portable folder of markdown for handing an AI agent the curated context it needs. 

And from looking at reaction online, I can see it's already split into two camps: one side says Google just took Andrej Karpathy's LLM-wiki idea, put a schema on top and stamped their name on it; the other says this is the first genuine open standard for agent knowledge.

So I read the spec, pointed Codex at my own full-stack app and asked it to walk the codebase and generate a full knowledge layer: one concept per markdown file, each one pointing at where the live code actually lives rather than copying it. Around 45 concept files, cross-linked into a browsable graph. 

In this video I walk through what I got and what's actually going on underneath.
