# How GenAI and LLMs are Changing B2B Buyer Research, and How to Respond

November 25, 2025schedule34 minBy Dan Hieb

![B2B buyer researching vendors on his laptop from home.](https://6sense.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/B2B-Buyer-researching-vendors-on-his-laptop-from-home-768x432.jpg)

Ever since ChatGPT entered the business mainstream, B2B marketing circles have been gripped by low-level panic. “All search traffic is going to disappear.” “Buyers won’t visit websites anymore.” “Intent data is dead.”

**Here’s what 6sense’s actual research on nearly 4,000 B2B buyers tells us:** Yes, things are changing. No, it’s not the apocalypse everyone fears.

Our [2025 Buyer Experience Report](https://6sense.com/science-of-b2b/buyer-experience-report-2025/) represents three years of longitudinal data tracking how B2B buyers actually behave — not how pundits think they behave. These are real buyers making real purchasing decisions with a median value of $300,000 to $400,000. They’re navigating complex buying committees averaging 10 people, evaluating multiple vendors over nearly a year, and yes, using Large Language Models (LLMs) like ChatGPT and Claude along the way.

![Graphic displays data from the following paragraph: 94% of buyers now use LLMs during their buying journey.](https://6sense.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Science-of-B2B-LLMs-Boom-Stat-Graphic-1024x1024.png)

But here’s the critical finding that should reshape how you think about AI’s impact: **94% of buyers now use LLMs during their buying journey, yet they maintain the same number of interactions with vendors as they did before AI tools became widespread**. That’s right: 16 interactions per person with the winning vendor in 2025, compared to 17 in 2024 and 16 in 2023.

LLMs aren’t replacing vendor engagement. They’re complementing it.

This whitepaper cuts through the hysteria with data-driven insights about what’s actually changing in B2B buyer behavior, what’s staying remarkably consistent, and how revenue leaders should respond.

We’ll explore how LLMs work, where they fit in the buyer journey, and why platforms like 6sense, built specifically to capture the “dark funnel” of anonymous buyer research, become even more valuable as AI tools expand.

If you’re a CMO, VP of Sales, or demand generation leader trying to separate signal from noise in the AI era, this report provides the roadmap you need.

## **Chapter 1: What 6sense data reveals: the 60/40 shift**

Let’s start with the headline finding from our 2025 research: Buyers are contacting vendors earlier than before. But not for the reasons you might think.

### **The numbers tell a surprising story**

For three years, 6sense has tracked a consistent pattern we call the “70/30 journey.” Buyers completed roughly 70% of their evaluation independently before first contacting a vendor, then used the final 30% for validation and final selection.

In 2025, that shifted to what we’re calling the “60/40 journey.”

**Point of First Contact (POFC) moved from 69% to 61% of journey completion.** In practical terms, buyers now reach out to vendors about 3.5 weeks sooner than they did in previous years, at 26.4 weeks into their buying journey versus 30 weeks.

This sounds like what you’d expect if LLMs were making vendor content obsolete, right? Buyers getting all their answers from ChatGPT and only contacting vendors at the last possible moment?

Wrong.

### **Why buyers are actually engaging earlier**

When we dug into the data, we discovered the shift has almost nothing to do with LLM efficiency replacing vendor interactions.

Instead, **nearly 58% of buyers told us they engaged vendors earlier specifically to validate how AI capabilities were being implemented in the solutions they were evaluating**.

Think about it from the buyer’s perspective. Your company is about to spend $300,000+ on a software solution. The vendor’s marketing materials claim their product now has “AI-powered” features. But what does that actually mean? How was it trained? What data does it use? How much will it cost? What are the privacy implications?

These questions can’t be answered by ChatGPT. They require direct vendor engagement.

As Kerry Cunningham, 6sense’s Chief Research Officer, explains in his analysis “ [The LLM-Ate-the-Dark-Funnel Hysteria: Let’s All Take a Breath Shall We](https://6sense.com/science-of-b2b/the-llm-ate-the-dark-funnel-hysteria-lets-take-a-breath-shall-we/)“:

_“For companies experiencing a decline in web traffic attributable to LLMs, it is likely the traffic that has gone missing is not from in-market buyers, but from the professionally curious and from future buyers. This is not to say that marketers should be unconcerned about the decline of web traffic. However, that decline is unlikely to impact current pipeline production.”_

### **What hasn’t changed (and what that tells us)**

Here’s what remained remarkably stable in our 2025 data:

- **Average buying group size: 10.1 people** (compared to 10.4 in prior years)
- **Interactions with winning vendor: 16 per person** (compared to 17 in 2024, 16 in 2023)
- **Vendors evaluated: 5.1** (up slightly from 4.5, indicating more thorough research)
- **Vendors with prior experience: 3.9** (meaning 76% of shortlisted vendors are already known)
- **Buyer-initiated contact: 79%** (buyers still control the timing)
- **Winner contacted first: 77%** (down from 83%, but still dominant)

These patterns reveal something crucial: **LLMs are tools buyers use within an existing evaluation process, not replacements for that process**. The fundamental dynamics of B2B buying (committee decision-making, extensive vendor validation, and risk mitigation) remain intact.

### **The global perspective**

Our 2025 research covered buyers across North America, APAC, Europe, and the UK. While regional variations exist (UK buyers contact vendors earliest at 51.5% of journey completion, North American buyers latest at 64.3%), the overall pattern holds globally. B2B buying remains a high-touch, relationship-driven process that requires human validation of high-stakes decisions.

The implications for B2B revenue teams are clear: Don’t restructure your entire go-to-market strategy around the assumption that buyers will stop engaging with vendors. Instead, understand where LLMs fit in the journey and optimize the reality of how they’re actually being used.

## **Chapter 2: Where LLMs fit in the buyer journey**

Understanding when and how buyers use AI tools is critical to adapting your strategy effectively. Our 2025 research provides specific insights into LLM usage patterns that challenge common assumptions.

### **LLMs complement, they don’t replace**

The [2025 Buyer Experience Report](https://6sense.com/science-of-b2b/buyer-experience-report-2025/) found that **94% of B2B buyers use LLMs during their buying process.** That’s a staggering adoption rate for any technology, let alone one that’s been mainstream for less than two years. Research from [Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/the-future-of-b2b-buying-will-come-slowly-and-then-all-at-once/) confirms this trend, showing that adoption went from essentially zero in January 2024 to 89% by mid-2024, with [87% of users reporting better business outcomes](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/the-future-of-b2b-buying-will-come-slowly-and-then-all-at-once/) from using generative AI.

But here’s what matters more: **Despite near-universal LLM adoption, buyers reported the same number of interactions with vendors as in prior years**. An average of 16 touchpoints per person with the winning vendor, which is unchanged from 2023 and down just one from 2024’s 17.

Why? Because buyers don’t trust LLMs alone for decisions of this magnitude.

### **When  buyers use LLMs**

Our research reveals that **buyers use LLMs primarily in the middle of their journey, not at the beginning or end**. This surprised us until we considered buyer experience levels.

In the 2025 data, **85% of buyers had direct prior experience with the vendors they evaluated**— the highest level we’ve recorded. For established product categories, buyers already know who the major players are before they start researching. They don’t need ChatGPT to tell them “the leading CRM platforms are Salesforce, HubSpot, and Microsoft Dynamics.” They already know that.

Where LLMs prove valuable is in the **comparison and synthesis phase**. Buyers ask things like:

- “What are the key differences between Salesforce and HubSpot for mid-market B2B companies?”
- “Create a comparison table of marketing automation platforms by feature set and pricing”
- “What are the implementation timelines for enterprise CRM systems?”

These queries help buyers become more informed before engaging vendors, leading to higher-quality conversations. As HubSpot’s [2024 survey](https://blog.hubspot.com/sales/b2b-buyers) found, 48% of buyers now use AI tools to research software, and 98% of those users say it’s been impactful to their process.

### **The validation imperative**

Here’s the crucial insight: **Buyers use LLMs to inform their evaluation, not to make their decision**.

![87% of sellers believe they provide transparency to buyers.](https://6sense.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/seller-transparency-1024x1024.png)

![Only 37% of buyers agree that sellers provide enough transparency, which is a key reason they rely on peer validation and review sites.](https://6sense.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/buyer-suspicion-1024x1024.png)

The average purchase in our study was $300,000 to $400,000. That’s a significant investment for any organization, and a significant risk for individuals making vendor selections. Even if LLMs were perfectly trustworthy sources of information (which they’re not, as we’ll explore), buyers would still verify everything through direct vendor engagement.

This validation behavior shows up clearly in broader market research. [TrustRadius found that 90% of buyers](https://solutions.trustradius.com/vendor-blog/bridging-the-trust-gap-b2b-tech-buying-in-the-age-of-ai/) click through to verify information provided by AI tools. They’re using AI to become better-informed buyers who ask more sophisticated questions during vendor interactions, but they’re not putting blind trust into what AI tells them.

### **Generational adoption patterns**

The data does show significant generational differences in AI adoption. [TrustRadius’ 2025 research](https://solutions.trustradius.com/vendor-blog/bridging-the-trust-gap-b2b-tech-buying-in-the-age-of-ai/#:~:text=Notably%2C%20the%20Gen%20Z%20buyers,%E2%80%9Conline%E2%80%9D%20than%20other%20generations.) found that Gen Z buyers show 2x adoption rates compared to overall averages, using AI “a lot” at 15% versus 8% across all demographics. With [64% of B2B decision-makers](https://research.g2.com/hubfs/2024-buyer-behavior-report.pdf) making purchases now Millennials or Gen Z, this behavioral shift will only intensify.

But, even amongst the highest AI adopters, the validation pattern holds. [G2’s data shows](https://research.g2.com/hubfs/2024-buyer-behavior-report.pdf) that AI Power-Users spend 30% of their time on research versus 36% for others — they’re more efficient, but they’re not skipping vendor engagement. They’re compressing timelines while maintaining the same validation touchpoints.

The takeaway for revenue teams: LLMs are accelerating buyer education, not eliminating the need for vendor relationships. The buyers engaging with you are better informed and further along in their evaluation, which should improve conversation quality and deal velocity.

## **Chapter 3: The trust landscape: Peers over vendors and analysts**

Understanding who buyers trust (and don’t trust) is essential to adapting your content and engagement strategy for the AI era. Our research reveals a dramatic trust shift that’s reshaping where buyers look for information.

### **The decline of traditional authority**

For years, B2B buyers relied on analyst firms like Gartner and Forrester to validate vendor selection. That’s changing rapidly.

[TrustRadius documented a stunning 60% decline](https://solutions.trustradius.com/vendor-blog/bridging-the-trust-gap-b2b-tech-buying-in-the-age-of-ai/) in analyst report consultation since 2022. Only 14% of buyers now consult analyst reports during their research, as buyers increasingly question “pay-to-play” research models where vendors fund the analysts evaluating them.

Vendor content faces similar skepticism. [TrustRadius reports](https://go.trustradius.com/rs/827-FOI-687/images/2024%20B2B%20Buying%20Disconnect%20Year%20of%20the%20Brand%20Crisis.pdf) that while 87% of vendors believe they are transparent about their products’ limitations, only 37% of buyers agree.

Our 2025 Buyer Experience Report provides context for this skepticism: **Buyers consumed an average of 13 content pieces during their journey** — eight from vendors, but critically, five from third parties. Those external sources often carried greater weight in final decisions, even though buyers consumed more vendor content by volume.

### **The rise of peer validation**

As trust in traditional authorities erodes, buyers are turning to peer experiences and independent review platforms.

[G2’s annual research](https://research.g2.com/hubfs/2024-buyer-behavior-report.pdf) shows that public product review websites jumped from 13% of buyers’ most consulted source in 2021 to 31% in 2024.

Review sites now outrank analyst firms at every stage of the buying journey. According to [G2’s 2024 Buyer Behavior Report](https://research.g2.com/hubfs/2024-buyer-behavior-report.pdf), buyers trust independent review sites more than analyst firms during discovery (51% vs. 45%), research (48% vs. 46%), evaluation (50% vs. 46%), and final decision (47% vs. 45%).

This shift connects directly to the 6sense data showing that **buyers evaluate an average of 5.1 vendors but already have prior experience with 3.9 of them**. When entering a new buying cycle, buyers start with a consideration set built from their experience, peer recommendations, and review site research — not vendor marketing or analyst reports.

### **How LLMs amplify the trust shift**

Here’s where LLM usage intersects with trust patterns: AI tools cite review platforms as authoritative sources.

When buyers ask ChatGPT or Claude for software recommendations, the models often reference G2, Capterra, and TrustRadius as “crowd-sourced intelligence” — treating aggregated peer reviews as more trustworthy than vendor marketing claims. This creates a reinforcing loop where review site presence drives both direct buyer research and AI-mediated discovery.

The dual benefit shows up in recent data: [72% of buyers now encounter Google AI Overviews](https://solutions.trustradius.com/vendor-blog/bridging-the-trust-gap-b2b-tech-buying-in-the-age-of-ai/) during their research, according to TrustRadius’s 2025 study, and [40% say AI makes finding information easier](https://solutions.trustradius.com/vendor-blog/bridging-the-trust-gap-b2b-tech-buying-in-the-age-of-ai/), doubling from 2024.

### **What this means for your strategy**

The 6sense research showing **77% of buyers contact the eventual winner first** (down from 83% but still very high) reveals the stakes: By the time buyers reach out to vendors, they’ve already formed strong preferences based on peer validation, review site research, and prior experience.

Your vendor content still matters — buyers consume it heavily — but third-party validation increasingly determines whether you make the shortlist in the first place. As we’ll explore in the practical strategies section, building authentic review presence and peer advocacy has become mandatory infrastructure, not nice-to-have marketing.

The trust landscape has fundamentally shifted. Adapt or become invisible.

## **Chapter 4: The Dark Funnel expands: 3 layers of invisibility**

At 6sense, we’ve been tracking what we call the “Dark Funnel” since our founding. It’s the vast majority of B2B buyer research that happens anonymously and outside of trackable channels. Our data consistently shows that **only 3% of website visitors self-identify through form fills**, meaning 97% of your buyer research is anonymous.

LLMs aren’t creating the dark funnel. They’re adding a new layer to research opacity that already existed and making account-based intelligence even more critical.

### **Layer 1: Anonymous open web research**

The first layer of the dark funnel consists of buyers visiting your website, reading your blog, consuming your content, checking your pricing page — all without identifying themselves. They’re researching, evaluating, and forming opinions. You have no idea who they are, what company they’re from, or where they are in their buying journey.

This layer has been the reality of B2B marketing for years. The only visibility comes from intent data platforms (like 6sense) that can identify companies visiting your site through IP tracking and account matching.

Our 2025 Buyer Experience Report confirms this dynamic remains unchanged: Buyers conduct extensive independent research before engaging vendors, consuming an average of 13 content pieces (8 from vendors, 5 from third parties) across their journey. The vast majority of these interactions happen anonymously.

### **Layer 2: Closed networks and private channels**

The second layer is even darker: research happening in channels where tracking is fundamentally impossible.

This includes:

- Private Slack workspaces where buyers discuss vendors with peers
- Microsoft Teams channels for internal buying committee deliberations
- Reddit direct messages requesting product experiences
- LinkedIn private conversations with people who’ve used the product
- Phone calls with trusted colleagues at other companies
- Professional community discussions (Pavilion, Revenue Collective, CMO Coffee Talk)

[Research shows that 84% of content sharing](https://orangeowl.marketing/social-media-marketing/what-is-dark-social/) occurs through private channels like these — what we’ll call “dark social.”

Our 2025 data shows the scale of this challenge: With **average buying committees of 10.1 people**, each conducting research independently across multiple channels, you’re looking at 100+ research interactions per deal — the vast majority outside the visibility of traditional analytics and attribution tools.

### **Layer 3: LLM-mediated research**

Now add a third layer: conversational research with AI tools that creates zero engagement signals.

When a buyer asks ChatGPT “What are the best marketing automation platforms for mid-market B2B SaaS companies?” and receives a synthesized answer, no click occurs. No website visit. No form fill. No intent data capture. No retargeting pixel fires. Zero signal reaches any vendor mentioned in the response.

The buyer has progressed from awareness to consideration without creating a single trackable datapoint.

This “automated research assistant” dynamic pushes first sales engagement later while simultaneously reducing the visibility that made traditional demand generation measurable. Even Google search — historically at least trackable through keyword and CTR data — now ends without a click [58.5% of the time](https://searchengineland.com/google-search-zero-click-study-2024-443869), according to Search Engine Land’s June 2025 analysis.

### **Buying committee complexity**

The dark funnel challenge multiplies when you consider committee dynamics. Our 2025 data shows:

- **10.1 average buying group size**
- **16 interactions per person with the winning vendor**

## **Chapter 5: How LLMs work: Training data and real-time search**

To understand why buyers still need vendor validation despite AI tools, you need to understand how LLMs actually function, and their inherent limitations for high-stakes B2B decisions.

### **The hybrid approach: Historical knowledge and live search**

Modern LLMs like ChatGPT and Claude operate through a hybrid approach that combines historical training data with real-time web search capabilities.

**Base Model Training** provides the foundation. GPT-4 was trained on approximately 13 trillion tokens from sources including:

- Common Crawl (massive web scraping)
- Books and academic papers
- Reddit discussions
- Code repositories
- Wikipedia
- YouTube transcripts

This training data has cutoff dates — the point beyond which the model has no inherent knowledge. GPT-4’s base knowledge cutoff is April 2023, while GPT-4o extends to October 2023. Claude Sonnet 4 (as of October 2025) has the most recent cutoff at March 2025.

**Real-Time Web Search** fills the gaps. Through a technique called RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation), modern LLMs can search the live web, retrieve relevant information, and synthesize it into responses. When a user asks for “recent” or “current” information, the model triggers web search rather than relying solely on base training.

This hybrid creates better currency for current events and recent product launches, but it doesn’t eliminate bias or ensure accuracy.

### **What LLMs cite (and why it matters)**

Understanding citation patterns helps explain why review sites and peer validation have become so critical. Research from [Backlinko analyzing 10,000+ queries](https://backlinko.com/llm-visibility) found that 90% of ChatGPT citations come from search results ranking in positions 21 or higher — not the top 10 traditional SEO positions.

LLMs mine deep into search results for comprehensive answers, favoring specific content types:

According to [analysis from Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/ai-platform-citation-patterns), ChatGPT overwhelmingly cites Wikipedia, followed by Reddit, Forbes, G2, TechRadar and Nerd Wallet; Google’s AI favors Reddit, YouTube, Quora, LinkedIn, and Gartner; Perplexity leans on Reddit, YouTube, Gartner, Yelp, and LinkedIn.

What do these platforms have in common? They provide a wealth of structured, comparative data, and they are mostly community-driven.

The implication: Being in traditional top-10 search results isn’t enough for LLM visibility. You need presence across the platforms LLMs trust as authoritative sources — especially Wikipedia, Reddit, YouTube, and review sites. For SaaS companies, focus on sites like G2, TrustRadius, and SourceForge that have active review communities and strong domain authority.

### **Systematic biases in recommendations**

Here’s where LLM limitations become critical for B2B buyers: The models carry systematic biases that can skew recommendations.

**Source Bias** favors structured data over narrative content. Research from [Princeton’s GEO study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.09735) found that adding statistics improved visibility by 27-36%, citing sources increased it by 20-34%, and including expert quotations boosted it by 20-35%. Conversely, keyword stuffing showed negative results, actually decreasing visibility by 10%.

LLMs prefer:

- Original statistics and quantitative data
- Expert quotes from credible sources
- Comparison tables and feature breakdowns
- Clear FAQ-style Q&A matching Reddit/Quora training
- Structured data with schema markup

**Brand Bias** creates compounding advantages for established players. Well-known brands with extensive web presence generate what researchers call “generative brand density” — repeated mentions across authoritative contexts that exponentially increase citation likelihood.

This connects directly to 6sense findings: **85% of buyers had prior experience with the vendors they evaluated**— the highest level we’ve recorded. LLMs amplify this incumbent advantage by citing well-documented brands more frequently than emerging competitors, regardless of product quality.

New vendors face a cold-start problem: Limited training data presence equals near-zero visibility in base model knowledge, post-cutoff launches are invisible without web search prompting, and small companies lack the “semantic consistency” LLMs look for across multiple sources.

Category creators capture market value partially because their language and framing become embedded in how LLMs understand and describe the solution space. If you defined the category, LLMs learn and repeat your terminology.

### **Why buyers don’t trust LLMs alone**

These limitations explain why our [2025 Buyer Experience Report](https://6sense.com/science-of-b2b/buyer-experience-report-2025/) shows **buyers maintaining 16 vendor interactions despite 94% LLM usage**. For $300,000+ decisions, buyers can’t afford to trust AI recommendations that might be:

- **Outdated**: Base models don’t know about anything after their cutoff date unless prompted to search
- **Biased**: Training data systematically favors incumbents over better alternatives
- **Incomplete**: Models miss nuances about pricing, implementation complexity, and AI capabilities
- **Unverifiable**: Black-box recommendations with unclear sourcing

This verification imperative shows up across market research. [TrustRadius found 90% of buyers](https://solutions.trustradius.com/vendor-blog/bridging-the-trust-gap-b2b-tech-buying-in-the-age-of-ai/) click through to verify AI-provided information. They’re not blindly trusting LLM output.

The practical implication: LLMs help buyers become better-informed before engaging vendors, but the validation phase remains essential. As 6sense data shows, the point of first contact moved earlier (60% vs. 70% of journey) specifically because buyers need vendor conversations to validate AI-related claims that LLMs can’t reliably answer.

Understanding these dynamics helps explain why account-based platforms that capture multi-signal intelligence become more valuable as research moves into AI-mediated channels. LLMs add opacity, but they don’t change the fundamental need for vendor validation in high-stakes B2B purchases.
