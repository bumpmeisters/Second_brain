# About invalid traffic

To ensure you get the most value from your ad budget, Google actively detects and filters invalid traffic. Invalid traffic refers to traffic that doesn't represent genuine interest in your business.

Google's global ad traffic quality team uses automated filters, machine learning, and manual reviews to detect and filter invalid traffic. The team often detects this traffic as it happens or shortly after. Google automatically removes any invalid traffic found before the end of your billing cycle from your campaign metrics and billing. If Google finds invalid traffic after you receive your invoice, you'll receive a credit where appropriate and possible.

Some examples of invalid traffic:

- **Non-human traffic**: Bots and automated software generate these clicks and impressions to mimic how humans browse.
- **Accidental clicks**: For example, clicks that happen because of poor ad placement, such as ads placed too close to navigation buttons.
- **Fraudulent ad placements**: This includes "clickjacking," which tricks you to click an invisible ad, or "ad stacking," where a publisher layers multiple ads so only the top ad is visible.
- **Other invalid user activity**: Intentional interactions from real people who lack genuine interest in your ad. This includes competitors who manually click to increase your spend, publishers who pay users to click ads, or invalid impressions that aim to artificially lower your clickthrough rate.

This list isn’t exhaustive because many types of invalid traffic exist.

**Note**: You aren’t charged for invalid traffic detected before billing. Invalid traffic (including impressions, clicks, interactions and conversions) detected before the end of the month is automatically removed from your billing and campaign reports, so **no refunds are necessary**. If invalid traffic is detected **after** you were billed for it, you'll receive **credits** in the form of a billing adjustment for the value of the invalid activity. These credits appear on your invoices and in your account transactions. You can track invalid clicks and interactions in your campaign reports.

## How Google defends against invalid traffic

Google uses advanced technology and expert research teams to protect your ad spend. Google's goal is to ensure you only pay for valid traffic. Google's multi-layered defense system protects you from invalid traffic through:

- **Automated protections**: Automated filters continuously scan your traffic data for interactions that don’t match normal human behavior patterns. A specialized Ad Traffic Quality team also conducts manual investigations to find invalid traffic.
- **Policy enforcement**: Google enforces strict quality standards and content policies. Google rejects sites or apps that fail to maintain high-quality inventory and keep bad actors out. Google also runs extensive, continuous checks to keep the network clean. If Google observes high levels of invalid traffic from a particular publisher, Google might suspend or remove the account to protect advertisers.

The Media Rating Council (MRC), an independent industry organization, [accredits Google's invalid traffic defenses](https://mediaratingcouncil.org/accreditation/digital) for specific services and metrics. This accreditation certifies that Google's measurement technology adheres to industry standards for interactive advertising metrics. It also certifies that the processes that support this technology are accurate.

### Automated protections filter invalid traffic before you receive your invoice

Google catches most invalid traffic shortly after the ad interaction. If Google identifies an interaction as invalid before the end of the billing cycle, you don’t pay for it. Google filters it out automatically before you receive your invoice. Google also updates your campaign metrics to remove these filtered invalid impressions, clicks, interactions, and conversions. This ensures that your campaign performance metrics reflect valid traffic.

### Google issues credits for invalid activity from previous billing cycles

While Google filters most invalid traffic before you receive your invoice, some traffic takes longer to detect. If Google detects invalid traffic after the billing cycle ends, Google automatically issues an invalid activity credit in the next billing cycle, when appropriate and possible. To review these credit amounts and their associated campaigns, look for "Invalid activity" adjustments on your invoice. To view campaign metrics adjusted for the credited traffic, check the Invalid Activity Credit Report in the Report Editor.

## Monitor invalid traffic

To track the number of invalid clicks that Google's automated protections filter out, add the Invalid clicks column to your campaigns data table:

1. Go to [**Campaigns**](https://ads.google.com/aw/campaigns) in the **Campaigns** menu ![Campaigns Icon](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/PYU3UjHzepAdY-dWS9ObIzq9WgLGS0SZeJS1hQ0Be3fxCIUQZaWCrR4HoYqriXSEbQ=w40).
2. Click the **Columns** icon ![](https://storage.googleapis.com/support-kms-prod/UtQ9nHVmzUMSM7DGvMnhYSV7VqMll86lmR64) above the data table.
3. Click the **Search** icon ![](https://storage.googleapis.com/support-kms-prod/unb5OznMhjYfiQf5p42tlt7cGjBjuTuRA8sT) next to "Modify columns for campaigns".
4. Search for “Invalid clicks”.
5. Select **Apply**.

Because invalid traffic fluctuates, these numbers change naturally over time. An increase in invalid clicks means Google's defenses actively protect your budget. Higher numbers show that Google caught and removed more invalid interactions from your campaign metrics (such as Clicks and Cost) and your bill.

## View credits for invalid traffic

Sometimes invalid traffic isn’t detected right away, and Google also analyzes traffic over longer time periods to find more complex patterns. When invalid traffic that you were already billed for is detected later, you’re credited for it, when appropriate and possible.

To view these credits:

1. Go to [**Summary**](https://ads.google.com/aw/billing/summary) within the **Billing** menu ![Billing Icon](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/SBNC3mPvb1j7dFlfvdpubuZznp50b1gi1YcWj9qoOY49fOCzNiwLPtdEjUGsvt31tdIS=w40).

You can find invalid traffic credits labeled as “Invalid activity” under the “Adjustments” dropdown in the “Month” card.

### Campaign metrics don’t adjust for past activity credits

Google freezes campaign metrics at the end of the month so your data aligns with your monthly invoice. Therefore, credits for past invalid activity may appear on your invoice but won’t retroactively adjust your campaign data in Google Ads. However, Google removes invalid traffic caught before the end of the bill cycle from your metrics.

To view your data with these credits applied, use the Invalid Activity Credit Report in the **Report Editor**. This report shows adjusted metrics, such as adjusted clicks, cost, and conversion rate, so you can view your true campaign performance after Google subtracts invalid activity. Learn more [About the Invalid Activity Credit Report](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16826168).

You may find many filtered invalid clicks in your campaign reports but small credit amounts in your billing documents. This is normal because **these numbers are not directly related**:

- The Invalid clicks or Invalid interactions columns in your campaign reports show traffic Google filters **before you were billed**.
- Credits apply to invalid activity that Google detects **after the billing cycle closes**. This includes invalid traffic and policy violations that affect your placements. To view the number of credited clicks, check the “Credited clicks” column in the [Invalid Activity Credit Report](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16826168).

## Protect your account from invalid leads

Google protects your budget by filters that remove invalid traffic, but this traffic can still reach your website and lead forms. Sometimes real users submit fake contact details or other invalid information.

Because you capture leads on your site and customer relationship management (CRM) system, Google systems cannot track or control visitor actions after they click your ad. Therefore, lead quality is a shared effort:

- **Google's role**: Automated protections detect invalid traffic as it happens or shortly after. This ensures you don't pay for bot clicks and other invalid traffic. These filters cannot block traffic from your site because they run after an ad serves, but you can take steps to protect your lead quality.
- **Your role**:

  - **Focus on lead-specific goals**: Choose Leads as your campaign objective and focus your campaigns on goals such as "qualified lead," "converted lead," "book appointment," or "request quote."
  - **Set up website safeguards**: Secure your website lead forms with tools like reCAPTCHA and server-side validation. Consider a double opt-in process, which requires users to click a link in a verification email, to confirm that a real person uses a valid email address.
  - **Connect first-party data**: Set up the [Google tag](https://support.google.com/tagmanager/answer/12002338) to track customer actions on your website. Use [enhanced conversions](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15712870) to measure actions accurately and improve campaign performance.

Learn more about [Best practices for generating high-quality leads](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13489421).

## Request investigation into possible invalid traffic

If you suspect your account has been affected by invalid traffic that wasn’t automatically filtered, you can submit an invalid traffic investigation request for activity within the last 60 days.

The “Invalid clicks” column in your campaign reports shows traffic that Google's systems already filtered. Google doesn't charge you for these clicks. If this number increases, it shows that Google filters actively protect your budget. Third-party tools might flag traffic as invalid (such as duplicate IP addresses or bot activity) that Google already filtered and excluded from your bill.

### Prepare for an invalid traffic investigation

To prepare for an invalid traffic investigation, gather the information below before you submit a request:

- **Customer ID**: Your 10-digit Google Ads account number.
- **Specific date range**: The exact dates when you noticed the suspicious activity. Google limits investigations to the past 60 days.
- **Campaign and ad group names**: The names of the campaigns and ad groups where you noticed the traffic spike or suspicious behavior.
- **Suspicious keywords**: Any keywords that drive the unusual traffic.
- **Web server logs**: Detailed logs of your website traffic. Specifically you should identify the following:

  - **IP addresses**: A list of IP addresses that generate repetitive clicks or clicks without conversions.
  - **Platforms and devices**: Browser or device information from the suspected activity.
  - **GCLIDs**: The Google click identifiers associated with the suspicious clicks. This helps Google map the visit back to a specific ad interaction.
- **Lead details**: If your issue relates to invalid leads, share a spreadsheet that contains:

  - GCLIDs for each lead
  - Your assessment of each lead, such as outcomes from emailing or calling them.
- **Trend indicators**: A brief explanation of why the traffic is suspicious. For example, a 300% spike in clicks without more conversions, or clicks that originate outside your targeted locations.

### Submit your report

To submit your request, use the [Click Quality Form](https://support.google.com/google-ads/contact/click_quality).

Google specialists evaluate your traffic from the last 60 days. This process typically takes several business days because specialists manually review a large volume of data. You'll receive an email with findings when the investigation ends. If the investigation finds additional invalid traffic that the automated filters missed, Google notifies you of any credits.

Our specialists use the data you provide, such as GCLIDs and IP addresses to assist with their review. However, an investigation won’t be able to confirm if specific clicks were marked as valid or invalid. Sharing those details could expose how we detect invalid traffic, making it easier for bad actors to avoid our defenses. By keeping this information confidential, we better protect your ad spend and keep the platform more secure.

## Frequently asked questions

## Does an unexpected spike in traffic mean there’s invalid traffic on my campaign?

An unexpected traffic spike or a daily budget that you spend quickly can cause concern, but it doesn't always indicate invalid traffic. Google automatically protects your budget if detected invalid traffic increases. When Google’s filters catch this invalid traffic, it’s removed from your billing and the cost is automatically restored to your campaign budget so it can be used for valid clicks. You don't need to take action because Google's defenses actively protect you.

Traffic spikes can also occur due to changes in customer demand or campaign settings, such as:

- **Seasonal trends or news events**: A sudden, genuine spike in interest.
- **Competitive landscape**: If a competitor stops ads, your ads might show more often and receive more clicks.
- **Campaign changes**: If you increase your budget, add keywords, or modify your audience and location settings, your ads may receive more impressions and interactions.

Check your Change history to understand shifts in campaign traffic. To help optimize your campaigns for your goals and brand, adjust your audience, location, and content suitability settings:

- **Adjust location options**: Change your location settings from "Presence or interest" to "Presence" to focus your reach, or [exclude ads from specific areas](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722040). Learn more about [Advanced location options](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722038).
- **Add negative keywords**: Use [negative keywords](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972) to exclude specific search terms and focus your campaign on what your customers want.
- **Refine content suitability settings**: Use [Content suitability settings](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12764663) to filter out content types or placements that don't align with your brand or goals.

## Does invalid traffic exhaust my Google Ads budget?

If Google identifies an ad interaction as invalid before the end of the month, Google restores those costs directly to your campaign budget. Google systems then attempt to spend those funds on valid traffic, within your normal monthly limits. This preserves your ad budget for real customers.

If Google detects invalid traffic after you receive your bill, Google issues a credit to your account for the cost of that traffic, when appropriate and possible. Check the credit line items in your bill details to see the original month of service and the campaign for each credit. Google prioritizes quick detection of invalid traffic to minimize these adjustments, but these credits ensure you pay only for valid traffic.

## Do zero-second visits or duplicate IP addresses indicate invalid traffic?

Common patterns in your website analytics can look unusual. Often, these patterns result from how analytics tools measure data, not from invalid traffic.

- **Short visit times**: A zero-second visit might cause concern, but it doesn't always mean the visitor left immediately. Many analytics tools require a second click to record visit duration. Therefore, a zero-second visit can mean the user didn’t leave your landing page.
- **Shared IP addresses**: An IP address isn’t always unique to one device. Most networks such as universities, offices, coffee shops, and mobile carriers use Network Address Translation (NAT). NAT allows hundreds or thousands of devices to share one public IP address.

## Why do I receive clicks from outside targeted locations?

Google identifies location based on several signals, such as Internet Protocol (IP) address, settings, device type, and behavior on Google applications. Therefore, Google doesn't guarantee location accuracy in every situation. You might receive clicks from outside your targeted areas for these reasons:

- **Location-specific searches**: Your ad can show to users outside your targeted area if their search query includes your targeted location name.
- **Country domains**: If you target a country, Google can show your ads to users who search on that country's domain, such as [google.fr](http://google.fr/).

To control who can view your ads, change your location settings to target only users in your specified area, or include location names in your ad text. Learn how to [Prevent clicks outside of your geo-targeted locations](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9376662) and other [advanced location options](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722038).

## Why does suspicious traffic appear in my website analytics?

Because Google's automated protections filter invalid clicks after they occur, Google Ads reports might show different data than your website analytics. For example, Google Ads removes filtered invalid clicks from your reports, but your website analytics might still record the visit. Learn more about [data discrepancies](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7457111#9) between Google Ads and other platforms and how to troubleshoot these differences.

You may want to know if Google flagged specific clicks as invalid, but Google cannot share these details. Google keeps this data confidential so bad actors cannot learn how to bypass Google defenses. This helps protect your budget and keeps the platform secure.

## Why do I receive invalid leads when reported invalid traffic is low?

Google's automated protections filter out invalid traffic, but they can't prevent all invalid leads. Because you capture leads on your website and CRM, bots or users can still submit forms even if Google identifies the ad interaction as invalid.

Lead quality is a shared effort. Google recommends that you add reCAPTCHA to your forms, use server-side validation, and optimize for lead-specific goals. Learn more about [Best practices for generating high-quality leads](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13489421).

## Can Google provide more details about invalid traffic sources, placements, or credits?

Google prioritizes advertiser protection. To prevent bad actors who attempt to reverse-engineer Google defenses, detection methods and specific invalid traffic sources remain confidential. Bad actors can use disclosed detection methods to change their approach and hide invalid traffic. Google keeps this information confidential to protect your ad spend and maintain a secure platform.

## What is the normal or standard invalid traffic rate?

There is no industry standard for a normal invalid traffic rate. Invalid traffic exists across the ad ecosystem and varies from simple ad events like accidental user clicks to more malicious and sophisticated invalid traffic. Google focuses on the detection of invalid traffic so you don't pay for it. The Media Rating Council (MRC) accredits Google's invalid traffic detection and filtration processes, and an independent third party audits them annually.

## Does Google remove conversions that result from invalid activity?

Google removes some invalid conversions if they originate from invalid traffic, or if they remain invalid for longer than 13 months. This removal can occur after conversions appear in your account, which might show as a small drop in conversions over time.

Learn more about [data discrepancies](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7457111#9) between Google Ads reports and other platforms, including conversion data, and how to troubleshoot these differences.

## Why does my third-party report show different data than Google Ads?

Third-party tools that monitor invalid traffic often report different data than Google Ads. Google cannot evaluate third-party reports because Google doesn’t know their detection criteria. However, these tools often flag invalid traffic that Google defenses already detected and filtered. To track how much invalid traffic Google's automated protections filtered, add the “Invalid clicks” column to your campaign reports.

Google cannot provide a click-by-click comparison with third-party reports. Google keeps this information confidential to prevent bad actors who attempt to reverse-engineer Google defenses.

## Why do I receive a high volume of credits, and how can I minimize them?

Google's automated protections prevent charges for invalid traffic when Google detects it before Google generates your invoice. However, Google issues credits when appropriate and possible when systems detect invalid traffic after you receive your bill. Google prioritizes the prevention of invalid traffic to reduce these credits, but invalid traffic isn’t steady. In rare cases, Google newly classifies a large volume of recent traffic as invalid, which results in an invalid activity credit in your Google Ads account.

Google is committed to protecting advertisers from invalid activity and constantly improves automated defenses. Google continuously launches new automated protections to improve detection and filtration of invalid traffic.
