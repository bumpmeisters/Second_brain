# Creative Prompting Frameworks for Marketing

## Executive conclusion

AI can contribute useful novelty, but asking it to “be creative” is a weak method. The human supplies strategic taste, lived observation, tension, point of view, and unusual inputs. The model is strongest as a rapid explorer, combinator, variator, and critic.

The practical model is:

> Human seed → structured divergence → forced collisions → strategic convergence → narrative execution → evidence and market testing.

## Current research findings

### Delay AI until the human has made a first creative move

A controlled experiment with 60 participants found that using an LLM at the beginning of ideation reduced original ideas, creative self-efficacy, and perceived ownership compared with introducing AI after independent ideation (source: [Qin et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06197)).

**Marketing implication:** Before asking AI for campaign ideas, write three observations, one tension, one contrarian belief, and several rough ideas yourself.

### Separate divergent and convergent modes

Creativity requires both expansion and selection. Research comparing direct-answer assistance with coach-like assistance suggests AI can boost performance during assisted tasks but may weaken later unassisted creative performance. Newer work explores divergent and convergent AI personas as complementary scaffolds (source: [Kumar et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03703); [Rosenbaum et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26490)).

**Marketing implication:** Do not ask one prompt to generate, judge, and finalize simultaneously. First prohibit evaluation; later switch to an explicit editor or strategist role.

### AI can improve individual output while homogenizing the field

Experiments found higher assessed quality for AI-assisted stories, particularly among less creative participants, but greater similarity across AI-assisted outputs. A separate ideation study found less semantic distinctness across ChatGPT users despite more and more detailed ideas (source: [Doshi & Hauser](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00506); [Anderson et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01536)).

**Marketing implication:** A polished idea is not necessarily a distinctive one. Evaluate distance from category conventions and likely competitor output, not merely fluency.

### Create partitions before generating ideas

Recent studies suggest personas and explicit semantic directions can increase diversity. Ordinary, specific perspectives may partition knowledge better than stereotypical “creative genius” personas. Parallel generation across preselected semantic territories also reduces anchoring on the first answer (source: [Deng, Brucks & Toubia, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20408); [Wan & Kalman, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13868); [Ibrahim et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30150)).

**Marketing implication:** Generate separately as a skeptical operations manager, new employee, procurement lead, cultural anthropologist, category outsider, and customer who rejected the product. Merge only afterward.

### Forced conceptual blending is a productive division of labor

PopBlends helped participants find roughly twice as many conceptual blends with lower reported mental demand. The technique connects a target topic with a distant cultural or conceptual domain (source: [Wang et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.04920)).

**Marketing implication:** The human chooses meaningful collision domains; AI maps correspondences and produces variants. Examples: customs compliance × air-traffic control, supply-chain visibility × weather forecasting, onboarding × escape rooms.

### Novelty without appropriateness is noise

Creativity is not mere semantic distance. Recent evaluation research argues that novelty must be judged together with contextual appropriateness (source: [Nakajima, Zuiderveld & Pezzelle, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20546)).

**Marketing implication:** Score ideas on originality, audience truth, strategic fit, proof, brand fit, and executability. Keep separate scores rather than averaging away fatal weaknesses.

### Repeated sampling and prompt design matter

Tests across multiple LLMs found substantial variation from the same model and prompt. Very few individual model outputs reached the highest human creativity range, even where model averages were strong (source: [Haase, Hanel & Pokutta, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12320)).

**Marketing implication:** Run multiple independent batches, compare overlap, and preserve rare outliers.

## The Creative Marketing Loop

This is an original composite adapted from the vault's creative playbook, creative briefs, storytelling models, and the external research above. It is not an official framework from any cited author.

### 1. Human seed

Supply a real observation, audience tension, point of view, emotional target, strategic objective, and three rough ideas before seeing AI output. Stop if these are absent.

### 2. Expand the map

Use separate, non-evaluative rounds: SCAMPER, First Principles, Generic Parts, counterfactual worlds, semantic territories, and stakeholder partitions. Generate independently to reduce fixation.

### 3. Force collisions

Combine the problem with distant domains, metaphors, formats, senses, rituals, subcultures, industries, or historical periods. Require a real transferred mechanism, not decorative vocabulary.

### 4. Select strategically

Judge originality, audience truth, objective fit, distinctive brand fit, evidence, emotional power, feasibility, and extensibility. Kill fluent but generic ideas and combine complementary fragments.

### 5. Shape the narrative

Choose the narrative by job: PAS for urgent pain, BAB for transformation, SCQA for executive argument, Challenger-style reframing for thought leadership, AIDA/ERA for conversion, STAR for proof, and hook templates for openings.

### 6. Red-team and test

Run fact, brand, emotional, category-cliché, legal/risk, and skeptical-customer reviews. Translate survivors into testable variants and define evidence that would confirm or reject them.

## Failure modes

- Empty human seed.
- Premature convergence.
- Single-thread anchoring.
- Celebrity-persona theater.
- Fluency mistaken for creativity.
- Framework cargo cult.
- Synthetic-customer hypotheses treated as facts.
- Endless “make it better” iteration.
- No market test.

## Recommendation

Use the full loop for campaign platforms, positioning, thought leadership, major launches, and new content franchises. Use one divergence card plus the selection scorecard for everyday social, email, and content work.

The highest-leverage habit is **human first, AI second, human last**.
