# Campaign Types and Funnel Stages

**Summary**: The campaign-type materials distinguish brand, demand generation, lead generation, conversion, and nurture roles across the customer journey.

---

The campaign-type deck distinguishes brand, demand generation, lead generation, conversion, and nurture as different campaign roles with different objectives, metrics, tactics, and execution patterns (source: 2025_campaign types_brnd_DemandDen_LeadGen_Conversion.pptx).

Brand campaigns focus on establishing, reinforcing, and elevating identity, values, and market presence (source: 2025_campaign types_brnd_DemandDen_LeadGen_Conversion.pptx).

Demand generation campaigns educate the market, create interest, and connect solutions to market pain points (source: 2025_campaign types_brnd_DemandDen_LeadGen_Conversion.pptx).

Lead generation campaigns capture and nurture interested prospects into contacts or qualified leads for CRM or marketing automation (source: 2025_campaign types_brnd_DemandDen_LeadGen_Conversion.pptx).

Conversion campaigns reduce friction and encourage high-intent prospects to take a desired final action (source: 2025_campaign types_brnd_DemandDen_LeadGen_Conversion.pptx).

The related Word document describes an objection-driven campaign approach that addresses potential hesitations throughout the customer journey (source: 2025_campaign types_brand_DemandGen_LeadGEN_Conversion.docx).

The new full-funnel clippings add a buying-stage lens: B2B campaigns need to handle both out-of-market accounts that require awareness and trust building and in-market accounts that need activation, proof, and lower-friction next steps (source: EP 48 The 955 Rule In-Market vs. Out-of-Market Buyers with John Dawes @ Ehrenberg-Bass Institute.md; source: EP 50 How to Run a Proper ABM Strategy with Mason Cosby @ Scrappy ABM.md).

For ABM, the stage model becomes account-based rather than contact-only: the sources distinguish unaware accounts, initially engaged accounts, meaningfully engaged accounts, marketing-qualified accounts, sales-qualified accounts, opportunities, and re-engagement candidates (source: EP 50 How to Run a Proper ABM Strategy with Mason Cosby @ Scrappy ABM.md).

The new signal-orchestration sources add a response layer to the stage model. When an account interpretation changes, seller outreach, nurture, paid media, website experience, and suppression should use the same stage and priority logic so the buyer does not receive contradictory channel reactions (source: Your GTM May Not Be Responding to the Right Signals.md; analysis: [[gtm-signals-and-contextual-intelligence]]).

Long-cycle accounts may pause without becoming lost. A useful lifecycle therefore supports lower-intensity nurture and later reactivation while preserving buying-group context, rather than forcing every account through a linear sequence (source: The Long Game How Healthcare Companies Can Run ABM That Actually Holds Up Over a 12-18 Month Sales Cycle.md).

## Stage-role media planning

A campaign role becomes executable only when it is connected to the audience job, the channel levers required for that job, one primary creative task, the closest observable outcome, and the evidence needed for the next decision. Do not ask one ad or one platform metric to represent awareness, consideration, conversion, and retention simultaneously. Treat the stage as a planning hypothesis and preserve a path for non-linear, paused, or lapsed journeys (source: 2026-08-04--9zBThLCpPh4.md; transcript 00:14:48–00:23:00; analysis: [[full-funnel-media-role-and-evidence-map]]).

Use [[full-funnel-media-role-and-evidence-map]] before activation or material reallocation. It separates observations and proxies from comparison-backed claims and records channel control, creative purpose, test boundaries, ownership, and the resulting decision.

## Event lifecycle contract

Plan an event as a before-during-after lifecycle rather than a live session with follow-up added later.

Before launch, document the event role, target audience, intended outcome, evidence needed, accountable owners, measurement checkpoints, response rules, and content-reuse plan. The role may be awareness, education, demand creation, customer or partner engagement, opportunity acceleration, or another explicit purpose; do not force every event into a pipeline-only success definition (source: Tech, Trends, and The Future of Event Strategy.md; source: What’s ahead for virtual events in 2026 — and what marketers should do about it.md).

During the event, capture attendance mode, sessions, questions, polls, resource use, stated interests, and explicit requests where appropriate. These are engagement observations, not automatic proof of fit, qualification, buying intent, or revenue causation.

After the event, distinguish live attendees, on-demand participants, registrants who were absent, highly engaged participants, and explicit hand raisers. Give each meaningful segment an owned next step, preserve the context behind the response, and set a service-level expectation for time-sensitive requests. Repurpose useful material into chapters, clips, summaries, guides, or later sessions only when it preserves the source meaning and serves a defined audience need. Review outcomes at time horizons appropriate to the original objective, then feed the learning into the next event brief (source: What’s ahead for virtual events in 2026 — and what marketers should do about it.md; vendor-hosted mixed practitioner webinar).

Reported pipeline multipliers, attendance and travel trends, anonymous polls, and vendor outcomes are excluded. Event influence remains distinct from causal attribution.
