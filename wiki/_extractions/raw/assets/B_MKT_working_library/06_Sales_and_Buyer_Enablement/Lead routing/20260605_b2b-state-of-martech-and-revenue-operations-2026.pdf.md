01 Enterprise revenue operations is becoming increasingly complex
The martech landscape has plateaued but the market is actively churning underneath,
with significant product turnover year on year. Average stack sizes have fallen while
integration complexity remains the top-cited barrier to maturity (51%) and buying
committees continue to grow. The operational environment is consolidating in size but
not in the demands it places on revenue teams.
02 Operational execution is not keeping pace with strategic goals
Process and Operations is the lowest-scoring pillar at 3.66 out of 5.0 and has recorded
the smallest improvement of any pillar over three years of benchmarking. People and
Teams (3.82) and Platform and Technology (3.81) lead, while Planning and Strategy has
plateaued at 3.72. Organisations invest in talent, tools, and experimentation ahead of the
processes and governance that connect them.

03 AI adoption is widespread but unevenly deployed
Conviction in AI’s transformative potential is near-universal, but deployment remains
concentrated in lower-risk use cases. Most organisations are still running initial
experiments or proof of concepts. Use cases that directly affect revenue, such as lead
qualification, pipeline forecasting, and routing, see far lower adoption. Content and
campaign creation leads at 46%, but AI lead routing and assignment sits at just 11%, the
lowest of any agent type measured. The gap between enthusiasm and operational
rollout defines the current moment.
04 Governance readiness is falling behind AI ambition
Only half of respondents are confident their organisation has the governance and
controls to deploy AI safely at scale. The majority agree that operational foundations
need to come first, yet AI investment is not waiting for those foundations to be built.
Skills readiness compounds the challenge, with organisations prioritising the ability to
use AI over the ability to govern it.
05 Revenue leaders face six clear imperatives for 2026
Audit operational discipline before scaling AI; extend governance across the full buyer
lifecycle; simplify platform fragmentation through workflow rather than procurement;
align revenue actions to the buyer journey; invest in governance that keeps pace with AI
adoption; and recognise the widening gap between operational leaders and laggards.
06 The path forward requires operational discipline to support AI ambition
The organisations that act on these priorities now will compound their advantages.
Those that wait risk widening the gap between what their technology can do and what
their operations can deliver.

A market consolidating in size butnot in complexity
Enterprise revenue operations have never been simple, but in 2026, the data
points to a market that’s crossed a structural threshold. The volume of tools,
the pace of consolidation, and the investment ambitions of revenue teams are
compounding faster than most organisations can absorb.
These are businesses with multi-region sales teams, layered go-to-market motions, and
technology estates that have grown over years of investment. The complexity described in this
chapter reflects the daily operating reality for the people who completed this survey, 85% of
whom agree that advancing marketing and sales operations capabilities is critical to driving
business performance.

Where pressure is landing hardest
The pressure described in the previous section is landing hardest in process
and operations. Across LXA’s five-pillar maturity framework, People and Teams
and Platform and Technology lead, both scoring above 3.8 out of 5.0. Process
and Operations trails at 3.66 out of 5.0. The gap is small in absolute terms, but
it suggests that organisations have invested in talent and tools while the
workflows that support those investments have lagged.
Process and Operations has been the lowest-scoring pillar across consecutive years of this
research. What makes it more consequential in 2026 is the context around it. As stacks
consolidate and AI programmes accelerate, the cost of weak process discipline rises.
The Fragmented Buyer Journey
Nearly a third (29%) of respondents identify lack of visibility into what happens after handoff
as a significant process gap in lead management. 42% cite poor alignment between marketing
and sales on lead qualification. 32% report duplicate or mismatched lead-to-account records.
These issues point to a lack of unified visibility across the buyer lifecycle. Both internal teams
and the data that connects them are siloed where disconnects create gaps in revenue
enablement.

Why Execution Gaps Are Getting More Expensive
These process gaps have always carried a cost, but two forces are making them more
consequential. The first is AI. As organisations deploy AI-driven prospecting, automated
inbound qualification, and content generation at scale, the volume of activity flowing through
these processes is increasing.
AI-generated leads still need to be routed, matched to accounts, and followed up within an
enforceable SLA. Without that operational backbone, more volume simply means more
leakage.
The second is the growing priority placed on marketing and sales alignment.
Alignment is not just a culture problem. It is a process and data problem. When leads are
misrouted, SLAs are not enforced, and neither team has a shared view of the funnel,
collaboration breaks down regardless of how well the people get on. The fact that alignment
remains a top priority after years of industry attention suggests the problem is structural, not
relational. Organisations have not yet built the shared processes, definitions, and governance
to make alignment work reliably across the full buyer lifecycle.
“Alignment between sales and marketing. Breaking down silos. Stronger
communication cross-departments, which has never happened before within our
company history. ”
Amanda Shelley, Senior Manager Marketing Operations · Rockwell Automation

Six priorities that form the roadmapforward
Our research points to a consistent gap between investment in revenue
operations technology and the operational readiness to make that investment
pay off. The organisations that close these gaps fastest will compound their
advantages. Those that do not will find the gap widening as AI accelerates the
pace of execution around them.
The cost of inaction is already visible in the data: leads that never reach the right person, AI
deployments concentrated where risk is lowest rather than where impact is greatest, and
governance capacity that is not keeping pace with the speed of adoption.
Six priorities emerge from the data that collectively form a clear roadmap forward.
1 Audit operational discipline before scaling AI
82% of respondents agree that clean data, defined processes, and reliable routing are
prerequisites for scaling AI. Start by auditing your routing, qualification, and lead-to-
account matching for SLA enforcement. Only 26% of peers have enforcement
mechanisms in place. AI on top of unmonitored routing accelerates leakage rather than
fixing it.
2 Extend governance across the full buyer lifecycle
Process gaps do not stay contained when AI capabilities are added. A broken handoff
between marketing and sales affects pipeline velocity, renewal timing, and expansion
opportunity downstream. Orchestration needs to span acquisition through retention, not
just the top of the funnel.
3 Simplify platform fragmentation through workflow, not procurement
Average stack sizes are falling, but integration complexity remains the top-cited barrier
(51%). Rationalisation should be driven by workflow requirements and data architecture,
not procurement cost alone. The goal is fewer points of failure between systems, not
fewer line items on the budget.

## Page 28

4 Align revenue actions to the buyer journey
42% cite poor marketing-sales alignment on lead qualification as a significant gap. The
buyer journey is fragmented by default. Internal operating models need to compensate for
that fragmentation through shared definitions, unified visibility, and enforceable SLAs
across functions.
5 Invest in governance that keeps pace with AI adoption
Only 50% are confident in their governance readiness. As AI moves from productivity tools
to autonomous agents acting in revenue-critical workflows, the cost of ungoverned
deployment rises. Governance ownership should be shared across RevOps, marketing
leadership, and IT rather than siloed in any one function.
6 Recognise the widening gap between operational leaders and laggards
Process and Operations remains the lowest-scoring pillar at 3.66 out of 5.0, with the
smallest improvement of any pillar over three years. Organisations are hiring ahead of the
infrastructure to make that talent effective. Those that treat operational discipline as the
binding constraint will compound their advantages while the rest accumulate operational
debt that AI surfaces faster than it solves.
