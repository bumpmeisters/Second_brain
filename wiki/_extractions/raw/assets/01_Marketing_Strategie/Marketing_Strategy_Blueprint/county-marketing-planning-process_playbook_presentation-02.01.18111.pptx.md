## Slide 11

4 Cs  – Lhasa

Driving Insight
“My life is fluid, and I tend to believe that less is more. When it comes to printers, it feels like they’re big and clunky and not really something that fits how I print or live.”

Customer

The core target are digital, mobile natives. They do not depend on the physical world, and that includes printing; yet, there are many practical reasons why they need to have a printer that works for and around them and not one that just sits there looking ugly and taking up limited space.
They are out to make a difference in the world. As they set out on their quest. They need help to be as efficient as they can be.

Competition

Culture

Company

“Always on” is not a new thing, it is just the expected MO. Their mobile devices are an extension of themselves and anything and everything that is going to help them be more efficient, also needs to work directly from their mobile.

Competition is rather inactive in the “beautifully designed” printer space. Most options are more conventional, box-like printers in standard black, white or gray colors.
Polaroid is making some efforts, but so far limited to portable, photo printers.

HP is still considered to be the leader in printing. Quality and reliability are synonymous with the brand… but then again, other brands such as Epson and Canon offer up compelling alternatives.
Differentiation between HP and competitive printers is considered gray area.
The opportunity is to break away from the expected  and delight customers with a non-conventional printer, that is equally reliable and solid than other HP options, but responds more overtly to the needs of millennials and Gen Zs.

‘Optimists who want to be discovered’
The target grew up in a world that promised them success and then only delivered limited opportunities. They aspire  to higher accomplishments, but are unsure how to get there. As a result, they don’t often feel “grown-up” and prioritize “living for the now, “ but they find inspiration from the personal goals and agendas
they have established.

Brands are embraced as meaningful symbols of the aesthetics, values and social agenda of the companies that stand behind them. Brands have cultural authority and symbolic aura that can reflect and help define who they are.
