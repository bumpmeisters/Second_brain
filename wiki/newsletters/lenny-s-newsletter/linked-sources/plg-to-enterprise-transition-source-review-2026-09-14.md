# PLG-to-Enterprise Transition Source Review

**Summary**: A sponsored practitioner essay supports a distinct PLG-to-enterprise boundary: separate product-generated from sales-generated demand, trigger assistance from account-level product evidence, measure incremental value, and preserve self-service. Numeric multipliers and growth effects remain excluded.

## What the source is

Elena Verna distinguishes enterprise contracts that expand from demonstrated product adoption from enterprise demand created by sales. The essay discusses team adoption, departmental spread, governance needs, plan constraints, premature sales contact, revenue credit, and enterprise differentiation.

## Retained contribution

- Separate product-generated enterprise demand from sales-generated demand because they use different acquisition motions, talent, and economics.
- Trigger sales assistance from account-level adoption, expansion, governance, and plan-limit signals rather than treating every signup as a lead.
- Measure incremental enterprise value separately from revenue migrated out of self-service.
- Preserve the self-service engine while enterprise capabilities solve enterprise-specific problems.

## Evidence limits and contradictions

- The article is sponsored practitioner advice with no sample, comparison group, or measured outcomes.
- Moving existing self-service revenue into an enterprise contract can appear as growth while adding support cost.
- Enterprise differentiation can address governance and scale needs without weakening the self-service product.

## Explicitly excluded claims

No universal ARPA multiplier, PQL threshold, timing rule, account-size cutoff, causal growth effect, or outreach instruction is retained.
