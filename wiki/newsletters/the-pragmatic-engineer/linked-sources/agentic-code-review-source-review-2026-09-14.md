# Agentic Code Review Source Review

**Summary**: The accessible portion supports risk-routed human review for agent-generated code, with scope, exit criteria, tests, plans, public interfaces, authentication, schemas, and irreversible state receiving explicit attention. The paid boundary prevents full analytical coverage.

## What the source is

The Pragmatic Engineer reports practitioner approaches to reviewing increasing volumes of AI-generated code. The accessible article ends at a paid-subscriber boundary before announced sections on producing less code, reviewing everything by hand, using no human review, and the final synthesis.

## Retained contribution

- Route review depth by blast radius, reversibility, and available evidence.
- Keep humans responsible for scope and exit decisions and for high-risk plans, tests, interfaces, authentication, schemas, and state changes.
- Require tests, observability, types or linting, and filtered automated-review evidence rather than multiplying review noise.

## Evidence limits and contradictions

- Company examples and before-and-after figures are self-reported and confounded.
- Delegating routine line review increases the importance of reviewing plans, tests, interfaces, and exit criteria.
- The inaccessible sections are not inferred.

## Explicitly excluded claims

No claim that human review is obsolete or that agent review is sufficient by itself is retained. Productivity, defect-rate, review-time, and adoption figures are not durable benchmarks.
