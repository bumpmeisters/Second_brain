# Scaling outcome-based business models: configuration and constraints

**Review scope:** PDF pp. 1, 4–5, 8–9 and selected p. 7 passages; not all 11 pages.

Lakka, Keränen, Mero, and Leppäniemi use 29 interviews across nine industrial firms in an abductive qualitative study. They distinguish customized pilots, standardized long-term services, and process-optimization/performance-contract configurations, and connect them to scale, scope, learning, and structure logics. These configurations are not a mandatory linear ladder; a more advanced configuration is not always strategically preferable. (source: Scaling-outcome-based-business-models--A-matur_2026_Industrial-Marketing-Man.pdf, pp. 1, 4–5, 7–9; DOI 10.1016/j.indmarman.2026.01.007)

Early offerings face repeatability constraints; later configurations face outcome measurement, customer control, coordination, and rigidity. Standardization versus fit and automation versus judgment remain design choices. Supplier-side interviews and a static qualitative design limit causal and longitudinal conclusions. (source: same PDF, pp. 8–9)

P58-C09 proposes an **outcome-based service scaling assessment**: identify configuration and intended outcome, test the fitting scaling logic against constraints, and document advance/stay/redesign with evidence. It is a decision aid derived from this study, not a validated score or guaranteed growth path. It differs from customer maturity progression because its object is the provider's service/business model.
