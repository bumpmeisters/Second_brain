# Career Aspiration to Opportunity Loop

**Summary**: Separate career-development conversations from performance and compensation, invite people to describe where they are at their best and what they want to experience next, and route that voluntary input into transparent, supported opportunities. Record matches, deferrals, and declines so access does not depend only on manager assumptions or informal sponsorship.

---

## Trigger

Use this workflow during periodic development planning, when allocating projects or mentoring, after a person asks for broader experience, or when a manager realizes that current job performance is being used as a proxy for future ambition. Run the conversation separately from performance evaluation, merit review, promotion decisions, and compensation negotiation.

Do not start the workflow unless participation can be voluntary, declining or limiting disclosure carries no penalty, and the organization can protect confidentiality, workload, accessibility, and fair access.

## Required inputs

- A willing participant and a facilitator who can explain the purpose, voluntary nature, confidentiality limits, and absence of any promised outcome.
- The participant's own description of strengths, conditions in which they do their best work, desired experiences, possible next roles, and any opportunities they do not want.
- The participant's consent about what may be recorded, who may see it, and when it should be reviewed or deleted under applicable policy.
- A visible inventory of bounded projects, mentoring, shadowing, rotations, and stretch assignments, including purpose, prerequisites, duration, workload, sponsor, and available support.
- Current responsibilities, capacity, work that can be removed or reprioritized, accessibility needs voluntarily disclosed through the appropriate process, and relevant employment or compensation constraints.
- Published, job-relevant matching criteria and a way to compare access across eligible people rather than rewarding only those with the strongest informal sponsorship.
- A named owner, confidential storage location, review date, and route to HR, accessibility, privacy, ethics, employee-representation, or legal support where required.

## Procedure

1. **Separate the conversation.** Schedule a development discussion outside performance, merit, promotion, and compensation reviews. Explain that participation and disclosure are voluntary, that silence or refusal will not be treated negatively, and that the conversation does not promise a role or opportunity.
2. **Agree the information boundary.** State what will be recorded, who can access it, how long it will be retained, and what confidentiality cannot be guaranteed. Do not request protected personal information, medical details, family circumstances, or broad "whole self" disclosure.
3. **Ask, rather than assume.** Invite the person to describe when they are at their best, which strengths they want to use, what they want to learn, which experiences interest them, and whether any possible next roles are worth exploring. Let the person decline individual questions.
4. **Create the aspiration brief.** Record the person's words, desired experiences, possible directions, non-goals, timing preferences, and unresolved constraints. Mark the record as an aspiration, not a performance assessment, succession decision, promotion commitment, or claim of readiness.
5. **Maintain an opportunity inventory.** List available projects, mentoring, shadowing, rotations, and stretch assignments with their learning purpose, prerequisites, decision rights, duration, time demand, sponsor, support, compensation or classification implications, and accessibility considerations.
6. **Apply a transparent match.** Compare the aspiration with job-relevant opportunity criteria, current capability, timing, support, and capacity. Check who else was eligible, how comparable opportunities have been allocated, and whether informal visibility, self-advocacy, proximity, working pattern, disability, caregiving, or another protected or access-related factor could produce unequal opportunity.
7. **Match, defer, or decline explicitly.** Record the decision and reason. For a deferral or decline, identify the relevant constraint, any evidence or prerequisite needed, alternatives considered, the decision owner, and a review date. Do not present an opportunity decision as a hidden performance rating.
8. **Charter a bounded experience.** For a match, agree the objective, scope, duration, expected hours, work to remove or reprioritize, sponsor or mentor, resources, decision authority, accessibility support, learning evidence, contribution credit, check-ins, and stop or exit conditions.
9. **Confirm voluntary acceptance.** Give the person the complete terms before accepting. Permit refusal or withdrawal without retaliation or adverse performance inference. Escalate material changes to duties, hours, pay, classification, location, or employment terms through the required formal process.
10. **Review experience and access.** At the agreed date, inspect workload, support, accessibility, learning evidence, contribution credit, and any harm or exclusion. Separately review allocation patterns across eligible people for repeated deferrals, concentrated access, or unsupported stretch load.
11. **Refresh the record.** Let the person revise or withdraw an aspiration, close or extend the opportunity, and record the next action, owner, rationale, and review date. Retain only information authorized by policy and the participant's agreed disclosure scope.

## Inspectable output

The workflow produces:

- a dated aspiration brief with disclosure and access permissions;
- a versioned opportunity inventory with prerequisites, workload, support, and owners;
- a match, deferral, or decline record with transparent reasons and a review date;
- for an accepted match, a bounded experience charter with scope, time, removed work, support, accessibility, credit, and exit conditions;
- a confidential review log showing learning evidence, participant feedback, workload effects, changes, and next actions;
- an aggregate fair-access check that identifies allocation patterns without exposing unnecessary personal information.

## Reuse boundaries

This workflow supports development conversations and practical learning opportunities. It does not replace performance management, succession planning, promotion governance, compensation review, job evaluation, workforce planning, grievance or disciplinary processes, accessibility accommodations, employment law, collective agreements, works-council or employee-representation duties, privacy review, or formal HR decisions. An aspiration is neither proof of readiness nor an entitlement, and a match is not a promotion promise.

Do not require career disclosure, treat stability as lack of ambition, penalize someone for declining stretch work, or make unpaid excess workload the price of development. Managers must not infer protected characteristics or seek sensitive personal explanations for availability. Use the organization's formal process when an opportunity changes duties, hours, pay, classification, reporting line, location, access needs, or employment terms. Provide a safe route outside the reporting line for confidentiality, fairness, accessibility, retaliation, or conflict concerns.

The practitioner interview supports asking when a person is at their best and maintaining a learning orientation at **32:41–33:39**; inviting thoughtful discussion of desired roles while acknowledging that a request may not be granted at **33:39–36:01**; and holding a separate career cadence focused on desired experiences and practical opportunity matching at **36:01–37:34** (source: Ed Breault on communicating with clarity and leading with curiosity.md; canonical body title: *Zarina Stanford on Mission-Driven Authentic Leadership*). The source does not establish that this workflow improves retention, performance, promotion equity, wellbeing, or career success. The confidentiality, fair-access, workload, accessibility, anti-retaliation, privacy, HR, and employment boundaries are governance additions.

The reported "nine times out of ten" opportunity anecdote at **36:53** is excluded as an outcome rate. A twice-yearly cadence is an example, not a universal requirement.
