# Contextual Next-Best-Action Loop

**Summary**: Convert account observations into a jointly chosen next meaningful touch without treating isolated intent or website activity as proof of purchase.

---

## When to use

Use when sales and marketing have a shared account list, a governed library of possible actions, and enough first-party and CRM context to interpret activity.

## Procedure

1. Collect recent first-party behavior, relevant third-party intent, CRM history, relationship context, and known buying-group roles.
2. Look for multi-session patterns, topic concentration, stakeholder expansion, or other changes rather than reacting to one event.
3. Update an explicit working account state and record the evidence and uncertainty behind the change.
4. Select from approved actions, offers, content, or experiences that fit the person, account, and buying-group state.
5. Review consequential outreach and state changes with the accountable sales and marketing owners.
6. Assign the next meaningful touch, observe the response, and update the state again.
7. Periodically remove noisy triggers and actions that do not support useful decisions.

### Planned cadence and triggered response

Treat this loop as the responsive layer of a longer account plan, not the whole plan. Maintain a separate, capacity-backed cadence of useful, stage-appropriate touches; when a new signal appears, review it against account history, relationship context, buying-group coverage, and the current working state before selecting or suppressing an action. The signal starts interpretation and next-action selection, not an automatic pitch (source: Untitled 7.md; canonical title: *How to Create an Account-Based Playbook for Long Sales Cycles and Multiple Decision-Makers*; practitioner webinar; analysis: P29-W4-C22).

The action record should state whether the action came from the planned cadence or a signal-triggered review, which context changed the decision, and whether consent, contact policy, account coordination, and delivery capacity permit execution. The volume or sequence of touches does not establish awareness, influence, attribution, or purchase intent.

## Output

An explainable account-state update, a named next action, an owner, and a record of the evidence used.

## Guardrails

The loop supports prioritization, not causal attribution or autonomous revenue optimization. Conversion and pipeline outcomes reported by vendors are excluded (sources: Live Case Study How to get an executive buy-in and transform your GTM strategy.md; Data-driven precision targeting Workday’s winning ABM formula  OnBase podcast.md; Your website is missing buying signals  Behavioral ABM with Radoj Glisik  OnBase podcast.md; analysis: P11-C03, P12-C02, P13-C05).
