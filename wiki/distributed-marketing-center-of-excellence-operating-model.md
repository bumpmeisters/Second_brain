# Distributed Marketing Center-of-Excellence Operating Model

**Summary**: Build a distributed marketing function around a coherent professional core while preserving explicit local authority. Scale only after bounded wins establish trust, and use recurring business reviews to revise either the shared core or justified local exceptions.

---

## Trigger

Use this operating model when regional or business-unit marketers need local responsiveness but fragmented reporting lines, piecemeal capability requests, inconsistent standards, or weak professional development prevent the function from operating coherently.

Do not begin with an organizational redesign alone. First establish a decision-sufficient baseline and demonstrate bounded wins that make the value of a broader operating change inspectable (source: Mary Gilbert on Implementing Systems Thinking in Marketing.md; body title: *Ellina Shinnick on How to Be a Fearless Leader*; transcript 8:34–10:47).

## Required inputs

- Current business goals, regional or business-unit priorities, and the marketing outcomes they require.
- An inventory of existing capabilities, capacity, workflows, assets, measures, reporting lines, and material gaps.
- Evidence from bounded work that shows where coordinated marketing can improve a real business decision or outcome.
- Named central discipline leads and local or field marketing owners.
- A draft set of shared principles, KPI definitions, process standards, reporting rules, and professional-development expectations.
- Documented local differences in customers, segments, language, culture, channels, regulation, capacity, and commercial practice.
- Executive sponsors and local business leaders willing to participate in recurring plan and evidence reviews.

## Procedure

1. **Diagnose the current operating constraint.** Map where capabilities are missing or duplicated, where standards diverge, how local work is requested, who makes decisions, and which business outcomes are difficult to support. Separate true capacity gaps from unclear ownership, weak process, or absent expertise.
2. **Earn authority through bounded wins.** Choose a small number of visible business problems, agree on measures and decision owners, deliver the work, and record the result and stakeholder feedback. Use the evidence to build partnership and advocacy before requesting a larger structural change (source: Mary Gilbert on Implementing Systems Thinking in Marketing.md; body title: *Ellina Shinnick on How to Be a Fearless Leader*; transcript 8:34–10:47).
3. **Define the complete capability portfolio.** Name the disciplines the business needs, such as brand, content, digital, analytics, demand generation, communications, field marketing, events, or partner marketing. For each capability, record its mandate, owner, inputs, outputs, service boundary, and required capacity.
4. **Assign central and local decision rights.** Keep professional leadership, shared principles, KPI definitions, reusable processes, core assets, reporting, and development paths coherent. Give local owners authority to translate business goals into regional plans and adapt execution where customer, market, language, culture, or channel evidence requires it (source: Mary Gilbert on Implementing Systems Thinking in Marketing.md; body title: *Ellina Shinnick on How to Be a Fearless Leader*; transcript 10:47–15:45).
5. **Create a core-versus-local decision matrix.** For every important workflow or asset, mark it as:
   - **Core** when consistency, comparability, governance, or reuse is essential.
   - **Locally adaptable** when the shared intent and evidence model remain fixed but execution should reflect local context.
   - **Local exception** when a documented legal, cultural, language, commercial, or capacity condition makes the core unsuitable.
6. **Install a common operating cadence.** Require local plans to state goals, audiences, activities, owners, dependencies, measures, and justified adaptations. Review each plan with the relevant business leader on a recurring cadence; the source describes monthly reviews as its working practice, not as a universal interval (source: Mary Gilbert on Implementing Systems Thinking in Marketing.md; body title: *Ellina Shinnick on How to Be a Fearless Leader*; transcript 12:40–15:45).
7. **Review variation as evidence.** Compare equivalent work across units. Determine whether a difference reflects market context, better local practice, enablement, capacity, process failure, or silent drift. Preserve the rationale and supporting evidence instead of allowing unexplained divergence.
8. **Update the operating contract.** Promote repeated useful local adaptations into the shared core, retain justified exceptions with owners and review dates, and remediate variation caused by missing support or broken orchestration.
9. **Scale at the pace of adoption.** Before expanding the model, verify that central and local owners understand their authority, reporting is usable, exceptions are visible, and stakeholders can influence changes. Bringing partners along may slow initial delivery but can improve the durability of the operating agreement; the source does not establish a universal performance effect (source: Mary Gilbert on Implementing Systems Thinking in Marketing.md; body title: *Ellina Shinnick on How to Be a Fearless Leader*; transcript 38:14–40:17).

## Inspectable output

The completed operating package contains:

- a current-state diagnosis and evidence from the bounded wins;
- a complete marketing capability map and service boundaries;
- named central discipline leads, local owners, and decision rights;
- a core-versus-local matrix with approved exceptions;
- shared principles, KPI definitions, process standards, asset rules, and reporting fields;
- local plan records and a recurring business-review calendar;
- an exception and learning register with owners, evidence, and review dates;
- a professional-development path for marketers working across the distributed model;
- a dated decision to scale, revise, pause, or narrow the model.

## Reuse boundaries

- This model does not establish that centralization causes growth, retention, executive influence, functional respect, or better marketing performance. Those outcomes in the source are self-reported and not independently verified.
- The source reflects an acquisition-heavy North American insurance organization. Adapt the capability portfolio, decision rights, cadence, and local boundaries to the actual business.
- Do not standardize away legal, regulatory, cultural, language, customer, or commercial differences. Route those conditions through the explicit exception process.
- Do not use the model when units lack a comparable marketing motion or when the proposed core has no accountable owner, usable measures, or capacity to support local teams.
- Common reporting creates comparability, not causal attribution. Local and commercial outcomes still require appropriate evidence and interpretation.
- The source filename mentions Mary Gilbert and systems thinking, but the body is an Ellina Shinnick interview and supplies no standalone systems-thinking method. No systems-thinking claim should be inferred from the filename.
- Creative campaign, culture, attention, employee-growth, and leadership anecdotes from the interview are outside this operating model (source: Mary Gilbert on Implementing Systems Thinking in Marketing.md; analysis: P18-C04).
