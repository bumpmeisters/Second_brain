# Export performance and dynamic capabilities: review scope and gaps

**Review scope:** PDF pp. 1, 6, 20–21 and selected passages of p. 18; methodological and conclusion review, not all 24 pages.

Dayangan and Aykol synthesize 53 studies in 50 articles through the end of 2024, classifying three capability orders across individual, group, organizational, and interorganizational units. Their review concentrates on higher/intermediate organizational marketing capabilities; individual/team and lower-order capabilities are less studied. Absence of research does not establish absence of value. (source: Dynamic-capabilities-behind-performance-of-exporting-fi_2026_Industrial-Mark.pdf, pp. 1, 6, 18, 20–21; DOI 10.1016/j.indmarman.2026.01.003)

Most included evidence is survey-based and heavily cross-sectional; the article reports 83% surveys and 79.2% SEM. Journal filtering, heterogeneous capability definitions, and limited contextual detail constrain generalization. This review is evidence about a literature, not a causal experiment establishing a universal performance lift. (source: same PDF, pp. 6, 20–21)

P58-C08 proposes that [[marketing-operating-system]] distinguish capability, unit of analysis, mechanism, outcome, and context when making capability claims. This is an evidence discipline, not authorization to resume the paused MOS implementation.
