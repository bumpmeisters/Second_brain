# Enterprise Sales Motion Selection

**Summary**: Select an initial enterprise sales motion as a testable market hypothesis. Use buyer exposure and the portability of credible proof to distinguish reference-led depth from broader replacement-led execution, then test the choice with real buyer evidence and a bounded reassessment point.

---

## Trigger

Use this framework when an enterprise product or AI startup must decide where limited selling, implementation, and reference-building capacity should go first. Apply it to one defined market or vertical at a time; the result is not a permanent company identity.

## Required inputs

- A defined market slice, buyer group, problem, current alternative, and intended outcome.
- Evidence about buyer exposure: operational, regulatory, legal, reputational, security, integration, and customer-facing consequences of a bad purchase.
- Evidence about proof portability: whether a credible reference from one buyer reduces uncertainty for sufficiently similar buyers.
- Existing budget or workflow, education burden, measurable economics, product readiness, implementation capacity, and a local unit-economics threshold.
- Real buyer conversations, objections, purchase behavior, or comparable evidence—not only founder preference or famous-logo appeal.
- An accountable commercial owner and, when a POC is possible, delivery and evaluation owners.

## Decision rule

| Evidence pattern | Provisional hypothesis | Primary learning job |
|---|---|---|
| High buyer exposure and credible proof travels between similar buyers | **Lighthouse** | Establish safety, fit, and reference evidence through a small set of deeply supported accounts. |
| Lower buyer exposure, an existing budget or workflow, demonstrable economics, and weaker dependence on marquee proof | **Landgrab** | Test whether a repeatable offer can win across a bounded segment without extensive custom reference work. |
| Axes point in different directions or the product requires both reference depth and repeatability | **Mixed or sequenced** | State which motion comes first, what the other motion must wait for, and the transition evidence. |
| Market boundary, exposure, proof portability, product readiness, or buyer evidence is weak | **Insufficient evidence** | Run discovery or a smaller buyer test before selecting a motion. |

These labels are working hypotheses. A well-known logo is useful only when the reference is credible and relevant to the next buyer; broad demand is useful only when the product can be delivered safely and repeatably (source: 2026-08-13--aakZLqxRQfo.md; practitioner framework; analysis: P33-W5-C01).

## Procedure

1. **Bound the market.** Name the buyer, vertical, problem, current alternative, geography if material, and decision period. Do not score an undefined “enterprise market.”
2. **Record buyer exposure.** Separate the cost of a bad purchase from the visibility of the product inside the buyer's operation or customer experience. Cite evidence and unresolved risks.
3. **Test proof portability.** Identify which future buyers could legitimately learn from a reference, what evidence they would trust, and why the situations are comparable. Do not substitute logo prestige for transferability.
4. **Cross-check commercial conditions.** Record existing budget, replacement versus category-creation burden, economic measurability, sales-cycle expectations, product readiness, implementation capacity, and the minimum locally acceptable economics.
5. **Select a provisional motion.** Choose lighthouse, landgrab, mixed/sequenced, or insufficient evidence. Record why the rejected options fit less well and which assumption could change the decision.
6. **Define the smallest buyer test.** For lighthouse, select a small, relevant reference set and specify the evidence the relationship must create. For landgrab, define a bounded segment, a repeatable offer, and the evidence needed before expanding volume.
7. **Contract any POC.** Set scope, permitted data and systems, owner, delivery obligations, fixed end date, product-level success and failure criteria, buyer adoption responsibilities, cost boundary, security and procurement gates, and purchase or stop decision. Prevent an open-ended science project.
8. **Review evidence, not activity.** Compare buyer behavior, implementation evidence, accepted outcomes, economics, failure modes, and reference portability with the original hypothesis. Meetings, calls, logos, trials, and generated pipeline are not success by themselves.
9. **Reassess deliberately.** Continue, narrow, sequence the other motion, or stop. Revisit the choice when product maturity, market education, vertical focus, delivery capacity, regulation, or buyer evidence changes.

## Inspectable output

The completed record contains:

- the defined market and dated decision;
- buyer-exposure and proof-portability evidence with confidence and gaps;
- the selected motion hypothesis and rejected alternatives;
- the bounded account set or segment, owner, capacity, and economic threshold;
- a buyer-test or POC contract with success, failure, purchase, and stop conditions;
- the review evidence and continue, narrow, sequence, switch, or stop decision;
- the next reassessment trigger or date.

## Reuse boundaries

- The source is an investor-produced practitioner discussion using portfolio-company and operator anecdotes. It does not validate the framework or the named companies' outcomes.
- Lighthouse and landgrab are not exhaustive market categories, guaranteed growth strategies, or permanent identities. Mixed and sequential motions can be appropriate.
- This framework does not authorize prospecting, outreach, data collection, trials, contracting, procurement bypass, deployment, or reference use. Apply contact, privacy, security, legal, delivery-capacity, and approval controls separately.
- Do not infer that a famous customer proves product safety, that a broad market proves repeatability, or that a successful POC caused adoption or commercial value.
- Exact ACV, quota, trial-length, account-count, and unit-economics thresholds must be set from local evidence. The source's current AI-market, company-performance, and timing claims are excluded.
