# Marketing Change Pilot Buy-In Memo

**Summary**: Separate agreement on the business problem from agreement on a proposed solution, then use a bounded pilot memo to make authorization, capacity, measurement, and review conditions explicit. The memo authorizes a learning test; it does not promise revenue or establish causal impact.

---

## Trigger

Use this decision gate before launching a consequential marketing or GTM change that has not yet been demonstrated in the organization. It is especially useful when leadership expects near-term evidence, the commercial motion has a long feedback cycle, or the proposal needs participation from marketing, sales, RevOps, customer success, or another capacity owner.

Do not begin by selling the preferred tactic. First establish whether the current operating problem is real, material, connected to an executive priority, and important enough to address now. Only after problem buy-in should change champions help define a bounded solution and pilot (source: Full-funnel marketing 2.0. Show marketing impact on revenue in 90 days 1.md; transcript 20:28-24:43).

## Required inputs

- The executive priority or business objective potentially affected.
- Evidence about the current process, including relevant buyer-journey, deal, account, funnel, or operating records.
- A bounded description of the current gap and the cost, risk, or opportunity cost of continuing unchanged.
- Known evidence limitations, contradictions, and assumptions; do not invent financial precision.
- Named decision owners and change champions from the affected functions.
- A proposed pilot scope, exclusions, dependencies, timeline, and stop conditions.
- Named roles, available capacity, required approvals, and any compensation or priority constraints.
- Baseline definitions and locally appropriate leading, lagging, quality, and guardrail measures.
- A review date and the evidence required for an approve, revise, defer, or reject decision.

## Procedure

1. **Frame the executive decision.** State the priority at risk, the decision required, the accountable decision owner, and why the decision is needed now.
2. **Establish problem buy-in.** Inspect current-process and buyer evidence, describe the material gap, and connect it to the stated executive priority. Separate observed evidence from inference and unresolved uncertainty.
3. **Describe the cost of continuing unchanged.** Record verified costs, risks, delays, or missed learning. Label estimates and qualitative risks explicitly; do not manufacture ROI or urgency.
4. **Confirm the problem decision.** Ask the decision owners to agree, revise, defer, or reject the problem statement before presenting the preferred solution. Record dissent and missing evidence.
5. **Co-design a bounded pilot.** With affected change champions, define the smallest useful change, its exclusions, expected learning, dependencies, stop conditions, and what existing work or resources it may displace. Present it as a limited test, not a hidden enterprise-wide transformation (source: Full-funnel marketing 2.0. Show marketing impact on revenue in 90 days 1.md; transcript 20:28-24:43).
6. **Write the pilot memo.** Include the problem, evidence, cost or risk of inaction, strategic relevance, proposed scope, exclusions, team, roles, capacity, timeline, assumptions, measures, dashboard owner, review cadence, and decision conditions (source: Full-funnel marketing 2.0. Show marketing impact on revenue in 90 days 1.md; transcript 27:38-28:47).
7. **Fix success and guardrail criteria before launch.** Define what observable evidence would support continuation, revision, stopping, or further investigation. Obtain written agreement so criteria are not moved retrospectively (source: Full-funnel marketing 2.0. Show marketing impact on revenue in 90 days 1.md; transcript 31:16-32:21).
8. **Run the authorization review.** Require an explicit `approve`, `revise`, `defer`, or `reject` decision. An approval must name the authorized scope, committed capacity, owners, constraints, review date, and escalation path.
9. **Confirm onboarding conditions.** Before work starts, verify that every participant understands the sequence, responsibilities, time commitment, measures, reporting view, and decision cadence. Reopen the decision if required capacity or assumptions have materially changed (source: Full-funnel marketing 2.0. Show marketing impact on revenue in 90 days 1.md; transcript 51:29-53:16).
10. **Predeclare the maturity decision.** State what evidence would keep the work experimental, justify another bounded pilot, permit documented operationalization, or support a scoped scale test. Automation, AI, or a new platform may support a sufficiently understood process after review; they are not evidence that the underlying method works (source: Untitled 9.md; canonical title: *Full-Funnel's Fundamentals*; practitioner webinar; analysis: P29-W4-C24).

## Inspectable output

Produce one versioned memo containing:

- Decision requested, decision owner, date, and current status.
- Executive priority and evidence-bounded problem statement.
- Current-process evidence, uncertainty, and cost or risk of inaction.
- Proposed pilot objective, scope, exclusions, timeline, and stop conditions.
- Named change champions, roles, capacity commitments, dependencies, and escalation path.
- Baseline definitions, success criteria, guardrails, evidence sources, and dashboard owner.
- Assumptions, unresolved questions, review date, and decision conditions.
- Recorded `approve`, `revise`, `defer`, or `reject` outcome, including rationale and any conditions.

An approved memo is complete only when the necessary capacity owners have confirmed their commitments and the success criteria are fixed before onboarding.

## Reuse boundaries

- The method structures an internal authorization decision; it does not demonstrate that a marketing intervention caused account movement, pipeline, revenue, or any other commercial outcome.
- Do not promise revenue impact within 90 days or reuse the source's 30-, 60-, or 90-day examples as universal timing or performance thresholds. The webinar itself acknowledges that long sales cycles may not produce pipeline or revenue during a short pilot (source: Full-funnel marketing 2.0. Show marketing impact on revenue in 90 days 1.md; transcript 25:15-25:48, 38:25-39:19, and 45:00-45:29).
- Replies, meetings, registrations, engagement, and account-state changes may be useful observations, but they do not by themselves prove demand, purchase intent, influence, or attribution.
- Shared team credit or an attribution label can support incentives, but it is not a causal measurement method.
- Pilot duration, scope, baselines, evidence thresholds, and guardrails must be adapted to the local buying cycle, risk, data quality, and decision.
- The source is mixed practitioner/vendor material promoting a book, courses, community, and consulting. Its customer outcomes, research numbers, touchpoint counts, benchmark ratios, and effectiveness claims are excluded.
- This gate precedes execution. Use [[abm-sales-marketing-operating-contract]] when the pilot needs a detailed cross-functional account-motion agreement and [[lean-weekly-abm-pilot-loop]] when an approved ABM pilot enters weekly operation.
- Legal, finance, HR, privacy, procurement, brand, accessibility, and employment approvals remain separate governance requirements where applicable.
