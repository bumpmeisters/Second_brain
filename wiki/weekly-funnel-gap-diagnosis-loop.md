# Weekly Funnel-Gap Diagnosis Loop

**Summary**: A weekly operating loop for turning a meaningful funnel discrepancy into a bounded diagnosis, a narrow process test, and an evidence-based scale, revise, or stop decision.

---

## When to use

Use this loop when a new-logo, expansion, or renewal funnel diverges materially from a target, a historical baseline, or a comparable region or segment. It is also useful after a meaningful process change when the team needs to detect intended effects and adjacent damage early.

Do not use it to infer causality from an aggregate dashboard alone. The loop narrows uncertainty; it does not replace controlled comparison or independent validation.

## Required inputs

- Decision-sufficient funnel definitions and data, with known limitations recorded.
- Separate views for new-logo, expansion, and renewal motions where applicable.
- A comparison point: target, historical period, region, segment, or equivalent cohort.
- Access to the underlying opportunity or deal records.
- A named owner for diagnosis and follow-through.
- One intended metric and at least one adjacent guardrail metric.

## Weekly procedure

1. **Name the decision.** State which operational decision the review must support and what level of data quality is sufficient for that decision.
2. **Decompose the funnel.** Separate new-logo, expansion, and renewal motions, then segment by the smallest useful region, period, account group, or stage.
3. **Locate the material gap.** Compare actual performance with the selected baseline and identify the narrowest discrepancy large enough to investigate.
4. **Inspect the underlying deals.** Review the records behind the aggregate difference. Distinguish a local anomaly, a data problem, an execution failure, and a potentially systemic issue.
5. **State a falsifiable hypothesis.** Describe the suspected mechanism, the evidence that would support it, and what would disconfirm it.
6. **Run the smallest useful correction.** Change one bounded process element with limited exposure, a named owner, and a review date.
7. **Monitor target and guardrails.** Track the intended metric alongside adjacent measures that could reveal displacement, quality loss, delay, or harm elsewhere in the funnel.
8. **Decide and record.** Scale, revise, stop, or gather more evidence. Record the diagnosis, test conditions, observed result, limitations, and next decision.

## Inspectable output

Each cycle should leave a compact diagnosis record containing:

- the observed gap and comparison basis;
- the deal-level evidence reviewed;
- the local-versus-systemic assessment;
- the test hypothesis, owner, scope, and duration;
- the intended and guardrail measures;
- the scale, revise, stop, or continue-learning decision.

## Reuse boundaries

The source supports the operating method, not Tufin's reported outcomes or a universal causal model. Company performance claims, attribution examples, private-equity heuristics, and third-party validation claims are excluded. Treat regional or historical differences as diagnostic signals until deal-level review and a bounded test provide stronger evidence (source: Brian Gladstein on cybersecurity marketing transformation at Tufin.md; transcript anchors 12:30-13:40, 14:30-15:27, 15:46-17:52, 18:06-18:40, and 20:11-21:37).
