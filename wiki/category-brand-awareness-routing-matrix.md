# Category-Brand Awareness Routing Matrix

**Summary**: Diagnose the awareness problem for a specific solution before choosing channels. Category maturity and brand familiarity determine whether the immediate job is to refresh perception, build familiarity, teach category value, or borrow external credibility.

---

## Trigger

Use this decision rule when an account-based, brand, or demand team knows that awareness is insufficient but has not established what kind of awareness problem it must solve. Apply it at solution level: a familiar company can still launch an unfamiliar offer in a new category.

## Required inputs

- The specific solution, offer, or proposition being routed.
- Evidence of category maturity, such as stable buyer language, known alternatives, established budgets, analyst or association coverage, and repeated customer recognition.
- Evidence of brand familiarity among the intended accounts and buying-group roles, such as direct customer research, aided or unaided recall, known-account engagement, seller evidence, and prior evaluation history.
- Current associations with the solution and whether they are accurate, current, and desirable.
- Available proof, credible experts, customers, partners, analysts, communities, or associations.
- Budget, capacity, geographic, legal, and channel constraints.
- A named owner and review date.

## Procedure

1. **Define the unit.** Name one solution, buying situation, target-account set, and relevant buying-group roles. Do not route a diversified company as one undifferentiated brand.
2. **Assess category maturity.** Mark the category as established, emerging, or uncertain and cite the evidence. Record disagreement rather than forcing a confident label.
3. **Assess brand familiarity.** Mark familiarity with this solution among the intended accounts and roles as high, low, or uncertain. Keep observed reach, inferred familiarity, and stated recall separate.
4. **Select the primary awareness job.**
   - **Established category + familiar brand — refresh perception.** Test whether buyers' current associations reflect the solution today, then correct stale, incomplete, or unwanted perceptions.
   - **Established category + unfamiliar brand — build familiarity.** Make the solution recognizable and give buyers a credible reason to include it in the known set.
   - **Emerging category + familiar brand — teach category value.** Explain the problem, the category's role, and when the approach is or is not useful before emphasizing product preference.
   - **Emerging category + unfamiliar brand — borrow external credibility.** Establish category relevance and brand legitimacy through credible customers, experts, partners, analysts, communities, or associations before expecting direct brand claims to carry the argument.
5. **Name the evidence gap.** State what buyers would need to know, believe, recognize, or verify for the selected job to advance.
6. **Choose channels only after routing.** Evaluate candidate channels and collaborators by audience presence, credibility, permission, format fit, cost, and the evidence gap. The quadrant does not prescribe paid social, events, email, partnerships, or any other universal channel.
7. **Set directional measures.** Define solution-appropriate indicators such as recall research, target-account reach, buying-group coverage, relevant engagement, perception change, or inclusion in evaluations. Label them as observations or contribution evidence, not causal attribution.
8. **Review and reroute.** On the review date, inspect new customer, account, seller, and performance evidence. Retain the route, change the execution, or move the solution to another quadrant with the rationale recorded.

## Inspectable output

A completed routing record contains:

- solution, buying situation, target accounts, and roles;
- category-maturity decision and cited evidence;
- brand-familiarity decision and cited evidence;
- uncertainty and conflicting observations;
- selected quadrant and primary awareness job;
- current associations and evidence gaps;
- candidate credibility sources and channel-selection criteria;
- directional measures, owner, and review date;
- retain, revise, or reroute decision after review.

## Reuse boundaries

The matrix selects an awareness problem; it does not prove that a category or brand is objectively mature, prescribe a universal channel mix, or establish that awareness caused evaluation, pipeline, or purchase. Website absence, intent data, event attendance, content engagement, and seller impressions are incomplete observations and cannot individually prove familiarity or buying status.

The source supports the two axes, the four routing jobs, and solution-level assessment at **36:03–38:59**. Its buyer percentages, shortlist claims, intent-data results, duration benchmarks, and causal claims about awareness, trust, engagement, or purchase are excluded. Claims about current LLM-mediated discovery are time-sensitive and require separate verification (source: How to create awareness among strategic accounts 2.md; transcript anchor 36:03–38:59).
