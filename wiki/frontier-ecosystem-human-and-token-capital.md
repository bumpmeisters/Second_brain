# Frontier Ecosystem, Human Capital, and Token Capital

**Summary**: The source argues that firms should own a model-independent learning loop in which human judgment and organizational AI capability compound together.

---

The source distinguishes human capital — knowledge, judgment, relationships, ingenuity, and pattern recognition — from token capital, the AI capability a firm builds and owns (source: A frontier without an ecosystem is not stable.md).

Its core strategic test is portability: if a company can replace the general-purpose model without losing accumulated expertise, then the durable advantage lives in the company's workflows, evaluations, knowledge base, and learning traces rather than in a vendor's model (source: A frontier without an ecosystem is not stable.md).

The proposed compounding loop is:

- humans set goals and exercise judgment;
- workflows turn judgment into repeatable action;
- private evaluations measure outcomes that matter to the organization;
- traces and accepted lessons improve the organization's intelligence layer;
- interchangeable models execute against that layer (source: A frontier without an ecosystem is not stable.md).

The essay further argues that a healthy AI economy needs an ecosystem in which companies and communities retain value from their own knowledge rather than ceding all returns to a few model providers. This is a normative argument, not an established forecast (source: A frontier without an ecosystem is not stable.md).

## Open questions

- What organizational knowledge should be captured as durable context, and what should remain tacit?
- Which private evaluations genuinely measure business outcomes?
- How can learning traces be retained without compromising privacy, security, or employee agency?
