# Customer Journey Mapping

**Summary**: Customer journey mapping describes how customers move from passive awareness through active evaluation and purchase, often with expanding and contracting consideration sets.

---

The HP customer journey template says the journey is not a simple funnel but an expanding and contracting universe of visible solutions (source: HP_Customer Journey Template.pptx).

The journey template names stages including passive awareness, active awareness and perception, consideration, active evaluation, and purchase (source: HP_Customer Journey Template.pptx).

This journey view is useful for [[campaign-types-and-funnel-stages]] because different campaign types can support different stages of awareness, consideration, evaluation, and purchase.

## Maturity as a continuing customer journey

A maturity model can complement journey stages when it describes the customer's operating capability rather than the seller's funnel position. Assess technology, process, people, skills, and change readiness across multiple stakeholders; connect the current state to customer-visible next steps, relevant education, realistic packaging, and a budget horizon. The same progression should continue after purchase so customer success can update the state through adoption evidence instead of restarting the relationship with a product-led upsell (source: Bryan Law on Brains, Data, and Distinction.md; canonical title: *Sydney Sloan on GTM, Leadership, and Business Alignment*; practitioner transcript).

Treat maturity as an evidence-backed working assessment. Preserve contradictory stakeholder views, cite the observed practices behind a stage change, and avoid assuming that maturity predicts revenue or that every customer should advance toward the same endpoint (analysis: [[marketing-sales-and-buyer-enablement-library]]).
