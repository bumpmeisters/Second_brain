# Merchant of Record vs. Payment Service Provider

**Summary**: The commercial source explains when a software company might move from operating its own global commerce stack around a payment service provider to outsourcing legal-seller, tax, compliance, and payment responsibilities to a merchant of record.

---

A payment service provider such as Stripe processes payments while the software company generally remains the merchant of record and retains responsibility for tax registration and filing, compliance, billing operations, fraud, disputes, reporting, renewals, and customer experience (source: Stripe Alternative for SaaS When to Consider an MoR.md).

A merchant of record becomes the legal seller for covered transactions and takes on more of the payment, tax, compliance, fraud, and chargeback responsibility. The tradeoff is reduced operational ownership in exchange for provider fees, platform dependency, and less direct control (source: Stripe Alternative for SaaS When to Consider an MoR.md).

The source suggests reassessing the model when global expansion, subscription complexity, localization, enterprise requirements, tax exposure, failed-payment optimization, and internal maintenance become material business burdens (source: Stripe Alternative for SaaS When to Consider an MoR.md).

## Caveats

- The article is published by Cleverbridge, a merchant-of-record provider, and is therefore commercially interested.
- Fee examples, Stripe product scope, and claims about effective transaction cost are time-sensitive and were not independently verified.
- Legal and tax treatment depends on jurisdiction and contract structure; this page is not legal, tax, or financial advice.
