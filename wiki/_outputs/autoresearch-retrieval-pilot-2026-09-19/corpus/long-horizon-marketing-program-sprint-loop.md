# Long-Horizon Marketing Program Sprint Loop

**Summary**: Convert an approved long-term marketing or GTM program into capacity-aware sprints. Each sprint delivers or learns something inspectable, separates activity from evidence and commercial outcomes, and changes the next sprint through an explicit decision.

---

## Trigger

Use this playbook after a strategic marketing or GTM program is fundamentally authorized but before its distant outcome becomes the only basis for judging progress. It fits programs involving several roles, delayed commercial feedback, material dependencies, changing capacity, and multiple planning horizons.

The source recommends decomposing a long-term program into bounded sprints with milestones, weekly reviews, retrospectives, capacity by role, early indicators, and cumulative reporting. It also distinguishes an executive overview from the team's detailed plan (source: Account based content.md; canonical title: *How to plan long term marketing and sales programs*; transcript 3:31-14:10, 24:42-36:10, and 43:13-50:33).

## Required inputs

- Authorized program objective, accountable owner, intended beneficiaries, and decision horizon.
- Current state, strategic assumptions, constraints, exclusions, and stop conditions.
- Known fixed dates, dependencies, approvals, and external commitments.
- Actual available capacity by role and planning period, confirmed by capacity owners.
- A working outcome path separating controllable activities, early observations, commercial outcomes, and guardrails.
- Stable metric definitions, evidence sources, owners, and known data limitations.
- Weekly operating owner, sprint decision owner, escalation path, and reporting audience.

## Procedure

1. **Restate the authorized boundary.** Record the program objective, horizon, scope, exclusions, owner, constraints, and the evidence that would require reconsideration. Do not reopen the whole business case in every sprint.
2. **Map the outcome path.** Distinguish what the team can deliver, what early audience or operating evidence may be observed, what commercial outcomes are delayed, and which guardrails must not deteriorate. Mark causal links as hypotheses.
3. **Create a sprint sequence.** Divide the horizon into the smallest meaningful learning or delivery stages. Each sprint must end with an inspectable decision, not merely a date.
4. **Confirm capacity by role.** Record realistically available time for each required function after existing commitments. Obtain confirmation from actual line, priority, and budget owners.
5. **Set the sprint contract.** Define objective, decision, scope, exclusions, deliverables, milestones, activities, early evidence, commercial observations, guardrails, dependencies, owners, dates, and escalation conditions.
6. **Fit scope to capacity.** Reduce, defer, sequence, or rescope work when capacity and dependencies cannot support the original sprint. Do not preserve an impossible scope by assuming unpaid or invisible work.
7. **Operate and review weekly.** Compare committed work, completed work, evidence, quality, blockers, capacity changes, and assumptions. Record changes without rewriting the original baseline.
8. **Close the sprint.** Run a retrospective and choose `continue`, `revise`, `stop`, or `rescope`. State the evidence, uncertainty, implications, and the owner of each corrective action.
9. **Update cumulative evidence.** Add the sprint's targets, actuals, definitions, effort, guardrails, decisions, and unresolved questions to the program dashboard.
10. **Authorize the next sprint.** Reconfirm objective, capacity, dependencies, and decision criteria using the accumulated evidence. Escalate when the required change exceeds the authorized program boundary.
11. **Choose the maturity transition.** After retrospective, decide whether the method remains an experiment, needs another bounded sprint, is ready for documented operationalization, or should stop. Add technology or AI support only to a sufficiently understood and reviewable process; scale to another segment, region, or team only after the operating contract, capacity, controls, and evidence remain valid there (source: Untitled 9.md; canonical title: *Full-Funnel's Fundamentals*; practitioner webinar; analysis: P29-W4-C24).

For account-based programs, represent both a planned value cadence and a signal-triggered reaction layer inside the sprint contract. Signals change the review queue; they do not independently establish buyer intent or authorize outreach (source: Untitled 7.md; canonical title: *How to Create an Account-Based Playbook for Long Sales Cycles and Multiple Decision-Makers*; practitioner webinar; analysis: P29-W4-C22).

## Inspectable output

Produce:

- a versioned program charter and multi-sprint roadmap;
- capacity-by-role table with owner confirmation;
- one sprint contract per stage;
- dependency, milestone, decision, and escalation map;
- cumulative dashboard separating activities, early observations, commercial outcomes, guardrails, definitions, owners, targets, and actuals;
- weekly decision and blocker log;
- sprint retrospective and explicit `continue`, `revise`, `stop`, `rescope`, or `operationalize` record;
- executive summary linked to the detailed team plan.

## Reuse boundaries

- No universal sprint duration, milestone count, activity target, engagement threshold, or program horizon is supported.
- Activity completion, content interaction, registrations, website visits, conversations, and account movement do not by themselves prove awareness, intent, pipeline, revenue, or causal impact.
- The outcome path is a testable operating hypothesis, not an attribution model or deterministic forecast.
- Do not reverse-engineer financial expectations, benchmarks, or “before/after” cases to justify a predetermined budget.
- Capacity must be confirmed by real owners; this playbook cannot assign hidden work or override portfolio, employment, finance, procurement, privacy, or project governance.
- Use [[marketing-change-pilot-buy-in-memo]] first when the program or pilot is not yet authorized.
- Use [[lean-weekly-abm-pilot-loop]] for the weekly account decisions inside a bounded ABM pilot; do not duplicate those account-level mechanics here.
- The source is vendor-hosted and promotes tools, training, workshops, and consulting. Its productivity, ROI, event, pipeline, response, and tool-fit claims remain excluded.
