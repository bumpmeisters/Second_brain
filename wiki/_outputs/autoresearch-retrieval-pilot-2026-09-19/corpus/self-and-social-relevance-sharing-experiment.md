# Self- and Social-Relevance Sharing Experiment

**Summary**: Test whether actively writing why verified content matters to oneself or to known others changes perceived relevance and sharing, without treating sharing intention as behavior or the result as a universal formula.

---

## Trigger

Use for important, verified content whose voluntary sharing matters and whose personal or social relevance to a defined audience is uncertain. The experiment is most useful before scaling distribution or when a qualitative Share Test has not explained weak audience response.

## Required inputs

- Verified content and an accountable owner.
- A defined audience sample and consented testing context.
- Three prompts: written self-relevance, written relevance to people the participant knows, and a neutral content-description control.
- Predeclared measures for perceived self-relevance, social relevance, broad or direct sharing intention, and actual sharing where observable and appropriate.
- A review owner, comparison period, privacy boundary, and stop rule.

## Procedure

1. Confirm that the content is accurate, appropriate to share, and consistent across conditions.
2. Assign participants to comparable conditions or randomize where feasible.
3. Ask the self condition to write why the content is relevant to them personally.
4. Ask the social condition to write why it is relevant to people they know.
5. Ask the control condition to write neutrally what the content is about.
6. Measure perceived self- and social relevance after the exercise.
7. Record sharing intention separately from actual sharing behavior; distinguish public broadcast from direct narrowcast sharing when the distinction matters.
8. Compare conditions, effect consistency, missing data, and sample limitations. Decide `continue`, `revise`, or `stop` before wider use.

The source's four studies found that active written framing was more effective than reflection alone, while effects in a more naturalistic context were smaller and actual sharing had a low base rate (source: Perceived self and social relevance of content motivates news sharing across cultures and topics.md; analysis: P29-W2-C12).

## Inspectable output

A dated experiment record containing content version, sample, assignment method, exact prompts, relevance ratings, separate intention and behavior measures, uncertainty, exceptions, and the resulting continuation decision.

## Reuse boundaries

This is not a virality hack and must not be used to increase sharing of false, misleading, manipulative, or harmful material. The underlying studies focused on high-quality climate and health news, primarily in American and Dutch samples; transfer to other topics, cultures, audiences, and commercial contexts requires new testing. Sharing intention is not actual sharing, and neither establishes downstream impact or revenue attribution. Neuroimaging is not required for practical reuse.
