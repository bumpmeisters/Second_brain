# Domain-Outsider Audience Learning Loop

**Summary**: When marketers begin outside a specialist field, acquire enough grounded audience knowledge before creating messaging or targeting. Combine expert hypotheses with transparent community participation, customer and usage evidence, and explicit uncertainty.

---

## Trigger

Use this workflow when a team must make consequential content, messaging, audience, or activation decisions for a specialist audience but lacks the language, job context, problem understanding, or decision evidence required to represent that audience accurately.

The source describes a technical-market case in which specialist communities, newsletters, customer interviews, internal data, product context, and internal subject-matter experts were combined. Expert input was treated as a starting hypothesis and refined through narrowly framed community questions and use-case analysis (source: Supercharge Your Go-To-Market Strategy with Full-Funnel Go-To-Market Template.md; canonical title: *Case Study: Converting Skeptical IT Buyers with ABM*; transcript 8:36-33:19 and 1:00:43-1:02:44).

## Required inputs

- The decisions the research must support and the cost of being wrong.
- A written list of knowledge gaps, assumptions, and terms that need clarification.
- At least one relevant internal or external subject-matter expert.
- Available customer, call, product-usage, support, win/loss, or account evidence.
- Relevant specialist communities, newsletters, forums, events, or professional groups.
- The access, quotation, recording, privacy, and reuse rules for each source.
- A named evidence owner and accountable audience-model reviewer.
- A claims and data-governance boundary for downstream use.

## Procedure

1. **Define the decision boundary.** State whether the evidence will inform an ICP, persona, buying role, message, content brief, channel choice, account cluster, or experiment. Avoid open-ended “learn the market” research.
2. **Create a gap and hypothesis register.** List what is unknown and what the team currently assumes. Mark each entry as observation, expert interpretation, inference, or unknown.
3. **Interview specialists.** Ask about real jobs, goals, recurring problems, triggers, constraints, vocabulary, decision contexts, role differences, common mistakes, and known exceptions. Record source and confidence for every material hypothesis.
4. **Map legitimate evidence paths.** Identify customer and internal records plus relevant public or private communities. Confirm access, consent, quotation, retention, and research-purpose rules before participating or collecting material.
5. **Enter transparently.** Use a truthful identity and explain role and purpose. Ask narrowly scoped, non-promotional questions that participants can answer without exposing restricted information.
6. **Triangulate.** Compare community responses with customer interviews, internal evidence, product or support records, further specialists, and contradictory cases. Do not promote a pattern from one evidence class alone.
7. **Cluster by work and context.** Group findings by use case, trigger, job, problem, responsibility, constraint, and decision situation. Use industry and job title only where evidence shows they meaningfully distinguish the need.
8. **Resolve hypotheses.** Mark each important hypothesis `supported`, `contradicted`, `mixed`, or `open`. Preserve dissent, scope, source limitations, and uncertainty.
9. **Authorize downstream use.** State which findings may inform audience models, briefs, content, targeting, or tests. Keep unresolved hypotheses visibly qualified.
10. **Review after use.** Feed campaign, sales, customer, and content evidence back into the pack and revise or retire findings when the domain, product, or audience changes.

## Inspectable output

Produce a versioned audience evidence pack containing:

- research purpose, supported decisions, owner, date, and review trigger;
- source and permission matrix;
- specialist vocabulary with meanings, disputed terms, and context;
- jobs, problems, triggers, responsibilities, constraints, and decision map;
- hypothesis register with source, evidence class, confidence, and disposition;
- use-case clusters and their inclusion or exclusion evidence;
- contradictions, negative cases, and unresolved questions;
- permitted and prohibited downstream uses;
- revision history and next evidence task.

## Reuse boundaries

- Community participants and expert informants are not representative samples by default. Do not turn response frequency into market prevalence.
- Do not conceal professional identity, affiliation, commercial purpose, or research intent.
- Do not bypass private-community admission rules, automate restricted collection, scrape personal networks, or publish private material without authorization.
- SMEs can reproduce institutional assumptions; historical customers and internal data can encode selection and hindsight bias.
- Separate verified observation, expert interpretation, inference, and unknown. AI may organize evidence but may not silently upgrade a hypothesis.
- Do not generalize one technical or professional audience to all IT, engineering, regulated, or specialist buyers.
- Apply privacy, confidentiality, contractual, legal, research, accessibility, and community-governance requirements separately.
- Use [[use-case-cluster-prioritization-workflow]] when several supported clusters compete for pilot resources. Use [[peer-co-creation-access-loop]] when a willing expert will co-create and distribute an audience-facing asset.
