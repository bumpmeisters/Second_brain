# Buying-Situation Message Calibration

**Summary**: Calibrate message emphasis to the buyer's current decision mode while keeping one stable promise and testing only bounded narrative expressions.

---

## Trigger

Use this workflow when messaging is generic, inconsistent across teams, or foregrounds competitive differences before the audience is actively evaluating alternatives.

## Required inputs

- A defined buying situation and best available evidence of decision mode.
- Customer language and a grounded description of the lived problem.
- One approved core promise.
- A distinctive category or situation association.
- Provable differences and current proof.
- Testable narrative variants and a named message owner.

## Workflow

1. **Classify the decision mode.** Distinguish broad situation recognition and recall-building from active comparison. Record uncertainty; buyer states are not perfectly observable or linear.
2. **Start with the customer story.** Describe the existing situation, tension, desired progress, and language without making the brand the hero.
3. **Choose the emphasis.** Before active evaluation, emphasize a stable distinctive cue and buying-situation association. During comparison, emphasize evidenced differences and relevant decision criteria.
4. **Lock the stable core.** Record the approved promise, proof boundaries, and elements that must remain consistent.
5. **Design bounded expressions.** Vary narrative, examples, format, or channel without silently replacing positioning.
6. **Test resonance.** Gather customer, buyer, seller, and performance evidence appropriate to the message's purpose.
7. **Decide and record.** Retain, revise, or stop an expression while preserving or explicitly reopening the core strategic decision.

## Inspectable output

The calibrated message brief records decision mode, customer situation, stable promise, distinctive cue or evidenced difference, proof, permitted variants, test, observed evidence, and review decision.

## Reuse boundaries

The transcript supports the practitioner distinction between distinctiveness before active evaluation and evidenced difference during comparison. It does not validate neuroscience, emotion effects, memory mechanisms, buyer percentages, category correlations, synthetic audiences, or AI-product claims. Differentiation must remain evidenced, and uncertain decision modes should be labeled rather than inferred as fact (source: Bryan Law on Brains, Data, and Distinction; source file: Zarina Stanford on Mission-Driven Authentic Leadership.md; transcript anchors 9:51-21:05).
