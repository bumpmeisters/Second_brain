# Long-Form Narrative Development Workflow

**Summary**: Develop a substantial narrative through durable project artifacts: test the premise, use theme as a guide, expose outline gaps, preserve continuity, gather source-rich observations, finish the draft, and resolve differentiated review through accountable author judgment.

---

## Trigger

Use this workflow when a fiction or narrative project is large enough that premise quality, thematic coherence, continuity, research, and several kinds of review cannot be managed reliably in one draft or conversation.

## Required inputs

- An accountable author who owns creative and editorial decisions.
- A one-page premise or jacket-style summary that states the intended reader commitment.
- A concise theme, an initial outline, and permission to leave unresolved gaps visible.
- A lawful research boundary covering sources, field observations, images, maps, and specialist input.
- A character and world continuity record appropriate to the project's complexity.
- Named review roles, such as general beta readers, subject specialists, and an accountable editor.

## Procedure

1. Draft a one-page, jacket-style summary and ask two separate questions: does the project merit the creator's time, and does the promise justify a reader's attention? Revise or stop before expanding a premise that cannot answer either question.
2. Choose a concise theme as a directional spine. Use it to test major decisions without forcing every scene to state the theme literally.
3. Expand the premise into an outline. Mark missing transitions, unresolved logic, and research needs explicitly; continue developing the structure instead of treating every gap as a blocker.
4. Draft the complete narrative before optimizing every passage. Preserve version history and keep unresolved questions visible.
5. Maintain a story, character, and world bible as complexity grows. Record names, relationships, chronology, locations, rules, and consequential changes.
6. Gather specific sensory and contextual observations through lawful fieldwork, maps, photographs, period sources, and qualified readers. Record provenance and distinguish observation from inference.
7. Route the complete draft to differentiated reviewers. Capture general-reader reactions separately from specialist corrections and editorial recommendations.
8. Resolve feedback through accountable author judgment. Record accepted, rejected, and deferred changes, then make the final editorial decision.

## Inspectable output

A versioned narrative project packet containing the accepted premise, thematic spine, outline and open gaps, continuity bible, research notes with provenance, complete draft, reader and specialist feedback, feedback dispositions, and editorial decision.

## Reuse boundaries

This is one experienced novelist's reported method, not a universal writing formula or evidence of commercial success. Source rights, privacy, physical safety, and cultural or historical claims require their own controls. Lived experience can enrich observation but does not establish universal truth; specialist and historical assertions need verification. The workflow does not authorize autonomous publication or transfer final authorship judgment to an agent (source: 2026-07-29--HfrbpbtO_8I.md; practitioner interview; analysis: P35-W5-C02).
