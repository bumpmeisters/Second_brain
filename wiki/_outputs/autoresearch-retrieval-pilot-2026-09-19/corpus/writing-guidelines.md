# Writing Guidelines

**Summary**: Writing guidelines connect genre, audience, purpose, evidence, rhetorical form, and accountable revision rather than reducing quality to fluent or uniformly concise prose.

---

The writing guidelines folder contains an editorial writing workshop PDF and an Ogilvy copywriting framework for juniors (source: raw/assets/03_Brand/Writing_Guidelines).

These sources should be used to develop practical guidance for tone, clarity, editorial discipline, and campaign copy once their detailed content is mined more deeply (source: raw/assets/03_Brand/Writing_Guidelines/Die Hirschen editorial workshop/250814_ZgH_DBS_Editorial Writing_Workshop.pdf; source: raw/assets/03_Brand/Writing_Guidelines/Ogilvy/20250828_Ogilvy's Copywriting Framework for Juniors.docx).

## Genre-aware AI revision

Do not accept an AI draft merely because it is fluent, grammatical, or consistent with a generic voice prompt. Review it against the intended genre, audience, purpose, and communicative situation. In a multi-corpus English-language study, instruction-tuned GPT-4o and Llama 3 variants showed recurring noun-heavy, informationally dense patterns and weaker variation across genres than the human comparison texts (source: Do LLMs write like humans Variation in grammatical and rhetorical styles.md).

A practical review should check whether abstractions hide actors or actions, information density fits the reader, stance and uncertainty match the task, and conspicuous stock vocabulary or syntax has displaced the expected genre. Use qualified human examples and an accountable editor where genre judgment matters. These features are revision prompts, not a universal AI detector; the evidence is bounded by the studied models, English corpora, and genres (analysis: P29-W2-C10).

## Rhetorical form as a purpose-fit layer

After the idea, audience, evidence, and genre are clear, choose rhetorical form for a named communicative job. Repetition and balance can support recall; sensory transfer and personification can make a description more vivid; paradox and hyperbole can create surprise; rhythm and intentional fragments can alter emphasis and pace. Read consequential lines aloud and keep only devices that reinforce meaning rather than compete with it (source: 2026-08-05--6ZNCJH8jJD0.md; transcript 00:35–51:44 and 01:05:02–01:13:50; analysis: P32-W7-C01).

This is a practitioner editing lens, not evidence that ornament is always better than concise language or that a named figure improves memory, persuasion, retention, or conversion. Operational reports may warrant restraint; narrative or expressive writing may legitimately use more form. Exact quotations, literary attributions, and the transcript's historical examples are not part of this rule.

## Revision separates diagnosis from prescription

Revise in two passes of judgment. First, identify the patterns, promises, and reader effects the draft is already creating; strengthen what belongs and remove material that remains attractive but structurally unrelated. Second, treat repeated reader discomfort as evidence about where the text fails while keeping diagnosis separate from the proposed remedy. The accountable author chooses the repair and then checks whether the revised whole now earns it (source: 2026-08-12--0kkAl04_0og.md; transcript 00:31–11:39, 18:10–29:25, and 01:09:42–01:12:38; analysis: P32-W7-C02).

This does not mean ignoring editors, avoiding outlines, drafting every work out of order, or organizing every text through recurring imagery. It preserves useful feedback while preventing a reader's first suggested fix from silently becoming the author's decision.

## Short-humor drafting loop

For short humor, begin with one specific real observation or opinion. Generate many candidate takes, select and escalate one precise idea, keep uninhibited drafting separate from editorial judgment, and design the hook, context, and closer deliberately. This is one practitioner's bounded method, not a universal filter or evidence for attention-span, ideal-duration, audience-response, or commercial-performance claims (source: 2026-07-22--fhD9YbGeUVU.md; practitioner interview; analysis: P36-W6R3-C02).
