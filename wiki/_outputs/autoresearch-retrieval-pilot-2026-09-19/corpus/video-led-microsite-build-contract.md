# Video-Led Microsite Build Contract

**Summary**: Turn one approved video and commercial concept into a scroll-driven microsite through separate creative, media, implementation, and verification stages. Keep the film primary, the claims governed, and the finished experience reconstructable.

---

## Trigger

Use this workflow when an existing video is the narrative spine of a bounded commercial experience and a normal landing-page template would detach the interface from the film's physical or emotional journey.

## Required inputs

- Approved audience, communication job, offer, conversion goal, owner, and review path.
- Licensed video with stable custody, filename, format, dimensions, and known delivery constraints.
- Approved brand, messaging, claims, evidence, and regulated-category boundaries.
- Narrative stages visible in the film and the information each stage must carry.
- Supported devices, browsers, accessibility standard, performance budget, analytics policy, hosting route, and technical verifier.

## Procedure

1. **Authorize the commission.** Record the audience, commercial objective, desired response, CTA, evidence boundaries, responsible owner, and why a film-led experience is justified.
2. **Lock the media contract.** Keep the supplied video immutable. Record its rights, path, metadata, codec, poster or fallback, loading behavior, and error state. Read actual runtime at execution instead of assuming a fixed duration.
3. **Derive the creative direction.** Define the brand expression, offer, headline, narrative chapters, palette, typography, navigation metaphor, and motion language from the approved concept and observable film stages. Do not invent unsupported proof, certification, or customer facts.
4. **Specify the experience.** Map document progress to the film and synchronize only useful chapter content, annotations, measurements, controls, and CTA moments. Keep the video legible and avoid generic cards or unrelated motion.
5. **Write the implementation contract.** State routes, component boundaries, media behavior, responsive rules, browser support, loading and failure handling, cleanup, security, privacy, analytics, and deployment requirements. Treat frameworks and versions as replaceable choices that require current validation.
6. **Define interaction and access.** Preserve keyboard and native scrolling, visible focus, semantic controls, sufficient contrast, reduced-motion behavior, touch support, manual pause, and a non-video fallback. Any auto-tour must remain stoppable and must not trap or override the user.
7. **Preserve reconstruction context.** Store the approved concept, media identity, creative direction, narrative, copy, visual system, interaction rules, architecture, and verification commands in a self-contained reconstruction brief linked from the project.
8. **Verify before acceptance.** Run the production build and automated checks; inspect runtime and console errors; test routes, media failure, responsive layouts, keyboard operation, reduced motion, performance, claims, brand, and CTA behavior. Record defects, waivers, reviewer, date, and release decision.

## Inspectable output

The completed package contains:

- an approved creative and commercial brief;
- a media custody and rights record;
- a chapter and interaction map;
- a build contract with acceptance criteria;
- the working microsite;
- build, runtime, accessibility, responsive, claims, and human-review evidence;
- a self-contained reconstruction brief and release decision.

## Reuse boundaries

The source supplies a detailed prompt contract, not evidence that the resulting site is accessible, secure, performant, distinctive, commercially effective, or “award-level.” Model names, frontend stacks, browser techniques, and timing assumptions are time-sensitive. Verify current documentation and the actual build. Do not copy an existing brand, modify unlicensed media, fabricate proof, expose restricted data, or infer conversion impact from attention or interaction (source: 1.
