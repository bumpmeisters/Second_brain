# HP Enterprise Growth System Source Summary

**Summary**: A three-part, internally authored North Star for an adaptive enterprise growth operating system spanning market demand, account-based demand, 1:few ABM, 1:1 strategic ABM, and customer expansion. The bundle separates system architecture, precise decision rules, and phased implementation.

---

## What The Source Is

The bundle contains three complementary documents, all labeled version 1.1 and dated July 2026:

1. **Strategic Blueprint** — defines the North Star, five motions, system boundaries, shared capabilities, governance, measurement, technology, AI role, maturity model, and leadership principles. (source: 20260718_Teil 1_HP_Enterprise_Growth_System_Strategischer_Blueprint_.docx)
2. **Model Legend** — defines the vocabulary, motion boundaries, portfolio and readiness decisions, buying groups, relationship capital, signal interpretation, orchestration, measurement, failure patterns, and review questions. (source: 20260718_Tei 2_HP_Enterprise_Growth_System_Modell_Legende_2.docx)
3. **Implementation Playbook** — translates the model into mobilization, assessment, target operating model, portfolio, governance, roles, workflows, data, content/experiences, measurement, technology, AI, enablement, roadmap, risks, and scale decisions. (source: 20260718_Teil 3_HP_Enterprise_Growth_System_Implementation_Playbook.docx)

Rolf provided the bundle as his ABM North Star. The filenames include `HP`, and the Blueprint's DOCX metadata names Rolf-Peter Ullrich as the last modifier, but the documents do not establish that the method is an official HP Inc. corporate methodology. The vault therefore treats it as an original internal framework, not as externally verified HP doctrine.

## Core Reasoning Mechanism

The system is built around two separate control axes:

- A **growth motion** determines the durable commercial operating mode, unit of management, investment intensity, ownership, governance, and outcome logic.
- The **Growth Execution Loop** determines how a material initiative inside a motion moves from commercial priority through intelligence, orchestration, content/experience, execution/optimization, and measurement/learning. (source: 20260718_Teil 1_HP_Enterprise_Growth_System_Strategischer_Blueprint_.docx; source: 20260718_Tei 2_HP_Enterprise_Growth_System_Modell_Legende_2.docx)

The five motions are not a mandatory funnel. An account can enter, move forward, move backward, move sideways, or exit as its fit, trigger, relationships, customer health, internal readiness, or capacity changes. (source: 20260718_Teil 1_HP_Enterprise_Growth_System_Strategischer_Blueprint_.docx)

| Motion | Unit | Mandate |
|---|---|---|
| Market Demand | market, category, buying audience | develop relevance and demand |
| Account-based Demand | selected account population | develop demand and interpretable buying-group signals at scale |
| 1:few ABM | small account cluster | progress one shared change and value hypothesis |
| 1:1 Strategic ABM | single strategic account | run an individual account growth operating model |
| Customer Expansion | customer, business unit, region, use case, or capability | realize, retain, and expand customer value |

(source: 20260718_Teil 1_HP_Enterprise_Growth_System_Strategischer_Blueprint_.docx)

## Key Claims And Decisions

- ABM is primarily the joint development of selected accounts or small account groups through business, relationship, and progression hypotheses; named-account media belongs to the distinct Account-based Demand motion unless deeper conditions are met. (source: 20260718_Tei 2_HP_Enterprise_Growth_System_Modell_Legende_2.docx)
- Tier, motion, and status are separate classifications: tier describes relative importance, motion describes the current operating mode, and status describes current progress or health. (source: 20260718_Tei 2_HP_Enterprise_Growth_System_Modell_Legende_2.docx)
- A high-value account does not qualify for 1:1 without a testable growth thesis, a realistic buying-group access path, active account-team commitment, delivery readiness, and sufficient orchestration capacity. (source: 20260718_Teil 1_HP_Enterprise_Growth_System_Strategischer_Blueprint_.docx)
- Buying groups, not individual leads, are the central unit for complex account growth; relationship capital adds coverage, strength, multithreading, executive access, champion quality, and continuity. (source: 20260718_Teil 1_HP_Enterprise_Growth_System_Strategischer_Blueprint_.docx)
- Intent and engagement are probabilistic signals, not purchase readiness. A signal becomes useful only after relevance, specificity, recency, source confidence, cross-signal confirmation, alternative explanations, ownership, and action risk are considered. (source: 20260718_Tei 2_HP_Enterprise_Growth_System_Modell_Legende_2.docx)
- A Commercial Alignment Contract makes objective, scope, motion, thesis, contributions, capacity, measures, risks, and review consequences explicit before material cross-functional initiatives begin. (source: 20260718_Teil 3_HP_Enterprise_Growth_System_Implementation_Playbook.docx)
- Measurement must follow each motion's mandate and distinguish activity, response, progression, commercial outcome, and customer value. Demotion or a disproved hypothesis can be a good outcome when it protects resources. (source: 20260718_Tei 2_HP_Enterprise_Growth_System_Modell_Legende_2.docx)
- Technology and AI scale validated decisions and workflows; they do not define the operating model. Sales outreach, relationship judgment, opportunity stage, executive engagement, and resource allocation remain human decisions. (source: 20260718_Teil 1_HP_Enterprise_Growth_System_Strategischer_Blueprint_.docx; source: 20260718_Teil 3_HP_Enterprise_Growth_System_Implementation_Playbook.docx)

## Implementation Logic

The implementation sequence is `assess → design → pilot → validate → scale → adapt`. The minimum viable system includes an executive sponsor, accountable program lead, real pilot accounts and owners, at least one scalable signal layer and one deeper account motion, shared account/buying-group/progression definitions, a portfolio board, alignment contracts, execution gates, visible next actions, and learning loops. (source: 20260718_Teil 3_HP_Enterprise_Growth_System_Implementation_Playbook.docx)

The playbook explicitly says that full technology consolidation, all five motions, perfect attribution, automated next-best-action, a full customer 360, and a large personalized-content library are not prerequisites for the first pilot. (source: 20260718_Teil 3_HP_Enterprise_Growth_System_Implementation_Playbook.docx)

The proposed roadmap covers mobilization in weeks 0–2, assessment in weeks 2–6, design in weeks 6–10, a pilot in months 3–6, scale in months 6–12, and an adaptive system after month 12. These are source-prescribed planning horizons, not independently validated benchmarks. (source: 20260718_Teil 3_HP_Enterprise_Growth_System_Implementation_Playbook.docx)

## Useful Artifacts

The bundle defines a compact operating artifact set:

- Implementation Charter and maturity heatmap;
- Motion Charters;
- Portfolio View with tier, motion, status, owner, capacity, and next decision;
- Living Account Context;
- Growth Thesis;
- Buying-Group and Relationship Map;
- Commercial Alignment Contract;
- Orchestration Plan and Execution Readiness Check;
- motion scorecards, portfolio dashboard, and KPI dictionary;
- Decision Log, risk register, enablement plan, and technology roadmap. (source: 20260718_Teil 3_HP_Enterprise_Growth_System_Implementation_Playbook.docx)

## Contradictions And Caveats

- The bundle uses a narrower ABM definition than some sources already in the vault: it reserves ABM mainly for 1:few and 1:1 and names the scalable target-account layer `Account-based Demand`. Other practitioner sources include one-to-many under ABM. The durable distinction is the operating contract and decision logic, not the label. (source: 20260718_Tei 2_HP_Enterprise_Growth_System_Modell_Legende_2.docx; analysis: [[account-based-marketing]])
- The framework is internally authored and self-consistent, but its effectiveness, capacity assumptions, service levels, maturity levels, and roadmap have not been independently validated through a live application in this vault.
- DOCX sidecars preserve readable text but not all visual layout. Use the original files for diagrams, page design, comments, or any layout-dependent interpretation.
- The name `HP Enterprise Growth System` is retained for provenance; external attribution to HP Inc. remains `needs verification`.
