# ABM Customer Expansion Playbook

**Summary**: Use account-based coordination after value has been demonstrated to explore a specific customer-expansion hypothesis. The playbook connects verified customer outcomes, whitespace, an adjacent buying center, role-specific proof, a governed proposal, and an adoption plan.

---

## Trigger

Use this playbook when an existing customer has credible evidence of realized value and adoption, a plausible adjacent use case or buying center, sufficient relationship access, and delivery capacity for the next outcome.

Do not trigger expansion merely because product usage, campaign engagement, intent data, a renewal date, or a seller request exists. These are observations to investigate, not proof that a new buying center has a problem, budget, or willingness to buy.

## Required inputs

- Evidence of current value, adoption, health, unresolved issues, and delivery capacity.
- A bounded whitespace hypothesis covering business unit, region, role group, use case, module, tier, or seats.
- Current account, relationship, sponsor, buying-center, consent, renewal, and commercial context.
- Product, support, research, and engagement observations with provenance, freshness, confidence, and counter-evidence.
- Relevant customer proof, outcome evidence, implementation requirements, and known adoption risks.
- Named owners across Customer Success, Sales, Marketing, Product, RevOps, and delivery.

## Procedure

1. **Confirm the value gate.** Record the customer outcome already achieved, the evidence supporting it, open service or adoption risks, sponsor status, and the reason expansion is appropriate now. Stop if the current promise is not being met.
2. **Define the account thesis.** State the whitespace, proposed next outcome, adjacent buying center, business reason, political or relationship risk, and evidence that would disconfirm the hypothesis.
3. **Instrument observations.** Assemble relevant usage, support, renewal, relationship, stakeholder-change, research, and engagement evidence. Keep facts, interpretations, and unknowns separate.
4. **Discover the next decision.** Validate pains, desired outcomes, constraints, stakeholders, decision criteria, and path to value with the relevant roles. Update or reject the thesis rather than treating the initial map as buyer truth.
5. **Design the engagement.** Select the smallest useful combination of role-specific proof, executive briefing, workshop, reference, event, or enablement. Coordinate customer-facing activity and prevent conflicting touches through [[multi-team-account-contact-coordination-gate]].
6. **Prove incremental value.** Show how the proposed change advances the adjacent group’s outcome, what must be adopted, what evidence is still missing, and which risks or dependencies remain.
7. **Gate the proposal.** Proceed only when the buyer problem, sponsor path, delivery capacity, adoption plan, commercial ownership, and success measures are explicit. Otherwise continue discovery, defer, or stop.
8. **Govern the expansion.** Review buying-center coverage, sponsor strength, adoption, customer health, commercial progression, discount pressure, delivery risk, counter-evidence, and agreed stop conditions. Preserve the decision and learning for later account planning.

The source contributes the sequence “Define → Instrument → Discover → Engage → Prove → Propose → Govern,” whitespace mapping, signal-informed timing, role-specific value stories, executive multi-threading, and shared expansion measures. The evidence gate, counter-evidence, consent, coordination, adoption, and stop controls are vault governance additions (source: What role does account-based marketing (ABM) play in expansion.md; sections “How ABM Powers Expansion,” “The ABM→Expansion Playbook,” and “Frequently Asked Questions about ABM for Expansion”; practitioner-promotional article; analysis: P27-C01).

## Inspectable output

An expansion account plan containing:

- current value and health evidence;
- whitespace and next-outcome thesis;
- buying-center, relationship, and sponsor map;
- facts, interpretations, unknowns, and counter-evidence;
- role-specific proof and coordinated engagement sequence;
- proposal prerequisites and decision;
- adoption and delivery plan;
- owners, measures, review date, and stop conditions.

## Reuse boundaries

This playbook does not prove that usage indicates intent, that engagement caused expansion, or that ABM activity caused retention, revenue, or account growth. The source is a promotional consulting article without independent outcome validation. Use the workflow to make a bounded, reviewable expansion decision—not to pressure a customer whose current value, health, consent, or relationship conditions are weak.

Commercial, privacy, consent, competition, contracting, discount, service, and customer-success controls remain authoritative. Route urgent opportunity interventions through [[servicenow-pursuit-marketing-sprint-playbook]] and account-contact conflicts through [[multi-team-account-contact-coordination-gate]].
