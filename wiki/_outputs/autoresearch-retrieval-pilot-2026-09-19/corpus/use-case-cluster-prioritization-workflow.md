# Use-Case Cluster Prioritization Workflow

**Summary**: Construct a versioned, provenance-aware use-case evidence set before choosing a bounded pilot cluster. When comparable history is insufficient, replace premature scoring and pipeline commitments with an explicit exploratory hypothesis branch.

---

## Trigger

Use this workflow when several groups of accounts share plausible use cases but the team lacks capacity to run a disciplined pilot for all of them. It also applies when a new product, market, or startup motion needs to build evidence before normal cluster comparison is defensible.

## Required inputs

- A declared strategic decision and the period or learning horizon it concerns.
- Won, lost, open, and disqualified records where comparable history exists.
- A versioned definition of a use case as the job and desired outcome, not merely an industry or company label.
- Provenance, confidence, period, and definition metadata for every included observation.
- Win rate, sales-cycle length, contract value, and loss evidence where available.
- An inventory of comparable customer proof.
- Available team capacity and service-level constraints.
- For evidence-poor motions, hypotheses, counter-hypotheses, permitted observation sources, and a minimum evidence or stop threshold.

## Workflow

1. **Define the decision.** State what the pilot should learn or improve: faster learning, stronger proof, higher value, shorter cycle, market entry, or another explicit objective.
2. **Choose the evidence branch.** Use normal comparison only when enough comparable records exist under sufficiently stable definitions and periods. Otherwise use the exploratory branch below.
3. **Version the evidence contract.** Record the use-case definition, inclusion and exclusion rules, time period, data sources, known gaps, and the decision the evidence is allowed to support.
4. **Classify the record set.** Group won, lost, open, and disqualified records by job and desired outcome. Preserve provenance and confidence rather than silently resolving uncertain labels.
5. **Inspect positive and negative paths.** Compare the largest and fastest wins with losses, disqualifications, contradictions, role patterns, buying questions, economics, and available proof. Historical deals are organizational evidence, not a representative market sample.
6. **Build the comparison table.** For each cluster, record volume, win rate, cycle length, value, available proof, data confidence, exclusions, contradictions, and operating requirements.
7. **Set objective-specific weights.** Explain which criteria matter most for the stated objective. Do not optimize on total revenue alone.
8. **Check feasibility.** Verify account availability, buying-group access, content and proof readiness, sales commitment, and capacity.
9. **Select one bounded cluster.** Record the chosen cluster, reasons, excluded alternatives, owner, service level, and review date.
10. **Review after the pilot.** Reassess the evidence and decide whether to continue, adapt, stop, or sequence the next cluster.

## Exploratory branch for weak history

When comparable history is absent or too weak:

1. Record each use-case hypothesis and at least one plausible counter-hypothesis.
2. Replace pipeline commitments with specific learning questions and permitted observations.
3. Collect confirming and disconfirming evidence with provenance, confidence, consent, and period.
4. Classify each hypothesis as `supported`, `mixed`, `contradicted`, or `open`; engagement and discovery alone do not establish product-market fit, intent, pipeline, or revenue.
5. Enter normal cluster comparison only after the declared evidence threshold is met. Otherwise adapt or stop and retain the unresolved questions.

For specialist audiences the team does not understand well enough to represent accurately, route audience learning through [[domain-outsider-audience-learning-loop]] before treating its output as cluster evidence.

## Inspectable output

The output is either:

- a versioned evidence set plus cluster comparison and decision record with weighting rationale, chosen pilot, deferred alternatives, capacity contract, owner, and reconsideration point; or
- an exploratory hypothesis ledger with confirming and disconfirming observations, dispositions, evidence threshold, owner, and explicit proceed, adapt, or stop decision.

## Reuse boundaries

The sources provide a decision method, not a universal scoring formula, mandatory historical window, or evidence threshold. Metric weights depend on strategic purpose and data quality. Historical CRM data can contain qualified-only selection, unstable definitions, inconsistent periods, incomplete loss reasons, and survivorship bias. Small samples and subject-matter-expert views do not establish prevalence. Third-party buyer statistics, touchpoint counts, stage benchmarks, vendor outcomes, pipeline results, platform and AI claims, and causal interpretations are excluded (source: B2B Demand Generation Playbook Webinar.md; transcript anchors 1:35:38-1:39:07; source: How to Create an Account-Based Playbook For Long Sales Cycles and Multiple Decision-Makers.md; transcript anchors 17:11-21:50, 22:27-27:09, 27:45-38:41, and 1:00:43-1:04:22).
