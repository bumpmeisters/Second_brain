# Pursuit Marketing: A Sprint-Based ABM Playbook for High-Stakes Enterprise Opportunities

**Summary**: Time-bounded enterprise ABM playbook for applying 1:1 insight, coordination, and measurement to a qualified high-stakes opportunity or account challenge.

## 0. Kontext & Kernaussage (Das Fundament)

### The starting point / the problem

Enterprise ABM teams face a capacity conflict. Full 1:1 ABM can support only a few strategic accounts over 12–24 months, while sales continually encounters urgent situations that need the same depth on a shorter clock: a major deal, an executive relationship problem, a renewal, M&A inside the account, downsell risk, or access to a new business unit. At ServiceNow, these requests had previously been handled in addition to practitioners' normal work, including evenings and weekends. A defined service was needed to concentrate ABM expertise on a material account situation without converting every request into permanent 1:1 coverage. (source: From ABM to PBM How ServiceNow Runs Deal-Based Marketing at Sprint Speed (Episode 66).md)

Speed alone is not the answer. A rushed intervention can become reactive sales support: sales asks for an asset, marketing produces it, and nobody agrees on the account change, buying group, evidence, follow-through, or success measure.

### The story / the use case

ServiceNow formalized **Pursuit Marketing (PBM)** as a branch of its ABM practice. Celia Slack, EMEA Pursuit Marketing Lead, describes it as applying ABM principles to a specific opportunity or challenge for a shorter period: **ABM is the marathon; Pursuit Marketing is the sprint**. Use cases include a large deal needing a “tiger team,” a new executive with a negative vendor perception, renewal or downsell risk, insufficient reach across the buying group, and an outdated view of the vendor's capabilities. (source: From ABM to PBM How ServiceNow Runs Deal-Based Marketing at Sprint Speed (Episode 66).md)

ServiceNow reported that a practitioner could cover roughly 20 opportunities or accounts in a year through this shorter model, versus roughly five under full 1:1 coverage. This is a practitioner-reported capacity illustration, not a universal benchmark or audited outcome. ServiceNow also described the service as new and still being refined. (source: From ABM to PBM How ServiceNow Runs Deal-Based Marketing at Sprint Speed (Episode 66).md)

### Core message

**Pursuit Marketing is a time-bounded, opportunity- or challenge-led application of 1:1 ABM—not a campaign type or emergency content desk.** It gives a qualified enterprise situation a temporary concentration of insight, coordination, tailored experience, and measurement, then returns ownership to the account team.

This is an **adapted operational reconstruction**. The interview supplies the definition, examples, principles, capacity illustration, and measurement approach. The explicit gates, decision rights, response rules, and handoff contract below are Enterprise Growth System adaptations; they are not presented as ServiceNow's official internal SOP.

## 1. Blueprint-Mapping

### Axis 1 — Growth Motion

**Primary motion: 1:1 Strategic ABM.** The unit is one strategic account, and the job is to orchestrate an individual account growth system around a material, testable change. The pursuit inherits 1:1 readiness requirements: high strategic value, a realistic buying-group access path, committed account leadership, delivery readiness, and sufficient capacity. The sprint is an initiative format; it does not replace the underlying motion.

**Conditional route: Customer Expansion.** When the primary state is adoption, customer health, renewal, retention, downsell prevention, or expansion into a unit or use case, use Customer Expansion. Weak health must be addressed as recovery before applying additional commercial pressure.

### Axis 2 — Growth Execution Loop

**Primary phase: Orchestrate.** Pursuit Marketing's distinctive contribution is to coordinate sales, marketing, executives, specialists, partners, timing, and customer-facing actions around one urgent situation. In the model legend's terms, it is ready only when **commitment, capacity, decision rights, risks, and follow-through are confirmed**.

Every pursuit nevertheless runs the complete loop in compressed form:

1. **Commercial Priority:** define the state change and investment case.
2. **Intelligence & Hypothesis:** separate facts, interpretations, assumptions, unknowns, and tests.
3. **Orchestrate:** confirm team, contributions, timing, rights, and response plan.
4. **Content & Experience:** design what helps the buying group progress.
5. **Execute & Optimize:** act against the decision window and adapt to response.
6. **Measure & Learn:** document progression, outcome, evidence quality, reuse, and next decision.

### Why this placement is correct

The method is neither broad demand generation nor scalable account targeting. It applies deep personalization to one account and requires an active account team. Therefore **1:1 Strategic ABM × Orchestrate** is the primary mapping. The Customer Expansion branch prevents a renewal or health problem from being mislabeled as a new-business sprint.

## 2. Die Taktik (Das Stand-alone Playbook)

### Prerequisites

- **GTM foundation:** Pursuit complements demand, field and industry marketing, customer success, and sales. It cannot repair an absent go-to-market strategy. If resources are limited, ServiceNow recommends establishing the fundamentals and piloting one account rather than treating ABM as a silver bullet.
- **Executive sponsorship:** Leadership must accept that deep ABM is resource-intensive, may not show results inside one quarter, and requires permission to decline or stop weak requests.
- **Strategic ABM skill:** The practitioner must diagnose an account quickly, distinguish relationship, education, commercial, and health problems, and challenge a requested deliverable.
- **Sales co-ownership:** The account executive contributes context, access, time, decisions, and follow-through. ServiceNow explicitly rejects the model in which sales submits requests and marketing simply returns with assets.
- **Shared evidence:** The team needs account priorities, opportunity or health context, buying-group contacts, relationship evidence, recent interactions, delivery constraints, and known unknowns.
- **Operating system:** CRM or account workspace, action ownership, decision log, audience controls, and qualitative relationship notes. ServiceNow combines dashboards and CRM tagging with manual or semi-manual evidence capture. (source for all points: From ABM to PBM How ServiceNow Runs Deal-Based Marketing at Sprint Speed (Episode 66).md)

### Step-by-step execution

#### Step 1 — Capture the trigger, not the asset request

Translate “we need an executive event,” “build a microsite,” or “send direct mail” into one commercial trigger:

- **Deal:** a material opportunity needs access, message, or consensus support.
- **Relationship:** trust, sponsorship, or perceived strategic relevance is weak.
- **Reach:** the account is single-threaded or confined to one function.
- **Education:** stakeholders misunderstand the vendor's capabilities.
- **Retention / recovery:** renewal, leadership change, M&A, dissatisfaction, or downsell risk threatens continuity.
- **Expansion:** a healthy customer has credible white space.

Record trigger source and date, decision window, value at stake, and consequence of no action.

#### Step 2 — Qualify the pursuit

Use three source-derived tests:

- **Opportunity:** Is the potential change commercially and strategically material?
- **Achievability:** Can marketing influence it within the available time, access, evidence, and capacity?
- **Collaboration:** Will the account team co-own diagnosis, access, execution, and follow-through?

Add blueprint readiness questions: Is one state change explicit? Is the account worth 1:1 capacity? Is there a testable hypothesis? Is there a realistic buying-group path? Can the company fulfill the promise? Are owner, capacity, review date, and exit condition named?

**Reject, defer, or reroute** if the request is production work, collaboration is absent, the desired change is vague, influence is implausible within the window, customer health makes expansion inappropriate, or a scalable motion can do the job.

#### Step 3 — Contract the partnership

Run an assessment and onboarding session. State:

> “This is a co-owned account intervention. Marketing brings research, orchestration, message, and experience design. The account team brings context, access, decisions, and follow-through. We will stop or redirect if either side cannot meet the commitments.”

Agree the start, decision moment, review cadence, exit date, owners, contributors, approval rights, explicit non-actions, and evidence that triggers continuation, adaptation, escalation, or stop. The source gives no standard sprint length; derive it from the customer's decision or risk window.

The minimum pursuit contract contains: account and owner; state to change; decision window; hypothesis; buying-group and relationship gaps; contributors and capacity; experience and action sequence; response rules; measures; exit and handoff.

#### Step 4 — Diagnose the real constraint

Build a rapid account view across:

1. **Business context:** priorities, transformation agenda, industry pressure, leadership or ownership change.
2. **Commercial context:** opportunity or renewal state, alternatives, decision process, timing, value, delivery constraints.
3. **Relationship context:** access, trust, champions, detractors, executive coverage, missing roles, single-thread risk.
4. **Perception context:** what the account believes the vendor is and whether it sees a strategic partner.

ServiceNow distinguishes two important patterns. If the account understands the platform but the relationship is weak, prioritize trust, access, and executive relevance. If relationships exist but the vendor is perceived narrowly—for example, only as an IT ticketing tool—prioritize education, strategic reframing, and broader business-unit reach.

Reconcile external research with sales, customer success, partners, field teams, and prior interactions. Classify each material statement as **confirmed fact, supported interpretation, working hypothesis, unknown, or disproved**.

#### Step 5 — Write one pursuit hypothesis

Use:

> **If** we help `[buying-group roles]` understand or experience `[decision-relevant value]` through `[coordinated intervention]` before `[decision moment]`, **then** `[observable progression]` should occur, **because** `[evidence-backed insight]`. Reconsider if `[disconfirming evidence or non-response]` occurs.

Examples:

- **Relationship:** If a new executive sees credible evidence that the vendor understands their transformation priorities and receives peer engagement instead of product promotion, they should accept an account-strategy conversation before renewal.
- **Education:** If operations and business leaders experience the platform as an enterprise capability rather than a narrow IT tool, the buying group should expand and accept a cross-functional workshop.
- **Deal:** If the buying group receives one role-specific value narrative tied to its decision criteria, fragmented evaluation should progress to executive validation.

Success means observable account progress—named-executive access, a new relationship, expanded buying-group coverage, an accepted workshop, decision-gate movement, restored confidence, renewal progression, or validated expansion—not asset delivery.

#### Step 6 — Map buying group and relationships

For each relevant role, record:

- decision role: sponsor, champion, economic buyer, evaluator, user, blocker, procurement, legal, security, partner, or unknown;
- relationship owner and strength, with dated evidence;
- priority, concern, and desired next step;
- required perspective or experience;
- access route: seller, executive, partner, advocate, event, peer, specialist, or direct outreach; and
- action owner, deadline, and follow-through.

Prioritize multithreading. A pursuit dependent on one contact is fragile.

#### Step 7 — Assemble the pursuit team

Include only contributors who can change the outcome:

- **Account executive:** commercial ownership, access, context, follow-through.
- **Pursuit marketer:** hypothesis, orchestration, experience strategy, adaptation, learning.
- **Industry / field marketing:** relevant narratives, megatrends, events, and existing programs.
- **Customer success:** health, adoption, realized value, renewal risk, advocacy.
- **Specialists:** technical, security, value, transformation, legal, procurement.
- **Executive sponsor:** peer access, credibility, escalation.
- **Marketing operations:** CRM tagging, controls, dashboarding, evidence capture.

Reuse strong work from field and industry teams and feed recurring account patterns back to them. Pursuit is not a separate marketing island. (source: From ABM to PBM How ServiceNow Runs Deal-Based Marketing at Sprint Speed (Episode 66).md)

#### Step 8 — Design the smallest decision-relevant experience

Customize for the account's actual challenge, not its logo. Options include an executive point of view, peer engagement, role-specific value workshop, specialist validation, relevant direct mail, a curated event moment, a buying-group briefing, or a relationship-recovery experience centered on listening and realized value.

Sequence each touch with an owner, intended response, and follow-through. In one ServiceNow example, coordinated channels including direct mail helped move from no CEO relationship to the CEO contacting the account executive and requesting a meeting. Treat this as qualitative objective achievement, not proof that direct mail alone caused the result. (source: From ABM to PBM How ServiceNow Runs Deal-Based Marketing at Sprint Speed (Episode 66).md)

#### Step 9 — Use GenAI as a governed accelerator

GenAI may create a first research pass, suggest role-specific language, identify patterns, or accelerate creative exploration. Continue beyond the first answer; then have knowledgeable humans verify sources, facts, tone, claims, privacy, and direction. ServiceNow calls AI a “kickstart,” not a replacement for human intervention. Never allow it to introduce unverified account claims, expose restricted information, automate executive outreach without approval, or replace account-team judgment. (source: From ABM to PBM How ServiceNow Runs Deal-Based Marketing at Sprint Speed (Episode 66).md)

#### Step 10 — Execute with response rules

At each review ask: What changed? What evidence supports it? Did the intended response occur? What follow-through is due? Does the hypothesis still hold? Continue, adapt, escalate, pause, or stop?

Examples:

- executive accepts a meeting → shift to meeting design and follow-through;
- new detractor or leadership change → reopen Intelligence & Hypothesis;
- no response → test access, relevance, and timing; do not automatically raise volume;
- account team misses commitments → reset once, then demote or stop;
- customer health worsens → stop expansion pressure and route to recovery;
- hypothesis is disproved → document, change the plan, or exit.

#### Step 11 — Measure progression, contribution, and learning

ServiceNow uses four lenses:

- **Reputation:** Did perception or strategic relevance change?
- **Relationships:** Did access, trust, executive connection, multithreading, or responsiveness improve?
- **Revenue:** Did the opportunity, renewal, expansion, or decision progress?
- **Retention:** Did the work protect continuity, confidence, renewal, or long-term value?

Make the pursuit objective the first measure. If the objective was CEO access and the CEO requests a meeting, preserve that qualitative success alongside commercial measures.

Record baseline and target state, relationship changes, fulfilled and missed commitments, commercial or customer progression, time to milestone, 4R evidence, capacity used, a comparison cohort where feasible, evidence limits, alternative explanations, and next decision.

ServiceNow reports using dashboards, CRM tagging, manual and semi-manual notes, and a scoring model comparing ABM with non-ABM accounts. Cohorts add context but do not prove causality because selected accounts may differ systematically. (source: From ABM to PBM How ServiceNow Runs Deal-Based Marketing at Sprint Speed (Episode 66).md)

#### Step 12 — Exit and preserve memory

Close when the state changes, the decision window passes, the hypothesis fails, collaboration disappears, or further investment is unjustified. Hand over current state and risk, relationships gained or missing, confirmed and unresolved evidence, commitments, reusable components, patterns, and the recommended portfolio decision.

The pursuit marketer exits; the account team retains commercial ownership. If continuous orchestration remains justified, explicitly promote the account into durable 1:1 Strategic ABM rather than letting the sprint continue indefinitely.

## 3. Blueprint-Bewertung & Adaption

### Alignment

**Overall alignment: strong, with governance additions required.** Pursuit Marketing reinforces **right account × right relationships × right moment × coordinated action** through dynamic prioritization, relationship intelligence, co-owned orchestration, account-relevant experiences, motion-specific measurement, and reusable learning.

It also clarifies that a **motion is a durable investment state, while a pursuit is a time-bounded initiative inside that motion**. This scales strategic depth without converting every urgent opportunity into permanent 1:1 ABM.

### Risks / failure modes

- **Reactive sales-support drift:** an asset request becomes a high-cost production service.
- **Magic-wand expectations:** the request arrives after access, trust, consensus, or value clarity has failed.
- **Motion confusion:** deal acceleration, recovery, renewal, and healthy expansion need different ownership and outcomes.
- **Short-termism:** the sprint creates attention without durable relationships or follow-through.
- **Single-thread risk:** dependence on one seller or customer contact.
- **Portfolio inflation:** demand exceeds capacity and recreates hidden overtime.
- **Over-customization:** bespoke production consumes resources without reusable learning.
- **Measurement bias:** anecdotes, selected accounts, manual notes, and unmatched cohorts exaggerate contribution.
- **GenAI risk:** invented personalization or unapproved executive language enters the account.
- **Customer-health blindness:** expansion pressure worsens a weak relationship.

### Concrete adaptation

1. **Define PBM as a service contract, not a sixth motion.** Publish entry criteria, exclusions, service level, capacity, rights, required sales contribution, and exit rules.
2. **Route twice.** Select the durable Growth Motion from the account state; then approve or reject a pursuit inside it. Put health and renewal work under Customer Expansion.
3. **Require the six-loop mini-gate.** Do not activate until priority, evidence, hypothesis, ownership, capacity, response rules, follow-through, and measures are explicit.
4. **Maintain living account context.** Store facts, signals, interpretations, hypotheses, buying group, relationships, opportunity or white space, actions, and confidence in one shared record.
5. **Make multithreading and customer value mandatory.** Show buying-group gaps, access paths, and customer-side progress; contact volume cannot substitute for relationship capital.
6. **Preserve the sales boundary.** Sales owns opportunity strategy, forecast, negotiation, and close. Pursuit owns insight quality, orchestration, relevant experience, action learning, and marketing contribution.
7. **Govern capacity as a portfolio.** Allocate fixed pursuit capacity, expose opportunity cost, and decline or demote weak work instead of creating invisible overtime.
8. **Pair metrics with a causal narrative.** State what changed, what marketing contributed, what else may explain it, and what decision follows.
9. **Build a pattern library.** Reuse challenge patterns, questions, buying-group risks, experience modules, response rules, and failure signals—not logo-swapped content.
10. **Enforce exit and handoff.** End with one decision: normal ownership, Customer Expansion, durable 1:1 Strategic ABM, scalable motion, or stop.

### Enterprise-ready launch rule

Launch only when all are true:

- **Right account:** value justifies scarce 1:1 capacity.
- **Right state change:** one challenge or opportunity is explicit and testable.
- **Right relationships:** a buying-group access path exists and multithreading can improve.
- **Right moment:** a real decision or risk window makes intervention relevant.
- **Coordinated action:** sales co-owns the work; roles, capacity, rights, and follow-through are confirmed.
- **Deliverable value:** the company can fulfill the promise if the account advances.
- **Measurable learning:** baseline, target progression, evidence capture, and exit decision are defined.

If any condition is missing, do not compensate with more activity. Research, defer, reroute, reduce scope, or stop.
