# Content Debt Schedule Source Brief

**Summary**: Practitioner treatment of old, duplicated, and contradictory published content as an accumulating maintenance obligation. It extends the existing content lifecycle with an inspectable debt schedule and a diagnosis-before-treatment boundary.

## Durable maintenance signal

- Every published asset remains a live promise that its claims are sufficiently true, current, and findable; ownership cannot end at publication (05:23–05:38 and 07:31–07:42).
- A machine-assisted inventory can surface last verification dates, claims, prices, statistics, product names, near duplicates, and contradiction pairs, then combine those flags with relevant use evidence (08:08–08:56).
- The source explicitly limits automation to candidate detection: “AI flags, humans verify,” because a model does not know the company's current commercial or legal truth (09:00–09:16).
- Diagnosis should precede changes; the source separates inventory from the later treatment decision (09:52–10:08).

## Caveats

- The universal presumption that content older than 24 months is wrong is too coarse. Review timing must follow volatility, evidence risk, obligations, and business importance.
- The Air Canada example, Forrester statistic, client case, scale estimates, and publishing-speed claims are not independently verified here and are excluded.
- Archiving, redirecting, consolidating, or deleting are distinct decisions. A machine-generated debt schedule does not authorize any of them.

## Decision recommendation

`extended-claim` for `wiki/maintained-content-distribution-lifecycle.md`: add a review inventory for volatile fields and contradictions, use automation only to flag candidates, require accountable human verification, and diagnose before changing the portfolio. Existing lifecycle and portfolio-audit pages already provide the appropriate home; no new artifact is needed.
