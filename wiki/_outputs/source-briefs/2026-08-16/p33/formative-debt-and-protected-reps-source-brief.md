# Formative Debt and Protected Reps Source Brief

**Summary**: Practitioner argument that AI can remove the work through which less experienced marketers learn to recognize quality. Its durable extension is operational: preserve low-stakes first-pass judgment in realistic work and require experienced reviewers to explain corrections rather than silently replacing the learner's work.

## Durable capability signal

- The source distinguishes execution from evaluation: generative tools can accelerate output without supplying the judgment needed to assess it (04:24–05:15).
- It frames realistic, low-consequence practice as the missing developmental mechanism: mistakes should occur where they can be reviewed before they count (05:17–06:05).
- Its concrete example places a less experienced reviewer beside an experienced one during a content audit and makes both argue asset decisions aloud (08:03–08:37).
- It asks experienced practitioners to narrate why they changed work instead of silently fixing it (08:38–08:51).

## Caveats

- This is a persuasive practitioner essay, not a controlled study of learning transfer, output quality, employment, or organizational performance.
- Claims about an industry-wide skills pipeline, eliminated roles, and what tools can “never” supply are too broad to promote.
- Protected practice does not mean publishing novice work unchecked or withholding useful automation. Consequential output still requires qualified review.

## Decision recommendation

`extended-claim` for `wiki/observable-capability-teach-back-loop.md`: when AI can perform the first pass, deliberately preserve a low-stakes learner rep in realistic work, have an experienced reviewer explain the judgment behind corrections, and require another demonstration. This belongs in the existing practice; it does not justify a separate framework or skill.
