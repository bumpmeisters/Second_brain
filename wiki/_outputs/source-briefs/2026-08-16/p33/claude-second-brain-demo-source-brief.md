# Claude Second-Brain Demo Source Brief

**Summary**: Long practitioner tour of a local Markdown vault combining sources, linked wiki pages, content analytics, project boards, scheduled collection, and strategy outputs. Most of the design matches existing vault structures; its unattended mutation and broad connector model omit essential selection, evidence, privacy, and action gates.

## What the source shows

- It presents local Markdown and Obsidian as a durable interface over raw material, linked wiki pages, entities, topics, analytics, and outputs (00:00–19:06).
- A link-level ingest updates several related pages, while Kanban boards connect knowledge with content and sponsor workflows (19:06–26:26).
- A separate output layer turns collected material into strategy claims intended to trace back to data, with proposed action-and-feedback loops (26:26–29:56).
- The setup distinguishes immutable raw files, a maintained wiki, an inbox, an index, and a log, then recommends a relatively flat structure (29:56–end).

## Caveats

- The demo uses synthetic sponsor data and self-reported analytics, costs, system behavior, and outcomes.
- Automatic daily collection, semantic “ripples,” scheduled rewriting, browser actions, cancellation tasks, and broad app/API connectivity require independent permission, privacy, provenance, injection, rollback, and publication controls.
- The creator sometimes presents model-generated topics, labels, trends, conclusions, and forecasts as known facts without showing a stable evaluation or approval trail.
- Claimed compounding, portability, autonomy, productivity, and universal usefulness are not demonstrated.

## Decision recommendation

Registered-only. [[llm-wiki]], [[raw-sources]], [[semantic-ingest-workflow]], [[agent-security]], and the vault's current operating contract already cover the useful design with materially stronger controls. No target-page change or reusable artifact is warranted.
