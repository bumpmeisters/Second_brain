# Revision Diagnosis and Coherence — Source Brief

**Summary**: Andrew Sean Greer describes revision as a diagnostic craft rather than compliance with an editor's proposed fix. The strongest transferable pattern is to discover what the draft is already organizing around, strengthen or remove material according to that emerging coherence, and treat reader feedback as evidence of where the experience breaks—not as authority over the remedy.

## Useful evidence

- Greer drafts scenes and vignettes before he knows their final order. During revision he looks for recurring images and echoes, strengthens the pattern, and removes even attractive material when it does not belong to the book's emerging concern (00:31–11:39).
- He compares non-linear drafting to filling the known answers in a crossword: completed scenes constrain and reveal the missing connections. This is presented as his process, not a universal alternative to outlining (03:51–11:39).
- He advocates reading “like a detective”: when a passage works or fails, inspect the structural choice—omission, scene break, narration, punctuation, perspective, pace, or transition—and decide whether it fits the intended work rather than copying a fashionable surface form (18:10–29:25).
- His character guidance deemphasizes exhaustive invented backstory. Specific observation, speech, behavior, and a few vivid details may do more practical work on the page (29:25–35:27).
- The closing editor analogy separates diagnosis from treatment. Multiple readers may correctly identify the same point of pain while prescribing the wrong fix; the author remains responsible for deciding whether to remove, prepare, intensify, move, or otherwise repair the element (01:09:42–01:12:38).

## Approved knowledge delta

`wiki/writing-guidelines.md` now includes this revision-diagnosis rule:

> Revise in two passes of judgment. First, identify the patterns, promises, and reader effects the draft is already creating; strengthen what belongs and remove material that remains attractive but structurally unrelated. Second, treat repeated reader discomfort as evidence about where the text fails, while keeping diagnosis separate from prescription. The accountable author chooses the remedy and then checks whether the revised whole now earns it.

The rule can support narrative, editorial, and thought-leadership work, but the interview does not establish that every text should be drafted out of order or organized through recurring imagery.

## Caveats and exclusions

- The source is one novelist's practitioner account. Its methods are experience-backed heuristics, not comparative findings about writing quality or reader behavior.
- The interview ranges widely across fiction, comedy, career, travel writing, and literary examples. Only the revision-and-feedback pattern is proposed for durable promotion.
- The automatically translated captions may distort names, quotations, and literary terminology. Do not promote those details from this transcript alone.
- Exclude award, sales, rejection-count, publishing-economics, sponsor, education, and career-outcome claims. They do not support the proposed method.
- Do not infer that editors should be ignored. The narrower rule is to respect observed reader difficulty while retaining accountable authorship of the solution.
