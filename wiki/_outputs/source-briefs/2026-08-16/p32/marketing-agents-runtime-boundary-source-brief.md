# Marketing Agents: Runtime Boundary — Source Brief

**Summary**: A promotional practitioner interview demonstrates outbound and organic-content systems assembled from scrapers, enrichment services, ordinary code, schedules, and LLM calls. Most of the GTM and publishing advice is already covered more safely in the vault or depends on unsupported product, legality, targeting, and performance claims. The narrow evidence value is corroboration that a bounded agent should reproduce an explicit process and reserve model inference for steps that actually require interpretation.

## Useful evidence

- The guest describes a marketing agent as ordinary code plus a thinking loop and live data, then argues against broad “agent in a box” autonomy in favor of reconstructing a specific human process (13:45–15:42).
- The demonstrated systems use schedules and deterministic data movement around narrower model-assisted decisions rather than asking a model to infer every action (12:59–15:42; 27:33–31:39).
- The guest explicitly questions spending inference on actions that could be stable code and recommends using the model only where it is needed (30:39–31:07).
- The second demonstration starts content generation from recurring human source material such as calls, conversations, and transcripts (31:41–37:21), but the source does not provide the participant-rights, confidentiality, provenance, approval, or publishing controls needed for adoption.

## Critical assessment

- The approved runtime-boundary evidence corroborates existing `wiki/agentic-systems.md` guidance; it does not establish a new marketing-agent method.
- The source treats likes and comments as a purchase signal or “hand raise.” Existing vault guidance is stronger: engagement is an observation, not intent, fit, consent, or outreach authority.
- Its scrape-enrich-contact flow uses personal data and automated outreach without a sufficiently specified lawful-access, privacy, consent, contact-policy, identity-confidence, or accountable-approval contract.
- Its content flow moves toward automatic scheduling and publication. Existing vault guidance correctly separates rendering, acceptance, scheduling, and publication authority.

## Approved evidence role

`wiki/agentic-systems.md` now uses this source as `corroborating` evidence:

> Keep stable scheduled execution and data movement in ordinary code, and invoke a model only at explicit interpretation or judgment points inside a bounded process.

This supports, but does not validate, the approved W9 lifecycle rule. It is a second practitioner citation on the existing concept page, not a new artifact or independent operating model.

## Caveats and exclusions

- Exclude all legality, compliance, data-broker, scraping, cold-email, contact-data, identity, deliverability, platform-policy, and consent claims. The speaker explicitly disclaims legal expertise and notes jurisdictional differences.
- Exclude all engagement-as-intent, targeting precision, audience coverage, send volume, response, conversion, revenue, impressions, earned-media value, virality, cost, tool, and commercial-performance claims.
- Exclude all current product stacks, pricing, provider comparisons, operational thresholds, and claims that every marketing action can or should become code.
- Do not promote automatic outreach, automatic reply handling, or unattended publication. Those actions remain subject to legitimate data access, privacy and consent controls, contact policy, source rights, separate publication authority, and accountable human approval.
- The automatic German transcript can support the broad process distinction but not exact quotations or specialized claims.

## Reusable-artifact decision

No new page, reusable artifact, template, or skill. Existing `agentic-systems`, GTM signal, and maintained-content lifecycle coverage is materially stronger. Only the narrow corroborating citation was retained.
