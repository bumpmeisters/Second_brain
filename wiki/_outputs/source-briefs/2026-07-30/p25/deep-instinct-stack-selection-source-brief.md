# Deep Instinct Stack-Selection Criteria

## Core thesis

Despite its TreviPay filename, the clipping describes Deep Instinct rebuilding a lean marketing stack. Its useful contribution is a three-part vendor-selection lens: minimize operating friction, preserve integration with multiple evidence sources, and reject opaque logic that operators cannot inspect or adapt as local learning changes.

## Structured outline

1. A lean marketing team reviewed its stack while adopting an account-based approach.
2. It retained Salesforce and HubSpot but reconsidered other components.
3. The team wanted lead-to-account mapping, visitor identification, multiple intent sources, analytics, and action delivery.
4. It compared two vendors on friction, integrations, and transparent, modifiable logic.
5. Account context was pushed into the seller's existing Salesforce workflow.
6. The remainder attributes conversion, pipeline, trust, and efficiency outcomes to the selected platform.

## Reusable mechanisms

- Start with the bounded decision and operator workflow, not a feature inventory.
- Count manual transfers, reconciliation steps, duplicate identities, and exception paths.
- Test whether required sources can join a governed account and buying-group model with retained provenance.
- Require operators to inspect, explain, challenge, and modify scoring or correlation logic.
- Deliver context into the system where the accountable user works while preserving the evidence trace.
- Pilot with known cases, record false positives and contradictions, and define rollback before admission.

## Caveats

Demandbase authored the selected-vendor case and does not identify the competing vendor, scoring rubric, costs, failed tests, data quality, or implementation exceptions. “Transparent” logic can still be wrong, and operator confidence does not establish validity. Web-identity, intent, journey-stage, attribution, engagement-minute, leading-indicator, conversion, pipeline, efficiency, product-performance, and causal claims remain excluded.
