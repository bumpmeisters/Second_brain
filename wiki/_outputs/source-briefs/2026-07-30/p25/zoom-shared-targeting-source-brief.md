# Zoom Shared-Targeting Case

## Core thesis

The clipping presents shared account criteria and data as a way to coordinate Zoom's marketing activation and sales prospecting. Marketing warms selected accounts across paid channels, while sellers receive prioritized lists and context through Salesforce and a propensity model.

## Structured outline

1. Zoom is described as having fragmented targeting and limited shared campaign criteria.
2. Marketing lacked scalable personalization and visibility into account research.
3. Sales lacked contextual prioritization and connected workflows.
4. A common data layer informed marketing audiences and seller account lists.
5. Campaign activation used display and paid social; sales used contextualized prospecting.
6. The article reports large commercial uplifts and proposes deeper integration, global expansion, and more specific personalization.

## Reusable mechanisms

- Define one governed target-account universe and shared eligibility criteria.
- Preserve source, recency, confidence, and uncertainty for observations used in prioritization.
- Give marketing and sales the same account state while tailoring the allowed action to each role.
- Carry context into the seller system rather than sending an unexplained priority score.
- Separate regional adaptation from changes to the shared core definition.
- Audit suppression, false positives, conflicting signals, and downstream feedback.

## Caveats

Demandbase authored the case about its customer and product. “Intent” does not establish purchase readiness, and a propensity score does not explain why an account should be contacted. The source omits model definitions, account selection, identity quality, regional differences, consent, negative outcomes, cost, and control construction. Opportunity, engagement, pipeline, conversion, MRR, ROAS, efficiency, personalization, and causal claims remain excluded.
