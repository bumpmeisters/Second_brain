# Demandbase Buying-Group Promotional Stub

## Core thesis

This 22-second Demandbase promotional transcript says that a B2B purchase involves multiple people with different influence, risk, priorities, and roles. It presents the buying group—not a single lead—as the relevant commercial unit and claims the vendor can identify influencers, approvers, and champions for coordinated sales and marketing activation.

## Structured outline

1. The opening frames the clip as a new era of go-to-market.
2. B2B buying is described as a multi-person decision.
3. Participants are said to have different influence, risk, and competing goals.
4. Demandbase claims to reveal the full buying group.
5. The product allegedly identifies influencers, approvers, and champions.
6. Sales and marketing can then activate the information.

## Reusable mechanisms

The clip names an operating object and three possible roles, but it does not explain how to define the decision, infer membership, validate identities, represent uncertainty, detect missing roles, route context, or correct errors. It also does not provide a customer example or independent evidence.

The vault already covers these questions in [[buying-groups-and-account-prioritization]], [[marketing-orchestration]], and [[enterprise-abm-scale-system-ten-operating-plays]]. Those pages require several observations, preserve role confidence and missing-role questions, and treat group membership and engagement as hypotheses. The short clip adds no implementable mechanism to them.

## Caveats

Demandbase is promoting its own product. A system cannot “reveal” an entire buying group with certainty from unspecified signals. Role labels may be incomplete, stale, or wrong; influence changes by decision and stage. Activation based on inferred identity carries privacy, consent, relevance, and account-relationship risks. No accuracy, completeness, outcome, or causal claim is supported.
