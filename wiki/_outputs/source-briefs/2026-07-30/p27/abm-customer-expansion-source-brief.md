# ABM for Customer Expansion

## Core thesis

Account-based marketing can continue after acquisition by coordinating Sales, Customer Success, Marketing, and Product around verified customer value and defined whitespace. The source proposes using product and relationship evidence to identify adjacent buying centers or use cases, then combining role-specific proof, executive sponsorship, enablement, and outcome governance in a named-account expansion plan.

## Structured outline

1. Map whitespace across business units, regions, roles, and adjacent use cases.
2. Wait for credible value realization and combine telemetry, renewal timing, stakeholder changes, and intent signals cautiously.
3. Discover the new buying center’s pains and desired outcomes.
4. Engage relevant roles through briefings, workshops, events, and enablement.
5. Prove the incremental value of the proposed module, tier, seats, or use case.
6. Make and govern the expansion proposal with shared commercial and adoption measures.

## Reusable mechanisms

- Define an account thesis covering whitespace, current value, political risk, buying-center coverage, and the next measurable outcome.
- Establish evidence thresholds before activation; do not turn usage or engagement into assumed intent.
- Create role-specific value narratives and proof for the adjacent decision.
- Assign Marketing, CS, Sales, Product, and RevOps responsibilities.
- Time the offer after agreed activation or outcome milestones.
- Review expansion pipeline, new buying-center coverage, adoption, sponsor strength, win/loss reasons, discount discipline, and retention together.
- Record counter-evidence and stop when prerequisites are absent.

This source adds a recognizable lifecycle extension: start from demonstrated customer value, map whitespace, open a new buying center, and couple the commercial proposal with an adoption and success plan. It passes the standalone-artifact test and could become `wiki/abm-customer-expansion-playbook.md`, linked from [[account-based-marketing]] and [[marketing-orchestration]].

## Caveats

The consulting-firm article is promotional and supplies no independent outcome study. Its client snapshot lacks enough evidence to support attribution. Product telemetry, intent, stakeholder activity, and event engagement are observations, not proof of willingness to buy. Revenue ownership is shared, and an expansion motion must not undermine customer value, consent, relationship trust, or renewal health.
