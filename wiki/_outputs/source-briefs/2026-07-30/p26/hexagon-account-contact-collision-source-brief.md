# Hexagon Account-Contact Collision Control

## Core thesis

Despite its Accela filename, the clipping describes Hexagon's challenge when several solution teams worked inside the same account and produced uncoordinated customer touches. The durable method is an account-level coordination gate: make planned and active communications visible, reconcile ownership and message conflicts, approve one coherent contact plan, and preserve suppressions and exceptions.

## Structured outline

1. Hexagon's broad solution portfolio allowed several internal teams to pursue one account.
2. Uncoordinated activities risked duplicated or conflicting customer communications.
3. The company created a cross-functional ABM Council spanning leadership, sales, and marketing.
4. The council reviewed data and initiatives and agreed a shared GTM path.
5. A unified account-activity view helped teams avoid overlapping outreach.
6. The remainder attributes personalization, advertising, engagement, and efficiency outcomes to Demandbase.

## Reusable mechanisms

- Trigger the gate when product, region, solution, partner, or account teams share an account.
- Inventory planned and active touches with stakeholder, message, offer, channel, timing, owner, and legal basis.
- Identify duplicate, contradictory, excessive, or sequence-breaking contacts.
- Assign a primary plan and accountable coordinator; merge, resequence, suppress, or reject conflicts.
- Record approved exceptions, expiration dates, and change notifications.
- Review recent responses, opt-outs, complaints, seller context, and relationship risk before release.
- Retain the decision and audit recurring collision patterns.
