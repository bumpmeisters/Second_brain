# Denodo ABX Operating Case

## Core thesis

Demandbase presents Denodo's ABX implementation as an operating-model change rather than a platform installation: one account view, a shared score, embedded CRM and automation data, weekly cross-functional review, seller enablement, and segment-level activation.

## Structured outline

1. Separate lead handling and inconsistent data made coordinated account work difficult.
2. A regional pilot was used to compare matching, enrichment, reporting, and integration.
3. Denodo combined demographic, behavioral, and account-level inputs into one readiness score.
4. Scores and engagement context were embedded in Salesforce and Marketo.
5. Marketing, sales, and BDR teams reviewed active accounts and next actions in weekly snapshot meetings.
6. Sales enablement covered signal interpretation, journey context, and message adaptation.

## Reusable mechanisms

- Treat a scoring model as a shared decision interface, not a marketing-owned number.
- Put the same evidence inside daily systems rather than requiring a separate analytics destination.
- Review account movement and next action jointly on a fixed cadence.
- Pair new data with enablement and inspect adoption before scaling.

## Lean analysis

The operating sequence is concrete enough to support existing vault practices, but it does not add a new method. Shared definitions, embedded context, weekly decisions, named actions, enablement, and pilot-first rollout already exist in the ABM operating contract and weekly pilot loop. Its value is therefore corroborating: a named implementation case shows these mechanisms being combined in one program.

## Caveats

This is a Demandbase-authored customer story designed to market Demandbase. The account-readiness score may combine fit, activity, and inferred intent in ways that hide weak inputs. The reported ROI, velocity, conversion, accuracy, adoption, and causal language lack an independent control or audit and should not be promoted. The case also does not describe privacy, false positives, model drift, competing explanations, or sales decisions that contradicted the score.
