# Academic ABM Conceptualization

## Core thesis

Severini, Terho, and Mero position ABM at the intersection of digital marketing, key-account management, and customer prioritization. Based on a Theories-in-Use study with leaders from 22 firms, the paper defines ABM narrowly as outbound digital identification of high-value account opportunities, deep account intelligence, and personalized engagement in close sales collaboration.

## Structured outline

1. Practitioner adoption has outpaced academic definition and evidence.
2. Existing ABM usage ranges from lead generation to strategic account development.
3. The study reviews practitioner literature and interviews knowledgeable managers.
4. Three core activities are reported: identify opportunities, develop intelligence, and personalize engagement.
5. The process is described across prospecting, sales negotiation, and relationship expansion, with prioritization varying by opportunity complexity and value.
6. Organizational outcomes are discussed as perceived benefits rather than independently established causal effects.

## Reusable mechanisms

This is primarily a conceptual boundary, not a standalone operating practice. It offers a compact diagnostic for inspecting whether an ABM motion actually contains account opportunity selection, account intelligence, personalized engagement, and close sales collaboration across relevant commercial stages.

## Lean analysis

The vault already treats ABM more broadly as a cross-functional account operating system spanning reputation, relationship development, opportunity support, adoption, retention, and expansion. The paper is valuable because it supplies a stronger empirical anchor while also exposing a definitional conflict: its “outbound digital marketing” boundary is narrower than several practitioner models in the vault. The durable update should preserve both definitions instead of declaring one universal.

## Caveats

The clipping contains the introduction, abstract-like claims, and partial section snippets; full findings, coding details, limitations, figure, and evidence quotations are behind access control. The 22-firm qualitative sample supports conceptualization, not prevalence or causal performance claims. Budget, ROI, pipeline, conversion, engagement, and retention statistics in the introduction come from practitioner or vendor sources and remain excluded.

The paper reports no commercial funding or declared competing interest in the visible capture, but those statements do not remove the access limitation or convert perceived outcomes into causal evidence.
