# AI Media Repurposing Control — Source Brief

**Summary**: An affiliate tutorial connects an agent to a clip-generation service and then to social publishing. Its strongest evidence is failure evidence: excess outputs and costs, a missed brand color, and captions obscuring a speaker show why rendering, asset acceptance, and publication require separate gates.

## Useful evidence

- The workflow configures a brand template, submits long-form media, renders clips, and chains selected results toward scheduling (03:36–09:42).
- A request for five clips reportedly produced about 25, consuming unwanted credits (09:44–12:18).
- Visual review found a missing brand color and captions obscuring a speaker (07:12–08:21; 12:18–13:16).

## Critical assessment

The failures support a narrow extension to `wiki/maintained-content-distribution-lifecycle.md`: reconcile requested and actual asset count and cost, visually inspect each candidate for caption accuracy and placement, subject occlusion, brand compliance, and preserved meaning, record per-asset acceptance, and keep external publication as a separately authorized stage. Prompted limits are not enforceable controls.

## Caveats and exclusions

- The creator discloses an affiliate relationship and says this is an early product version.
- The source demonstrates permission bypass and weak credential handling; these are not reusable practices.
- Product plans, authentication, integrations, templates, publishing rules, and quality scores are time-sensitive or opaque.
- Exclude efficiency, performance, quality, platform reach, “any agent” compatibility, and autonomous-publication claims.

`extended-claim` for `wiki/maintained-content-distribution-lifecycle.md`, subject to approval. No change to the stronger MCP permission model and no new reusable artifact.
