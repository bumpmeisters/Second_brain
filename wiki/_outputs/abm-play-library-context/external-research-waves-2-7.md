# ABM Play Library External Research — Waves 2–7

**Summary**: Twenty Firecrawl search-and-scrape queries across the five Situation Play families, Play Formats, and System Plays produced a complete 63-entry coverage pass. The evidence supports the portfolio's broad operating jobs but does not justify new top-level Plays. Four current entries are better treated as patterns, milestones, components, or methods.

---

## Collection and evidence boundary

- Eighteen family and system queries returned five scraped results each.
- Two additional searches tested buyer-skeptic language and reciprocal Sales–Marketing working agreements.
- Results were screened for relevance, source type, commercial bias, and whether they described a distinct operating job.
- Nineteen leads were retained in the Discovery Source Map; four Wave-1 sources were archived.
- Search-result presence is recorded as coverage, not validation.
- Weak, irrelevant, and negative search results remain visible in the disposition ledger.
- No normal web search was used.

## Wave 2 — Build Account Demand & Relevance

External practice recognizes account warming, problem education, initial account access, competitive displacement, and reactivation. The strongest boundary is between a target population and a Play: named accounts, competitor installs, or dormant records are not sufficient without a current condition, owner, hypothesis, and stop rule.

The dormant-account query returned mostly irrelevant consumer-banking results. This is negative evidence about discoverability, not evidence that dormant-account reassessment is invalid. `Wake the Sleeping Giant` therefore remains context-specific and only moderately supported.

## Wave 3 — Develop Buying Groups & Relationships

Buying-group mapping, multithreading, champion development, executive engagement, reputation work, and ecosystem access are widely recognizable mechanisms. Challenger's buyer-profile language treats internal resistance as information about consensus and change, supporting the less adversarial name `Earn the Skeptic`.

Direct external evidence for transferring relationship capital when a champion changes employers remains limited. `Chase Our Champion` stays in the research baseline because it names a distinct relationship event, but with an explicit evidence gap.

## Wave 4 — Create & Accelerate Opportunities

Buyer-enablement and pursuit sources support distinct jobs around initiative creation, business-case development, no-decision, confidence, RFP shaping, deal rescue, and re-entry after change. They do not support a meeting itself as the material state change.

Commercial, legal, security, and procurement work appears as part of decision enablement and risk reduction. The research does not yet justify a separate top-level procurement Play; the safer move is to extend `Close the Confidence Gap` with a commercial-assurance route.

## Wave 5 — Realize & Expand Customer Value

Customer-success and account-growth sources distinguish onboarding/adoption, realized value, friction recovery, relationship expansion, whitespace discovery, adjacent use cases, co-creation, and advocacy. Geographic expansion is less often named as a separate Play, but the internal partner case shows that local rights, route, regulation, and stakeholder context can make it operationally distinct.

Whitespace analysis remains an intelligence step that routes into `Plant the Next Use Case` or `Expand Into the Customer Organization`; it is not added as another top-level Play.

## Wave 6 — Protect, Recover & Renew Revenue

Renewal, retention, churn recovery, win-back, executive change, and post-M&A retention sources support the family's lifecycle logic. Evidence is more operational than causal and is dominated by vendors and practitioners.

Renewal readiness, acute renewal rescue, healthy-base defense, downsell prevention, relationship repair, broad account recovery, and former-customer win-back remain different decisions. M&A and reorganization evidence supports extending `Survive the Reorg`, not adding a separate merger Play.

## Wave 7 — Formats and System Plays

External taxonomies strongly recognize 1:1, 1:few, and 1:many ABM, orchestration, buying-group management, data quality, privacy, personalization, channel selection, collaboration agreements, AI governance, and decision-oriented measurement.

The searches did not establish `Tiger Team` as a distinct peer ABM format. It is better represented as a temporary resourcing component inside Pursuit, recovery, or transformation work. `Lift and Shift` is similarly better represented as a controlled learning-transfer method inside a 1:few Cluster Play.

Sales–Marketing SLA examples support a reciprocal working agreement. They do not support coercive wording; `Contract Before Campaign` is the recommended editorial name for SY-08.

## Portfolio-level conclusions

1. Keep memorable names, but pair each with a precise subtitle and visible level.
2. Treat `Find the Spark` as a hypothesis pattern under signal interpretation.
3. Treat `Turn Interest Into a Meeting` as a progression milestone beneath opportunity creation.
4. Treat `Tiger Team` as a resourcing component, not a peer format.
5. Treat `Lift and Shift` as a transfer method within 1:few work.
6. Extend `Plant the Flag`, competitive displacement, and late-stage confidence with sharper admission boundaries.
7. Rename `Win Over the Blocker` and `Make Sales Sign the Contract`.
8. Do not add a procurement, whitespace, M&A, community, partner-recruitment, or executive-mandate Play without a distinct uncovered operating job.

## Evidence limitations

- Most operational sources are vendors, consultancies, practitioners, or commercial research publishers.
- Buyer-side, procurement-side, partner-side, audited, longitudinal, and resource-constrained evidence remains thin.
- Search language is inconsistent: the market uses `play` for programs, filters, workflows, tactics, and state changes.
- The research supports taxonomy decisions more strongly than performance claims.
