# ABM Play Library External Wave 1 — Taxonomy Stress Test

**Summary**: Firecrawl discovery across ten queries and fifty scraped results tests the five structural questions prioritized at Review Gate 1. Four decision-relevant sources were subsequently archived and registered in P10. No P10 evidence row has been approved, and the v0.1.0 Library remains unchanged.

---

## Collection

- Firecrawl authentication and a live scrape test passed on 2026-07-24.
- Ten successful search-and-scrape queries returned fifty results.
- Queries covered Play definitions, signals versus Plays, meeting conversion, competitive displacement, Pursuit Marketing, Tiger Teams, Momentum ITSMA, Demandbase, 6sense, and exact Play terminology.
- Ten sources were retained as discovery leads after title, domain, content, relevance, and commercial-bias review.
- Search-quality feedback was sent for all ten successful searches.
- INFUSE, Fractorial, Madison Logic, and Inflexion were selectively archived through the approved source inbox; the Madison Logic PDF has a validated searchable sidecar.

## Main finding: the market does not use “Play” consistently

External sources use `Play` for at least four different objects:

1. a cross-functional intervention that moves an account from intent to opportunity or revenue;
2. a configured sales workflow specifying whom to contact, why, and what to do next;
3. a campaign or tactic sequence;
4. a signal-filter or audience definition that makes accounts eligible for action.

Momentum describes cross-functional Plays that turn intent into opportunity and opportunity into revenue. Demandbase describes Plays as configurable criteria, fields, filters, and seller-facing actions. INFUSE labels buying-group activation, intent activation, co-orchestration, sequencing, and personalization as Plays while also calling them tactics. These differences support the Library's need for visible level labels and precise subtitles. They do not establish the project's three-level taxonomy as an industry standard.

## Gate 1 question findings

### D1 — Is Find the Spark a Situation Play?

**Preliminary result:** Lean toward `reclassify`; keep open until broader signal research.

- Fractorial describes intent identification as the first step in a process that still requires ICP fit, combined first- and third-party evidence, message/action selection, handoff, and target-list refresh.
- Demandbase Play examples combine signals with a defined account state and action, such as a stalled opportunity showing renewed signals or a closed-lost account with returning interest.
- INFUSE calls intent activation a Play, but the described operating job is thresholding and conversion to outreach rather than changing a customer/account state by itself.

**Interpretation:** A signal is not a Play. `Find the Spark` becomes a defensible bounded intervention only if it owns evidence triangulation and an explicit activate/monitor/reject decision. That still resembles a System Play or Intelligence & Hypothesis step more than a commercial Situation Play.

### D2 — Is Turn Interest Into a Meeting a Situation Play?

**Preliminary result:** Stronger `reclassify` recommendation.

- No reviewed source defined the meeting itself as the material ABM Play.
- INFUSE treats qualified meetings as a possible result of intent activation and threshold discipline.
- Generic ABM guides describe meetings, handoffs, calls, and events as tactics or progression events.

**Interpretation:** The meeting is evidence of movement, not the durable state-changing job. The useful underlying mechanism may be `Transfer the Context` or it may sit beneath `Create the Opportunity`.

### D3 — Does Plant the Flag require an ABM routing boundary?

**Preliminary result:** `extend` supported.

- INFUSE distinguishes broad demand generation from ABM activation against a defined account list and buying group.
- Momentum links account prioritization and buyer-journey mapping to cross-functional Plays.

**Interpretation:** Market entry alone is not an ABM Play. `Plant the Flag` belongs in the Library only when the managed unit includes a deliberately selected account population and an account-level learning or relationship decision.

### D4 — Does competitive displacement require more than a competitor list?

**Preliminary result:** `extend` supported, with vendor-evidence caveat.

- Madison Logic's displacement blueprint segments accounts by buyer readiness and distinguishes active switching interest from accounts not ready to change.
- Its approach also requires an understanding of incumbent deficiencies, the account's pain, buying-group roles, brand awareness, engagement, and sales qualification.
- The source is a commercial vendor blueprint and does not independently validate performance claims.

**Interpretation:** Competitor use identifies a population. A Play requires an evidenced switching condition, material unmet need or risk, a credible transition thesis, and an accountable route.

### D5 — Is Tiger Team a distinct ABM format?

**Preliminary result:** `insufficient evidence`; stronger hypothesis that it is a component.

- Two targeted searches found multiple Pursuit Marketing definitions but no substantive evidence that `Tiger Team` is used as a distinct ABM format.
- Inflexion's practitioner material emphasizes starting early, working with sales or bid teams, securing budget, using phased work, understanding the customer need, and helping the buyer decide.
- These are consistent with Pursuit Marketing and may include a temporary cross-functional team, but do not establish a separate Tiger Team category.

**Interpretation:** Keep Tiger Team in the research baseline, but treat it provisionally as an orchestration component inside Pursuit, recovery, or another bounded Play Format.

## Contrarian and cautionary findings

- Industry vocabulary may favor pragmatic adoption over taxonomic purity. A tightly governed definition could feel less familiar than vendor “plays,” which often package filters and actions.
- The project's memorable names can coexist with market language if every entry keeps its precise subtitle and level.
- Absence from ten searches is not proof that a Tiger Team format does not exist.
- Current evidence is vendor- and practitioner-led. It is useful for terminology and operating mechanisms, not causal outcomes.
- Several 2026 sources make quantitative claims based on proprietary reports; those claims were not used.
