## Durable synthesis

Start with a mapped workflow and a business problem, not a tool. Give the workflow approved internal context, define what starts it, retain human review at ambiguous or consequential points, and attach a success measure before scaling. Repetitive steps can be made deterministic and checked by a verifier; judgment-heavy steps remain bounded AI or human work.

The strongest addition is to design AI around handoffs. Meeting notes, research findings, briefs, and the client problem statement become governed shared context across strategy, content, media, account teams, and sales. This reduces repeated reconstruction and keeps outputs aligned to the same problem. Adoption then needs people, process, platform, and proof: training and peer learning; AI-consumable workflows and retrospectives; hypothesis-led tool trials; and job-appropriate measures.

## Disposition

- Extended claim: shared context at cross-functional handoffs plus the four-part adoption checklist.
- Corroborating: deterministic scripts, verifiers, staged autonomy, workflow mapping, and channel-specific measurement.
- Registered only: product tricks, tool-specific advice, speculative predictions, and unverified performance claims.

## Caveats

All three sources are practitioner or vendor-platform discussions. Product claims, efficiency percentages, attribution outcomes, and market predictions remain excluded. A “common brain” also requires permission, freshness, provenance, and retention controls not fully covered by the interviews.
