# Stop bolting on AI: Rebuild your go-to-market from the foundation up

## Core thesis

AI creates more operating value when it connects team handoffs around shared business context than when it is added as an isolated productivity feature. The source proposes a common project “brain,” supported by organization-wide fluency, explicit process changes, hypothesis-led tool selection, and outcome evidence.

## Structured outline

- Diagnose the limits of isolated AI features and disconnected individual experiments.
- Anchor each project in a shared client story and problem statement.
- Capture meeting notes, findings, briefs, and team contributions in reusable common context.
- Build fluency through baseline training, assessment, peer learning, and shared practices.
- Govern adoption through four dimensions: people, process, platform, and proof.
- Preserve human judgment for differentiation, interpretation, and consequential choices.

## Reusable mechanisms

- Put AI “into the handoffs”: make approved context available across strategy, content, media, account management, and sales rather than forcing every team to reconstruct it.
- Start tool trials with a problem statement and a falsifiable hypothesis; compare candidate tools on the same job and document whether they fit.
- Run short recurring peer-learning sessions after baseline training. Treat adoption as an internal behavior-change program, not a license deployment.
- Attach an appropriate KPI to each initiative before scaling it. Measures may include time, quality, adoption, or commercial movement, but should match the job being changed.

## Lean analysis

The vault already says that AI-native GTM requires bounded workflows, internal context, human decision rights, and observable outcomes. The useful extension here is organizational: shared context should specifically repair handoffs, while the four-part adoption frame links capability building to process redesign, platform evaluation, and proof. This could sharpen [[ai-native-gtm-operating-model]] and [[marketing-orchestration]] without creating a new concept page.

The “AI fluency score” is less durable. Tracking participation can reveal champions and support needs, but participation is not proof of business impact.

## Caveats

- This is one practitioner’s agency experience, presented on a Demandbase podcast.
- Claimed efficiency and downstream performance gains are anecdotal and not independently verified.
- Custom GPTs and a single shared brain create access, freshness, privacy, and provenance risks that the interview does not fully address.
- The four-P framework is a useful checklist, not a validated maturity model.
