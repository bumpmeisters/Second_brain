# How an Ex-Google Marketing Manager Increased SQL Quality

## Core thesis

Marketing and sales can improve qualification by jointly examining why scored records are rejected, correcting criteria from case-level evidence, and tracking quality as a shared measure. For long-cycle accounts, a separate progression loop can define observable states, create a time-bounded baseline, and inspect transitions and stagnation without treating activity as awareness, intent, attribution, or forecast certainty.

## Structured outline

1. Standardize sales rejection and disqualification reasons.
2. Review discrepant cases using profile, behavior, score, sales notes, and outcome.
3. Classify failure modes and revise qualification criteria.
4. Establish shared ownership, a quality measure, and a recurring review cadence.
5. Define observable account states and map normalized marketing and sales events with timestamps.
6. Build a current baseline, then reconstruct historical transitions, roles, channels, and dwell time.
7. Investigate stagnation and use local historical ranges for bounded planning only.

## Reusable mechanisms

- Treat sales rejection as evidence to inspect, not automatically as ground truth.
- Join contact-level and account-level context before changing qualification rules.
- Separate qualification calibration from account progression measurement.
- Carry recency and uncertainty into every inferred account state.
- Use transition and velocity tables for diagnosis, never as causal campaign attribution.

## Lean analysis

The vault already covers shared ABM ownership, next-best actions, account progression, and cautious reporting. The source adds two executable extensions. A rejection-reason calibration loop turns marketing–sales disagreement into a controlled criteria-review process. An account-progression baseline and velocity loop turns general stage language into a defined state inventory, event normalization, historical transition analysis, and stagnation review. Both pass the artifact test and warrant separate proposed pages because their triggers, inputs, sequences, outputs, and failure boundaries differ.

## Caveats

The title’s 35%-to-85% result is self-reported and lacks sample, denominator, stable definitions, comparison, and independent verification; it must not be promoted. Exclude claims that generic LLMs can replace scoring expertise, that dozens of signals reliably predict next actions, and that impressions, opens, clicks, or visits prove awareness or qualification. A 90-day window is not universal. Historical transitions may be non-stationary and cannot be linearly extrapolated or treated as evidence of campaign effect.
