# Ellina Shinnick on How to Be a Fearless Leader

## Core thesis

A distributed marketing organization can scale through a center-of-excellence contract that keeps capabilities, professional leadership, shared principles, measures, processes, assets, and reporting coherent while giving field marketers authority to adapt plans to local business goals and culture. Trust is built through small verified wins before broader structural change.

## Structured outline

1. Diagnose fragmented capacity, inconsistent standards, and local business needs.
2. Demonstrate small wins and build advocacy before requesting a larger operating change.
3. Define the complete capability portfolio and central functional leadership.
4. Specify which principles, KPIs, processes, assets, reporting, and development paths remain common.
5. Give regional or field marketers clear authority over local plans and execution.
6. Review local plans with business leaders on a monthly cadence.
7. Feed recurring learning back into the shared core or document a justified local exception.

## Reusable mechanisms

- Replace piecemeal capacity requests with an explicit capability portfolio.
- Separate functional leadership and professional development from local commercial accountability.
- Use a core-versus-local decision matrix instead of choosing total centralization or uncontrolled variation.
- Combine shared measurement with locally meaningful planning and business review.
- Treat exceptions as evidence that can revise the core, not as silent divergence.

## Caveats

This is a self-reported CMO case in HUB International’s acquisition-heavy North American insurance context. Do not infer that the model caused employee growth, retention, executive influence, functional respect, or business performance. Centralization must not erase legal, cultural, commercial, or language differences. Creative campaigns, town halls, newsletters, attention anecdotes, and personal leadership advice are not effectiveness evidence.
