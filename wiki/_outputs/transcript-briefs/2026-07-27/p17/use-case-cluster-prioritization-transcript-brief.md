# B2B Demand Generation Playbook Webinar

## Core thesis

When several shared-use-case clusters could anchor an account-based pilot, compare their historical economics, available proof, strategic fit, and operating feasibility. Select one bounded cluster rather than choosing by total revenue alone or spreading limited capacity across every plausible segment.

## Structured outline

1. Identify customers and deals from a useful historical period.
2. Classify them by shared use case rather than company label alone.
3. Compare win rate, sales-cycle length, contract value, and available customer proof.
4. State the pilot's strategic objective and weighting logic.
5. Select one cluster and record why alternatives were deferred.
6. Assign a bounded team, review cadence, and future reconsideration point.

## Reusable mechanisms

- Treat cluster selection as an evidence-based portfolio decision.
- Combine commercial history with the ability to support claims using real customer evidence.
- Use the strategic objective to resolve tradeoffs between volume, value, speed, and proof.
- Sequence clusters when team capacity cannot support several learning loops at once.

## Lean analysis

The vault already says that useful ABM clusters share a challenge or use case. The new contribution is an executable decision method for choosing among several valid clusters. This is an `extended-claim` suitable for [[account-based-marketing]], [[buying-groups-and-account-prioritization]], and a dedicated [[use-case-cluster-prioritization-workflow]]. The rest of the webinar mainly corroborates existing demand-level content, signal governance, next-action, attribution, and weekly-pilot practices.

## Caveats

The webinar mixes practitioner instruction, self-examples, third-party statistics, and course promotion. TrustRadius/Pavilion buyer claims, HockeyStack touchpoint counts, stage-duration benchmarks, performance anecdotes, pipeline and ROI claims, the 16,000-MQL anecdote, and platform-superiority assertions are excluded. The correct metric weights depend on the pilot's purpose; the source does not establish a universal scoring formula.
