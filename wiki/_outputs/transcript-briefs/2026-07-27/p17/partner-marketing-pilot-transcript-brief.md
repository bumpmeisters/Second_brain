# Anne Baker on Partner Marketing in Niche Industries

## Core thesis

Treat partner marketing as a small, evidence-gated portfolio. Start with a few strategically complementary partners, define a crisp joint customer value and memorable proof, align marketing with business development and sales, progress deliberately from partner enablement to joint activation and partner-led reach, then continue, remediate, or exit based on reciprocal evidence.

## Structured outline

1. Select a bounded set of partners whose credibility, reach, and capabilities complement the company.
2. Define the joint customer problem, value proposition, and usable proof.
3. Align partner marketing, business development, sales ownership, and measures.
4. Enable the partner with ready-to-use messaging and assets.
5. Activate with the partner through joint marketing and co-selling.
6. Expand through the partner only after evidence of participation and reciprocity.
7. Review outcomes and effort to scale, remediate, or exit.

## Reusable mechanisms

- Use a few committed partners instead of opening a broad unmanaged list.
- Progress through to-partner, with-partner, and through-partner operating states.
- Make participation easy with approved kits, clear ownership, and explicit incentives.
- Separate referrals, partner-sourced, partner-influenced, and co-sold results.
- Include an exit rule so non-reciprocal relationships do not consume indefinite capacity.

## Lean analysis

The vault covers cross-functional orchestration and bounded pilots but lacks an executable partner-marketing operating model. The selection, progression, reciprocity, and exit logic therefore supports a `new-claim`, additions to [[marketing-orchestration]] and [[campaign-reporting-and-operations]], and a dedicated [[partner-marketing-pilot-and-scale-loop]].

## Caveats

This is first-hand practitioner evidence in a vendor-hosted interview, not independent outcome research. Large impact claims, attendance figures, revenue anecdotes, awards, and causal ROI interpretations are excluded. Partner types do not share identical economics. Incentive legality, tax, contracts, deal registration, channel conflict, privacy, and data sharing require separate review.
