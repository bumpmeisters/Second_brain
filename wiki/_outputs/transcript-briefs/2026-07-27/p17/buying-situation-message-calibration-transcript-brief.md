# Bryan Law on Brains, Data, and Distinction

## Core thesis

Calibrate messaging to the buyer's current decision mode. Before active evaluation, begin with the buyer's existing situation and use a stable, distinctive category or buying-situation association so the brand can be recalled. During active comparison, foreground evidence-backed differences and decision criteria. Keep the core promise stable while testing narrative expressions.

## Structured outline

1. Identify the buying situation and the best available evidence of decision mode.
2. Start from the customer's lived problem and language, with the brand as helper.
3. Before evaluation, emphasize a consistent distinctive cue and category association.
4. During comparison, emphasize provable differences and relevant decision criteria.
5. Lock the approved core promise and proof boundaries.
6. Test narrative executions and customer resonance without repeatedly replacing strategy.
7. Record which expression changed, what remained fixed, and what evidence justified the next decision.

## Reusable mechanisms

- Separate broad recall-building from active competitive evaluation.
- Use customer context as the narrative starting point.
- Distinguish being memorable from proving a consequential difference.
- Treat creative iteration as testing expressions around a stable strategic core.
- Require evidence before turning differentiation into a claim.

## Caveats

The interview offers a practitioner heuristic, not validated cognitive science. Neuroscience, memory, emotion, trial-narrative, buyer-percentage, category-correlation, and consistent-brand-growth claims lack primary citations and are excluded as facts. Synthetic-audience tools, AI anecdotes, medical self-interpretation, and generic productivity claims are not evidence. Buyer states may be incomplete, non-linear, and difficult to observe.
