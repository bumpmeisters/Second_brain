# Steve Armenti on Building a Trust Bank

## Core thesis

The interview frames professional trust as the cumulative result of consistent work, follow-through, collaboration, openness to feedback, and reciprocal mentoring rather than self-promotion or isolated high-visibility wins.

## Structured outline

1. Early sales work and self-directed experimentation can create transferable marketing capability.
2. Psychological safety allows people to challenge templates, surface ideas, debate, learn, and recover from failure.
3. A “trust bank” compounds through reliable actions and behavior over time.
4. Leaders should evaluate both what people deliver and how they collaborate and communicate.
5. Durable mentoring often develops through repeated feedback, demonstrated receptivity, and increasing decision importance.

## Reusable mechanisms

- Make performance expectations include both outcomes and operating behavior.
- Identify small actions that build or erode trust, such as follow-through, communication, meeting readiness, and dependable delivery.
- Recognize reliable contributors in ways appropriate to them.
- Turn informal feedback relationships into a continuing loop: receive, apply, follow up, and ask for higher-altitude guidance.
- Break confidence or participation goals into a small observable next behavior.
