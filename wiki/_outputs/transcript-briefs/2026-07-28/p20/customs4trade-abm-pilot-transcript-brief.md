# ABM Program to Engage and Land Enterprise Sales Opportunities

## Core thesis

This Customs4trade case describes a small cross-functional ABM pilot built from existing activities rather than a separate campaign machine. Marketing, sales development, account executives, subject-matter experts, and content contributors reviewed a shared account set weekly, updated account states, assigned the next relevant action, and exposed capacity constraints. The process is useful corroboration for an existing vault practice, not a new method.

## Structured outline

1. Start with a bounded account set and the current operating system.
2. Define shared roles for marketing, SDR, AE, SME, and content work.
3. Review account state and observations together each week.
4. Select a relevant next action for each account.
5. Assign an owner and test whether capacity is genuinely available.
6. Separate early signals and qualitative observations from commercial outcomes.
7. Adjust the pilot without promising revenue within a long sales cycle.

## Reusable mechanisms

- Use the weekly account review to make ownership and bottlenecks visible.
- Treat account-state movement as an intermediate observation, not proof of purchase intent.
- Prefer useful, non-immediate-sales touchpoints for accounts that are not ready.
- Reuse existing content and motions before adding more infrastructure.

## Caveats

The consulting vendor interviews its own client. Reach, impression, reply, meeting, inbound, active-account, RFI/RFQ, productivity, and brand-awareness claims lack a stable baseline or independent causal test. The pilot had run for roughly three months while the described sales cycle was about two years, so revenue and ROI conclusions are not justified. Website intent, replies, and content engagement do not prove demand. Tool and stack guidance is contextual and time-sensitive. Interviews used as a “foot in the door” require transparent intent and voluntary participation.
