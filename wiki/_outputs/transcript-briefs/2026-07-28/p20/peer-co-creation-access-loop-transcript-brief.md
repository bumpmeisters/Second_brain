# Demand Generation in Action: Real-World Examples

## Core thesis

Most of this vendor-hosted session repeats existing demand-generation, content, attribution, and use-case guidance. Its distinct contribution is a consent-based peer co-creation loop: create useful content with a credible customer, expert, partner, association, or community member, then work with that person to identify and authorize relevant peer distribution or introductions.

## Structured outline

1. Define a useful peer problem and the audience or buying-group context.
2. Select a willing co-creator with genuine relevance, not merely reach.
3. Agree on purpose, role disclosure, rights, claims, and allowed distribution.
4. Produce a useful interview, story, or other co-created asset.
5. Ask the guest which colleagues, peers, or communities would benefit.
6. Obtain explicit permission for each share or introduction.
7. Record reactions and relationships, then choose continue, adapt, or stop.

## Reusable mechanisms

- Treat co-creation as both evidence production and a possible access path.
- Make the co-creator's role and relationship transparent.
- Keep an authorization map for distribution and introductions.
- Measure relationship and audience response without claiming revenue attribution.

## Caveats

The source is mixed practitioner/vendor material and promotes related services. Its pipeline, opportunity, inbound, reach, touchpoint, and survey claims are self-reported or methodologically unclear. A public connection graph does not authorize outreach. Never imply that a guest is a customer, endorser, or referrer when that is untrue; the transcript's favorable treatment of such ambiguity must be rejected. Interviews must not be concealed sales calls. Customer stories require consent, confidentiality, privacy, and claims review. “Dark social” supports triangulation, not automatic attribution.
