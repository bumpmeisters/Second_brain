# Dave Gerhardt on Marketing Leadership and Team Building

## Core thesis

Persistent micromanagement after formal delegation can indicate an organizational capability problem rather than only a leader-behavior problem. Start from the strategy and business objective, derive the roles and capabilities required, inspect the current team mix, then recruit, reassign, develop, or narrow the work before returning clear ownership.

## Structured outline

1. Identify work that has supposedly been delegated but repeatedly returns to the leader.
2. Reconfirm the business objective and the strategy the work must support.
3. Derive the roles, judgment, craft depth, and coordination capabilities required.
4. Compare those requirements with the current team and workload.
5. Distinguish unclear ownership from a capability, capacity, role-design, or support gap.
6. Choose a bounded correction and restore explicit ownership and review conditions.
7. Observe whether the leader can leave execution detail without abandoning accountability.

## Reusable mechanisms

- Diagnose delegation failure at both behavior and organization levels.
- Design roles from strategy and objectives rather than fitting strategy around current people.
- Preserve enough craft knowledge for leaders to evaluate work without taking it back.
- Make capability and support gaps discussable before judging individual performance.

## Caveats

The source is a branded practitioner interview and relies on retrospective personal experience. Exclude claims that remote work reduces loyalty, that top CMOs spend a fixed share of time recruiting, or that social reach guarantees opportunities. Equity, exit, career, sports, and network anecdotes are not operating evidence. Capability diagnosis must not become disguised performance management or a justification for biased reassignment.
