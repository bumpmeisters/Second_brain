# John Common on the Old Marketing Playbook Versus New

## Core thesis

The interview contrasts a linear automation, MQL, SDR, and acquisition playbook with a more targeted and cross-functional operating model. It recommends starting from a consequential business growth goal, defining the target audience and value proposition, aligning roles and process, and testing the change in one bounded division before expanding.

## Structured outline

1. Describe how automation and lead-volume incentives produced a linear marketing-to-SDR waterfall.
2. Reframe marketing around a measurable business growth goal rather than a channel request.
3. Specify the audience and relevant value proposition.
4. Align strategy, roles, ownership, process, data, and measurement before selecting technology.
5. Isolate one important goal or division for a bounded test.
6. Review the pilot and expand the operating change only after evidence and adoption.

## Reusable mechanisms

- Work backward from the business problem and intended growth outcome.
- Treat targeting, personalization, data, measurement, and orchestration as a connected operating system.
- Use one bounded pilot instead of declaring an enterprise-wide transformation.
- Resolve strategy, roles, and process before adding technology.

## Caveats

This is a Demandbase-hosted interview with a GTM agency founder. “Old” and “new playbook” are rhetorical bundles, not validated categories. Macro, interest-rate, valuation, Salesforce, AI, and market-change statements are time-sensitive or unsupported. The cited customer pilot has no auditable baseline, comparison, or attribution. Do not infer that the proposed playbook caused growth, efficiency, or adoption.
