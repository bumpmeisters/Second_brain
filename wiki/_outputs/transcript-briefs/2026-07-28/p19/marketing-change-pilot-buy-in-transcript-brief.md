# Full-Funnel Marketing 2.0

## Core thesis

Most of the webinar repeats established full-funnel and ABM practices. Its distinct contribution is an internal change-buy-in sequence: first establish agreement on the business problem and cost of inaction; then co-design a bounded solution with change champions; finally capture scope, roles, capacity, measures, assumptions, and review conditions in an executive pilot memo before work begins.

## Structured outline

1. Understand executive priorities and the strategic goal at risk.
2. Inspect the current process, buyer journey, deal evidence, and operational gaps.
3. Quantify or qualify the cost and risk of continuing unchanged.
4. Secure problem buy-in before presenting the preferred solution.
5. Involve change champions and jointly define a bounded pilot.
6. Write the pilot memo with scope, team, roles, resources, timeline, measures, and assumptions.
7. Obtain an explicit approve, revise, defer, or reject decision.
8. Lock success criteria and capacity commitments before onboarding and review.

## Reusable mechanisms

- Separate problem buy-in from solution buy-in.
- Present a pilot as a bounded evolution rather than a hidden enterprise transformation.
- Record non-action costs and strategic relevance without inventing financial precision.
- Prevent retrospective movement of success criteria by confirming them before launch.

## Lean analysis

The vault already covers account states, weekly next actions, cross-functional ownership, leading and lagging measures, documentation, and automation after learning. Those elements should not be promoted again. The executive buy-in memo is narrower and genuinely useful: it joins evidence, decision rights, capacity, measurement, and change adoption into one inspectable authorization artifact. It supports an `extended-claim` in [[marketing-orchestration]] and a proposed [[marketing-change-pilot-buy-in-memo]].

## Caveats

The webinar promotes a book, courses, community, and consulting. “Revenue impact in 90 days” is misleading for long sales cycles; the speakers themselves shift toward early account movement and leading indicators. Exclude customer outcomes, CMO research numbers, touchpoint counts, 30/60/90-day thresholds, 80/20 rules, and causal attribution. Engagement, replies, events, and search intent do not prove demand or purchase intent.
