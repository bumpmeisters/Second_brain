# Zarina Stanford on Mission-Driven Authentic Leadership

## Core thesis

Career aspirations should be discussed separately from performance and compensation. Ask voluntarily what work a person does best, which experiences they want next, and whether they seek another role; record the aspiration without promising an outcome; then match suitable projects, mentoring, or stretch opportunities with explicit support, workload, fairness, and review boundaries.

## Structured outline

1. Schedule a development conversation separate from performance or merit review.
2. Explain confidentiality, voluntariness, limits, and the absence of promotion promises.
3. Ask when the person is at their best and which experiences or roles they want next.
4. Record a concise aspiration brief and unresolved constraints.
5. Review a transparent inventory of projects, mentoring, shadowing, and stretch roles.
6. Match, defer, or decline an opportunity with a reason, owner, support, workload check, and review date.
7. Observe the experience and periodically refresh the aspiration and opportunity record.

## Reusable mechanisms

- Separate development demand from performance evaluation and compensation negotiation.
- Replace leader assumptions about ambition with explicit, voluntary questions.
- Treat an aspiration as input to opportunity design, not an entitlement or commitment.
- Make deferred and declined matches visible so access does not depend only on informal sponsorship.
- Review stretch load and support rather than equating development with additional unpaid work.

## Lean analysis

The vault contains team-working, feedback, teach-back, and capability-development practices, but no method connecting individual aspirations to an inspectable inventory of practical opportunities. This is a genuine `new-claim`. The trigger, inputs, sequence, output, and boundaries are sufficiently distinct to propose [[career-aspiration-to-opportunity-loop]], with a limited connection to [[marketing-orchestration]].

## Caveats

The source is one CMO’s practitioner experience, not evidence that the method improves retention, performance, or promotion equity. A twice-yearly cadence is not universal. Participation must be voluntary and confidential; people must be allowed to prefer stability or decline stretch work. The practice cannot replace HR policy, succession planning, employment law, accessibility review, compensation, or formal performance processes. Exclude mission, fun, presence, gender, “uptalk,” whole-self disclosure, and career-success generalizations.
