# Modern ABM Strategies: Outcomes, AI, and Buying Groups

## Core thesis

The interview recommends starting ABM from a customer or business outcome rather than from the familiar one-to-one, one-to-few, and one-to-many taxonomy. It then works backward through account choice, buying groups, relationships, relevance, measurement, and playbooks. This framing is sensible, but the vault already contains more complete operating models for each mechanism.

## Structured outline

1. Identify the customer and business problem before choosing an ABM format.
2. Select an acquisition, growth, retention, advocacy, or competitive-defense outcome.
3. Work backward through ICP, account choice, prioritization, tiering, and value proposition.
4. Map the people and relationships needed for the account outcome.
5. Distinguish interest from evidence of intent.
6. Agree shared accounts and baseline performance across marketing, sales, and customer teams.
7. Iterate playbooks while treating AI as acceleration rather than strategy.
8. Review commercial outcomes without claiming simple ABM attribution.

## Reusable mechanisms

- Route the account motion from the desired customer outcome.
- Treat buying-group identification as a starting point, not a handoff.
- Add relationship and access paths to stakeholder maps.
- Separate interest, intent, account response, and commercial outcome.

## Caveats

The guest leads an ABM agency and promotes playbooks, training, subscriptions, AI expertise, and a client case. The interview provides no independent audit, sample, comparison group, or causal design. Exclude claims about forty proven playbooks, AI speed or prediction, lifetime value, profitability, universal ABM history, peer-to-peer effects, and an unsubscribe as a buying signal. The AVEVA/GSK relationship, pipeline, revenue, and award numbers are not promoted. Relationship mapping requires consent, privacy, confidentiality, verified identity, and sender authority.
