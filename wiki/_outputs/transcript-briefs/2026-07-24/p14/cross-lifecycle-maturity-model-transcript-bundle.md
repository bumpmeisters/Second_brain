# Cross-lifecycle maturity model transcript bundle

**Canonical title**: *Sydney Sloan on GTM, Leadership, and Business Alignment*

**Core thesis**: A maturity model becomes commercially useful when it is a shared customer progression model, not merely an internal segmentation score. It should connect discovery, multiple stakeholder perspectives, enablement, packaging, customer education, and the next visible step.

## Structured outline

1. **Diagnose maturity, not size**: Large enterprises may have immature practices while smaller firms may operate sophisticated systems.
2. **Assess several dimensions**: Technology, process, people, skills, and change readiness can mature at different rates.
3. **Exchange value during discovery**: The assessment should teach the buyer something and collect perspectives from multiple members of the buying group.
4. **Map the commercial system**: Each stage should connect to discovery questions, quality deliverables, relevant capabilities, packaging, and an intelligible budget horizon.
5. **Enable by role**: SDRs, sellers, and customer-success teams need different training for the same progression model.
6. **Continue after purchase**: Customer education and adoption should update the maturity state and reveal the next useful step without leading with an upsell.

## Lean analysis

The vault already contains maturity models and account progression. The new contribution is the use of maturity as a cross-lifecycle operating contract. It creates one customer-facing language while preserving role-specific behavior: SDRs identify the initial state, sellers deepen multi-stakeholder discovery, and customer-success teams use the same map for education, adoption, and expansion readiness.

This pattern is useful only if the stages are evidence-based and do not become a disguised product ladder. Teams should record contradictory stakeholder assessments, separate observed practice from inferred maturity, and avoid treating the model as proof of likely revenue. Reported retention, NPS, customer-event, and valuation figures in the interview are excluded.

## Decision rule

Use the model when it helps a customer understand its present state and choose a useful next capability. Do not use it merely to relabel account tiers or force every buyer toward the largest package. A stage change should cite observed practices and stakeholder evidence, record disagreement, and identify a next step that remains valuable even if no purchase follows.
