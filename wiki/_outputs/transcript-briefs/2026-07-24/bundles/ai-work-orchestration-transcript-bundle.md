## Durable synthesis

Delegated work becomes easier to supervise when humans and agents share one persistent task surface. Tasks move through explicit states—capture, ready, doing, waiting, and done—rather than disappearing into separate chat sessions. Low-friction capture keeps work entering the same system; machine evals reject obvious failures before review; the human gate applies judgment and taste. The important shift is managerial: scope work, define constraints, review exceptions, and control the token and attention budget (anchors: 1:20-3:46, 11:37-15:33, and 15:42-17:57 in the practical workflow transcript).

For GTM, the strongest new mechanism is not AI-written “personalization.” It is selection: humans create credible offers, content, and experiences, while AI chooses a relevant sequence from that governed library based on the current person, account, and buying-group state. This is closer to next-best-action orchestration than mail merge (anchors: 17:29-20:43 and 21:57-24:02 in the Jon Miller transcript).

Adoption needs an organizational learning loop around those workflows. A cross-functional council can bring together motivated operators, workflow owners, legal, security, and data roles to establish usable policy and share lessons. Leadership must use the tools enough to set credible expectations, and failed experiments should be recorded as learning rather than hidden (anchors: 7:08-9:19, 11:45-16:22, and 27:40-28:31 in the adoption transcript).

## Caveats

The speakers promote their expertise or businesses. Revenue, productivity, tool-security, legal, employment, and predicted channel effects are not accepted as evidence. The adoption-council pattern supplements—rather than replaces—the vault’s stronger risk and governance controls.
