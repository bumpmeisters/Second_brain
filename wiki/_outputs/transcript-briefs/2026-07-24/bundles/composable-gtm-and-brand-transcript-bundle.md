## Durable synthesis

A stack is not composable merely because tools exchange data. A connected stack can still force operators to inspect each platform separately and reconcile conflicting account views. The stronger design uses modular components around a unified account model, so teams can compare activity and outcomes across channels and replace or test components without rebuilding the whole system. Start with a small set of channels, connect ICP, creative, activation, and measurement, then expand only after the slice works (anchors: 11:18-16:26 and 17:29-20:47 in the composable-marketing transcript).

Brand and ABX require a parallel operating contract. Brand builds recognition and receptivity over time; ABX applies relevance to a particular account and buying-group context. Before activation, sales, marketing, and brand should be able to answer one question: will the buying group hear one story or several conflicting stories? The suggested measurement model also works in layers: brand recognition and trust signals; account reach and engagement; and pipeline or commercial outcomes. The analytical task is to study where these layers move together, without pretending that correlation proves causation (anchors: 0:00-1:04, 30:04-33:02, 34:21-39:27).

Together, the sources imply that composability is both technical and narrative. Tools need shared account context, while teams need a shared promise. Modularity without coordination creates orchestration debt; account activation without prior brand memory lands as an isolated message.

## Caveats

Both interviews appear on a vendor podcast and include vendor products or customer stories. Reported reach and engagement uplifts, named-tool advantages, claims about attribution, and predictions about autonomous campaigns are excluded. The models are operating heuristics, not validated causal frameworks.
