# GTM change and capability transcript bundle

**Core thesis**: Enablement should verify that a person can apply and explain a skill, not merely record that training was consumed. AI is useful as a bounded practice and feedback aid; accountable people still judge context, empathy, and field performance.

## Structured outline

1. **Define observable capability**: Translate the operating method into behaviors a learner can demonstrate.
2. **Teach back and apply**: Ask the learner to explain, rehearse, and use the method in a realistic scenario.
3. **Combine feedback sources**: Use peers, managers, call evidence, and bounded AI feedback rather than a completion score alone.
4. **Make competence visible**: Capture accepted examples and recurring gaps so teams can learn from strong practice.
5. **Expand after proof**: Roll out only when demonstrated behavior survives real work and review.

## Lean analysis

The CrossKnowledge case study repeats mechanisms already established in the vault: diagnose declining lead economics, treat internal change as a buyer journey, recruit early adopters, run a bounded pilot, align sales and marketing, and use leading indicators during long deal cycles. Its pipeline, conversion, named-account, database, and consultant-effectiveness claims are self-reported and excluded. After full review it adds no material knowledge delta.

The teach-back interview adds a sharper verification mechanism to existing AI-adoption and sales-enablement rules. Completion, exposure, or a high AI-generated score is weak evidence of capability. A stronger loop is learn, teach back, apply, receive feedback, and demonstrate again. AI may supply practice, retrieval, or first-pass feedback, but it should not reward verbatim scripts, infer human competence from fluent delivery, or become a surveillance mechanism. Claims about retention rates, sales uplift, churn, onboarding failure, and named customer outcomes are vendor assertions and excluded.

## Decision rule

Use a capability gate when a workflow depends on judgment or interpersonal execution. Define the behavior, require a realistic demonstration, keep the evaluation rubric visible, and include human review for consequential interpretation. Store accepted examples only with appropriate consent and governance. Measure transfer into real work rather than training completion.
