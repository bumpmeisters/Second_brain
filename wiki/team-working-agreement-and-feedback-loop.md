# Team Working Agreement and Feedback Loop

**Summary**: Translate leadership values and team preferences into observable, reciprocal working rules. Review actual use, burden, exceptions, delegation, and contribution credit so the agreement remains a coordination tool rather than a leader's unilateral user manual.

---

## Trigger

Use this workflow during a leadership transition, team formation or expansion, material reorganization, or after repeated friction involving meetings, communication, focus time, feedback, delegation, escalation, or recognition.

## Required inputs

- The team's purpose, responsibilities, decision scope, and current organizational policies.
- Participants, role boundaries, time zones, accessibility needs, and material workload constraints.
- The leader's stated values, working preferences, expertise limits, and non-negotiable accountabilities.
- Team members' communication, participation, focus, feedback, and support needs.
- Existing meeting inventory, recurring handoffs, decision rights, and escalation routes.
- A safe method for private or anonymous input where power differences make open challenge difficult.
- A named agreement owner, review cadence, and path to HR, legal, accessibility, or safeguarding support when required.

## Procedure

1. **Draft bounded leadership expectations.** State relevant values, communication preferences, decision responsibilities, expertise limits, and what the leader will and will not disclose. Authenticity does not require unrestricted personal disclosure.
2. **Invite reciprocal completion.** Ask team members what they need from the leader and one another. Collect open, private, or anonymous input as appropriate, and identify where a stated preference would create unequal burden.
3. **Agree communication rules.** Define channel purposes, expected response windows, urgent paths, documentation norms, time-zone expectations, and when synchronous discussion is necessary.
4. **Set meeting and focus rules.** Require a purpose, agenda, needed decision, accountable owner, and clear attendance status. Protect focus blocks and permit optional attendance where the person's contribution is not required.
5. **Define feedback and escalation.** Establish frequent multi-directional feedback, how to raise a concern, who decides unresolved issues, confidentiality limits, and routes outside the reporting line.
6. **Make expertise and delegation explicit.** Record which decisions remain with the leader, which belong to domain experts, what can be delegated, and what support or context the delegate needs.
7. **Preserve contribution credit.** Record material contributions, name the people who created the result, and carry that credit into reviews, presentations, and stakeholder communication.
8. **Publish and test the agreement.** Store the agreement where the team can find it, use it during onboarding and recurring work, and record exceptions rather than silently normalizing them.
9. **Review actual use and burden.** On the agreed cadence, inspect meeting load, focus interruptions, response pressure, feedback use, unresolved escalations, delegation quality, accessibility, and contribution visibility.
10. **Revise with the team.** Keep, change, or retire rules based on observed work and team input. Record the change, rationale, owner, and next review date.

### Strategy-first capability and delegation diagnostic

When a leader repeatedly re-enters work after it was formally delegated, do not begin by reclaiming the task or judging the delegate. Restate the business or customer objective, derive the roles and capabilities required to reach it, and compare the current assignment across ownership, available capacity, craft or domain expertise, and access to context, tools, decisions, and support. Correct only the bounded gap: clarify an outcome or decision right, rebalance capacity, add coaching or specialist support, fund an approved resource, or change the assignment when necessary. Then restore explicit ownership and set a proportionate review point rather than converting support into permanent leader control.

This diagnostic is an operational analysis derived from the source's strategy-first view, shared objective, cross-functional roles, and relay-style ownership; the interview does not itself validate this exact diagnostic or claim that it improves performance (source: Ed Breault on communicating with clarity and leading with curiosity.md; transcript anchors 8:56–16:32). Capability judgments must use role-relevant evidence and consistent criteria, give the delegate a safe opportunity to explain constraints, and avoid stereotyping or shifting chronic understaffing onto an individual. Budget or headcount changes require the appropriate approval. Reassignment, accommodation, compensation, employment-status, disciplinary, or performance-management decisions remain subject to company policy, HR, legal, accessibility, collective-agreement, and fairness requirements.

## Inspectable output

The practice produces:

- a reciprocal working agreement with version and review date;
- communication, meeting, focus-time, feedback, and escalation rules;
- an expertise and delegation map;
- documented confidentiality and disclosure boundaries;
- a contribution and credit record;
- a review log containing observed friction, burden, exceptions, agreed changes, owners, and follow-up dates.

## Reuse boundaries

This agreement supports day-to-day coordination; it cannot replace employment law, company policy, collective agreements, HR processes, formal performance management, accessibility accommodations, grievance or disciplinary procedures, safeguarding, or protected reporting channels. A leader's preference is not automatically a team obligation. Power asymmetry, privacy, cultural differences, caregiving, disability, and time-zone burden require explicit review and safe routes for dissent.

The source supports translating values and bounded transparency into working rules at **10:43–13:30**, meeting and focus practices at **13:30–18:40**, and expertise, delegation, contribution records, and visible credit at **20:06–25:49**. It does not establish that these practices cause performance, retention, wellbeing, or psychological safety. The governance, privacy, HR, policy, accessibility, and anti-retaliation boundaries are necessary operating additions rather than outcome claims from the interview (source: Amanda Dyson on Nurturing and Growing Authentic Leadership.md; transcript anchors 10:43–18:40 and 20:06–25:49).
