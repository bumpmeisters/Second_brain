# B2B Persona Development

**Summary**: B2B persona development turns customer roles, needs, buying triggers, objections, and journey behavior into usable audience intelligence.

---

The persona/audience folder contains persona analyses, persona templates, prospect journey decks, SMB persona decks, and commercial-services persona/journey materials (source: raw/assets/04_Persona_und_Audience).

The B2B persona blueprint files frame personas as a strategic architecture that can move from profile to predictive asset, but the Gemini-related files should be treated as `needs verification` until their claims and citations are checked (source: 20250626_B2B Persona Blueprint Creation_Gemini.docx).

Persona templates in the folder provide reusable structures for mapping audience roles, journeys, and decision behavior (source: raw/assets/04_Persona_und_Audience/Persona_Templates).

## Living ICP research loop

Treat the ideal customer profile as a governed qualification system, not a static description. Record both inclusion and disqualification criteria, assign a named owner, preserve version history, and state why each material change was made (source: Your ICP should be more like a wiki than a document.md; practitioner article).

Refresh the ICP and related personas from recurring evidence: wins, losses, churn, unexpected successes, product or segment changes, customer interviews, surveys, sales-call observation, and buyer language found in relevant communities. Revisit evidence after initial analysis and actively test assumptions that the team has begun to treat as facts (source: Your ICP should be more like a wiki than a document.md; source: Modern Disco Formats and Tips for Primary Research in B2B Marketing.md).

AI may help organize or compare the inputs, but a responsible human should decide whether the ICP changes. The sources do not validate a universal review frequency, so cadence should reflect the rate of meaningful market, product, and customer change.

## ICP evidence triangulation

Validate the account-level profile by combining three evidence classes:

1. **Quantitative evidence** from current and past customers, such as contract or lifetime value, sales-cycle length, acquisition cost, satisfaction, renewals, expansion, and referrals.
2. **Qualitative evidence** from customer-facing teams, customer interviews, observed buying triggers, losses, disqualification reasons, and market conversations.
3. **Predictive evidence** that may reveal additional patterns after known customer and account data has been analyzed.

No class should stand alone. Anecdotal stakeholder consensus can encode bias; internal data can reflect yesterday's market or selection effects; predictive tools can reproduce the limitations of their inputs. Use predictive profiling as a supplement, not as the author of the ICP, and record which evidence justified each meaningful revision (source: 20260722_Defining_An_ICP_For_Manufacturers_HeinzMarketing.pdf).

The four account-level blocks—firmographics, buying-group demographics, technographics, and company psychographics—are documented in [[account-based-marketing#Four-block ICP architecture|Account Based Marketing]]. Together, the architecture and validation loop turn the ICP into a testable qualification model rather than a fictitious description of an ideal buyer.

## Buying-group role classification

Map buying roles by power, action, and concern rather than job title alone. Power distinguishes decision-makers who can authorize a choice, validators who can block it, and influencers who shape it. Action distinguishes a passive participant from a champion who actively researches, mobilizes colleagues, and advances the decision. Concern records the technical, business, legal, compliance, risk, or operational issue each person represents (source: Persona Classification and the Buying Committee The What, How, and Why - Pt. 1.md; vendor practitioner article).

This is a practical mapping heuristic, not a universal taxonomy. The same person can play different roles across decisions or stages, so role assignments should be treated as current hypotheses and updated from observed behavior.

## Qualification rejection as persona evidence

Use structured sales rejection reasons as one input to persona and ICP revision. Review discrepant cases against the profile, account context, observed behavior, score, seller notes, and eventual outcome before changing a criterion. Repeated disagreement may reveal a weak persona assumption, a scoring defect, missing account context, inconsistent seller judgment, or a taxonomy problem; it does not by itself establish which team is correct (source: How ex-Google group marketing manager increased SQLs from 35% to 85% in one year.md; transcript anchors 1:23-6:43; analysis: [[qualification-rejection-reason-calibration-loop]]).

Preserve the cases, error class, decision, owner, and review date so persona changes remain auditable. The source's headline result is excluded.

## Learning a specialist audience from outside the domain

When the marketing team lacks sufficient specialist knowledge, treat SMEs as hypothesis sources rather than substitutes for buyer evidence. Define the decisions the research must support, interview experts about jobs, language, problems, triggers, and likely misconceptions, then test those hypotheses through transparent community questions, customer and product evidence, internal data, and contradictory cases. Cluster supported findings by use case, trigger, job, responsibility, and decision context rather than defaulting to broad industry labels or job titles (source: Supercharge Your Go-To-Market Strategy with Full-Funnel Go-To-Market Template.md; canonical title: *Case Study: Converting Skeptical IT Buyers with ABM*; transcript anchors 8:36-33:19 and 1:00:43-1:02:44; analysis: [[domain-outsider-audience-learning-loop]]).

Community responses and specialist opinions are not representative market statistics. Preserve source, permission, uncertainty, contradictions, and permitted downstream use, and never conceal identity or bypass private access rules.
