# Account-based Marketing Strategy Deck (2023 Ideas)

**Summary**: Rolf's 27-slide working deck connects ABM business objectives to account selection, audience development, coordinated marketing and sales activity, pipeline acceleration, and customer-renewal planning. ## Section map

1. **Objectives and strategy architecture (slides 3-5).** The deck separates new-customer and existing-customer revenue objectives, then connects them to account identification, relationship development, buying-center reach, reputation, and a portfolio of ABM use cases.
2. **Account-centric marketing and segmentation (slides 6-7).** A closed-loop operating model links ICP definition, data enrichment, audience modelling and activation, experience, engagement, lead matching, sales cadence, CRM insight, and measurement. Segmentation can be static or dynamically triggered by fit, behavior, opportunity, territory, product whitespace, competitor interest, or seller activity.
3. **Mid-market pipeline acceleration (slides 9-21).** A traditional seller journey is used to ask where marketing can warm, identify, extend, and sustain engagement. The section explores coordinated email, LinkedIn, paid media, display, direct mail, seller landing pages, cookie capture, and sales-engagement sequences.
4. **Sales-stage orchestration (slides 22-25).** Marketing intensity is highest in early sales stages and declines as seller intensity rises. The model asks which content and experiences fit each stage.
5. **Contractual-customer planning (slides 26-27).** A T-minus model aligns customer deployment, usage, renewal, incumbent expansion, competitive acquisition, and marketing activity over a 60-to-12-month horizon.

## Initial interpretation against the current ABM project

The deck's durable strategic idea is broader than a channel plan: **ABM is a coordinated growth system that changes its job according to the commercial and customer situation.** That idea is strongly compatible with the Enterprise Growth System's distinct Growth Motions and shared execution logic (analysis based on source: raw/assets/20260723_Account-based Marketing Strategy.pptx; comparison: [[projects/abm-operating-system/frameworks/enterprise-growth-system|Enterprise Growth System]]).

Several deck elements now need sharper boundaries before reuse:

- “In-market” identification, ICP fit, MQA, intent, and engagement are signals or hypotheses, not sufficient admission criteria for deeper ABM.
- Pipeline acceleration, sales stages, T-minus programs, channels, campaigns, and service depth are implementation choices; they should not become Growth Motions.
- Buying-center expansion should mean decision-relevant relationship and role coverage, not only audience reach or contact acquisition.
- Marketing and sales coordination should expand to customer, delivery, regional, executive, and partner owners when the situation requires it.
- Stage-based touch levels are useful design hypotheses, but “no marketing touch” in late stages is too absolute; the required contribution depends on the decision, relationship, proof, and experience gap.
- Cookie, IP, contact enrichment, lead matching, and automated cadence practices require current privacy, identity, consent, and decision-quality controls.
- Measurement should combine progression and commercial evidence with explicit causal restraint rather than treating engagement or attribution as proof of revenue impact.

These are discussion hypotheses, not approved framework changes (analysis based on source: raw/assets/20260723_Account-based Marketing Strategy.pptx; comparison: [[projects/abm-operating-system/wiki/patterns/index|Candidate Pattern Intelligence]]).

## Discussion questions

- Was the core 2023 thesis “ABM supports pipeline,” or the broader idea that different commercial states require different account-based interventions?
- Should the use cases on slide 3 be preserved as a reusable situation library beneath the five Growth Motions?
- In the mid-market journey, which problem mattered most: weak account intelligence, low seller capacity, the dark period, or fragmented channel execution?
- Was the declining marketing-touch curve intended as an ownership handoff, a budget rule, or a hypothesis about buyer needs?
- Is the T-minus model mainly a renewal defense, an expansion architecture, a competitive-displacement play, or a combined customer lifecycle?
- Which elements came from Rolf's own operating experience, and which were adapted from HP, DB Schenker, Seismic, Outreach, 6sense, or Eloqua examples?
