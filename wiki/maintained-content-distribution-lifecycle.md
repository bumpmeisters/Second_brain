# Maintained Content Distribution Lifecycle

**Summary**: Treat strategically important content as a maintained asset whose publication begins a governed cycle of distribution, adaptation, resurfacing, refresh, and retirement.

---

## When to use

Use for proven or strategically important content that should create value beyond its first publication window.

## Procedure

1. Identify the core asset, audience problem, evidence, owner, and expected useful life.
2. For an existing portfolio, build a debt schedule with owner, last verification date, volatile claims, prices, statistics, product names, near duplicates, contradictions, relevant use or performance context, and the next review decision.
3. Use automation to flag candidates and contradiction pairs, never to certify current company truth. Route material claims and classifications to accountable humans for verification.
4. Diagnose the portfolio before changing it. Prioritize by volatility, evidence risk, obligation, business importance, and observed use rather than applying one universal age cutoff.
5. Attach a distribution brief at publication with channels, people, communities, account uses, timing, and review dates.
6. Distinguish:
   - **reposting**: redistribute the same asset;
   - **repurposing**: adapt verified material to a real channel context;
   - **refreshing**: revise evidence, claims, examples, or structure;
   - **retiring**: remove or redirect material that is no longer reliable.
7. Resurface the asset over time rather than treating publication as completion.
8. Preserve source traceability and human review in AI-assisted adaptation.
9. Before accepting AI-generated derivatives, reconcile the requested and actual asset count and cost. Inspect every candidate for caption accuracy and placement, subject occlusion, brand-template compliance, and preservation of source meaning; record a per-asset accept or reject decision (source: 2026-07-31--4RPFO8ytYaY.md; affiliate practitioner tutorial; analysis: P32-W4-C02).
10. Treat rendering, asset acceptance, scheduling, and external publication as separate authority stages. A successful render, vendor quality score, or available publishing connector is not publication approval.
11. Review the evidence and decide to keep, update, combine, archive, redirect, or retire. When a new asset materially overlaps an existing one, record which asset it supersedes or why both should remain; preserve required compliance exceptions and reversible archival (source: 2026-08-18--w9IANBfsECY.md; practitioner evidence; analysis: P43-W6R5-C03).

## Output

A distribution brief, adaptation inventory, refresh history, review cadence, and lifecycle decision.

## Guardrails

Reuse must not detach claims from their evidence or simulate people without consent and disclosure. Automation must not overwrite locked verified fields, conceal removals, resolve uncertain classifications without review, or publish directly. Pipeline, SEO, productivity, bestseller, time-saving, and platform-sensitive claims are excluded (source: Marcus Sheridan on Building Trust and Transparency in the AI Era.md; practitioner transcript; analysis: P15-C03).

Prompted quantity and cost limits are not enforceable controls. Use tool-side caps where available, stop on material variance, and never let rendering authority silently propagate into publishing authority. Permission-bypass and credential-handling practices from the source are explicitly excluded (source: 2026-07-31--4RPFO8ytYaY.md; analysis: P32-W4-C02).

A debt schedule is a diagnostic artifact, not mutation authority. Automation may surface stale or contradictory candidates, but accountable humans must verify the current truth and approve consolidation, archival, redirects, or retirement. The source's fixed 24-month presumption, reported cases, statistics, and scale claims are excluded (source: 2026-07-30--8y90Li5feJM.md; analysis: P33-W2-C02).
