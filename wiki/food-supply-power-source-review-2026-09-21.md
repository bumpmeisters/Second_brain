# Power asymmetry in Australian food supply relationships

**Review scope:** PDF pp. 1, 5, 10–12; selected methods, findings, and limitations.

Bogomolova and colleagues analyze 50 Australian interviews: 16 producers, 16 retailers, and 18 policymakers. Their qualitative account distinguishes micro, meso, and macro mechanisms of power and a continuum of asymmetry. Viable alternatives, including hospitality and tourism channels, can reduce dependence on a dominant buyer in this setting. (source: Exploring-the-intensity-of-power-asymmetry-in-food-sup_2026_Industrial-Marke.pdf, pp. 1, 5, 10–12; DOI 10.1016/j.indmarman.2026.01.002)

Consumers were excluded; coverage spans four states and small role subgroups. Interview accounts, possible social-desirability effects, and sector context do not establish causal effects, quantified thresholds, or a universal diversification recommendation. (source: same PDF, pp. 11–12)
