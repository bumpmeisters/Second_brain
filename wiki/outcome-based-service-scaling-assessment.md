# Outcome-Based Service Scaling Assessment

**Summary:** Assess how an outcome-based service can scale within its configuration and constraints.

## Trigger

Use when an outcome-based pilot needs a scaling decision or an existing offer encounters repeatability, measurement, or coordination constraints.

## Required inputs

- Promised outcome, customer contribution/control, and delivery responsibility.
- Current service configuration, operational evidence, costs, and strategic objectives.
- Measurement feasibility, data access, resource constraints, and an accountable decision owner.

## Procedure

1. Define the customer outcome and how its realization could be observed. Stop if the outcome or necessary customer participation is undefined.
2. Identify the closest current configuration: customized pilot, standardized long-term service, or process-optimization/performance-contract offer. Record mismatches; do not force a hybrid into a rigid stage.
3. Examine potential logics of **scale** (repeatability), **scope** (shared capabilities across offers), **learning** (improvement from accumulated experience), and **structure** (coordination and arrangement of delivery). These are analytical prompts, not quantitative scores.
4. Test each plausible logic against binding constraints: repeatability, outcome measurement, customer control/access, coordination, and rigidity. Explicitly consider standardization versus fit and automation versus judgment.
5. Compare advance, stay, or redesign against strategic fit and available evidence. A supposedly more advanced configuration is not automatically preferable.
6. Specify one bounded next test with an owner, outcome measure, prerequisites, and stop/review condition. Record why rejected paths or immediate expansion are not supported.

## Inspectable output

Produce a decision record containing current configuration, desired outcome, selected scaling logic, binding constraint, alternatives considered, advance/stay/redesign choice, uncertainty, and owned next test. Include the evidence needed to revisit the decision.

## Reuse boundaries

Derived from a qualitative supplier-side study of nine industrial firms and 29 interviews. It is not a validated quantitative instrument, causal growth model, or mandatory maturity ladder. Cross-sector transfer needs testing; contractual, commercial, and operational commitments require their own decision authority. The method evaluates a provider's service configuration, not a customer's maturity.
