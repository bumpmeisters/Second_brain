# AI Search Measurement

**Summary**: AI-search measurement combines a stable prompt panel, answer-quality assessment, and downstream buyer and revenue evidence instead of relying on referral traffic alone.

---

## Why ordinary attribution is insufficient

AI answers can influence discovery, consideration, and vendor perception without producing a directly attributable website visit. Referral traffic is therefore useful but incomplete. Measurement should combine leading indicators of visibility and representation with lagging commercial evidence (source: [[ai-search-measurement-2026-07-15]]; practitioner framework, unverified).

## Minimum measurement panel

1. Define a stable set of high-intent prompts connected to buyer problems and priority use cases.
2. Run the same panel on a regular cadence for a bounded period, such as four weeks.
3. Record whether the company appears, which competitors appear, and whether the answer is accurate, relevant, and well supported.
4. Track changes in visibility and answer quality without treating volatile single observations as trends.
5. Connect the panel monthly to buyer attribution, sales-call evidence, win rate, influenced pipeline, and revenue where available.
6. Preserve the prompt set, model or surface, date, geography, and evaluation rules so changes remain interpretable (source: [[ai-search-measurement-2026-07-15]]; analysis).

## Measurement stack

Track AI-search influence as a sequence of distinct signals:

1. **Visibility**: Does the brand appear for a stable prompt panel?
2. **Representation**: Is the answer accurate, relevant, and appropriately framed?
3. **Direct referral**: Does a measurable click arrive from an AI surface?
4. **Mention-to-visit**: Does a brand-site visit follow an AI answer that mentioned the brand, even without a referral parameter?
5. **Business outcome**: Does the account or buyer progress, convert, or create revenue?

Profound's observational panel analysis provides a usable example of mention-to-visit measurement. It defines exposure as a brand appearing in an AI answer without appearing in the prompt, compares a seven-day post-exposure window with forecasted prior windows for the same user and brand, and reports user-clustered bootstrap intervals. The analysis is vendor-produced, non-randomized, and measures visits rather than purchases or revenue; it is therefore contributory evidence, not causal proof or a universal benchmark (source: [Profound, The AI Mention Effect](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/the-ai-mention-effect)).

Do not import the reported uplift percentages as durable benchmarks. The transferable knowledge is the separation of visibility, representation, direct referral, indirect downstream visits, and business outcomes.

## Interpretation rules

- Separate visibility from answer quality; appearing in an answer is not automatically positive.
- Treat AI-search influence as contributory evidence rather than forcing last-click certainty.
- Keep vendor benchmarks and claimed market averages outside the durable model until independently verified.
- Use a stable core panel for comparison and a smaller exploratory set for emerging prompts.
- Review the panel when buyer priorities or product positioning change.

## Application to B2B and ABM

For ABM, prompts can be organized around buying situations, account pains, evaluation criteria, implementation risks, and category comparisons. Sales-call mentions and account progression can then provide downstream evidence without pretending to establish single-touch causality (analysis based on [[account-based-marketing]] and [[ai-search-measurement-2026-07-15]]).
