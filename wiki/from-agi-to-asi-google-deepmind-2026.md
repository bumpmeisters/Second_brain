# From AGI to ASI — Google DeepMind, 2026

**Summary**: A 57-page Google DeepMind report maps four non-exclusive pathways from human-level artificial general intelligence to artificial general superintelligence, the frictions that may slow them, and the research needed to reduce uncertainty.

---

## What the source is

`From AGI to ASI` is a June 10, 2026 arXiv report by Tim Genewein and coauthors from Google DeepMind and partner institutions (source: 20260619_Deepmind_from AGI to ASI.pdf).

The report informally defines AGI as at least median human performance across a very broad set of cognitive tasks. It defines ASI as general superhuman intelligence exceeding large, coordinated groups of human experts working over extended periods (source: 20260619_Deepmind_from AGI to ASI.pdf).

## Four pathways

1. Scaling compute, models, and data: continue increasing effective compute, training scale, test-time compute, and data (source: 20260619_Deepmind_from AGI to ASI.pdf).
2. Algorithmic paradigm shifts: discover more capable or more compute-, data-, or energy-efficient architectures and learning paradigms (source: 20260619_Deepmind_from AGI to ASI.pdf).
3. Recursive improvement: use AI to accelerate AI research, experiments, hardware, and data production, potentially creating a self-reinforcing cycle (source: 20260619_Deepmind_from AGI to ASI.pdf).
4. Group-agent formation: obtain collective superintelligence from large, coordinated or self-organizing groups of AI agents (source: 20260619_Deepmind_from AGI to ASI.pdf).

The pathways are not mutually exclusive and may compound each other (source: 20260619_Deepmind_from AGI to ASI.pdf).

## Main frictions

The report discusses economic and physical resource limits, hardware and energy production, data scarcity or degradation, diminishing returns in the current paradigm, abstraction barriers, increasing research difficulty, experiment and training time, limits to recursive improvement, coordination overhead in agent groups, and governance or oversight constraints. It treats the significance of each friction as an open research question rather than a settled conclusion (source: 20260619_Deepmind_from AGI to ASI.pdf).

## Distinctive contribution

The report uses Universal AI and the AIXI framework as a theoretical endpoint for thinking about machine intelligence. It also highlights properties of digital intelligence that differ from biological intelligence: software can be copied, paused, resumed, accelerated, transferred to new hardware, backed up, and supplied with very large memory and input/output bandwidth (source: 20260619_Deepmind_from AGI to ASI.pdf).

## Caveats

- The report explicitly emphasizes forecasting uncertainty.
- Its AGI and ASI definitions are analytical choices, not universal standards.
- Pathway speed, effective-compute trends, and bottleneck severity are forecasts or research questions, not established facts.
- The PDF was text-extracted in full. Visual rendering was unavailable in the local runtime, so tables were checked through extracted text rather than page images.
