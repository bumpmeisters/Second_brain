# Marketing Peer Experiment Exchange

**Summary**: Use a small recurring practitioner exchange to discover experiments worth testing locally. Capture context, evidence, uncertainty, and constraints before deciding whether an idea transfers.

---

## Trigger

Use this practice when internal marketing learning would benefit from relevant outside experience and a stable peer group can discuss experiments its members actually ran. The exchange is useful only when participants can distinguish observation from interpretation and the receiving organization can run a bounded local test.

The source describes a recurring community of domain practitioners asking what people have tried and what results they observed. It supports contextual learning and adaptation, not generic networking or copying tactics (source: Aleasha Bahr on Sales as Market Research Unlocking Insights for Marketing Success.md; canonical title: *Ryan Hawk on Lessons from the World's Top Leaders*; transcript 31:19-34:36).

## Required inputs

- A defined marketing or GTM problem area.
- A small group with relevant operating experience and voluntary participation.
- A facilitator and a recurring but locally appropriate cadence.
- A standard experiment-report format.
- Rules for confidentiality, privacy, employer approval, NDA obligations, and competition-law boundaries.
- Local owners able to assess transferability and authorize a bounded test.
- Measures, guardrails, and a review date for any selected test.

## Procedure

1. **Define the learning territory.** State the problem or decision the exchange should illuminate. Do not convene a general promotional networking call.
2. **Set participation boundaries.** Agree what may be shared and prohibit confidential customer, employee, pricing, pipeline, product-roadmap, procurement, and competitively sensitive information.
3. **Collect experiment reports.** Ask each contributor to record the original context, intended mechanism, intervention, observed evidence, uncertainty, constraints, and what they would change.
4. **Interrogate the evidence.** Separate direct observations from interpretation. Note selection effects, missing baselines, inconsistent definitions, and plausible alternative explanations.
5. **Assess transferability.** Compare audience, market, offer, team capability, channel, data, timing, and governance conditions with the receiving organization.
6. **Choose a disposition.** Mark the idea `test`, `hold`, or `reject`. A `test` decision must define the smallest useful local experiment, owner, measure, guardrails, stop conditions, and review date.
7. **Run the local test.** Treat the peer report as a hypothesis source, not as proof. Record deviations and local conditions.
8. **Close the loop.** Return the result, uncertainty, and revised transfer conditions to the next peer exchange without exposing restricted information.

## Inspectable output

Produce a versioned peer experiment register containing:

- learning territory and exchange date;
- contributor and shareability classification;
- original context, intervention, observations, and uncertainty;
- transferability assessment and material differences;
- `test`, `hold`, or `reject` decision with rationale;
- for tests: scope, owner, measures, guardrails, stop conditions, and review date;
- local result and what may safely be returned to the group.

## Reuse boundaries

- Peer reports are self-selected practitioner evidence. They do not establish causality, prevalence, benchmarks, or likely local performance.
- Do not rank employees or organizations by participation, confidence, visibility, or reported success.
- Do not solicit or share confidential, personal, contractually restricted, or competitively sensitive information.
- Obtain necessary employer, legal, privacy, NDA, procurement, and competition-law review. Avoid discussion of future prices, market allocation, customers, bids, compensation, or other restricted competitive topics.
- Keep commercial solicitation separate. The exchange must not become concealed lead generation or a condition for access to peers.
- The monthly call and annual gathering described in the source are examples, not required cadences.
