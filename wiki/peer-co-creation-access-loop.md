# Peer Co-Creation Access Loop

**Summary**: Co-create genuinely useful peer material, disclose every participant's role, and request distribution or introductions only with explicit authorization. Relationship access is a possible next step, not a hidden entitlement created by the asset.

---

## Trigger

Use this workflow when direct channels do not credibly reach an important peer audience or buying-group context, while a relevant customer, practitioner, expert, partner, association, publisher, or community member is willing to contribute to useful content.

The source sharpens a broader co-creation idea into an access sequence: produce material with a credible contributor, identify colleagues or peers who may benefit, and ask the contributor to authorize sharing or a joint introduction (source: Capturing demand signals.md; canonical title: *Demand Generation in Action: Real-World Examples*; transcript 44:30-55:11 and 59:39-1:03:56).

A second technical-audience case corroborates this mechanism: specialist interviews and narrow question-led formats were converted into useful content and follow-up conversations, while communities, experts, and power users acted as possible indirect access paths. It does not validate the case's deal, pipeline, webinar, reach, or “technical buyers” generalizations (source: Supercharge Your Go-To-Market Strategy with Full-Funnel Go-To-Market Template.md; canonical title: *Case Study: Converting Skeptical IT Buyers with ABM*; transcript 34:21-46:44 and 1:06:45-1:12:39; analysis: P21-C02).

## Required inputs

- A specific peer problem, intended audience, and buying-group or community context.
- A relevant, willing co-creator selected for expertise and fit rather than reach alone.
- A truthful description of the contributor's role and relationship to the organization.
- Written agreement on purpose, editorial control, recording, claims, usage rights, attribution, edits, withdrawal, and allowed channels.
- A claims and evidence review for customer or performance statements.
- A named relationship owner and an approved list of possible distribution paths.
- Privacy, confidentiality, brand, legal, accessibility, and platform requirements.
- Response signals and a review rule that does not equate engagement with revenue.

## Procedure

1. **Define audience value.** State the practical question the asset will answer and why the intended peer audience should care independently of the seller.
2. **Qualify the co-creator.** Confirm expertise, relevance, willingness, conflicts, and the truthful public relationship. Do not select someone merely because their network is visible.
3. **Agree the participation contract.** Record consent, role disclosure, editorial review, rights, claims boundaries, approved channels, and withdrawal or correction procedures.
4. **Create and verify the asset.** Produce the interview, story, discussion, or other format; verify factual and customer claims; obtain final approval before publication.
5. **Map possible peer routes together.** Ask which colleagues, buying-group members, associations, communities, publishers, or experts would genuinely benefit. A public connection graph is only a discovery clue.
6. **Authorize each action.** For every share or introduction, record whether the contributor will share directly, permits the organization to reference them, or agrees to make a joint introduction. Silence is not consent.
7. **Distribute transparently.** State the contributor's real role and why the material is relevant. Never phrase cold outreach as a referral or imply customer or endorsement status.
8. **Observe separately.** Log delivery, response quality, relationship development, content feedback, and agreed next steps as distinct signals.
9. **Review the path.** Choose `continue`, `adapt`, or `stop` for the asset and each access route. Refresh permissions and claims before later reuse.

## Inspectable output

Produce:

- the approved co-created asset and stable source location;
- contributor role, consent, rights, claims, and approval record;
- audience and buying-group context;
- authorized distribution and introduction map with owners;
- delivery and response log;
- corrections, withdrawals, or permission changes;
- a dated `continue`, `adapt`, or `stop` decision.

## Reuse boundaries

- Co-creation does not make a participant a customer, partner, endorser, referrer, or sales prospect. Describe the relationship truthfully.
- Do not exploit ambiguity if an audience incorrectly assumes customer or endorsement status. Correct material misunderstandings.
- Interviews, podcasts, and research conversations must not be concealed sales calls.
- A public professional network does not authorize contact, scraping, referral language, or disclosure of private relationships.
- Customer stories and performance claims require explicit consent, evidence, confidentiality review, and applicable legal or brand approval.
- Responses, reach, engagement, introductions, meetings, and “dark social” reports do not prove purchase intent, pipeline influence, or revenue attribution.
- The source is vendor-hosted practitioner material. Its opportunity, inbound, reach, touchpoint, survey, and pipeline narratives remain excluded.
