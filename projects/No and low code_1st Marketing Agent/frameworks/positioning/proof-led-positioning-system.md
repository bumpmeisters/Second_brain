# Proof-Led Positioning System

## Framework Job

Connect audience context, market alternatives, promise, differentiation, proof, and claim constraints into a defensible positioning system.

## Classification And Fidelity

Original project composite combining value proposition, proof pillars, message house, and claim governance.

Project adaptation: the composite architecture and its ContextOps handoff are authored by this project.

## Use When

- Segmentation and audience context exist and external messaging needs a strategic foundation.

## Do Not Use When

- Claims are ungoverned or audience/alternatives are still generic.

## Minimum Required Inputs

- Market, buying-context, segmentation, audience, claim, and framework-fit packets.

## Core Model

Define category, audience, buying moment, tension, promise, differentiator, proof pillars, caveats, and safe message hierarchy.

## Question Engine

### Minimum Viable Questions

- For whom is this positioning relevant?
- In what buying situation?
- What alternative would they otherwise choose?
- What tension matters?
- What promise can the offer credibly make?
- What capability or difference enables it?
- What proof supports each claim?
- What must not be said?

### Deepening Questions

- Is the category clear to buyers?
- Which proof addresses the strongest objection?
- What is differentiated versus merely positive?

### Counter-Questions

- Does the promise outrun the proof?
- Would a competitor credibly say the same?
- Are we positioning for an unvalidated segment?

### Optional Modules

- Message house.
- Value proposition.
- Proof pillars.
- Safe messaging variants.

## Branching And Stop Rules

If evidence is weak, produce an internal hypothesis and proof gap, not external-ready language.

## Evidence Standard

Every factual or comparative element needs a source or hypothesis label. Claim governance controls external use.

## Business-Model Adaptations

- B2B: include account/use case, committee proof, ROI/risk, and sales evidence.
- B2C/D2C: include occasion, emotion, reviews, product experience, and purchase friction.
- B2B2C: position separately for partner and end customer.

## Output Contract

Positioning hypothesis, category/audience, value proposition, message house, proof pillars, caveats, do-not-say guidance, and gaps.

## Failure Modes

Clever copy before strategy, generic benefit stacks, mixed aspiration/proof, and unsupported superiority.

## ContextOps Handoff

Feeds content, SEO/GEO, campaigns, landing pages, lifecycle, and sales enablement.

## Evaluation Notes

Applied in MirrorSoft message-house and approved-claim work.

## Evolution Triggers

Revise when positioning fails audience resonance, proof sufficiency, or downstream usability.
