# Nested B2B Segmentation

## Framework Job

Move from broad account structure toward operating, purchasing, situational, and participant-level differences that change B2B buying behavior.

## Classification And Fidelity

Composite of Wind/Cardozo macro-micro segmentation and Bonoma/Shapiro's nested B2B approach. Project additions define evidence and stopping rules.

Project adaptation: combine the two traditions into one operational ContextOps sequence and stop nesting when no decision changes.

## Use When

- Account, application, procurement, buying situation, and participant differences matter.

## Do Not Use When

- The purchase is individual consumer demand or firmographics already explain enough.

## Minimum Required Inputs

- Account universe, use cases, operating variables, purchasing approach, situations, and role evidence.

## Core Model

Move inward only as needed: demographics/firmographics, operating variables, purchasing approach, situational factors, and buyer characteristics.

## Question Engine

### Minimum Viable Questions

- Which industries, sizes, regions, or ownership forms differ materially?
- Which technologies, capabilities, and usage patterns matter?
- How is purchasing organized?
- Which criteria and supplier relationships shape buying?
- What urgency, application, order size, or risk changes the decision?
- Which buying roles or attitudes differ?
- At which nest do segments become strategically distinct?
- Can the organization reach and serve them differently?

### Deepening Questions

- Which outer variable predicts an inner difference?
- Which use case overrides firmographics?
- What procurement rule changes access?

### Counter-Questions

- Are we adding nests without changing decisions?
- Is job title being mistaken for buying role?
- Would account-level segmentation hide user-level differences?

### Optional Modules

- Partner/channel.
- Regulated procurement.
- Account potential and readiness.

## Branching And Stop Rules

Stop nesting when additional variables do not change offer, proof, route, or resource allocation.

## Evidence Standard

Use CRM, opportunities, interviews, win/loss, implementation, account, procurement, and usage evidence.

## Business-Model Adaptations

- Enterprise: include governance and security.
- SMB: roles may collapse.
- B2B2C: add separate end-customer layer.
- Partner-led: add partner capability and incentives.

## Output Contract

Nest tree, candidate account segments, role distinctions, evidence, reachability, and stopping rationale.

## Failure Modes

Firmographic-only ICP, excessive nesting, tiny cells, assumed role behavior, and no activation path.

## ContextOps Handoff

Feeds audience understanding, account tiering, positioning, and GTM.

## Evaluation Notes

Supported by the segmentation strategy reference; application evidence remains limited.

## Evolution Triggers

Revise when repeated cases require clearer use-case or partner layers.

## Sources And Provenance

Primary source:

- Bonoma and Shapiro, "How to Segment Industrial Markets": https://hbr.org/1984/05/how-to-segment-industrial-markets

Source tradition: Wind and Cardozo macro-micro industrial segmentation.
