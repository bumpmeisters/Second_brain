# Source Taxonomy

Classify every source before using it.

| Source type | Examples | Trust use | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Official company source | Website, datasheet, brochure, product page, annual report. | Evidence for what the company says. | Not independent proof. |
| User-provided company document | Deck, notes, sales material, internal brief. | High relevance. | Check date, scope, and bias. |
| Raw primary evidence | Contracts, product labels, IFU, certificates, datasets, transcripts. | Strong source for specific facts. | Preserve unchanged. |
| Third-party source | Media, analyst report, directory, customer review. | Useful external perspective. | Verify recency and reliability. |
| Competitor source | Competitor website, price page, deck. | Useful for competitor positioning/claims. | Do not treat as neutral. |
| Regulator/standard source | Official agency, standard body, public registry. | Strong for regulatory facts. | Must be current and jurisdiction-specific. |
| AI-generated research | Deep research, model summary, synthetic report. | Lead source only. | Mark unverified unless checked. |
| Generated output | Agent-created dossier, matrix, synthesis. | Secondary analysis. | Cite underlying sources for factual claims. |

## Status Labels

- `new`: present but not summarized.
- `summarized`: source summary exists.
- `linked`: connected to relevant wiki pages.
- `verified`: important claims checked against primary sources.
- `needs verification`: useful but not yet reliable enough.
- `archived`: saved but not analytically important.
