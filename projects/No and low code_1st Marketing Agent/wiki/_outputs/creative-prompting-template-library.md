# Creative Prompting Template Library

**Summary**: Historical prompt cards retained for provenance. New content runs use the Content Operating System's Direction and Brief frameworks and templates.

---

Do not use this library as the canonical workflow for a new run. Route complete upstream context to `projects/content-operating-system/`, which preserves the underlying human-first principles through Strategic Creative Direction and Content Execution.

## 2. Delayed-AI Divergence

```text
I have completed a human first pass and upstream context check.

[PASTE CONTEXTOPS CREATIVE INPUT CARD]

Act only as a divergent-thinking partner. Do not select, rank, polish, or
write final copy.

Generate 24 concept directions in 6 clearly different semantic territories:
1. customer behavior;
2. operational reality;
3. culture or ritual;
4. science or nature;
5. entertainment or play;
6. contrarian industry belief.

Create four ideas per territory. Avoid synonyms and cosmetic variations.
For each idea provide:
- concept in one sentence;
- creative mechanism;
- which approved proof or audience tension it uses;
- why it differs from category convention;
- what would need verification before external use.
```

## 3. SCAMPER Campaign Transformer

```text
Transform this existing marketing idea using SCAMPER:
[IDEA]

Audience:
Campaign role:
Approved claims:
Blocked claims:
Objective:
Constraints:

Produce two materially different options for each:
- Substitute
- Combine
- Adapt
- Modify or magnify/minify
- Put to another use
- Eliminate
- Reverse

Change the experience, mechanism, sequence, participant, channel, value
exchange, or proof structure. Do not merely rewrite the wording.
```

## 4. Generic Parts / Functional-Fixation Breaker

```text
Format or convention to rethink: [e.g. webinar, lead magnet, product page]

Step 1: Describe it using only generic properties, components, relationships,
and constraints. Do not mention its conventional purpose or name.
Step 2: Identify other experiences or systems that share those properties.
Step 3: Transfer one mechanism from each distant system back to the marketing
challenge.
Step 4: Produce 10 concepts that do not depend on the original format.

Respect these claim and proof boundaries:
[APPROVED / BLOCKED CLAIMS]
```

## 5. First-Principles Assumption Demolition

```text
Marketing challenge:
Audience context:
Positioning:
Proof available:
Constraints:

List 15 assumptions behind the usual solution.
Classify each as:
- legal or physical requirement;
- evidence-backed audience requirement;
- organizational constraint;
- category convention;
- untested belief.

Remove every category convention and untested belief.
Rebuild five solutions using only the remaining requirements.
For each, state what familiar marketing behavior it refuses.
```

## 6. Forced Collision

```text
Marketing challenge:
Collision domain:
Audience tension:
Approved proof:
Blocked claims:

Map the collision domain's actors, rituals, incentives, sensory cues,
sequence, status symbols, failure modes, and feedback loops.

Transfer at least four mechanisms, not surface vocabulary, to the marketing
challenge. Generate eight concepts and explain each mapping.

Reject decorative metaphors that do not change the idea.
```

Suggested domains: air-traffic control, escape rooms, weather systems, open-source communities, museums, matchmaking, kitchens, detective fiction, sports leagues, urban planning.

## 7. Stakeholder Partitioning

Run separately for each perspective without sharing previous outputs.

```text
You are [specific stakeholder with distinct experience].

From this perspective, generate 10 ideas for:
[BRIEF]

Use the stakeholder's real priorities, vocabulary, constraints, anxieties,
and sources of pride. Do not imitate a caricature. Do not evaluate ideas.

Label any customer belief or objection that is a hypothesis rather than
source-backed evidence.
```

Useful perspectives: skeptical procurement manager, frontline operator, new employee, lost customer, compliance lead, sales engineer, CFO, support agent, implementation partner, industry outsider, returning customer, gift buyer.

## 8. Counterfactual Worlds

```text
Create six alternative worlds for this marketing challenge:
[CHALLENGE]

- the budget is 100 times smaller;
- the budget is 100 times larger;
- the audience cannot see;
- the audience cannot read;
- competitors can copy everything within 24 hours;
- the product cannot be mentioned.

Generate five ideas per world. Then extract mechanisms that remain useful in
the real world while preserving approved claims and evidence boundaries.
```

## 9. Category-Cliche Escape

```text
For this category:
[CATEGORY]

List common claims, visual codes, story arcs, proof points, emotional tones,
and calls to action.

Mark necessary category signals versus lazy conventions.
Create 12 concepts that preserve comprehension while breaking at least two
lazy conventions.

For each, state:
- which convention it breaks;
- which approved proof it can use;
- what the audience may misunderstand;
- what must be tested.
```

## 10. Hook Generator

```text
Core idea:
Audience:
Proof available:
Blocked claims:

Write three hooks for each structure:
1. X without Y
2. X while also Y
3. Old way / new way
4. How I/we achieved X
5. The big mistake
6. Transformation from X to Y

Reject hooks that overclaim, manufacture conflict, or cannot be supported.
Label the curiosity gap, promised value, and evidence requirement of each.
```

## 11. Narrative-Framework Selector

```text
Concept:
Audience state now:
Desired belief, feeling, or action:
Evidence:
Channel and length:
Claim constraints:

Compare PAS, Before-After-Bridge, SCQA, AIDA/ERA, STAR, and a
Challenger-style reframe.

For each, show a five-line outline and its main risk.
Recommend one based on the communication job.
Do not draft final external copy until I approve the route.
```

## 12. Strategic Convergence Scorecard

```text
Evaluate the ideas below without rewriting them:
[IDEAS]

Score each 1-5 on:
- originality relative to category;
- audience truth;
- relevance to campaign role;
- fit with positioning;
- approved evidence/provability;
- emotional power;
- feasibility;
- extensibility across channels.

Apply fatal flags separately:
- unsupported factual premise;
- blocked claim or risky implication;
- contradicts brand or strategy;
- depends on a stereotype;
- likely competitor clone;
- unclear audience value.

Return:
1. score table;
2. top three for different reasons;
3. promising fragments to combine;
4. ideas to kill and why;
5. questions the human must decide.

Do not choose a final winner.
```

## 13. Six-Perspective Pre-Mortem

```text
Review this concept before production:
[CONCEPT]

White hat: What facts, claims, and assumptions need evidence?
Red hat: What is the audience's immediate emotional reaction?
Black hat: How could this fail, offend, confuse, or become unbelievable?
Yellow hat: What is genuinely strong and worth protecting?
Green hat: What improvements preserve the core insight?
Blue hat: What decision, owner, test, and next step are required?

Add:
- skeptical-customer review;
- category-cliche review;
- claim-governance review.

Keep criticism and revision separate.
```

## 14. Testable Creative Variants

```text
Selected concept:
Audience:
Channel:
Objective:
Approved proof:

Create three variants that test different hypotheses:
- A changes the tension or problem frame.
- B changes the emotional promise.
- C changes the proof or participation mechanism.

For each specify:
- hypothesis;
- changed variable;
- elements held constant;
- success metric;
- disconfirming result;
- learning to preserve.
```

## Recommended Short Workflow

1. ContextOps Creative Input Card.
2. One divergence or collision card.
3. Strategic Convergence Scorecard.
4. Narrative selector or hook generator.
5. Six-perspective pre-mortem.
6. Testable creative variants.

## Boundary Reminder

If the input card cannot be completed because company context, audience context, positioning, or claim boundaries are missing, route upstream before ideation.
