## Selected audience situation

- **Reader**: an ambitious, non-technical marketing practitioner who actively uses, evaluates, or explains AI at work.
- **Trigger**: the reader encounters confident claims that a model is either `only autocomplete`, genuinely thinking, or close to consciousness.
- **Job**: form and communicate a defensible interpretation without requiring mechanistic expertise or borrowing certainty from a headline.
- **Current barrier hypothesis**: the public debate collapses observed mechanism, causal evidence, analogy, interpretation, and speculation into one claim.
- **Desired progress**: use a small evidence ladder to distinguish what was observed, what intervention changed, what interpretation is plausible, and what conclusion does not follow.
- **Proof requirement**: concrete J-Space interventions, first-party attribution, model and method limits, and an explicit consciousness boundary.

## What this application can and cannot show

The application can show which profile fields constrain a J-Space Direction and whether the topic can produce a segment-relevant decision job. It cannot show that ambitious marketing practitioners actually want this topic, use the assumed language, or find the resulting content useful. Those require reader or performance evidence.

## Produces

- **Primary audience**: one ambitious, non-technical marketing practitioner in an active AI-claim evaluation situation.
- **Communication job**: turn J-Space into a worked example of disciplined reasoning about AI claims.
- **Desired shift**: from binary labels to an evidence ladder separating observation, intervention, interpretation, and excluded conclusion.
- **Topic boundary**: preserve all approved v1 evidence and consciousness limits; do not create a marketing-specific scientific claim.
- **Personal Take basis**: the exact v1 wording is approved for Direction v1. Reuse in v2 requires explicit review because the Direction and audience context change.
- **Blocked claims**: current AI is conscious; J-Space explains all reasoning; the three examples form a progression to consciousness; marketers generally care about J-Space; this framing improves professional decisions.
