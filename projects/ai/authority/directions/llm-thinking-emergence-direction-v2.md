# Strategic Creative Direction: J-Space als Test für besseres Urteilen über AI

## Decision job

Decide a channel-neutral argument that uses J-Space as a worked example for how ambitious, non-technical marketing practitioners can evaluate AI claims without dismissing observed mechanisms or turning functional evidence into claims about consciousness.

## Audience and desired change

- **Audience profile and exact version**: `personal-content-audience-v0-1`
- **Primary audience**: ambitious marketing practitioners who actively use, evaluate, or explain AI but are not interpretability specialists.
- **Situation or trigger**: they encounter confident claims that a model is `only autocomplete`, is genuinely thinking, or may be close to consciousness.
- **Question, tension, or job**: What was actually observed, what was causally tested, and which larger interpretation is still only a possibility?
- **Current belief or default behavior**: Working hypothesis: public AI language encourages a binary choice between reductive dismissal and anthropomorphic overclaiming.
- **Desired belief, decision, or action**: Treat J-Space as real functional evidence within a bounded study, distinguish it from evidence of subjective experience, and apply the same evidence ladder to future AI claims.
- **Communication job**: explain one difficult finding, model disciplined uncertainty, and leave the reader with a reusable diagnostic.

## Direction

- **One-sentence thesis**: J-Space beweist nicht, dass Claude bewusst ist; der Fund zeigt vielmehr, wie wir AI-Behauptungen besser beurteilen können: ernst nehmen, was beobachtet und kausal getestet wurde, und zugleich klar benennen, welche größere Schlussfolgerung daraus nicht folgt.
- **Strategic angle**: Treat the J-Space research as an evidence-reading case, not as a consciousness story with a professional-use paragraph attached. Explain the mechanism, intervention, and limits; compare it with two earlier learned-feature examples; then extract a reusable claim-evaluation rule.
- **Rolf's original contribution**: A disciplined middle position already approved in Direction v1, now connected to a professional judgment practice: neither `only autocomplete` nor consciousness language should replace inspection of the actual evidence.
- **Why this matters now**: Marketing practitioners increasingly evaluate, recommend, and explain AI while much public language collapses model behavior, mechanism, analogy, and philosophical interpretation. J-Space makes those layers unusually visible.
- **Narrative logic**:
  1. Begin with the concrete puzzle: Claude can internally represent a concept that appears neither in the prompt nor in the answer.
  2. Explain J-Space in ordinary language and show why swaps and ablations matter: intervention changes selected reporting and reasoning behavior.
  3. Separate four layers: observation, causal intervention, functional interpretation, and consciousness speculation.
  4. Use Sentiment Neuron and Golden Gate Claude to show that learned internal properties differ in kind and do not form a proven ladder toward consciousness.
  5. Apply the evidence ladder to the two simplistic reactions: `only autocomplete` explains too little; human-like mind claims infer too much.
  6. Introduce Rolf's substrate-open Personal Take as a qualified philosophical possibility, not a research result.
  7. End with four questions the reader can reuse when the next AI claim appears.
- **Practical implication or decision rule**: Ask: What exactly was observed? Did an intervention change behavior? To which models, tasks, and methods does the result apply? Which attractive conclusion does the evidence still not establish?
- **Fit conditions**: AI-interested marketing practitioners who need to evaluate or explain claims and want conceptual depth without a technical-paper walkthrough.
- **Non-fit conditions or limitations**: Not a campaign tactic, deployment guide, AI governance framework, general explanation of LLM cognition, or substitute for specialist review of the paper.
- **Strongest reasonable counterargument**: J-Space may be intellectually interesting but professionally irrelevant to most marketers. Resolution: do not manufacture an operational use case; the piece earns relevance only for readers who already evaluate or communicate AI claims. Otherwise it remains general AI thought leadership, and audience fit is unproven.
