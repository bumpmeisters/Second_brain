# Strategic Creative Direction: Denk nicht an die Brücke

## Audience and desired change

- **Audience**: ambitious, non-technical practitioners who are curious about AI internals.
- **Situation**: encountering claims that language models have hidden thoughts or an internal workspace.
- **Current model**: the model receives text and produces text; everything important is visible in the output.
- **Desired model**: some internal representations can be present, controlled imperfectly, and used without appearing in the output.
- **Communication job**: create an accurate, memorable discovery story.

## Direction

- **Thesis**: The remarkable part of J-Space is not that Claude can be primed with a concept, but that researchers can observe the model trying—and partly failing—to keep that concept out of a small, verbally accessible internal workspace.
- **Angle**: use a current discovery narrative. Begin inside the bridge experiment, establish that Anthropic published the J-Space research on 6 July 2026, connect it to the white-bear family of human thought-suppression effects, then show what the J-lens adds: direct internal observation and causal intervention.
- **User-confirmed contribution**: Rolf's white-elephant analogy, qualified into priming versus suppression.
- **Narrative**: Three-Act Discovery Narrative with an In Medias Res opening and SCQA logic: current experiment and discovery -> mechanism and causal evidence -> meaning and boundary.
- **Why now**: Anthropic published the primary J-Space work on 6 July 2026; the article should present it as a current research development, not timeless background knowledge.
- **Memorable conclusion**: we can begin to distinguish what a model says, what is internally active, and what it uses to reason.
- **Counterargument**: words surfaced by a lens may tempt anthropomorphic interpretation. Resolve by distinguishing measured activation, researcher interpretation, and subjective experience.

## Personal Take Checkpoint

- **Outcome**: qualified.
- **Origin**: user-supplied.
- **Type**: interpretation and analogy.
- **Disclosure**: L1; no personal experience asserted.
- **Approved meaning**: the children's-game intuition is useful, but the closest scientific analogy is thought suppression rather than color-answer priming.
- **Critical check**: the analogy explains a structural resemblance and must not imply identical human and model mechanisms.

## Invariants

- Stay close to J-Space.
- Do not force a marketing, prompting, or business application.
- Explain both priming and suppression.
- Preserve the distinction between internal activation and subjective experience.
- Use the bridge experiment as the main story and other J-Space findings only to establish breadth.
