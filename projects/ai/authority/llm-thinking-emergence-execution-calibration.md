# AI J-Space Execution Calibration

## Outcome

Five correction waves changed the article at different levels:

1. The first execution was rejected even though the upstream brief and evidence were accepted.
2. The methodological storyline was accepted, but v0-2 sounded too dry.
3. The more personal v0-3 still asked the reader to retain too much.
4. The lighter v0-4 became retellable, but independent criticism exposed the causal intermediate as the stronger discovery.
5. The more accurate v0-5 still carried too many explanatory goals. Rolf confirmed that the conversation contained enough context; the problem was accumulated scope rather than missing input.

Direction v6 therefore imposes a hard budget: one event, one explanation, one meaning, and one boundary. Article v0-6 was written from a blank page under that budget.

## Human decisions captured

1. Open with the Tchaikovsky-to-Beethoven intervention.
2. Do not make prompting a core or secondary payoff.
3. Do not use “Prompt Engineering to Cognitive Engineering.”
4. Do not claim that interpretability began with J-Space or that earlier research only saw input and output.
5. Do not present Sentiment Neuron, Golden Gate Claude, and J-Space as a ladder toward consciousness.
6. Avoid dry research-summary voice.
7. Mix warm discovery with direct skeptical interruption.
8. Avoid formulaic AI vocabulary, decorative punctuation, balanced contrast templates, and repetitive punchlines.
9. Personal tone alone is insufficient when the reader still has to carry too many concepts.
10. Optimize for one story and one retellable sentence.
11. End at a bounded meaning of emergent properties; exclude the consciousness Personal Take.
12. Treat external AI review as criticism to verify, not as instructions to copy.
13. Make the causally used intermediate representation, rather than the absent name, the Aha moment.
14. Release earlier structural constraints where they weaken that goal.
15. The available conversation is sufficient. More context or examples are not the answer to excessive explanatory scope.
16. Use a hard narrative budget: one event, one explanation, one meaning, and one boundary.
17. Remove the France-to-China example, next-token discussion, chess analogy, extended workspace explanation, prompting, marketing, and governance.
18. A restrained first-person sentence is allowed when it creates warmth without making the article about the author.

## Current message contract

- **Event**: Tchaikovsky is replaced with Beethoven inside Claude and Russian becomes German.
- **Explanation**: The changed intermediate is used in later processing, so the answer changes with it.
- **Meaning**: Training produced a useful internal solution that nobody programmed feature by feature.
- **Boundary**: This does not establish human understanding or consciousness.
