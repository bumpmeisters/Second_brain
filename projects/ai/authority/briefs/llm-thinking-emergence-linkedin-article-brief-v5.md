# Content Brief: Tschaikowski wird zu Beethoven

## Execution purpose

Create a light German LinkedIn article that a non-technical reader can understand and retell after one reading. The article shares one discovery rather than teaching the paper.

## Controlling direction

- **Creative Direction**: projects/ai/authority/directions/llm-thinking-emergence-direction-v6.md.
- **Approved version**: direction-ai-llm-thinking-emergence-01-v6, version 6.0.0.
- **Core takeaway**: Beim Training können in KI-Modellen nützliche interne Lösungen entstehen, die niemand einzeln programmiert hat.
- **Retellable sentence**: Forschende tauschen in Claude Tschaikowski gegen Beethoven aus. Das Modell arbeitet mit dem neuen Zwischeninhalt weiter, und aus russisch wird deutsch.

## Execution contract

- **Voice**: curious, direct, conversational, and precise enough to trust.
- **Reader relationship**: tell the reader what surprised Rolf without lecturing.
- **Opening**: `Claude antwortet: deutsch.`
- **Narrative budget**:
  1. Establish why German is the wrong answer.
  2. Reveal the Tchaikovsky-to-Beethoven intervention.
  3. Explain why the changed downstream answer makes the intermediate functionally relevant.
  4. Connect this learned internal solution to a bounded meaning of emergence.
  5. State once that this does not establish human understanding or consciousness.
- **Length**: approximately 350 to 450 German words excluding the source.
- **Structure**: short paragraphs; no explanatory list in the publication copy; no more than one body heading if needed.
- **Excluded**: France-to-China, Sentiment Neuron, Golden Gate Claude, next-token explanation, chess analogy, detailed J-Space method, prompting, marketing application, governance, interpretability history, and consciousness speculation.
- **Accuracy boundary**: Describe one source-reported intervention in selected models and tasks. Do not imply readable thought, universal behavior, human understanding, magic, or consciousness.
- **Tone exclusions**: academic summary voice, artificial enthusiasm, generic AI phrases, em dash, en dash, semicolon, repeated contrast formulas, and generic CTA.
