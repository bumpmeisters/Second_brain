# LinkedIn Channel Profile

**Channel job**: Earn attention from relevant professional peers, make one useful idea easy to grasp, and invite substantive recognition or discussion.

## Calibration status

`partially-calibrated`: two public LinkedIn posts by Rolf were reviewed on 2026-07-27. They support initial structural and tonal observations but are too small a corpus to establish stable preferences for length, language, emoji, hashtag use, warmth, or performance.

Observed strengths to preserve:

- open with a clear contrast or system shift;
- use one memorable metaphor to make an abstract operating change visible;
- translate a technology trend into consequences for marketers, roles, workflows, or decisions;
- build a progression from current state to next operating model.

Observed risks to correct:

- source-summary voice without enough Rolf-specific interpretation;
- absolute predictions and unsupported product, privacy, or market claims;
- inflated intensifiers that weaken credibility.

## Best-fit content

- a point of view grounded in a real operating tension;
- a practical distinction or decision rule;
- a compact framework explanation;
- a case interpretation with evidence boundaries;
- a personal learning that changes professional practice;
- a useful derivative of a deeper website asset.

## Transformation rules

- Use one central idea and one primary audience.
- Make the opening carry the tension or observation without clickbait.
- Keep enough context for the post to stand alone.
- Use short, scan-friendly paragraphs without mechanically isolating every sentence.
- Attribute externally sourced facts and label analysis as analysis.
- When a post starts from an external podcast, article, or tool, add a non-transferable Rolf layer: a decision rule, practical implication, experience, framework, or reasoned disagreement.
- Distinguish current observation, inference, scenario, and forecast.
- Use one controlling metaphor and test its limits instead of stacking several dramatic comparisons.
- Prefer a practical implication over a generic summary.
- Use links, hashtags, tagging, and calls to action only when they serve the reader or provenance.
- If the post derives from a website asset, adapt the argument for conversation; do not merely truncate the article.

## Useful structures

```text
Recognizable tension
-> reframe
-> reasoning or evidence
-> operating consequence
-> decision rule or substantive question
```

```text
Observed pattern
-> what it does not prove
-> what can still be learned
-> where it applies
```

## Tone defaults

- direct;
- useful;
- professionally conversational;
- confident about reasoning, explicit about uncertainty.

Avoid inflated certainty, manufactured contrarianism, engagement bait, and platform clichés.

## Channel quality gate

- Can the main idea be stated in one sentence?
- Does the opening accurately represent the argument?
- Is there enough evidence or reasoning to earn the conclusion?
- Is the post useful without requiring a click?
- Is the response invitation substantive rather than algorithmic?
- Could the post have been written by any competent AI commentator? If yes, add a Rolf-specific judgment, framework, experience, or decision rule before polishing.
- Are forecasts and product claims dated, bounded, and supported strongly enough for their wording?
- Is the argument non-generic without confusing distinctiveness with Rolf's voice?
- Does Rolf assign `recognisably-mine` to the exact expression?
- If sharing is an explicit job, what specific value does the reader gain by sharing it?
- Does the piece pass the shared craft and channel-expression review?

## Publication boundary

Drafting and approval do not authorize posting. Record the exact approved variant in `projects/content-operating-system/publishing/publication-register.md` and publish only after direct instruction.
