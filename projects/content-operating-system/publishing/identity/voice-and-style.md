# Voice and Style

**Summary**: A testable voice hypothesis and practical writing rules. Two public LinkedIn examples now provide an initial evidence base; the profile remains provisional until a broader, cross-channel corpus exists.

---

## Voice hypothesis

### 1. Evidence-led, not falsely neutral

Make evidence and uncertainty visible, then offer a clear interpretation.

- Do: distinguish what a source reports from what Rolf concludes.
- Do not: hide behind endless qualification or present opinion as established fact.

### 2. A systems interpreter, operationally useful

Reframe tools and trends as changes to the wider operating model, then connect that system-level view to decisions, behaviors, and execution.

- Do: explain both why a model matters and what changes in practice; use a vivid analogy when it genuinely compresses a complex system shift.
- Do not: stop at abstractions or reduce every issue to a checklist.

### 3. Direct, not theatrical

State the tension and point of view plainly.

- Do: challenge weak conventions with reasons and alternatives.
- Do not: manufacture outrage, certainty, or a contrarian hook unsupported by the argument.

### 4. Precise, not academic

Use the necessary distinction in clear language.

- Do: define specialist terms and prefer concrete verbs.
- Do not: use jargon as a substitute for reasoning or make the reader decode long nominal phrases.

### 5. Constructive, not performatively critical

Critique should improve a decision.

- Do: name the failure mode, its conditions, and a more useful approach.
- Do not: attack people, dismiss practitioners, or critique without an actionable implication.

### 6. Personal with purpose, not self-centred

Use first-person experience or judgment when it strengthens trust and meaning.

- Do: explain what changed Rolf's mind, what he has observed, or where his confidence is limited.
- Do not: add personal detail only to imitate platform conventions.

### 7. Forward-looking, not prediction-drunk

Rolf's public writing shows a natural interest in emerging systems and their likely operating consequences.

- Do: distinguish an observed signal from an interpretation, scenario, forecast, and recommendation.
- Do: make the practical consequence of a forecast explicit.
- Do not: turn possibility into inevitability or use intensity as a substitute for evidence.

## Personal Take discipline

A Personal Take is an optional, content-specific expression of judgment, interpretation, practice, changed thinking, uncertainty, or confirmed experience. It is not a requirement for authentic writing and does not automatically change this Voice and Style profile.

- Prefer a useful decision, distinction, or honest uncertainty over performative personality.
- Let selection, weighting, contrasts, and conclusions carry the point of view. Do not add Ich, mich, or similar first-person markers merely to announce an opinion; use first person only when confirmed experience or source separation genuinely requires it.
- Keep personal experience, professional practice, opinion, interpretation, and factual claim visibly distinct.
- Never convert a plausible inference about Rolf into a first-person experience.
- Use concrete experience only when Rolf has confirmed both the experience and its publication boundary.
- Do not manufacture contrarianism, vulnerability, emotion, or autobiographical detail.
- Preserve the approved take's meaning across channel variants; channel adaptation may change expression only.
- Recalibrate the shared voice only after repeated approved examples and an explicit review decision.

The operating interaction, disclosure levels, critical check, and allowed outcomes are defined in `projects/content-operating-system/templates/creative-direction.md` and `projects/content-operating-system/AGENTS.md`.

## Style defaults

- Lead with the central tension, decision, or observation.
- Develop one main idea per short-form piece.
- Prefer short and medium sentences; use a longer sentence when the relationship genuinely needs it.
- Use short paragraphs for scanning, but avoid turning every sentence into its own paragraph by habit.
- Prefer concrete nouns and verbs over inflated business language.
- Explain frameworks through decisions, contrasts, cases, or consequences.
- Use a strong metaphor only when the following sentences unpack its mechanism, limit, or operating consequence.
- When curating someone else's idea, move beyond summary by adding Rolf's own interpretation, decision rule, practical implication, or reasoned disagreement.
- Separate current observation from future scenario; use confidence proportionate to the available evidence.
- Make claims proportionate to their evidence.
- Use headings and lists only when they improve comprehension.
- End with an implication, decision rule, question, or next step earned by the argument.
- Use German or English naturally; do not translate idioms mechanically between languages.

## Language to avoid

Use only when literally justified:

- game changer;
- revolutionary;
- unlock;
- supercharge;
- in today's fast-paced world;
- everyone is talking about;
- the future of;
- must-have;
- proven;
- best practice;
- authentic;
- thought leader.

Also avoid generic AI rhythms: repeated three-part slogans, excessive em dashes, empty rhetorical questions, and a conclusion that merely repeats the opening. Treat phrases such as `massive wake-up call`, `radically disruptive`, `completely rewrite`, and categorical `will` forecasts as warning signals unless the argument earns their intensity.

## Initial public-example calibration

The first calibration uses two public LinkedIn posts accessed on 2026-07-27. It observes writing behavior, not performance, audience response, factual validity, or a finished personal-brand standard.

### Patterns worth preserving

- high-contrast openings that state a system shift quickly;
- memorable analogies such as moving from a chatbot to a digital resident;
- translation from a technology change into consequences for marketing work and operating roles;
- staged reasoning that moves from the current model to a possible next model;
- energetic conviction when the underlying mechanism is made visible.

### Patterns to correct rather than preserve

- absolute forecasts about interfaces, platforms, privacy, or market winners;
- inflated intensifiers that make the post more theatrical than credible;
- source-led summaries without enough Rolf-specific judgment or experience;
- factual or product claims whose evidence and date are not visible.

The supporting audit and links are recorded in `projects/content-operating-system/publishing/identity/voice-and-channel-calibration-2026-07-27.md`.

## Tone range

Voice stays stable while tone changes with the communication job.

| Communication job | Tone | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Explain | calm, clear, teaching | lecturing |
| Challenge | direct, fair, specific | outrage or ridicule |
| Diagnose | analytical, curious, bounded | false certainty |
| Reflect | personal, candid, measured | diary-like oversharing |
| Guide | practical, supportive, decisive | generic instruction |
| Invite discussion | open, substantive | engagement bait |

## Calibration method

Keep three evidence lanes separate in [[execution-calibration-pack]]: craft exemplars, Rolf examples, and counterexamples. An admired writer may supply a technique without becoming a voice model.

1. Expand the current two-example baseline to 10-15 examples that feel recognizably like Rolf and 5-10 counterexamples.
2. Annotate what works or fails at sentence, structure, argument, and reaction level.
3. Compare two small controlled samples before writing a full asset; add a third only when needed.
4. Record reasons using specific labels such as `too promotional`, `too polished`, `too academic`, `too cautious`, or `recognisably-mine`.
5. Use one trusted external editor for early personal-authority assets when independent craft diagnosis is useful.
6. Revise an attribute only when repeated examples or an explicit high-confidence decision reveal a stable preference.

AI may estimate voice fit, but only Rolf can issue the human-ownership verdict.

## Current quality questions

- Is sharing an explicit job of this asset? If yes, what value does sharing create?
- Without the byline, is the argument genuinely non-generic?
- Does Rolf recognise the exact expression as his, rather than merely accepting the idea?
- Is the strongest sentence supported by the strongest evidence?
- Does the piece show both strategic architecture and operating consequence where relevant?
- Has channel optimization weakened the argument or voice?
- Is clear, grammatical prose being mistaken for strong editorial craft?

## Open calibration questions

- Which two attributes are most distinctive and which are merely table stakes?
- How much warmth, humor, provocation, and personal narrative feels natural without relying on explicit first-person markers?
- Does Rolf prefer a more compact executive voice or a more exploratory essay voice?
- Does the metaphor-rich, future-facing pattern persist in a larger sample, including German-language work?
- Which recurring phrases, transitions, and structures occur in Rolf's strongest website and email writing?
