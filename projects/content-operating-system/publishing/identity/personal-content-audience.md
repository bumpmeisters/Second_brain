# Personal Content Audience

**Summary**: A provisional, evidence-aware audience context for Rolf's personal authority content. It converts job-title shorthand into responsibility and decision situations without pretending that current hypotheses are reader evidence.

## ContextOps purpose

- **In scope**: personal authority content written under Rolf's name.
- **Out of scope**: client audiences, commercial ICP selection, positioning, a final content strategy, channel planning, copy, and publication.
- **Consumes**: Rolf's selected audience hypothesis and the current [[editorial-positioning]].
- **Produces context for**: Content Context Packets and Strategic Creative Directions.
- **Handoff rule**: every Direction selects one segment and one active situation; it must not address all segments at once.

Client work must use an audience profile owned by the client or source project. This personal profile is never a silent fallback.

## Evidence state

The only direct audience evidence in this version is Rolf's working hypothesis that CMOs, Heads of Marketing, and ambitious marketing talent have heard many promises, approach new claims skeptically, and seek evidence plus operating relevance. The description is plausible and decision-useful, but it is not validated audience truth.

Kieran Flanagan's phrase `cautiously impatient` is an instructive analogous example, not evidence about Rolf's audience. The two currently reviewed Rolf LinkedIn posts are voice evidence, not audience evidence.

## Selected responsibility segments

### 1. Senior marketing system owners

**Typical titles**: CMO, Marketing Director, Head of Marketing. Titles are routing hints; decision responsibility is controlling.

**Responsibility context**: accountable for a marketing system that must connect strategy, evidence, team capacity, execution, and commercial reality.

**Working situations**:

- evaluating whether a fashionable model or AI capability is operationally useful;
- diagnosing why strategy does not survive handoffs into execution;
- deciding what to standardize, test, fund, stop, or govern;
- explaining a system change to peers, executives, or the team.

**Desired progress hypothesis**: move from attractive claims and disconnected practices to a defensible decision with fit conditions, evidence limits, and an operating consequence.

### 2. Ambitious marketing practitioners

**Typical titles**: strategist, content, demand, ABM, GTM, marketing operations, or emerging marketing leader. Seniority is less important than active capability building.

**Responsibility context**: wants to reason above the task level, improve professional judgment, and translate strategic ideas into credible practice.

**Working situations**:

- learning a complex method without wanting a superficial formula;
- preparing to challenge or improve an existing team practice;
- seeking examples, distinctions, and decision rules that can be used at work;
- building the language and confidence to participate in senior decisions.

**Desired progress hypothesis**: move from consuming trends and templates to applying a clearer model, asking better questions, and producing more defensible work.

## Language and vocabulary map

No Voice-of-Customer corpus currently supports exact reader language. Until one exists:

- do not fabricate representative quotes;
- prefer plain decision language already used in Rolf's positioning: evidence, fit conditions, operating reality, tradeoffs, limits, decision rule;
- treat phrases such as `evidence-hungry`, `cautiously impatient`, or `lied to by vendors` as unvalidated hypotheses, not audience voice;
- record recurring words from conversations, comments, replies, search, and interviews with source context before promoting them.

## Minimal validation plan

Do not launch a research program or monthly profiling workflow. Build evidence through normal authority work:

1. In the next five relevant conversations, record role, active situation, question, rejected claim, proof need, and exact wording.
2. For the next five relevant published assets, capture qualified replies, saves when available, useful conversations, misunderstandings, and segment fit in the existing Performance Record.
3. Ask at least two senior system owners and two ambitious practitioners:
   - Which marketing or AI claim have you recently dismissed, and why?
   - What would have made it credible enough to investigate?
   - Where do frameworks usually fail when you try to use them?
   - Which example, evidence, or limitation helps you act?
4. Review this profile after either five evidence-bearing conversations or five relevant publications. Changes remain proposals until Rolf approves them.

## Handoff requirements

- **Content Context Packet**: reference this exact `profile_id`, choose one segment and one situation, and label unsupported assumptions.
- **Strategic Creative Direction**: define one reader decision or change; do not blend executive governance and practitioner learning in one Direction without an explicit reason.
- **Content Brief**: preserve the selected audience and load the correct voice profile; channel expression may not redefine the reader.
- **Quality review**: AI audience-fit findings remain hypotheses without reader evidence.

The applications suggest that a Creative Direction needs fewer audience fields than a general profile. Segment responsibility, active situation, accessibility need, proof treatment, and desired reader progress constrained the J-Space Directions. The fourth Direction adds a sharper lesson: for a discovery story, retellability can matter more than transferring the full explanatory model. Channel behavior, exact vocabulary, and influence sources belong later or remain research gaps. The case still fits the ambitious-practitioner hypothesis better than the senior-system-owner hypothesis. Do not change the profile schema from one case; look for repetition across further Directions.
