# Enterprise Growth System

## Framework Job

Design and operate one adaptive commercial system across market demand, target-account demand, strategic ABM, and customer expansion without collapsing those distinct jobs into one funnel or one campaign model.

The framework must improve five management decisions:

1. where to develop demand;
2. which accounts deserve investment and at what intensity;
3. which business, buying-group, and relationship hypothesis should guide action;
4. how functions coordinate their contributions; and
5. what evidence should cause continuation, adaptation, promotion, demotion, or exit.

## Classification And Fidelity

This is an **original** framework reconstructed from Rolf's three-part, internally authored Enterprise Growth System, version 1.1. The source files use `HP` in their filenames, but this project artifact does not claim that the method is an official Hewlett-Packard or HP Inc. corporate framework. The blueprint supplies the North Star and system architecture; the model legend supplies definitions and decision rules; the implementation playbook supplies assessment, operating-model, control, and roadmap logic.

This canonical document compresses the source into an inference-ready two-axis system. It does not change the five motions, the Growth Execution Loop, the readiness logic, or the human decision rights. Its modular question engine and ContextOps handoff are project adaptations.

## Use When

- A complex B2B organization must coordinate demand generation, named-account work, strategic ABM, and customer growth.
- Multiple buying roles, long decisions, relationship development, and cross-functional delivery materially affect commercial outcomes.
- Account investment must be dynamically allocated rather than fixed by a static target-account list.
- Marketing, sales, customer success, delivery, product, experts, executives, operations, and analytics need shared decisions without centralized ownership of every activity.
- The organization wants to pilot or mature an account-based growth operating model before buying or automating a large technology stack.

## Do Not Use When

- The purchase is low-value, self-serve, individually decided, or too transactional to justify account-level orchestration.
- The immediate job is only product strategy, sales-stage management, customer-success methodology, or brand strategy; this framework defines their commercial interfaces but does not replace them.
- There is no sponsor, no portfolio decision right, no participating account owner, or no capacity for follow-through.
- A team wants to relabel named-account advertising, personalization, or a campaign as strategic ABM without a business hypothesis and joint account-team commitment.
- The task is to automate sales outreach or relationship judgment from intent data.

## Minimum Required Inputs

- Business scope: markets, regions, business units, offers, and customer types.
- One explicit commercial state that the system should improve.
- A candidate account or customer population with available fit and value evidence.
- At least one scalable signal/demand motion and one deeper account motion for a pilot.
- Named commercial, motion, and account owners with realistic capacity.
- Available market, account, buying-group, relationship, opportunity, and customer evidence.
- Current governance, data, workflow, and measurement constraints.
- A review horizon and criteria for `scale`, `continue`, `adapt`, `hold`, `demote`, `exit`, or `stop`.

If these inputs do not exist, produce a mobilization and evidence plan rather than a finished operating model.

## Core Model

### North-Star Formula

`right markets × right accounts × right relationships × right moments × coordinated action`

The system is constrained by its weakest component. More activity in one component does not compensate for a missing investment case, weak account access, irrelevant intervention, or absent follow-through.

### Three Growth Levels And Five Motions

| Growth level | Motion | Unit of management | Strategic job | Primary progress |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Market development | Market Demand | market, category, or buying audience | build relevance and demand | audience and market movement |
| Account development | Account-based Demand | defined account population | build demand and interpretable signals in selected accounts | account and buying-group movement |
| Account development | 1:few ABM | small account cluster | advance one shared change and value hypothesis | hypothesis, relationship, and opportunity progression |
| Account development | 1:1 Strategic ABM | one strategic account | orchestrate an individual account growth system | reputation, relationship, and revenue progress |
| Customer development | Customer Expansion | customer, business unit, region, use case, or installed capability | realize, retain, and expand customer value | adoption, retention, expansion, and advocacy |

The motions are investment states, not mandatory funnel stages. Accounts may enter, move forward, move backward, move sideways, or exit.

### Two-Axis Reasoning System

**Axis 1 — Growth Motion:** Which durable commercial operating mode fits the current market, account, or customer state? This determines the unit of management, investment, ownership, governance, and outcome logic.

**Axis 2 — Growth Execution Loop:** How should one material initiative inside that motion be led from priority to learning? This determines readiness, hypothesis, orchestration, experience, action, optimization, and review.

| Loop phase | Mandatory decision | Ready when |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial Priority | Which state should change, for whom, and why is it worth investment? | business relevance, scope, owner, and expected value are explicit |
| Intelligence & Hypothesis | What is known, inferred, assumed, unknown, and worth testing? | facts, interpretations, hypotheses, alternatives, and uncertainty are separated |
| Orchestrate | Which roles, relationships, capabilities, timing, and rights must be coordinated? | commitment, capacity, decision rights, risks, and follow-through are confirmed |
| Content & Experience | What perspective or experience will help the buyer group make progress? | audience, customer value, context difference, substance, and follow-through are defined |
| Execute & Optimize | What response should cause scale, continuation, adaptation, or stop? | owner, cadence, response rules, and risk controls are operational |
| Measure & Learn | What changed, what did not, and what decision follows? | progression, outcome, evidence quality, reuse, and consequence are documented |

### Shared Management Capabilities

- dynamic portfolio management;
- account, buying-group, opportunity, relationship, and customer intelligence;
- relationship capital and multithreading;
- signal interpretation and next-best-action judgment;
- content and meaningful-experience orchestration;
- motion-specific measurement and contribution analysis;
- common data objects, workflows, and organizational memory;
- cross-functional governance and federated enablement.

Capabilities are shared; commercial priority, account context, customer action, and resource decisions remain with the accountable motion or account lead and the relevant governance forum.

## Question Engine

### Minimum Viable Questions

1. What specific market, account, relationship, opportunity, or customer state must change?
2. Which unit is being managed: market/audience, account population, cluster, single account, or customer/white space?
3. Which motion fits that unit and current state, and why is a broader or deeper motion inappropriate?
4. What are the account's strategic fit, realistic value, delivery economics, and partnership or white-space value?
5. What current trigger, growth thesis, buying-group evidence, relationship path, and internal commitment justify the proposed intensity?
6. Which facts, signals, interpretations, working hypotheses, unknowns, and disproved assumptions are present?
7. Which buying-group roles influence the change, and where are coverage, consensus, champion, executive-access, or single-thread gaps?
8. What one observable progression should the next initiative create?
9. Which roles and federated capabilities must contribute, and who owns the commercial decision and follow-through?
10. Which Growth Execution Loop gate is open, and what evidence is required to pass it?
11. Which leading, progression, commercial, and customer-value measures fit this motion's mandate?
12. What evidence and review date will cause `scale`, `continue`, `adapt`, `hold`, `demote`, `exit`, or `stop`?

### Deepening Questions

- Compared with what alternative, status quo, or competing priority is the change valuable?
- What would make the growth thesis false, mistimed, commercially unattractive, or impossible to deliver?
- Is the apparent cluster held together by a shared change and buying situation, or only by industry and size?
- Who benefits from the change, who absorbs risk, and who can block or delay it?
- Does repeated engagement show buying-group movement, or repeated activity by one non-critical person?
- What customer information must travel between roles for a decision to progress?
- Which intervention provides unique customer value rather than merely exposing a message?
- What context difference justifies bespoke content instead of a reusable module?
- Which resource is actually scarce: account-team time, intelligence, experts, executives, content, operations, delivery, or analytics?
- What was learned that should update the thesis, motion, portfolio, play, module, or decision standard?

### Counter-Questions

- Are we using account size or a tier label as a substitute for motion readiness?
- Are we interpreting intent as purchase readiness or an alert as a sales mandate?
- Are job titles standing in for real buying-group roles and influence?
- Are we calling segmented demand generation `1:few` without a common business hypothesis and active sales leadership?
- Are personalized assets hiding the absence of a 1:1 account operating model?
- Are activity, response, progression, commercial outcome, and customer value being conflated?
- Is pipeline being used as the sole measure for an earlier-stage motion?
- Would observing, researching, recovering customer health, or moving to a lighter motion create more value with less relationship risk?
- Is a shared tool being mistaken for shared definitions, decisions, and workflows?
- Would the customer describe the planned moment as useful, credible, participatory, and worth a follow-up?

### Optional Modules

Load only the modules required by the decision:

- **Portfolio and capacity:** tier/motion/status separation, scoring, account slots, and `enter/promote/hold/demote/exit` decisions.
- **1:few cluster design:** shared trigger, outcome, buying situation, buying group, value logic, and account deltas.
- **1:1 account system:** account ambition, growth thesis, reputation/relationship/revenue, white space, competitive position, executive sponsorship, and meaningful moments.
- **Customer expansion:** adoption, health, value realization, renewal, white space, advocacy, and recovery-before-expansion.
- **Buying groups and relationship capital:** role, influence, stance, strength, internal owner, multithreading, executive access, and continuity.
- **Signal-to-action:** relevance, specificity, recency, frequency, source confidence, cross-signal confirmation, alternatives, owner, and lowest-risk useful action.
- **Content and experience:** relevance ladder, context delta, modular architecture, briefing, release gate, progression, follow-through, and reuse.
- **Measurement:** outcome hierarchy, KPI dictionary, portfolio dashboard, contribution, and execution-effectiveness scorecard.
- **Data and technology:** common objects, stewardship, privacy, workflow-first use cases, and configure/buy/build decisions.
- **AI Account Copilot:** approved context layers, source-bound outputs, human decision rights, evaluation, and incident controls.
- **Implementation:** assess, design, pilot, validate, scale, and adapt with explicit phase gates.

## Branching And Stop Rules

### Motion Branches

- If the job is market or audience development and no account-level decision is required, use **Market Demand**.
- If accounts are deliberately selected but evidence or readiness is still shallow, use **Account-based Demand** for scalable relevance, monitoring, research, and qualified movement.
- If a small group shares the same concrete change, outcome, buying situation, and value hypothesis—and sales actively co-leads—test **1:few ABM**.
- If one account has high strategic value, a testable growth thesis, a realistic buying-group access path, committed account leadership, delivery readiness, and capacity, test **1:1 Strategic ABM**.
- If an existing customer's adoption, health, realized value, renewal, or white space is the primary state, use **Customer Expansion**; route weak health to recovery before commercial expansion.

### Readiness Branches

- If fit is high but trigger, relationship, hypothesis, or commitment is low, hold the account in a scalable motion rather than inventing 1:1 work.
- If new evidence changes scope, motion, customer risk, budget, executive use, or growth thesis, reopen the relevant gate.
- If a hypothesis is disproved, change the plan, demote, or stop; do not compensate with more activity.
- If routine action remains inside agreed guardrails, do not add a new approval gate.

### Stop Conditions

Stop, reduce, or defer the system effort when:

- no active sponsor or portfolio decision right exists;
- account teams do not provide reliable capacity and follow-through;
- the required account/customer data cannot be used for the agreed purpose;
- deep motions repeatedly show no progression and are not demoted;
- initiatives repeatedly bypass commercial ownership, alignment, or readiness gates;
- technology scope grows faster than validated use cases;
- customer health is ignored in favor of expansion;
- AI outputs cannot be source-bound, access-controlled, and human-reviewed;
- the economics and decision complexity do not justify an account operating model.

## Evidence Standard

Classify every material account statement as:

| Evidence class | Required treatment |
|---|---|
| Confirmed fact | cite source, date, scope, and owner where relevant |
| Supported interpretation | show supporting facts, reasoning, and plausible alternatives |
| Working hypothesis | state test, owner, confidence, decision criterion, and review date |
| Unknown | record the research question and why it matters |
| Disproved | record the evidence and the consequence for thesis, motion, or action |

Signals retain source, timestamp, identity/role specificity, confidence, and uncertainty. Relationship ratings require an owner, date, and observable evidence. Scores may support decisions but never replace account-team judgment.

## Business-Model Adaptations

- **Enterprise technology:** emphasize architecture, security, governance, migration, technical validation, and executive sponsorship.
- **Professional services:** emphasize transformation triggers, diagnostic credibility, trust, executive access, and hypothesis-to-workshop progression.
- **Logistics:** emphasize network, resilience, sustainability, multi-region buying groups, relationship coverage, delivery economics, and geographic/service white space.
- **Industrial manufacturing:** emphasize site and network structure, operational proof, IT/OT risk, pilots, plant coverage, and rollout economics.
- **Financial services:** emphasize risk, compliance, legal, data, controlled pilots, and explicit access/privacy controls.
- **Market entry:** begin with Market Demand learning and early-adopter Account-based Demand; use 1:few to validate repeatable business hypotheses before broad scaling.
- **Low-value or product-led self-serve:** use only selected market-demand, signal, and customer-value modules; do not impose portfolio boards or deep account governance without economic justification.

## Output Contract

A complete application produces one decision-ready system packet containing:

1. **Framework job and scope** — business objective, boundaries, pilot horizon, and constraints.
2. **Motion architecture** — active motions, mandates, units, entry/transition rules, owners, resources, and measures.
3. **Portfolio view** — tier, motion, status, owner, investment case, capacity use, next decision, and review date.
4. **Living context** — facts, signals, interpretations, hypotheses, buying group, relationships, opportunities/white space, actions, and confidence.
5. **Commercial alignment contract** — objective, scope, motion, thesis, functional contributions, capacity, progress measures, risks, and review decision.
6. **Execution loop state** — open gate, required evidence, owner, next action, reaction rules, and follow-through.
7. **Measurement model** — leading, progression, commercial, customer-value, execution-effectiveness, source, owner, cadence, and limitations.
8. **Governance and decision log** — forum, decision right, rationale, owner, due/review date, and later learning.
9. **Explicit non-actions** — what will not be activated, personalized, automated, escalated, or scaled yet.

## Failure Modes

- Treating all five motions as a linear funnel.
- Treating tier, motion, and status as one classification.
- Portfolio inflation: too many 1:1 accounts for available account, expert, executive, content, and operations capacity.
- Marketing-only ABM without active account ownership.
- Intent overload and automated outreach without context.
- Persona lists in place of opportunity-specific buying groups.
- Contact counts in place of relationship capital.
- Custom content without a decision-relevant context difference.
- Activity dashboards in place of progression and decision quality.
- Tool-first implementation before manual decisions and workflows work.
- Federated capability teams taking over commercial ownership.
- Expansion before adoption, health, or value realization.
- AI-generated account claims without sources, access controls, uncertainty, or human review.

## ContextOps Handoff

### Consumes

- market boundaries and strategic priorities;
- ICP, account-tier, and segment hypotheses;
- audience and buying-context evidence;
- approved positioning, value, proof, and claim constraints;
- offer, delivery, customer-success, and commercial capacity context.

### Produces

- a motion and portfolio operating model;
- account, cluster, buying-group, and relationship decision packets;
- orchestration and meaningful-experience requirements;
- motion-specific measurement and governance contracts;
- explicit evidence, access, and human-decision requirements for data, technology, and AI.

### Prohibited Downstream Leakage

The framework does not authorize unreviewed customer communication, autonomous outreach, opportunity-stage changes, relationship scores, resource allocation, or external claims. It does not replace sales, product, customer-success, legal/privacy, brand, or delivery decision rights.

### Compatible Frameworks

- `icp-account-tiering`
- `b2b-buying-committee-intelligence`
- `nonlinear-journey-question-proof-map`
- `campaign-role-architecture`
- `sales-enablement-translation`
- `claim-evidence-risk-use-matrix`
- `contextops-stage-contract`
- `evidence-led-recursive-learning`

### Conflicts Or Overlaps

- Static funnel models conflict when they force motion changes to follow one universal sequence.
- Lead-centric scoring conflicts when it substitutes one person's activity for account and buying-group progression.
- Revenue-only attribution conflicts when it evaluates early motions outside their mandate.
- Generic lifecycle or campaign frameworks may contribute execution modules, but they must not replace motion routing, account readiness, or customer-health gates.

## Evaluation Notes

### Architecture Tournament

Four architectures were compared: source-native compendium, implementation roadmap, account-only ABM playbook, and the selected modular two-axis system. The two-axis system scored highest because it preserves both distinctive control mechanisms while allowing optional modules to be loaded only when a decision needs them. Full scoring and rationale are recorded in `wiki/_outputs/enterprise-growth-system-framework-engineering-2026-07-20.md`.

### Synthetic Evaluation Cases

- **Normal fit:** An enterprise provider has 200 selected accounts, one seven-account transformation cluster, three strategic accounts, and expansion customers. The framework keeps the population in Account-based Demand, routes the shared hypothesis to 1:few, admits only capacity-backed accounts to 1:1, and gates expansion by customer health.
- **Weak evidence:** A high-fit account has one intent spike, no direct relationship, and no available account owner. The correct output is research/observe in Account-based Demand, not sales outreach or 1:1 promotion.
- **Adjacent wrong fit:** A low-value self-serve product has a short, individual purchase. The deep account system stops; only selected market-demand and customer-value modules remain useful.

## Evolution Triggers

Review or revise when:

- a live case cannot be routed cleanly to one primary motion;
- promotion/demotion decisions are inconsistent across reviewers;
- the question engine produces activity plans without better account decisions;
- required artifacts duplicate existing systems without decision value;
- repeated cases reveal a missing role, data object, gate, or customer-health branch;
- execution-effectiveness measures fail to predict or explain better decisions;
- AI recommendations repeatedly violate evidence, access, or human-right boundaries;
- at least two materially different live applications support activation or structural change.

Current version: `0.1.0`. Known weakness: validated against the source corpus and synthetic cases only. Next evaluation: one live enterprise account-portfolio or ABM pilot design.
