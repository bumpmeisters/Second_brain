### Evidence confidence profile

Use qualitative judgments rather than an arithmetic composite. State confidence in the described practice separately from confidence in reported outcomes or causality.

Confidence boundaries:

- Duplicates, derivatives, and repeated claims from one evidence family do not increase independence.
- With only one evidence family, overall confidence normally cannot exceed `medium`; explain any exception.
- Confidence describes evidential support, not publication permission or authority to promote a canonical change.

### Claim-state rules

- **Verified:** independently checked against primary evidence.
- **Source-reported:** stated in the source but not independently verified.
- **Inferred:** analytical conclusion drawn from cited evidence.
- **Adapted:** project-created operating guidance, not attributed to the source.
- **Unknown:** material information the available evidence does not establish.
- **Contradicted:** a claim for which credible counter-evidence is recorded.

## Review Question Engine

### Minimum questions

1. What business or customer state was the organization trying to change?
2. What does the source report, and what is independently verified, inferred, adapted, unknown, or contradicted?
3. Which operating context, maturity, resource, and commercial conditions materially enabled the practice?
4. Which Growth Motion fits the managed unit and current state, and why are adjacent motions wrong?
5. How does the practice traverse the six Growth Execution Loop phases?
6. Which blueprint element is confirmed, extended, contradicted, contextualized, or rejected?
7. What mechanism remains useful after the company name, tool, and channel are removed?
8. What would make the mechanism fail, reverse, or require a lighter motion?
9. Which claim can safely generalize, and which must remain case-specific?
10. Does the evidence justify a canonical change, an observation-only candidate, or no action?

### Counter-questions

- Are repeated derivatives being mistaken for independent evidence?
- Is a reported outcome being treated as causal or universal without a baseline and alternatives?
- Is a campaign, sprint, service, or channel being mistaken for a Growth Motion?
- Is maturity, capacity, sales commitment, customer health, or delivery readiness being omitted?
- Would the customer-side account of progress differ from the vendor's account?
- Is anonymization hiding a recognizable company case rather than producing a generalized principle?

### Stop rules

- Stop promotion when source identity, claim state, or company context cannot be separated.
- Stop generalization when the mechanism depends on one company's untested resources or definitions.
- Stop public preparation when confidentiality, source rights, recognizability, or publication approval is unresolved.
- Preserve a contradiction or unknown rather than forcing the case to fit the blueprint.
