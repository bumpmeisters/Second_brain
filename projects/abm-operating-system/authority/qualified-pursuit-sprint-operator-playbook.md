# The Qualified Pursuit Sprint

## An operator playbook for high-stakes enterprise opportunities and account challenges

> **Private draft — not approved for publication.** This playbook remains inside the ABM Operating System until its exact content and website channel pass Gate G4.

Pursuit Marketing concentrates 1:1 ABM depth on one material opportunity or account challenge for a bounded period. Its job is to create observable progression without transferring commercial ownership from the account team to marketing.

This playbook is a project adaptation informed by ServiceNow's publicly described Pursuit Marketing practice and tested against the Enterprise Growth System. It is not ServiceNow's official SOP.

For the strategic case analysis and evidence boundaries behind the method, read [What ServiceNow Teaches Us About Pursuit Marketing](testing-enterprise-growth-system-01-servicenow.md).

## 1. Use the sprint for the right job

Use a pursuit sprint when all of the following are true:

- A material deal, relationship, reach, education, recovery, renewal, retention, or expansion problem is visible.
- A real decision or risk window exists.
- Marketing can plausibly influence the next state.
- The account team will co-own diagnosis, access, action, and follow-through.
- The team has enough evidence and capacity to act responsibly.
- One observable progression target can be named.

Do not use it for:

- an asset request without a commercial or customer-state problem;
- broad demand generation disguised as account work;
- negotiation or customer-success ownership;
- low-value transactional opportunities;
- expansion pressure when adoption, health, or realized value is weak;
- accounts without a committed owner, evidence, access path, or follow-through capacity.

## 2. Keep the Growth Motion stable

A pursuit is a bounded intervention, not a new Growth Motion.

Route the account before designing the sprint:

| Account state | Likely Growth Motion | Pursuit implication |
|---|---|---|
| Named account with weak evidence or access | Account-based Demand | Build readiness before high-touch pursuit work |
| Shared problem across a small account cluster | 1:few ABM | Use a common hypothesis with account-specific adaptations |
| One strategic account with strong context and access | 1:1 Strategic ABM | Concentrate temporary strategic depth |
| Existing customer with healthy adoption and credible white space | Customer Expansion | Advance retention, value, adoption, or expansion |
| Existing customer with weak health or value realization | Recovery before expansion | Stabilize the relationship; do not force growth |

Urgency changes execution cadence. It does not decide the Growth Motion.

## 3. Run the admission gate

Score the request through four lenses. Do not add the scores mechanically; use them to expose the decision.

### Opportunity

- What material commercial or customer state could change?
- What is the value, risk, or strategic importance?
- What is the decision window?
- What happens if the organization does nothing?

### Achievability

- Can marketing influence the constraint within the available window?
- Is there enough account, relationship, buying-group, and customer evidence?
- Is there a credible access path?
- Can the organization deliver on the promise it intends to make?

### Collaboration

- Is there a named commercial owner?
- What context, access, time, decisions, and follow-through will sales contribute?
- Which executive, customer-success, product, solution, field, or partner contributors are committed?
- Will the account team accept a stop or demotion decision?

### Capacity and opportunity cost

- Which practitioner owns the sprint?
- What work will be delayed or displaced?
- Is the expected progression worth that trade-off?
- Can the team preserve quality without hidden evening or weekend work?

### Admission decision

Choose one:

- **Admit:** the state, influence path, owner, evidence, and capacity are credible.
- **Conditionally admit:** begin only after named gaps are closed by a set date.
- **Build readiness:** route to Account-based Demand or intelligence work first.
- **Recover:** repair customer health or value realization before expansion.
- **Decline:** influence is implausible, collaboration is absent, or opportunity cost wins.

## 4. Contract the sprint

Create a one-page pursuit contract before producing content.

| Field | Decision |
|---|---|
| Account and Growth Motion | Which durable investment state governs the work? |
| Trigger | What changed or became urgent? |
| Desired progression | What observable state should change? |
| Decision window | By when must evidence or action appear? |
| Commercial owner | Who owns the account decision and follow-through? |
| Pursuit lead | Who owns diagnosis and orchestration? |
| Contributors | Who provides intelligence, access, expertise, content, delivery, or approval? |
| Evidence baseline | What is known, inferred, disputed, and unknown? |
| Non-actions | What will the sprint explicitly not do? |
| Review cadence | When will the team continue, adapt, pause, or stop? |
| Measures | Which progression, contribution, capacity, and evidence signals matter? |
| Exit | What ends the sprint and who receives the handoff? |

Use this state-change statement:

> For **[account]**, help **[roles]** move from **[current evidenced state]** to **[next observable state]** before **[decision date]**, because **[commercial or customer consequence]**.

If the team cannot complete that sentence with evidence, it is not ready to design the experience.

## 5. Diagnose the actual constraint

Separate evidence from interpretation:

- **Observed:** directly supported by reliable account or customer evidence.
- **Source-reported:** stated by a stakeholder or public source but not independently verified.
- **Inferred:** reasoned from observations; plausible but unconfirmed.
- **Hypothesized:** deliberately testable explanation.
- **Unknown:** material gap that still affects the decision.
- **Contradicted:** evidence points in different directions.

Ask:

- What business priority is the buying group trying to advance?
- What commercial decision or customer outcome is at stake?
- What changed in timing, leadership, risk, competition, adoption, or account health?
- Where is the relationship strong, weak, absent, or single-threaded?
- Which roles influence the decision, and what does each need to believe or do next?
- How is the company currently perceived?
- What alternative explanations could account for the observed behavior?
- What can the organization credibly deliver if the pursuit succeeds?

Do not let incomplete intelligence become invented certainty. Record the unknown and design the next action to reduce it.

## 6. Write one falsifiable pursuit hypothesis

> If we help **[specific buying roles]** understand, experience, or validate **[decision-relevant value]** through **[intervention]**, they will move from **[current state]** to **[next observable state]**, because **[evidence-backed reason]**. We will reconsider this hypothesis if **[disconfirming evidence]** appears by **[review point]**.

Weak hypothesis:

> Personalized content will increase engagement.

Stronger hypothesis:

> If the incoming operations leader can test the proposed transformation logic with an independent peer and the account's technical sponsors, the buying group will move from inherited skepticism to a jointly defined validation step before the renewal decision. We will reconsider if the leader declines both peer and technical validation or if customer-health evidence deteriorates.

## 7. Map the buying group and relationship system

| Role/person | Decision role | Current stance | Evidence | Relationship owner | Access path | Main concern | Next observable step |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |

Check for:

- missing decision, finance, technical, security, procurement, user, or executive roles;
- relationships that exist only through one seller;
- internal supporters without decision influence;
- account assumptions based on job title rather than behavior;
- partner or regional ownership that changes the access route.

## 8. Assemble the smallest viable pursuit team

Add contributors only when they own a decision, evidence source, relationship, experience, or delivery obligation.

Typical roles include the account executive as commercial owner, the pursuit marketer as diagnostic and orchestration lead, an executive sponsor, customer success, solution expertise, field or partner marketing, operations, and a content or experience specialist after the hypothesis is clear.

A large team is not evidence of strategic importance. Every participant needs a defined contribution and decision right.

## 9. Design the smallest decision-relevant experience

Possible experiences include an executive or peer exchange, problem-framing workshop, technical or value-validation session, customer listening moment, tailored decision brief, site or partner experience, focused direct mail that earns a relevant conversation, or a buying-group briefing.

Apply three tests:

1. **Decision relevance:** what belief, relationship, evidence, or action should change?
2. **Context difference:** what makes this account or buying group require a different experience?
3. **Delivery credibility:** can the organization support the promise after the experience?

Surface personalization fails all three tests. A logo and job title do not create account relevance.

## 10. Execute with response rules

At each review, choose one action:

- **Continue:** evidence supports the hypothesis and commitments hold.
- **Adapt:** the target remains valid but the intervention needs to change.
- **Escalate:** an executive, delivery, risk, or ownership decision is required.
- **Reopen intelligence:** the account state or evidence changed materially.
- **Pause:** a temporary constraint blocks responsible action.
- **Recover:** customer health or realized value must improve before growth work.
- **Demote:** the account no longer justifies pursuit-level capacity.
- **Stop:** the window passed, the hypothesis failed, influence is implausible, collaboration collapsed, or opportunity cost wins.

Reset missing account-team commitments once. Repeated non-participation is a portfolio signal, not a prompt for marketing to compensate silently.

## 11. Measure progression without pretending causality

Use four outcome lenses:

- **Reputation:** has the buying group changed how it understands or evaluates the company?
- **Relationships:** has access, trust, multithreading, or role coverage changed?
- **Revenue:** has the opportunity, renewal, expansion, or commercial decision progressed?
- **Retention/customer value:** has adoption, health, realized value, or renewal confidence improved?

Add two operating lenses:

- **Capacity:** practitioner time, contributor load, reused modules, displaced work, and exit quality.
- **Evidence quality:** baseline, source, confidence, alternatives, comparison context, and unknowns.

Record a progression claim like this:

> After **[intervention]**, **[observable change]** occurred relative to **[baseline]**. The pursuit may have contributed through **[mechanism]**. Alternative explanations include **[factors]**. The next evidence required is **[evidence]**.

This preserves learning without turning correlation or anecdote into attribution.

## 12. Exit and preserve memory

End the sprint when the agreed progression is achieved, the window closes, the hypothesis is disproved, the account state changes materially, collaboration or delivery readiness fails, another account has higher justified priority, or durable 1:1 coverage is explicitly approved.

The handoff must contain:

- current account and buying-group state;
- decisions made and unresolved;
- relationships opened, weakened, or still missing;
- evidence and confidence;
- reusable modules versus account-exclusive context;
- follow-through owner and date;
- next Growth Motion or portfolio decision.

The sprint is complete only when the account team can continue without hidden dependency on the pursuit marketer.

## 13. Adapt the footprint—not the discipline

### Mature enterprise ABM

Use formal intake, portfolio review, CRM tagging, dashboards, specialists, and reserved pursuit capacity. Preserve manual qualitative evidence where automation cannot represent relationship reality.

### Immature enterprise

Pilot one sponsor-backed pursuit. Use a shared document and weekly decision meeting. Borrow expertise if needed. Do not promise scale before the first handoff proves the model.

### Resource-constrained mid-market

Admit only high-materiality cases. Shorten the intake form, reuse modules, limit contributors, and set a hard work-in-progress ceiling. Do not shorten evidence, ownership, or exit decisions.

### Traditional or industrial enterprise

Expect manual intelligence, fragmented systems, site-level influence, technical proof, regional rights, and longer relationship paths. Measure diagnostic access, validation, trust, adoption, and partner coordination where digital engagement is a weak proxy.

### Channel-led business

Map partner roles and rights alongside the buying group. Do not use direct access in a way that undermines the partner. Make orchestration and handoff responsibilities explicit across company, region, seller, and partner.

## 14. Minimum viable launch checklist

- [ ] Durable Growth Motion selected.
- [ ] Opportunity, achievability, collaboration, and capacity reviewed.
- [ ] One-page pursuit contract complete.
- [ ] Evidence states separated.
- [ ] Buying-group and relationship gaps visible.
- [ ] One falsifiable hypothesis written.
- [ ] Smallest decision-relevant experience selected.
- [ ] Response and stop rules agreed.
- [ ] Progression, capacity, and evidence measures defined.
- [ ] Exit and handoff owner named.

The fastest way to make Pursuit Marketing unscalable is to admit work that was never a pursuit.
