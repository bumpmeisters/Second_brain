# Content Brief: HP DemandGen Box Model LinkedIn Article

## Execution purpose

Express the approved strategic argument as a reflective, analytical, and practical long-form LinkedIn article for senior B2B marketing and demand-generation leaders. Help them distinguish an asset plan from a buyer-belief architecture and inspect whether their own campaign brief makes that architecture executable.

## Controlling direction

- **Creative Direction**: `projects/abm-operating-system/authority/directions/hp-demandgen-box-model-direction.md`
- **Approved version**: Direction `direction-abm-hp-demandgen-box-model-01-v1`, version 1.0.0, approved on 2026-07-27. This brief and its asset are separate `in-review` objects and are not approved.
- **Direction invariants to preserve**:
  - the five-Box buyer-belief sequence from problem recognition to portfolio;
  - the context-dependent conclusion that the product belonged in the last Box;
  - HP naming;
  - the confirmed L3 Personal Take that the architecture was further developed than Rolf's ability at the time to enable reliable agency execution;
  - the retrospective operating rule that each Box needs buyer change, evidence, content job, exclusions, owner, and transition criterion;
  - explicit fit limits and the absence of performance or causal claims.
- **Strategic decisions this brief must not reopen**: Primary audience, thesis, strategic angle, narrative logic, evidence boundary, Personal Take meaning, fit and non-fit conditions, counterargument, HP naming, and agency-blame exclusion.

## Variant-bundle boundary

- **Channel**: LinkedIn
- **Format**: Long-form article
- **Communication purpose**: Reflect, explain, and guide
- **Primary audience**: CMOs, Heads of Marketing, and senior DemandGen leaders in complex B2B organizations who commission or govern linked campaign content with internal teams and external agencies.

## Execution contract

- **Voice profile**: `projects/content-operating-system/publishing/identity/voice-and-style.md`
- **Channel profile**: `projects/content-operating-system/publishing/channels/linkedin.md`
- **Selected voice attributes**: Evidence-led; direct without theatre; a systems interpreter who is operationally useful; precise without academic distance; constructive rather than accusatory; personal with purpose.
- **Tone**: Candid, analytical, professionally conversational, and confident about the reasoning while explicit about evidentiary limits.
- **Opening approach**: Lead with the 2019 campaign tension: HP had a security answer, while the campaign brief assumed that many intended buyers did not yet recognize the underlying endpoint-security question. State early that this made product-first messaging the wrong starting point for this situation.
- **Required structure and dramaturgy**:
  1. Open with the recognizable tension and identify it as the campaign's premise, not a proven market fact.
  2. Introduce the Box Model as Rolf's five-stage buyer-belief architecture, not an asset list or universal funnel.
  3. Explain the distinct decision job of each Box and make the product-last logic cumulative.
  4. Turn at the midpoint from the 2019 architecture to Rolf's present-day retrospective; state personal responsibility without blaming the agency or claiming a single cause.
  5. Translate the lesson into the six-part execution contract and explain how it changes a brief.
  6. State fit and non-fit conditions.
  7. Close with one inspectable decision rule; do not add an engagement-farming question.
- **Primary next action**: Have readers inspect whether each stage of a current campaign names the intended buyer change, admissible evidence, content job, exclusion, owner, and transition criterion.
- **What must change for this channel and format**: Translate governance language into a standalone first-person narrative; use descriptive German Box headings and retain only the minimum original English labels needed for source fidelity; use short, scan-friendly sections without mechanically isolating every sentence.
- **What must remain unchanged in meaning**: All direction invariants and claim exclusions.
- **Length or production constraints**: Approximately 850-1,100 words of publication copy; one title and optional subtitle; no emoji, hashtag block, generic CTA, decorative quote, or unnecessary external link; internal evidence notes remain visibly outside the publication copy.
- **Accessibility requirements**: Headings must describe the argument; numbered items must remain meaningful without visual styling; avoid unexplained abbreviations and define `DemandGen` through context rather than jargon stacking.
