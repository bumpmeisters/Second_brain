# The ABM Play Library

## A situation-led catalogue of memorable account-based growth plays

**Private canonical working version · Version 0.2.0 · 24 July 2026**

**Publication status:** This document is a private umbrella draft for research, framework development, and future LinkedIn-series design. It does not approve any post, claim, example, or publishing channel.

---

## Executive premise

ABM does not need another undifferentiated list of channels and campaign tactics. It needs a memorable library of interventions that starts with the commercial or customer situation and ends with an explicit decision.

The working formula is:

> **A trigger is not a play. A channel is not a play. A play is a bounded attempt to change a defined market, account, buying-group, opportunity, relationship, or customer state.**

The names should be vivid enough to travel: **Wake the Dead**, **Chase Our Champion**, **Ping for Buyer's Remorse**, **T-Minus Renewal**. The operating definition beneath each name must remain disciplined enough to prevent superficial activation.

This library therefore separates three operating levels plus one supporting catalog layer:

1. **Situation Plays** — the commercial or customer state to change.
2. **Play Formats** — how specialist depth and coordination are organized.
3. **System Plays** — the operating mechanisms that make repeated execution governable.
4. **Supporting entries** — memorable hypothesis patterns, progression milestones, resourcing components, or transfer methods that sit beneath a top-level Play.

Version 0.2.0 contains **43 top-level Situation Plays, six top-level Play Formats, ten top-level System Plays, and four named supporting entries**. All 63 memorable catalog entries remain available, but only 59 are treated as top-level Plays. The library is intentionally versioned and expandable; it is not a claim that every possible ABM intervention has been discovered.

## 1. What counts as an ABM Play?

An ABM Play must specify:

- a material state worth changing;
- a defined unit of management: account population, cluster, strategic account, buying group, opportunity, customer, business unit, region, or installed capability;
- a plausible and falsifiable hypothesis;
- accountable commercial ownership;
- the relationships and capabilities required for coordinated action;
- an experience or intervention whose substance changes because of the context;
- observable progress evidence;
- a continue, adapt, stop, handoff, or exit rule.

A play is **not**:

- an isolated signal such as an intent spike, MQA, click, event attendance, or engagement score;
- a channel such as LinkedIn, email, display, direct mail, events, or executive outreach;
- an asset such as a landing page, video, report, or gift;
- a permanent Growth Motion;
- an urgent sales request without qualification, capacity, ownership, or follow-through.

The Enterprise Growth System provides the governing sequence:

`commercial/customer state → Growth Motion → Play → activation pattern → tactics and channels → progression evidence → portfolio decision`

Growth Motions are durable investment states. Plays are bounded interventions inside a motion. Tactics and channels are ingredients inside a play. (framework reference: [Enterprise Growth System](../frameworks/enterprise-growth-system.md))

## 2. Naming system

Each library entry combines:

- **Editorial name:** short, vivid, and easy to remember.
- **Strategic subtitle:** the precise job the play performs.
- **Play card:** use condition, hypothesis, admission gate, progress evidence, stop rule, and likely Growth Motion.

Good editorial names create curiosity without overclaiming. The subtitle prevents the name from becoming the method.

## 4. Situation Plays

### Family A — Build Account Demand & Relevance

These plays develop relevance and interpretable movement in selected accounts before deep strategic coverage is justified. Their normal home is Account-based Demand, with selective movement into 1:few or 1:1 after stronger evidence and readiness.

### 1. Warm the Account — Build relevance before the buying window

**Strategic job:** Increase informed awareness and relevance in a selected account without pretending that attention equals buying intent.

**Use when:** The account fits the investment thesis, but direct evidence, relationships, or timing are still shallow.

**Progress looks like:** Broader role engagement, meaningful response, account-team learning, a plausible trigger, or permission for a more direct interaction.

**Stop rule:** Do not escalate because of engagement volume alone.

**LinkedIn angle:** Why account warming is not retargeting with a company logo.

### 3. Teach the Problem — Build shared recognition of an important change

**Strategic job:** Help relevant roles recognize, name, and understand a material problem before promoting a solution.

**Use when:** The account may face the issue, but the buying group lacks shared language, evidence, or urgency.

**Progress looks like:** The problem enters internal discussion, additional roles engage, or the account requests diagnostic or peer evidence.

**Stop rule:** Do not manufacture urgency where account evidence does not support it.

**LinkedIn angle:** The first ABM conversion may be agreement that the problem is real.

### 4. Plant the Flag — Establish relevance in a new market or account population

**Strategic job:** Create a credible initial position across a deliberately selected market, territory, industry, or account population.

**Use when:** The organization has a real strategic reason to enter, a deliberately selected account population, an accountable owner, and insufficient account-level evidence for deeper motions.

**Progress looks like:** Improved market learning, clearer ICP boundaries, emerging account clusters, qualified relationship paths, and an explicit portfolio decision.

**Stop rule:** Without a selected account population and account-level learning decision, route the work to Market Demand rather than disguising generic market entry as ABM.

**LinkedIn angle:** Market entry should produce learning before it produces personalization.

### 5. Break Into the Account — Create the first legitimate relationship path

**Strategic job:** Move from anonymous or peripheral awareness to a permissioned, decision-relevant account relationship.

**Use when:** The account is strategically relevant but lacks a credible access path.

**Progress looks like:** A relevant role accepts a conversation, introduction, peer exchange, or diagnostic interaction.

**Stop rule:** Contact enrichment is not access; identity is not qualification.

**LinkedIn angle:** The difference between finding a contact and earning a path into the account.

### 6. Target the Competitor's Clients — Develop a credible displacement thesis

**Strategic job:** Build relevance with accounts that use a competing solution by identifying a material unmet need, change, or risk.

**Use when:** The incumbent context is understood, a switching condition or material unmet need or risk is evidenced, the buying-group context is plausible, and a differentiated transition path can reduce change risk.

**Progress looks like:** The account acknowledges a gap, explores alternatives, tests transition feasibility, or accepts a comparative or diagnostic conversation.

**Stop rule:** Competitor usage alone is a population attribute, not a Play admission condition.

**LinkedIn angle:** Competitive displacement starts with the customer's change, not your battle card.

### 7. Ping for Buyer's Remorse — Re-enter after a disappointing decision

**Strategic job:** Reopen relevance when a recent purchase, implementation, or strategic decision is producing credible friction or doubt.

**Use when:** There is direct or contextual evidence of unmet expectations and a responsible alternative path exists.

**Progress looks like:** The account explores remediation, comparison, contingency, or a future decision window.

**Stop rule:** Do not exploit assumed dissatisfaction or interfere with an implementation without evidence.

**LinkedIn angle:** Buyer remorse can open a door, but only if you bring a better recovery path.

### 8. Wake the Sleeping Giant — Reassess a dormant high-potential account

**Strategic job:** Re-evaluate an important account that has remained commercially quiet despite structural potential.

**Use when:** New evidence suggests that the prior account thesis, timing, route, or relationship map may have changed.

**Progress looks like:** A refreshed account decision: activate, monitor, demote, or exit.

**Stop rule:** Do not keep strategic accounts permanently active because of logo value.

**LinkedIn angle:** Some accounts need waking; others need removing from the list.

**Evidence confidence:** Low. The operating job is distinct, but direct external evidence for dormant-account portfolio reassessment remains limited.

### Family B — Develop Buying Groups & Relationships

These plays change access, role coverage, trust, and relationship capital. They can support Account-based Demand, 1:few, 1:1 Strategic ABM, or Customer Expansion.

### 9. Map the Room — Discover how the decision really works

**Strategic job:** Replace title-based persona assumptions with a living map of decision roles, influence, relationships, unknowns, and evidence.

**Use when:** The opportunity or account depends on several roles and the actual decision system is unclear.

**Progress looks like:** Better role coverage, fewer unknowns, and a testable path through the decision.

**Stop rule:** A purchased contact list is not a buying-group map.

**LinkedIn angle:** Most buying-group maps show people; the useful ones show decisions.

### 10. Open the Door — Earn access to a missing critical role

**Strategic job:** Create a legitimate relationship path to a role whose involvement materially affects progress.

**Use when:** The team knows which role is missing and why that role matters.

**Progress looks like:** An introduction, accepted conversation, contribution to a workshop, or evidence that another route is preferable.

**Stop rule:** Seniority alone does not make a role decision-critical.

**LinkedIn angle:** Executive access is valuable only when the executive has a job in the decision.

### 11. Build the Bench — Reduce single-thread risk

**Strategic job:** Create resilient relationship coverage across sponsors, champions, evaluators, users, commercial roles, and potential blockers.

**Use when:** Account progress depends disproportionately on one person.

**Progress looks like:** Multiple relevant relationships with distinct roles, value, and ownership.

**Stop rule:** More contacts do not equal multithreading if they add no decision coverage.

**LinkedIn angle:** A crowded CRM can still be a single-threaded account.

### 12. Make a Champion — Help an internal supporter become effective

**Strategic job:** Equip a credible supporter to mobilize internal attention, evidence, and alignment.

**Use when:** A contact has motivation and credibility but lacks the case, network, or organizational support to act.

**Progress looks like:** The champion brings in roles, advances the internal conversation, or tests the value hypothesis.

**Stop rule:** Do not label enthusiasm as influence.

**LinkedIn angle:** Champions are not discovered; they are enabled and tested.

### 13. Chase Our Champion — Transfer relationship capital after a move

**Strategic job:** Follow a proven relationship when a champion changes role or company, while requalifying the new context.

**Use when:** The prior relationship is real and the champion's new environment may contain a relevant problem.

**Progress looks like:** A renewed, permissioned conversation and a new context-specific hypothesis.

**Stop rule:** A champion's move does not automatically create a new opportunity.

**LinkedIn angle:** Follow the relationship, not the old pitch.

**Evidence confidence:** Low. The relationship event is distinct, but external evidence for a repeatable champion-portability Play remains limited.

### 14. Bring in the Boss — Create purposeful executive-to-executive engagement

**Strategic job:** Use executive involvement to address a decision, trust, ambition, risk, or relationship gap that operating teams cannot solve alone.

**Use when:** The executive has a defined role and a credible peer-level contribution.

**Progress looks like:** A decision obstacle changes, sponsorship strengthens, or a strategic conversation advances.

**Stop rule:** Executive theatre without a decision job wastes relationship capital.

**LinkedIn angle:** The executive sponsor should bring leverage, not just a title.

### 15. Earn the Skeptic — Address legitimate resistance and improve the decision

**Strategic job:** Earn enough trust and understanding to turn legitimate skepticism into usable evidence about interests, risks, alternatives, and decision rights.

**Use when:** A relevant role has a material, addressable concern.

**Progress looks like:** The concern is clarified, reduced, accepted, or correctly recognized as a stop condition; decision quality improves even when the answer is no.

**Stop rule:** Do not manipulate or route around legitimate risk, compliance, procurement, or user concerns.

**LinkedIn angle:** Your blocker may be the only person protecting the quality of the decision.

### 16. Reset the Reputation — Change an outdated or damaging perception

**Strategic job:** Replace a narrow, obsolete, or negative account perception with credible evidence and experience.

**Use when:** The current perception blocks access or consideration and the underlying reality has genuinely changed.

**Progress looks like:** New roles engage, the account updates its assumptions, or a previously closed strategic conversation reopens.

**Stop rule:** Messaging cannot repair a proposition, delivery, or trust problem that remains true.

**LinkedIn angle:** Reputation change requires evidence of change, not a refreshed tagline.

### 17. Partner the Path — Use the ecosystem to create the right access route

**Strategic job:** Decide whether the route should be partner-first, customer-first, or jointly orchestrated.

**Use when:** A partner, integrator, distributor, or alliance controls access, rights, trust, delivery, or incentives.

**Progress looks like:** Clear ownership, aligned incentives, a credible joint value hypothesis, and a permissioned customer path.

**Stop rule:** Do not treat the partner as a media channel when it is part of the commercial system.

**LinkedIn angle:** In channel businesses, the first ABM decision may be whom to engage first.

### Family C — Create & Accelerate Opportunities

These plays create, shape, progress, recover, or close a material commercial decision. Their likely homes are Account-based Demand, 1:few ABM, or 1:1 Strategic ABM.

### 19. Create the Opportunity — Turn an account hypothesis into a commercial initiative

**Strategic job:** Help the account and commercial team define a concrete change worth exploring.

**Use when:** A material problem, relationship path, and value hypothesis have enough evidence for direct work.

**Progress looks like:** A jointly recognized initiative, decision process, owner, and next commitment.

**Stop rule:** Do not manufacture an opportunity to satisfy pipeline targets.

**LinkedIn angle:** Opportunity creation begins when the customer recognizes a decision, not when CRM opens a record.

**Supporting pattern:** `Turn Interest Into a Meeting` can provide progression evidence when a meaningful response becomes a context-rich, permissioned dialogue.

### 20. Help Land the Account — Coordinate the path to an initial win

**Strategic job:** Support a material new-account decision with insight, relationships, proof, experience, and coordinated follow-through.

**Use when:** The opportunity is qualified, the account team is committed, and marketing can change a specific decision condition.

**Progress looks like:** Better buying-group access, accepted proof, decision progression, reduced risk, or commercial commitment.

**Stop rule:** Marketing does not own forecast, negotiation, or close.

**LinkedIn angle:** Helping land an account is bigger than surround advertising.

### 21. Shape the RFP — Influence the decision before the document freezes it

**Strategic job:** Help the buying group clarify outcomes, criteria, risks, and implementation requirements before a formal RFP fixes the frame.

**Use when:** The team has legitimate access and substantive expertise before or early in formal procurement.

**Progress looks like:** Better-defined requirements, evaluation logic, stakeholder involvement, or recognition of differentiating value.

**Stop rule:** Do not manipulate procurement or write requirements that undermine buyer choice.

**LinkedIn angle:** By the time the RFP arrives, the most important decisions may already be made.

### 22. Build the Case — Help the buying group justify change

**Strategic job:** Equip internal roles to explain the economic, operational, strategic, and risk logic of action.

**Use when:** The change is plausible but internal justification is incomplete or fragmented.

**Progress looks like:** A credible shared case, clearer assumptions, stronger role alignment, and an explicit decision path.

**Stop rule:** Do not turn speculative ROI into false precision.

**LinkedIn angle:** The best business case helps the buyer think, not just helps the seller close.

### 23. Break the No-Decision — Help the buying group move beyond the status quo

**Strategic job:** Address ambiguity, internal misalignment, perceived risk, or weak priority that favors no decision.

**Use when:** The problem is real but the organization cannot yet make a confident collective choice.

**Progress looks like:** Shared decision criteria, resolved role conflict, a smaller next step, or an explicit stop.

**Stop rule:** Do not mistake buyer caution for an objection to overcome.

**LinkedIn angle:** Your biggest competitor may be the organization's inability to decide.

### 24. Close the Confidence Gap — Reduce late-stage decision risk

**Strategic job:** Provide the proof, experience, access, commercial assurance, or implementation confidence needed for a responsible decision.

**Use when:** The buying group understands the value but still faces material product, delivery, security, legal, procurement, commercial, governance, or implementation uncertainty.

**Progress looks like:** Validation of critical assumptions, risk-owner confidence, or agreement on security, commercial terms, procurement, implementation, and governance.

**Stop rule:** More content cannot close a product, delivery, security, or commercial gap that remains unresolved.

**LinkedIn angle:** Late-stage buyers often need confidence, not more persuasion.

### 25. Rescue the Deal — Stabilize a strategically important opportunity at risk

**Strategic job:** Diagnose and address a material relationship, perception, proof, access, competitor, or decision-process problem.

**Use when:** The opportunity is important, recoverable, and supported by committed owners and capacity.

**Progress looks like:** The risk changes, a missing role engages, the account accepts a corrective interaction, or the team exits responsibly.

**Stop rule:** Do not convert every forecast problem into an ABM emergency.

**LinkedIn angle:** A deal rescue starts with diagnosis, not a last-minute executive dinner.

### 26. Wake the Dead — Reactivate a stalled opportunity

**Strategic job:** Reopen a previously active opportunity by finding what materially changed—or confirming that nothing did.

**Use when:** New evidence affects priority, people, economics, risk, competition, or timing.

**Progress looks like:** A renewed decision process, a reframed smaller step, or a documented exit.

**Stop rule:** Repeating the old sequence is not reactivation.

**LinkedIn angle:** Dead opportunities do not need another follow-up; they need a new reason to live.

### 27. Reopen Closed-Lost — Requalify after a meaningful change

**Strategic job:** Revisit a lost decision when account conditions, leadership, incumbent performance, proposition, or timing have materially changed.

**Use when:** The prior loss is understood and a new hypothesis is evidence-backed.

**Progress looks like:** A legitimate re-entry, new learning, or confirmation that the account should remain closed.

**Stop rule:** Calendar time alone is not a change event.

**LinkedIn angle:** Closed-lost is a decision history, not a permanent account identity.

### Family D — Realize & Expand Customer Value

These plays begin with customer state, value, health, adoption, and relationship evidence. Their normal home is Customer Expansion.

### 28. Make the Promise Real — Turn the sale into successful adoption

**Strategic job:** Align expectations, stakeholders, experience, and evidence so the customer can realize the promised value.

**Use when:** A new customer or new use case is moving from commercial commitment into implementation and adoption.

**Progress looks like:** Agreed outcomes, role readiness, adoption milestones, customer confidence, and early value evidence.

**Stop rule:** Do not treat onboarding communications as proof of adoption.

**LinkedIn angle:** The first customer-expansion play starts before anyone asks for expansion.

### 29. Prove the Value — Make realized value visible and credible

**Strategic job:** Help customer and account teams document what changed, for whom, under which conditions, and with what confidence.

**Use when:** Value may exist but is fragmented, invisible, or not connected to stakeholder priorities.

**Progress looks like:** Validated value evidence, stakeholder recognition, stronger sponsorship, and informed next decisions.

**Stop rule:** Do not convert influence or activity into unsupported outcome claims.

**LinkedIn angle:** Value realization becomes strategic only when the customer recognizes it.

### 30. Fix the Friction — Recover adoption before pushing growth

**Strategic job:** Identify and address barriers in use, enablement, process, trust, delivery, or ownership.

**Use when:** Adoption or experience is weaker than expected but recoverable.

**Progress looks like:** Reduced barriers, improved use, clearer ownership, restored confidence, or a responsible limitation.

**Stop rule:** Do not sell around unresolved customer-health problems.

**LinkedIn angle:** Expansion pressure is the wrong response to an adoption problem.

### 31. Extend the Business Relationship — Broaden strategic relevance

**Strategic job:** Move from a narrow transactional relationship toward additional relevant outcomes, roles, and shared planning.

**Use when:** Existing value and health justify a broader relationship hypothesis.

**Progress looks like:** New strategic conversations, stronger multithreading, additional shared priorities, or co-owned planning.

**Stop rule:** Relationship expansion without customer value becomes account extraction.

**LinkedIn angle:** Share of wallet should follow share of relevance.

### 32. Expand Into the Customer Organization — Move into a new business unit

**Strategic job:** Transfer a validated value pattern into another unit, function, subsidiary, or buying group.

**Use when:** The new unit has a relevant problem and the existing relationship creates legitimate access or proof.

**Progress looks like:** A new unit-specific hypothesis, sponsor, decision path, and evidence plan.

**Stop rule:** The logo relationship does not make the new unit a copy of the old one.

**LinkedIn angle:** Same customer, new buying group: expansion is not duplication.

**Routing note:** Whitespace discovery is an intelligence step that may route here when the uncovered opportunity requires entry into a new organizational unit.

### 33. Plant the Next Use Case — Develop adjacent value

**Strategic job:** Identify and validate a next use case connected to customer priorities and demonstrated capability.

**Use when:** Current value is credible and an adjacent problem merits exploration.

**Progress looks like:** A validated use-case hypothesis, engaged roles, and a bounded next step.

**Stop rule:** Product adjacency alone is not customer adjacency.

**LinkedIn angle:** Cross-sell becomes useful when it begins as problem discovery.

**Routing note:** Whitespace discovery may route here when the opportunity is an adjacent use case within the current organizational context.

### 34. Cross the Border — Expand geographically with context

**Strategic job:** Extend a customer relationship into another region or country while adapting to local decision rights, needs, partners, and operating conditions.

**Use when:** The value pattern may transfer, both global and local owners are engaged, and local rights, partner route, regulation, economics, or stakeholder conditions differ materially.

**Progress looks like:** Local validation, aligned ownership, an adapted hypothesis, and a credible route to adoption.

**Stop rule:** A global contract does not eliminate local context; if geography changes none of the material operating conditions, treat this as organizational expansion rather than a separate Play.

**LinkedIn angle:** Global expansion fails when the account plan crosses borders but the context does not.

### 35. Grow Together — Co-create a new capability or solution

**Strategic job:** Build a new use case, service, or operating model jointly with a strategically important customer.

**Use when:** Both sides have aligned value, capacity, rights, risk controls, and learning intent.

**Progress looks like:** A bounded experiment, shared evidence, explicit ownership, and a decision about scale.

**Stop rule:** Co-creation must not hide unfunded customization or unclear IP.

**LinkedIn angle:** The most strategic expansion play may begin as a shared experiment.

**Evidence confidence:** Low. Co-creation is recognizable, but direct external evidence for this exact ABM operating job remains limited.

### 36. Turn the Customer Into an Advocate — Develop credible advocacy

**Strategic job:** Enable a satisfied customer to share relevant experience through references, communities, peer exchange, or joint learning.

**Use when:** Value is real, the customer consents, and advocacy benefits both parties.

**Progress looks like:** Willing reference participation, peer contribution, or customer-authored evidence.

**Stop rule:** Do not pressure customers or script away their independent perspective.

**LinkedIn angle:** Advocacy is earned customer evidence, not a marketing deliverable.

### Family E — Protect, Recover & Renew Revenue

These plays protect continuity or restore customer health and confidence. Recovery normally precedes expansion.

### 37. T-Minus Renewal — Start renewal readiness before the deadline

**Strategic job:** Work backward from the renewal decision to align adoption, value, relationships, risks, proof, and commercial planning.

**Use when:** The renewal horizon is known and sufficient time remains to change underlying conditions.

**Progress looks like:** Health and value gaps surface early, owners act, relationships strengthen, and renewal confidence becomes evidence-based.

**Stop rule:** A communication calendar cannot compensate for weak value realization.

**LinkedIn angle:** Renewal is a customer-value process with a contract date attached.

### 38. Save the Renewal — Recover an at-risk renewal

**Strategic job:** Coordinate a bounded response to a material renewal risk.

**Use when:** The risk is explicit, recoverable, and supported by accountable owners and delivery capacity.

**Progress looks like:** Root causes change, trust improves, value is restored or reframed, and a responsible renewal path emerges.

**Stop rule:** Marketing cannot message its way out of unresolved delivery failure.

**LinkedIn angle:** Renewal rescue begins with the cause of risk, not the renewal deck.

### 39. Defend the Base — Protect a healthy strategic customer

**Strategic job:** Maintain relationship, relevance, value visibility, and competitive resilience before an acute threat appears.

**Use when:** The customer is healthy and strategically important, but complacency or competitive exposure could erode the position.

**Progress looks like:** Stronger multithreading, current value evidence, aligned future priorities, and known competitive risks.

**Stop rule:** Defense should not become unnecessary contact volume or restrictive behavior.

**LinkedIn angle:** The best competitive-defense play may happen before the competitor arrives.

### 40. Prevent the Downsell — Protect justified scope

**Strategic job:** Address the adoption, value, budget, organizational, or relationship conditions that threaten reduced scope.

**Use when:** Downsizing risk is evidence-backed and the retained scope can still create legitimate customer value.

**Progress looks like:** Clear root causes, restored use or value, agreed scope, or an honest right-sizing decision.

**Stop rule:** Do not protect revenue by defending unused or unnecessary capacity.

**LinkedIn angle:** Sometimes preventing downsell means proving value; sometimes it means accepting the right scope.

### 41. Repair the Relationship — Restore trust after failure or conflict

**Strategic job:** Rebuild credible working relationships after disappointment, escalation, misalignment, or broken expectations.

**Use when:** The underlying issue is acknowledged and accountable owners can change the experience.

**Progress looks like:** Shared facts, corrective action, restored access, clearer expectations, and evidence of follow-through.

**Stop rule:** Reputation messaging cannot substitute for repair.

**LinkedIn angle:** Relationship recovery starts when the account sees changed behavior.

### 42. Welcome the New Boss — Reset the account after leadership change

**Strategic job:** Reopen the account hypothesis, priorities, relationships, risks, and value story when a critical leader changes.

**Use when:** The new leader materially affects sponsorship, decision rights, strategy, or continuity.

**Progress looks like:** Updated priorities, a new relationship path, validated value, and a revised account decision.

**Stop rule:** Do not assume inherited commitments or replay the old executive narrative.

**LinkedIn angle:** A leadership change is not just a trigger; it invalidates parts of your account model.

### 43. Survive the Reorg — Rebuild the operating map after structural change

**Strategic job:** Revalidate ownership, budgets, relationships, buying groups, use cases, and delivery paths after reorganization, M&A, divestiture, or structural integration.

**Use when:** Structural change makes the prior account plan unreliable.

**Progress looks like:** A refreshed account map, new owners, revised hypotheses, and explicit continuation or demotion decisions.

**Stop rule:** Do not keep executing against an organization that no longer exists.

**LinkedIn angle:** Reorgs do not merely move boxes; they rewrite the account.

### 44. Recover the Account — Restore health before growth

**Strategic job:** Coordinate customer, delivery, product, success, commercial, and executive action around a material health problem.

**Use when:** The account is strategically important, the problem is recoverable, and owners can change underlying conditions.

**Progress looks like:** Improved health, confidence, adoption, delivery, relationship, or an honest exit path.

**Stop rule:** Recovery is not an expansion campaign with softer language.

**LinkedIn angle:** The most important customer-growth decision may be to stop selling and start repairing.

### 45. Win Back the Customer — Rebuild a lost customer relationship

**Strategic job:** Re-enter after churn or contract loss with a genuinely changed proposition, operating reality, relationship, or customer context.

**Use when:** The reason for loss is known and credible evidence supports a new path.

**Progress looks like:** Permission to re-engage, acknowledgment of change, a new bounded initiative, or a responsible no.

**Stop rule:** Nostalgia, logo value, or elapsed time does not justify win-back.

**LinkedIn angle:** A win-back play must answer one brutal question: what is different now?

## 5. Cross-cutting Play Formats

Formats organize specialist depth and coordination. They may contain one or more Situation Plays.

### Pursuit Marketing — The qualified ABM sprint

Apply 1:1 ABM principles to one qualified opportunity or account challenge for a bounded period, then hand commercial ownership back to the account team. Pursuit Marketing can deliver **Help Land the Account**, **Rescue the Deal**, **Reset the Reputation**, **Save the Renewal**, **Open the Door**, or another situation-specific play. It is a format inside 1:1 Strategic ABM or Customer Expansion, not a sixth Growth Motion. (source: `wiki/servicenow-pursuit-marketing-sprint-playbook.md`; scheduled for recovery wave 11)

**Admission gate:** Material opportunity or challenge, plausible achievability, committed commercial collaboration, buying-group path, sufficient capacity, and explicit handoff.

**LinkedIn angle:** ABM is the marathon; Pursuit Marketing is the qualified sprint.

### Embedded Account Management — Put the practitioner inside the account rhythm

Embed an ABM practitioner close enough to the account team to translate context into coordinated customer progress while central standards preserve evidence quality, governance, and learning. (source: `wiki/ntt-data-embedded-account-management-playbook.md`; scheduled for recovery wave 11)

**LinkedIn angle:** ABM should not be delivered to the account team; it should operate with the account team.

### 1:few Cluster Play — One shared change, several accounts

Coordinate a small cluster only when the accounts share a specific change, value hypothesis, role pattern, and reusable experience logic.

**LinkedIn angle:** A segment becomes 1:few only when the same meaningful hypothesis can survive across accounts.

### Executive-to-Executive — Use senior relationships with a defined job

Deploy executive engagement only when peer authority, trust, decision leverage, or strategic perspective can change the situation.

**LinkedIn angle:** Executive involvement is a format; the account problem still determines the play.

### Partner-First — Route through the ecosystem deliberately

Choose partner-first, customer-first, or joint orchestration based on rights, trust, access, incentives, and delivery responsibilities.

**LinkedIn angle:** Partner-led ABM starts with route design, not co-branded assets.

### ABM Pilot Lab — Learn before scaling the operating model

Run a bounded pilot with a quality baseline, selected context, explicit service definition, adoption evidence, stop rules, and a non-champion scale test.

**LinkedIn angle:** A pilot proves that a team can learn; it does not automatically prove that the model can scale.

## 6. System Plays

System Plays do not replace commercial Situation Plays. They create the decision infrastructure required to run them repeatedly.

### 1. Keep the List Alive — Dynamic portfolio management

Continuously admit, promote, demote, monitor, or exit accounts based on strategy, evidence, readiness, capacity, and opportunity cost.

### 2. Turn Signals Into Stories — Contextual signal interpretation

Combine fit, behavior, business change, relationships, opportunities, customer state, and account-team evidence into a hypothesis with alternatives. `Find the Spark` is the named hypothesis pattern beneath this System Play.

### 3. Map the Buying Group Before You Message It — Role and relationship intelligence

Model decision roles, coverage, evidence, relationship ownership, unknowns, and single-thread risk before activation.

### 4. Fix the Data Before the Message — Maintained account infrastructure

Resolve account identity, hierarchy, contact validity, role context, provenance, and stewardship before scaling personalization.

### 5. Put Privacy at the Gate — Lawful identity and activation

Make purpose, permissions, data source, retention, recipients, geography, suppression, and human accountability explicit before identity resolution or activation.

### 6. Build the Micro-Experience — Change substance where context matters

Create a distinct experience only when the account or cluster context changes the problem, proof, role, timing, or follow-through—not merely the logo and salutation.

### 7. Choose the Channel, Not All Channels — Selective activation

Choose channels according to role behavior, context, permission, creative fit, cost, and measurable contribution.

### 8. Contract Before Campaign — Agree reciprocal contributions before activation

Define what Marketing and commercial owners each contribute before activation: context, access, priorities, evidence, decisions, capacity, response, and follow-through.

### 9. Give AI a Lane — Bounded AI assistance

Use AI for defined research, synthesis, preparation, mapping, or adaptation tasks with provenance, evaluation, review, write controls, and rollback. Humans retain motion, investment, relationship, and customer-contact decisions.

### 10. Build a Dashboard That Decides — Decision-specific measurement

Give each role the evidence needed for the next decision rather than forcing all stakeholders into one activity dashboard.

These ten System Plays adapt the governed operating chain in the Enterprise ABM Scale System. The underlying vendor/practitioner source is useful for architecture but does not independently validate outcomes. (source: `wiki/enterprise-abm-scale-system-ten-operating-plays.md`; scheduled for recovery wave 11)

## 7. Supporting catalog entries

These entries remain memorable and reusable, but they do not describe independent top-level Situation Plays, Play Formats, or System Plays.

### Find the Spark — Hypothesis pattern under Turn Signals Into Stories

Convert a material business, leadership, technology, regulatory, financial, or relationship change into a bounded account hypothesis. Several contextual inputs, an accountable owner, an alternative explanation, and an activate, monitor, reject, or investigate decision are required.

**Stop rule:** An isolated vendor intent score is not a spark.

**LinkedIn angle:** Signals do not tell you what to do; they tell you what to investigate.

### Turn Interest Into a Meeting — Progression pattern under Create the Opportunity

Turn a meaningful, context-rich account response into a useful, permissioned interaction. The meeting is progress evidence; the top-level state-changing job remains creation of a jointly recognized initiative or dialogue.

**Stop rule:** Do not trigger an indiscriminate sales cadence from low-context engagement.

**LinkedIn angle:** The handoff is not a lead transfer; it is a context transfer.

### Tiger Team — Temporary resourcing component

Temporarily assemble the smallest credible cross-functional team around a material pursuit, recovery, transformation, or account decision. The parent Situation Play and Play Format define the actual operating job.

**Stop rule:** A Tiger Team needs an admission gate, accountable owner, capacity boundary, handoff, and exit rule.

**LinkedIn angle:** A Tiger Team without an exit rule becomes invisible permanent capacity.

### Lift and Shift — Controlled learning-transfer method

Transfer a validated 1:1 learning kernel into a 1:few Cluster Play only after removing account-exclusive context and independently validating shared cluster fit. (source: `wiki/ntt-data-embedded-account-management-playbook.md`; scheduled for recovery wave 11)

**Stop rule:** Do not scale confidential detail, one-account assumptions, or untested personalization.

**LinkedIn angle:** The scalable asset is the reasoning pattern, not the personalized content.
