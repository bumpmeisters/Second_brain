# Strategic Creative Direction: ServiceNow Pursuit Marketing Authority Pilot

## Retrospective status

This direction was reconstructed from artifacts and decisions completed on 2026-07-22. It did not control the original drafting or Gate G4 decision. The exact artifact fingerprints and channel decisions in the controlling review override this reconstruction.

## Audience and desired change

- **Primary audience**: International VPs and Heads of ABM, Demand, and GTM.
- **Situation or trigger**: The reader needs to decide how bounded, high-intensity account support fits within a durable account-based operating model.
- **Question, tension, or job**: How can a team concentrate 1:1 depth on a material opportunity without creating reactive sales support or a sixth ABM motion?
- **Current belief or default behavior**: Pursuit Marketing may be interpreted primarily as a sprint, a campaign type, or a transferable coverage model.
- **Desired belief, decision, or action**: Separate the source-reported practice from Rolf's adaptation, then use admission, context, co-ownership, measurement, and exit controls to judge applicability.
- **Communication job**: Explain, diagnose, and challenge overgeneralization.

## Direction

- **One-sentence thesis**: Pursuit Marketing is best treated as a bounded account-based initiative whose scalability depends on service-design controls, not as a sixth Growth Motion or a coverage benchmark.
- **Strategic angle**: Copy transferable governance and decision logic, not the memorable reported account number.
- **Rolf's original contribution**: Mapping the public case to the Enterprise Growth System, preserving a no-change framework verdict, distinguishing durable motions from bounded initiatives, and translating the case into explicit operator controls.
- **Why this matters**: Without admission, ownership, context, measurement, and exit discipline, concentrated account support can become reactive work that consumes strategic capacity.
- **Narrative logic**: Attribute the public practice; separate report from analysis; test it against the Enterprise Growth System; preserve the no-change verdict; translate it into bounded operator controls.
- **Practical implication or decision rule**: Copy the governance and decision logic only when the situation and operating preconditions fit; do not copy the reported account number.
- **Fit conditions**: A material opportunity or account challenge, a real decision window, plausible marketing influence, account-team co-ownership, sufficient context, and a bounded exit.
- **Non-fit conditions or limitations**: One public practitioner interview; unaudited coverage; no causal performance, universal taxonomy, or cross-case validation.
- **Strongest reasonable counterargument**: A bounded initiative can still become reactive support even with written controls if incentives, capacity, and account-team ownership are weak.
