# Strategic Creative Direction: HP DemandGen Box Model

## Decision job

Decide the channel-neutral argument that helps senior B2B marketers see why a campaign for an insufficiently recognized problem should sequence changes in buyer belief before presenting products, and why that narrative architecture needs an execution contract.

## Audience and desired change

- **Primary audience**: Senior B2B marketing and demand-generation leaders working with internal teams or agencies.
- **Situation or trigger**: They are designing a campaign for a complex offer whose underlying problem is not yet salient to the intended buyer.
- **Question, tension, or job**: How should content move a buyer from an unrecognized problem to a credible product choice without introducing the product too early?
- **Current belief or default behavior**: Start with an asset list, product messages, and funnel-stage labels.
- **Desired belief, decision, or action**: Define the required belief change, evidence, content job, owner, and transition signal for each stage before production.
- **Communication job**: Reflect, explain, and guide.
- **Evidence status**: The architecture and Rolf's role are user-confirmed and documented in internal source material. Campaign performance is unknown.

## Direction

- **One-sentence thesis**: The product belonged in the last box; a strong buyer-belief architecture becomes executable only when every box also has an operational contract.
- **Strategic angle**: Contrast buyer-belief progression with asset-first campaign planning.
- **Rolf's original contribution**: Rolf developed the five-part Box Model for the HP DemandGen concept and now adds a retrospective implementation lesson: the architecture was clearer than the instructions and governance needed to make it reproducible through an agency.
- **Why this matters now**: AI and faster content production make it easier to produce assets before the intended change in buyer belief and the handoffs between assets are clear.
- **Narrative logic**: Start with HP having an answer to a question many intended buyers did not yet appear to ask; explain the five Boxes; show why product belonged last; surface the implementation gap; end with the operating contract Rolf would add today.
- **Practical implication or decision rule**: For every box, specify the buyer change, evidence, content task, exclusions, owner, and transition criterion before commissioning assets.
- **Fit conditions**: Complex B2B offers; low or uneven problem awareness; category education; several linked content experiences; external production partners.
- **Non-fit conditions or limitations**: The product need not come last when buyers already recognize the problem and are actively evaluating named solutions. The model is a design heuristic, not a universal funnel or a claim of campaign effectiveness.
- **Strongest reasonable counterargument**: Execution shortfalls can have several causes, and a sound architecture alone does not establish that briefing or governance was the decisive weakness.

## Personal Take Checkpoint

### 1. Unprompted reaction

- **Participation**: offered and completed
- **Raw reaction received**: yes
- **Raw reaction retention**: not-retained

### 2. Candidate cards

Discarded candidates and the raw reaction are not retained. The selected direction combines a user-confirmed experience with a retrospective operating rule.

### 3. Critical check

- The self-assessment is Rolf's interpretation, not a verified diagnosis of every execution problem.
- Product-last is context-dependent and must not be presented as a universal rule.
- The story must not blame the agency, imply proven outcomes, or disclose confidential details.
- The personal reflection adds decision value only when translated into a concrete execution contract.

### 4. Decision record

- **Outcome**: revised
- **Origin**: user-supplied
- **Disclosure level**: `L3`
- **Selected take**: Rolf developed the Box Model; in retrospect, the architecture was stronger than his ability at the time to enable reliable agency execution.
- **Critical-check resolution**: Present the implementation gap as Rolf's retrospective learning, avoid agency blame and causal certainty, state fit conditions, and provide the operating details he would add today.
- **Approved wording**: I developed the Box Model because HP had a strong answer, while many intended buyers did not yet appear to be asking the underlying question. The product therefore belonged in the last box. In retrospect, the architecture was further developed than my ability at the time to operationalize it so an agency could execute it reliably. Today I would give every box a defined buyer change, evidence base, content job, owner, and transition criterion.
- **Meaning every variant must preserve**: Sequence content around changes in buyer belief, keep the product until the problem and evaluation logic are credible, and convert narrative architecture into an explicit execution contract.
- **Details or claims that must not be published**: Names, contact details, budgets, targets, internal KPIs, unsupported competitor comparisons, unverified rankings, campaign-performance claims, or confidential implementation details.

## Direction invariants

- Preserve the five-Box sequence, product-last insight, personal implementation lesson, contextual limits, and absence of outcome claims.
- Execution may change expression, structure, length, and channel conventions.
- Execution may not turn product-last into a universal rule, blame an agency, or imply proven performance.
