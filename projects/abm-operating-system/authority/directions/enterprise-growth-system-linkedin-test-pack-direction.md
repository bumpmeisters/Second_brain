## Audience and desired change

- **Primary audience**: Heads of Marketing in large enterprises.
- **Situation or trigger**: Fragmented campaigns, competing motions, weak operating alignment, or pressure to explain commercial progression.
- **Question, tension, or job**: Does the framework help diagnose and discuss recognizable management problems?
- **Desired belief, decision, or action**: Qualified resonance, challenge, or discussion about the operating-model distinctions.

## Direction

- **One-sentence thesis**: Enterprise marketing improves when commercial motions, dynamic portfolios, execution gates, governance, measurement, and enabling systems operate as one adaptive growth system.
- **Strategic angle**: Teach the system through management tensions, diagnostic situations, and decision rules rather than a conventional framework summary.
- **Rolf's original contribution**: The Enterprise Growth System and its translation into recognizable management tensions and decisions.
- **Desired response**: Readers recognize, question, or discuss the distinctions without treating resonance as validation.
- **Practical implication or decision rule**: Use the posts to test comprehension and resonance, not framework truth.
- **Fit conditions**: Large-enterprise marketing contexts in which coordination, portfolio choices, and commercial progression are meaningful management problems.
- **Non-fit conditions or limitations**: The posts are private drafts, the framework lacks real-world validation, and engagement cannot establish framework truth or causal performance.
- **Strongest reasonable counterargument**: A coherent explanation may resonate because it is memorable rather than because the system improves decisions or outcomes.
