# Testing the Enterprise Growth System #1: What ServiceNow Teaches Us About Pursuit Marketing

> **Private draft — not approved for publication.** This article remains inside the ABM Operating System until its exact content and website channel pass Gate G4.

ABM teams are often forced into a false choice: give a handful of strategic accounts meaningful attention, or spread shallow personalization across a larger portfolio.

ServiceNow's Pursuit Marketing model suggests a third option: preserve the depth of 1:1 ABM, but concentrate it on one qualified opportunity or account challenge for a bounded period.

The obvious lesson is speed. The more important lesson is service design.

A sprint does not make account-based work scalable by itself. It becomes scalable only when the team controls admission, protects commercial ownership, works from account context, measures progression, and exits deliberately. Without those controls, “pursuit” becomes a more attractive name for reactive sales support.

That distinction is what I wanted to test against the Enterprise Growth System.

## The case in context

In a public *Let's talk ABM* interview, ServiceNow EMEA Pursuit Marketing Lead Celia Slack described Pursuit Marketing as a branch of ABM. Where durable account coverage resembles a marathon, a pursuit is a sprint aligned to a specific opportunity or challenge.

The triggers can include a major deal, an access or relationship problem, a leadership change, renewal, retention risk, downsell risk, or an expansion opportunity. ServiceNow's team reportedly combines proactive planning with sales leadership and the capacity to respond to urgent requests.

Slack also described three selection considerations: **opportunity, achievability, and collaboration**. Sales is expected to contribute context, access, time, and follow-through. If collaboration does not materialize, the ABM team can move its scarce capacity elsewhere.

The interview reported that one practitioner could cover roughly 20 pursuits in a year, compared with roughly five accounts under durable 1:1 coverage. That number is useful as a capacity hypothesis, not as a benchmark. The interview did not provide normalized workload, cohort definitions, audited commercial outcomes, or the detailed operating procedure behind the comparison.

This matters because ServiceNow was not describing a beginner's shortcut. The case sits inside a mature enterprise ABM environment with executive sponsorship, experienced practitioners, sales alignment, CRM tagging, dashboards, manual evidence capture, and access to wider field, industry, customer, and executive resources.

Copying the sprint while ignoring that system would copy the visible tactic and leave behind the conditions that make it work.

## The Enterprise Growth System test

The Enterprise Growth System separates two questions that ABM teams regularly collapse:

1. **Where should the business invest?** This is the Growth Motion decision.
2. **How should the team execute and learn?** This is the Growth Execution Loop.

Its five Growth Motions are Market Demand, Account-based Demand, 1:few ABM, 1:1 Strategic ABM, and Customer Expansion. They describe durable investment states—not campaign formats, tiers, channels, or temporary initiatives.

That leads to the first and most consequential conclusion.

### Pursuit Marketing is not a sixth Growth Motion

A pursuit is an initiative format inside a selected motion.

A high-stakes new-logo opportunity may justify temporary 1:1 Strategic ABM depth. A renewal or downsell risk belongs in Customer Expansion or recovery. A weakly evidenced account without access or sales commitment may belong in Account-based Demand until its readiness changes.

Urgency tells us how quickly to act. It does not tell us which Growth Motion is correct.

If every new delivery format becomes a new motion, the operating model degenerates into a catalogue of campaigns. Keeping the motion stable while allowing temporary interventions gives the organization both strategic consistency and execution flexibility.

## What the case confirms

### 1. Collaboration is an admission criterion, not a cultural aspiration

“Sales alignment” is usually discussed as a relationship goal. Pursuit Marketing turns it into a capacity decision.

If the account team does not contribute context, access, decisions, and follow-through, marketing cannot manufacture a credible account intervention. Continuing anyway rewards asset requests, hides opportunity cost, and leaves marketing accountable for a commercial state it does not own.

The transferable principle is stronger than “work closely with sales”:

> Scarce ABM capacity should enter an account only when the commercial team accepts explicit co-ownership.

### 2. Context must precede content

The ServiceNow account scenarios begin with different problems: relationship, reach, education, deal acceleration, retention, or growth. Those differences should change the hypothesis, buying-group focus, experience, evidence, and exit condition.

An executive dinner, direct-mail package, workshop, or personalized landing page is not a strategy. It becomes useful only when it removes a specific constraint in a specific account state.

The sprint therefore begins by translating the request for an asset into a customer or commercial progression problem.

### 3. The account team must retain commercial ownership

Pursuit marketers can diagnose, orchestrate, create perspectives, improve access, and accelerate coordinated action. They are not the sales team, the negotiation owner, or the customer-success function.

A bounded intervention needs an explicit handback. Otherwise the sprint ends on the calendar while its work remains attached to marketing indefinitely.

### 4. Qualitative progress belongs in the measurement system

The interview described reputation, relationships, revenue, and retention, alongside CRM tags, dashboards, manual notes, and cohort comparisons.

For complex accounts, progression can include access to a previously unreachable executive, broader buying-group coverage, a validated workshop, improved relationship evidence, or movement from an untested assumption to a shared decision.

Those signals are not substitutes for commercial outcomes. They are evidence about whether the account is becoming more capable of producing them. A responsible measurement model records both the signal and its limitations; it does not turn one executive response into a causal revenue claim.

## What should not be copied

Three elements need strong boundaries.

First, the reported 20-versus-five coverage comparison should not become a staffing promise. Capacity depends on maturity, account complexity, sprint duration, practitioner experience, available content, data quality, sales participation, and the scope of “coverage.”

Second, ServiceNow's environment should not be treated as the minimum viable technology stack. A traditional enterprise or resource-constrained mid-market organization can pilot the mechanism with manual research and a shared tracker. It cannot remove the need for evidence, ownership, prioritization, and exit rules.

Third, one practitioner's view that one-to-many is not “real ABM” should not be promoted into a universal taxonomy. The useful warning is against shallow personalization. The Enterprise Growth System keeps Market Demand and Account-based Demand as legitimate motions with different jobs and investment logic.

## The transferable operating model

A qualified pursuit sprint can be reduced to ten decisions:

1. Translate the incoming request into a deal, relationship, reach, education, recovery, or expansion state.
2. Select the durable Growth Motion from the account state—not from urgency or channel.
3. Test opportunity, achievability, collaboration, evidence, access, delivery capacity, and opportunity cost.
4. Contract the desired state change, window, owners, contributions, non-actions, measures, and exit.
5. Diagnose the business, buying-group, relationship, perception, delivery, and customer-health constraints.
6. Write one falsifiable pursuit hypothesis.
7. Map roles, influence, stance, relationship evidence, access paths, concerns, and single-thread risk.
8. Design the smallest experience capable of advancing the decision.
9. Continue, adapt, escalate, pause, recover, demote, or stop from observed evidence.
10. Record progression, commercial contribution, capacity cost, evidence limitations, learning, and handoff.

The companion [Qualified Pursuit Sprint operator playbook](qualified-pursuit-sprint-operator-playbook.md) turns these decisions into an admission gate, sprint contract, hypothesis template, buying-group map, response rules, measurement logic, and exit checklist.

This is my project adaptation of the case, not ServiceNow's official operating procedure.

## Where it fits—and where it does not

The model is strongest for complex enterprise or upper-mid-market accounts with a material opportunity or customer-state problem, a real decision window, a committed commercial owner, and enough evidence to form a credible hypothesis.

It needs adaptation in four common environments:

- **Immature ABM organizations:** start with one sponsor-backed pursuit and borrowed expertise. Use the pilot to build the operating discipline, not to claim scale.
- **Resource-constrained teams:** reserve a small capacity pool, admit only high-materiality cases, and reuse modules while protecting account-specific reasoning.
- **Traditional enterprises:** expect fragmented data, manual intelligence, regional rights, and heavier dependence on account teams.
- **Industrial, services, or channel-led businesses:** redefine progression around technical proof, diagnostic access, sites, partner relationships, adoption, or trust—not SaaS engagement signals.

Do not use pursuit as a wrapper for production requests, broad demand generation, negotiation support, or expansion pressure when customer health and realized value are weak.

## The blueprint verdict

The ServiceNow case does not justify changing the Enterprise Growth System.

It confirms four existing principles:

- Growth Motions are durable investment states, not campaigns.
- Readiness includes value, hypothesis, access, ownership, delivery, and capacity.
- Relationship capital and buying-group coverage are operating assets.
- Marketing can accelerate commercial work without taking commercial ownership.

It also creates one useful pattern to keep testing: **time-bounded strategic depth inside a durable Growth Motion**.

That pattern remains an observation. One practitioner interview from one mature SaaS environment is not enough to make it universal. The next useful evidence would be an independently documented pursuit model in a non-SaaS or resource-constrained business.

The practical conclusion is nevertheless clear:

> The scalable part of Pursuit Marketing is not the sprint. It is the discipline that decides which work deserves a sprint—and when the sprint must end.

That is a stronger operating principle than “move faster,” and a much safer one to transfer.
