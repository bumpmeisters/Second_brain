# ABM Situation Diagnostic Synthetic Evaluation

## Decision Status

- **Purpose:** Test whether diagnostic version 0.1.0 produces materially different recommendations for an advanced enterprise, an immature enterprise, and a resource-constrained mid-market organization.
- **Evidence boundary:** All organizations, inputs, decisions, and expected consequences are synthetic.
- **Allowed conclusion:** The diagnostic discriminates among the three constructed scenarios and remains internally consistent with the Enterprise Growth System.
- **Prohibited conclusion:** The diagnostic is validated, predicts business outcomes, improves real decisions, or is ready for client use.
- **Review gate:** G6 pending.

## Evaluation Design

The three scenarios deliberately vary:

- account economics and buying complexity;
- operating maturity;
- sales and executive commitment;
- data and workflow readiness;
- content and proof;
- team and budget;
- customer-state coverage;
- degree of repeatability.

The same diagnostic version is applied to all three. Recommendations pass only if situation, motion, resources, first step, measures, and stop rules differ materially.

## Scenario A — Advanced Global Enterprise

### Synthetic input

| Dimension | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Business | Global enterprise technology provider |
| Commercial objective | Improve growth across 250 selected accounts while protecting scarce specialist capacity |
| Economics | High-value, 12-24 month complex sales; significant installed customer base |
| Current model | Two years of regional ABM programs, defined tiers, CRM workflows, and an established central team |
| Evidence | Repeatable account-stage records and seller adoption in four regions; attribution remains uncertain |
| People | Central ABM/ops/content team, regional field marketers, account executives, customer success, executive sponsors |
| Data/technology | Integrated CRM and marketing data, explainable signals, role mapping, permissions, dashboards |
| Weakness | Regions use inconsistent service levels; 1:1 demand exceeds capacity; customer and prospect work compete for the same specialists |
| Content/proof | Strong proof library and reusable modules; account-exclusive insight varies |

### Decision brief

#### Evidence boundary

Every fact above is a synthetic assumption. The routing demonstrates expected framework behavior only.

#### Dominant intervention need

**Scaling**, with a targeted **Transformation** workstream for service consistency and portfolio governance.

Rationale:

- prerequisites, workflows, adoption, and repeated operation are assumed to exist;
- the primary decision is how to expand coverage without weakening service, evidence, or response capacity;
- inconsistent regional service contracts and competing capacity require operating-model correction, not a return to Foundation.

#### Growth Motion portfolio

- **Account-based Demand:** Maintain selected-account coverage for the 250-account population.
- **1:few ABM:** Admit only clusters with one shared change, buying situation, and value hypothesis.
- **1:1 Strategic ABM:** Limit to a small, capacity-backed portfolio with account-exclusive thesis, access, owner, and evidence.
- **Customer Expansion:** Manage healthy installed customers separately from net-new accounts; route poor-health customers to recovery.
- **Market Demand:** Use selectively for category relevance and market learning, not as the account-portfolio control system.

#### Resource configuration

**Federated specialist configuration.**

- central team owns doctrine, data objects, service definitions, measurement, reusable components, and quality;
- regions own local commercial context and activation;
- named account/customer owners lead decisions;
- a shared portfolio board allocates scarce 1:1, pursuit, content, and executive capacity.

#### First bounded validation step

Run a synthetic portfolio recalibration on one region:

1. classify accounts by managed state and current evidence;
2. separate strategic tier, current priority, motion, and service depth;
3. apply explicit 1:1/1:few admission and exit rules;
4. compare capacity demand before and after routing;
5. identify decisions that differ from the current tier model.

This remains a design exercise until applied to approved real inputs.

#### Measures

- portfolio decisions and reasons;
- capacity requested versus available;
- service-level consistency;
- buying-group and relationship coverage;
- account/customer progression;
- demotions, holds, exits, and unresolved evidence;
- no causal revenue claim from influenced pipeline.

#### Stop / defer

- no broader automation until service objects, evidence traces, and response ownership are consistent;
- no automatic 1:1 admission from account value or regional pressure;
- no expansion activation for unhealthy customers;
- no claim that scale readiness has been empirically demonstrated.

## Scenario B — Immature Enterprise

### Synthetic input

| Dimension | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Business | Traditional B2B services enterprise |
| Commercial objective | "Launch ABM" to repair weak pipeline within two quarters |
| Economics | Deal values vary widely; account profitability and target concentration are unknown |
| Current model | Sales nominated 500 accounts from preference lists; marketing uses MQLs and campaigns |
| Evidence | Fragmented CRM, duplicate accounts, little buying-group data, no common progression definitions |
| People | Two demand marketers; no ABM owner; sales commitment is verbal and inconsistent |
| Executive support | Sponsor wants a platform and 1:1 personalization quickly |
| Customer state | Existing customers and prospects mixed in one list; health and white space are not documented |
| Content/proof | Generic assets; limited use-case proof; no modular account content system |

### Decision brief

#### Evidence boundary

Every fact above is a synthetic assumption.

#### Dominant intervention need

**Foundation.**

Rationale:

- economics, target logic, customer state, ownership, evidence, capacity, and proof are insufficient for reliable account investment;
- the request for technology and 1:1 execution precedes an operating decision;
- launching a broad program would scale ambiguity.

#### Growth Motion portfolio

- **Market Demand:** Continue category and problem learning where buyer relevance is unclear.
- **Account-based Demand:** Use only a small research cohort after minimum ICP and economic criteria are defined.
- **1:few ABM:** Defer until a shared change and value hypothesis is evidenced.
- **1:1 Strategic ABM:** Do not start.
- **Customer Expansion:** Separate customers from prospects and establish health/value evidence before routing.

#### Resource configuration

**Lean Foundation configuration.**

- name one accountable program owner;
- recruit a small sales/customer working group;
- use a manual evidence and decision ledger;
- repair only the identity and CRM fields needed for the first decision;
- do not buy an ABM platform.

#### First bounded validation step

Build a 15-25 account evidence cohort:

1. define the commercial problem and minimum ICP/economic criteria;
2. separate prospects, customers, and unhealthy customers;
3. record known roles, relationships, use cases, triggers, and unknowns;
4. require named commercial owners and follow-through;
5. test whether a smaller actionable portfolio can be constructed.

#### Measures

- percentage of cohort with clear economics, state, owner, and next decision;
- identity and ownership exceptions;
- evidence gaps resolved;
- seller follow-through;
- accounts excluded or rerouted;
- no pipeline target presented as proof of Foundation progress.

#### Stop / defer

- no 1:1 program, advanced personalization, AI scoring, platform procurement, or large target-account launch;
- stop if sales will not provide owners, context, or follow-through;
- do not promise pipeline repair inside an arbitrary two-quarter window.

## Scenario C — Resource-Constrained Mid-Market

### Synthetic input

| Dimension | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Business | Mid-market B2B software provider |
| Commercial objective | Create more qualified opportunities in 65 named accounts |
| Economics | €80k-€250k annual value; six-to-nine month cycle; concentration supports selected-account focus |
| Current model | Basic CRM and marketing automation; one useful target-account definition |
| Evidence | Three recurring use cases; modest first-party engagement; seller knowledge is stronger than third-party intent |
| People | Two marketers, six sellers, one RevOps generalist; one credible sales champion |
| Budget | Limited; no dedicated ABM platform or 1:1 content team |
| Customer state | Mostly net-new; existing-customer health data is incomplete |
| Content/proof | Strong case evidence for one use case; weak modularity elsewhere |

### Decision brief

#### Evidence boundary

Every fact above is a synthetic assumption.

#### Dominant intervention need

**Activation**, bounded by lean capacity.

Rationale:

- economics, account population, owner group, and one repeatable use case are sufficient for a small test;
- the organization should not imitate a specialist enterprise operating model;
- one champion is enough to start learning, but not enough to claim Scaling.

#### Growth Motion portfolio

- **Account-based Demand:** Maintain light, coordinated coverage for the 65-account population.
- **1:few ABM:** Test one six-to-eight-account cluster sharing the same trigger, outcome, buying situation, and use-case proof.
- **1:1 Strategic ABM:** Defer; economics and specialist capacity do not justify account-exclusive service.
- **Customer Expansion:** Defer as an explicit motion until health and adoption evidence improve.
- **Market Demand:** Continue narrowly where the broader category needs explanation.

#### Resource configuration

**Lean Activation configuration.**

- one marketer owns the cluster and evidence;
- one seller champion co-leads, with explicit actions from participating sellers;
- RevOps maintains a simple account and buying-group view;
- use existing CRM, manual research, one reusable proof module, and limited channels;
- cap active cluster size and weekly workload.

#### First bounded validation step

Run one six-to-eight-account 1:few design test:

1. verify one shared change and value hypothesis;
2. map expected roles and relationship gaps;
3. define one customer-useful experience and seller follow-through;
4. set leading progression evidence and exit rules;
5. compare the cluster with a small Account-based Demand observation group without claiming causality.

#### Measures

- shared-hypothesis validity;
- role and relationship coverage;
- two-way conversations and next-decision evidence;
- seller actions completed;
- time and capacity consumed;
- accounts removed because the cluster assumption failed;
- opportunity evidence reported separately from influence.

#### Stop / defer

- no platform purchase, complex scoring, broad personalization, or 1:1 program;
- do not scale beyond the cluster until another seller can reproduce the operating contract;
- stop 1:few if accounts share only industry or content interest.

## Cross-Scenario Comparison

| Decision | Advanced enterprise | Immature enterprise | Resource-constrained mid-market |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dominant need | Scaling + targeted Transformation | Foundation | Bounded Activation |
| Portfolio breadth | Multiple coordinated motions | Market learning + small Account-based Demand research cohort | Account-based Demand + one 1:few test |
| 1:1 | Capacity-gated small portfolio | Prohibited | Deferred |
| 1:few | Multiple validated clusters possible | Deferred | One six-to-eight-account test |
| Customer Expansion | Separate governed motion | First separate customers and establish health | Defer until health evidence improves |
| Resource model | Federated specialist | Lean Foundation | Lean Activation |
| Technology | Govern and rationalize existing stack | Do not procure platform | Use existing tools and manual evidence |
| First step | Regional portfolio recalibration | Build minimum evidence cohort | Run one bounded cluster test |
| Primary failure risk | Scaling inconsistency and capacity overload | Tool/campaign-first activity without readiness | Copying enterprise complexity or scaling from one champion |

The recommendations differ materially in intervention need, motion mix, resource configuration, technology posture, first validation step, and stop rules.

## Findings

1. A weighted maturity score is unnecessary and would hide decisive missing prerequisites.
2. Situation routing and Growth Motion routing must remain separate.
3. The resource configuration changes as much as the motion recommendation.
4. "Do not build yet" is a required output, not an optional caveat.
5. Resource-constrained firms need narrower scope and stronger stop rules, not a diluted enterprise checklist.
6. Synthetic testing can expose internal contradictions and generic recommendations, but it cannot test adoption, usefulness, outcome quality, or organizational politics.

## Framework Consequence

- **Canonical Enterprise Growth System change:** None.
- **Diagnostic change:** None required after the first design-trace review.
- **Lifecycle status:** Diagnostic remains `draft`; framework remains `draft`.
- **G6 consequence:** The synthetic evaluation package is ready for review, but private consulting use remains prohibited because the work lacks real validation and no client-data contract is in force.
- **Next evidence path:** Continue public-evidence challenges that specifically target Market Demand, 1:few validity, mid-market constraints, customer/partner perspectives, and failed implementations.
