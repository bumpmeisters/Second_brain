# Universal Robots Channel-First Customer Expansion

## Decision Status

- **Record purpose:** Challenge Customer Expansion and account orchestration in an industrial, channel-led environment where a partner or integrator can be both route to market and managed relationship.
- **Current review gate:** Gate G5 approved by Rolf on 2026-07-23.
- **Allowed use:** Private research, comparison, framework challenge, and evidence planning.
- **Prohibited use:** Publication, Universal Robots endorsement claims, causal outcome claims, or canonical framework edits without explicit approval.

## Executive Case Capsule

- **Business problem:** Universal Robots sought to expand from one plant or country into other sites of a global manufacturer while preserving partner relationships and creating a consistent customer experience. (source: Channel-First ABM How Universal Robots Wins Expansion Deals (Episode 71).md)
- **Operating context:** Collaborative robots are sold through regional partners and integrators. The end customer, local plant, global account team, regional organization, and partner may hold different access, incentives, ownership, and information. (source: Channel-First ABM How Universal Robots Wins Expansion Deals (Episode 71).md)
- **Mechanism:** Select expansion accounts using commercial potential plus collaboration readiness; decide whether to lead through the partner, the end customer, or a joint route; coordinate a shared cadence; and revalidate ownership and priorities when account personnel or KPIs change.
- **Reported consequence:** The source describes improved access, awareness, orchestration, and multi-site expansion practice, but supplies no audited revenue, expansion-rate, or causal result.
- **Why it matters to the Enterprise Growth System:** The case tests whether Customer Expansion needs an explicit ecosystem-route object in addition to account, buying group, relationship, and initiative objects.

## Review Question Engine

1. Is the managed state multi-site expansion, net-new entry into a plant, partner development, or a mixture requiring separate objects?
2. When does an integrator act as a route-to-market partner, an influenced buyer, a co-seller, or a second customer?
3. Which party owns customer access, account truth, next action, and commercial follow-through?
4. Does existing customer success justify Customer Expansion even when a new site behaves like a new logo?
5. What evidence supports partner-first, end-customer-first, or joint activation?
6. Which relationship, incentive, legal, data, and capacity conditions must be true?
7. What changes when the account owner, regional priority, KPI, partner, or customer sponsor changes?
8. Which experiences require local customization and which can use a shared industrial or use-case pattern?
9. What customer progression is observable before revenue, and what does not establish causality?
10. Does this case justify a canonical ecosystem extension or only a channel-led adaptation?

Counter-questions: Is the framework treating a channel as just another tactic? Is an existing logo hiding a genuinely net-new buying group? Is partner cooperation assumed because a contract exists? Would the partner and end customer describe the same value and experience?

Stop rules: Stop promotion if the partner role cannot be distinguished from customer and seller roles. Stop deep service when collaboration, access, customer health, commercial ownership, or capacity is absent. Revalidate routing when owners, KPIs, partners, regions, or customer sponsors change.

## Enterprise Growth System Mapping

### Growth Motion

- **Primary motion:** Customer Expansion, because the managed state is growth across sites, countries, or business units of an existing customer relationship.
- **Conditional motion:** 1:1 Strategic ABM when one site/account has a distinct high-value thesis, access, owner, and scarce service capacity; 1:few ABM when manufacturers share a validated use case and context; Account-based Demand for scalable awareness and warming.
- **Why:** Site-level newness changes the route and relationship problem, but does not automatically erase existing-customer evidence or convert the whole account to net-new acquisition.
- **Routing exclusions:** Partner enablement is not itself a Growth Motion. Pursuit or RFP support is a bounded initiative inside the motion.

## Transferable Mechanism

### Decision job

Choose and govern the route by which an existing customer account can expand into another site or business unit without creating conflicting partner, seller, and customer experiences.

### Minimum prerequisites

- Credible expansion hypothesis and customer value.
- Named end-customer, seller, regional, and partner owners.
- Clear access, data, incentive, and follow-through rights.
- Healthy enough existing relationship and delivery capacity.
- Shared evidence record and permission to pause or reroute.

### Operating sequence

1. Define the customer, site, current relationship, desired state, value, and time window.
2. Map the end-customer buying group and the partner/integrator relationship separately.
3. Decide partner-first, end-customer-first, or joint route with explicit rationale.
4. Set RACI, service depth, evidence sources, cadence, and exit rules.
5. Orchestrate one customer progression across digital, human, event, expert, and partner actions.
6. Review shared evidence and decide continue, change route, reduce depth, pause, or exit.
7. Revalidate the contract after any owner, KPI, region, partner, sponsor, or customer-state change.
8. Capture reusable industrial/use-case learning without exposing account-exclusive context.

### Decision and stop rules

- Do not run deep ABM without an accountable sales owner and an actionable partner route.
- Do not infer expansion readiness from installed product alone.
- Do not bypass a partner without explicit commercial and relationship justification.
- Reduce depth when access, health, capacity, collaboration, or value no longer supports it.

### Measures

- Relationship and role coverage across customer and partner.
- Site/business-unit progression and next-decision evidence.
- Partner and seller follow-through.
- Opportunity, expansion, adoption, and retention outcomes with attribution limits.
- Capacity consumed, conflicts, pauses, reroutes, and reusable learning.

## Applicability Boundaries

### Strong fit

- Industrial or technology businesses with indirect, partner, distributor, or integrator routes.
- Existing customers with multi-site, multi-country, or multi-business-unit expansion potential.

### Adapt before use

- Direct-sales businesses, mixed direct/indirect models, partner concentration, regulated data sharing, or weak installed-base health.

### Do not use

- As justification to bypass partner agreements, treat every new site as 1:1 ABM, or claim expansion impact from engagement alone.

## Risks And Failure Modes

- Dual ownership becomes no ownership.
- Partner and customer receive conflicting messages or incentives.
- A new account owner or KPI silently invalidates earlier commitments.
- Global strategy ignores local plant economics, safety, procurement, or partner reality.
- Direct access is pursued at the cost of trust.
- Manual evidence becomes a reporting burden without decision rules.
