# Backbase AI-Native Financial-Services Transformation

## Decision Status

- **Record purpose:** Test whether an AI-native ABM transformation preserves motion routing, explicit account decisions, evidence quality, and accountable human control.
- **Current review gate:** Gate G5 approved by Rolf on 2026-07-23.
- **Allowed use:** Private research, framework challenge, AI-assurance design, and comparison.
- **Prohibited use:** Publication, Backbase or Fullfunnel endorsement claims, verified performance claims, production AI approval, or canonical framework edits without explicit review.

## Executive Case Capsule

- **Business problem:** Backbase had a narrow global market of about 3,000 mid-to-large banks, 18-24 month sales cycles, six regional teams, and siloed marketing functions with inconsistent ABM definitions and KPIs. It also sought to reposition around an AI-native banking operating system in a conservative buying environment. (source: 🕵🏻‍♂️ ABM Case Study How Backbase built an AI-native ABM motion.md)
- **Operating context:** Complex global fintech sales require long-horizon account progression, regulated-buyer trust, local sales participation, buying-group coverage, and careful use of first-, second-, and third-party signals.
- **Mechanism:** Pilot in a receptive region; define a five-stage account progression; establish a manual quality benchmark; build a transparent multi-source signal layer; automate prioritization, research, committee mapping, and content adaptation under human review; retain human decisions for 1:1 allocation, outreach, and executive relationships.
- **Reported consequence:** The source reports six discovery calls in the pilot and a change from 60-70% cold accounts to 60-70% aware, signaled, or engaged accounts after nine months. These figures are unaudited and do not establish causal impact.
- **Why it matters to the Enterprise Growth System:** The case directly tests the framework's rule that AI should assist evidence and preparation while humans retain commercial priority, motion, depth, and consequential relationship decisions.

## Review Question Engine

1. Which business decision improved: account priority, stage, buying-group coverage, content relevance, seller action, opportunity, or revenue?
2. Which parts are deterministic workflow, model inference, generative output, or human judgment?
3. What manual baseline defined acceptable quality, and what eval cases, graders, and thresholds replaced it?
4. Are the five account stages observations, inferred states, workflow queues, or Growth Motions?
5. Who decides entry into 1:1, 1:few, Account-based Demand, or Customer Expansion?
6. What evidence explains why an account is prioritized and which response follows?
7. How are identity uncertainty, stale data, false positives, contradictory seller knowledge, and sensitive data handled?
8. What human review occurs before CRM writes, seller delivery, content publication, and customer contact?
9. What model, prompt, policy, data, or workflow change triggers reevaluation?
10. Do the reported account-state and discovery-call shifts establish value, or only operating activity?

Counter-questions: Is "AI-native" a positioning label masking manual operations? Is a stage framework replacing motion decisions? Is the human merely approving recommendations too quickly to provide meaningful oversight? Is automation scaling a sound workflow or scaling error?

Stop rules: Stop automation expansion when evidence provenance, eval quality, owner, lawful use, or rollback is unknown. Stop promotion when reported activity is treated as revenue causality. Keep commercial depth allocation and consequential relationship actions human-owned.

## Enterprise Growth System Mapping

### Growth Motion

- **Primary motion:** Account-based Demand for prioritizing and warming the narrow 3,000-account universe.
- **Conditional motion:** 1:few ABM for validated clusters with a shared change hypothesis; 1:1 Strategic ABM for human-selected Active Focus accounts with access, value, readiness, and capacity; Customer Expansion for existing customers with adoption, health, or white-space evidence.
- **Why:** The five stages describe account state and workflow, not five separate Growth Motions.
- **Routing exclusions:** AI priority, Future Pipeline, Active Focus, and opportunity support are operating states or initiatives; none is a sixth motion.

## Transferable Mechanism

### Decision job

Scale account intelligence and preparation with AI while preserving explainable evidence, human commercial allocation, controlled writes, and reviewable customer-facing actions.

### Minimum prerequisites

- Explicit account states and Growth Motion mapping.
- Manual reference workflow with accepted quality examples and known failures.
- Named business, technical, data, risk, and human decision owners.
- Approved data sources, identity rules, access controls, and retention.
- Task-specific evals, review gates, monitoring, versioning, and rollback.

### Operating sequence

1. Define the commercial decision and separate state, motion, service depth, and action.
2. Run the workflow manually and capture accepted, rejected, ambiguous, and high-risk cases.
3. Build an evidence-transparent signal layer with provenance, freshness, and confidence.
4. Automate one bounded task at a time: priority recommendation, research, mapping, enrichment, or adaptation.
5. Evaluate each task against the manual set and measure errors, abstentions, reviewer edits, and time saved.
6. Keep motion, depth, budget, customer contact, executive relationship, and sensitive exception decisions human-owned.
7. Gate CRM writes and publication; log versions, evidence, approvals, actions, and overrides.
8. Monitor downstream progression, ignored recommendations, false positives, stale priorities, and incidents.
9. Reevaluate after model, prompt, data, permission, workflow, market, or regulatory change.

### Decision and stop rules

- No activation from a single signal or unexplained score.
- No production expansion without task-specific eval evidence and accountable owners.
- No automated customer contact or consequential CRM write without authorized controls.
- Abstain or escalate when identity, stage, context, privacy, or seller knowledge conflicts.
- Stop if faster output increases review burden, error, or unsafe action.

### Measures

- Evidence coverage, freshness, identity confidence, and explainability.
- Task accuracy, false positives/negatives, abstentions, reviewer edits, overrides, and time-to-approve.
- Account-state transitions, buying-group coverage, seller action, opportunity, pipeline, and revenue.
- Incidents, suppressed actions, stale priorities, model drift, and cost.
- Causal claims only with appropriate comparison or clearly limited language.

## Applicability Boundaries

### Strong fit

- Narrow account universes, long cycles, repeated research/mapping work, mature RevOps, and clear human commercial ownership.

### Adapt before use

- Regulated markets, sensitive data, weak CRM identity, global data transfer, immature workflows, or limited reviewer capacity.

### Do not use

- As evidence that generative AI can autonomously run ABM, that account engagement proves revenue impact, or that a white-box label alone establishes explainability or compliance.

## Risks And Failure Modes

- Stage and score definitions create false precision.
- Automation amplifies stale or biased data.
- Buying-group enrichment misidentifies people or exposes sensitive inferences.
- Review becomes ceremonial or overloaded.
- CRM writes compound errors across regions.
- Content adaptation creates unsupported banking, product, or AI claims.
- Activity metrics are celebrated while opportunities and customer value remain unchanged.
- Model, prompt, or data changes invalidate the manual-quality claim.
