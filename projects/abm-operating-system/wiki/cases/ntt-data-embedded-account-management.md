# NTT DATA Embedded Account Management

## Decision Status

- **Purpose:** Test whether embedded account orchestration and controlled 1:1-to-1:few learning transfer strengthen the Enterprise Growth System.
- **Gate:** Comparative G3b model review approved on 2026-07-22.
- **Allowed:** Private research, framework challenge, and non-sensitive consulting-method development.
- **Prohibited:** Publication, canonical framework edits, NTT DATA endorsement claims, or reuse of reported figures as benchmarks.

## Executive Case Capsule

- **Business problem:** NTT DATA had a Center of Excellence model for ABM, but field execution struggled because central guidance could not by itself earn account-team trust, interpret local commercial pressure, or translate account knowledge into credible action. (source: “ABM Works 100% of the Time” - NTT Data’s Sloan Newman on Real Results (Episode 67).md)
- **Operating context:** The source describes a mature global IT-services organization with executive sponsorship, common ABM training, regional practitioners, several dozen 1:1 accounts, 1:few vertical programs, and account teams willing to invest time and access. These are enabling conditions, not universally available inputs.
- **Mechanism:** An ABM practitioner works as an “embedded reporter” inside the account team: listening before prescribing, translating context into outcome-focused experiences, coordinating specialist capability, and extracting reusable learning that may later be adapted for a tightly defined 1:few cluster.
- **Reported consequence:** The interview reports account and pipeline influence, awards, experimentation results, and reuse of a personalized asset in fewer than five working days. The available source does not provide audited baselines, denominators, causal analysis, or underlying judging material.
- **Blueprint relevance:** The case tests Customer Expansion x Orchestrate, the boundary between motion and service depth, and whether learning can move from 1:1 to 1:few without leaking account-exclusive context.

## Operating Context And Preconditions

### Company and commercial environment

- Global IT-services provider with complex offerings, existing customer footprints, long relationship histories, and regional variation.
- Predominantly existing-account growth for full 1:1 coverage, with conditional deal acceleration and new-logo use.
- Central ABM doctrine and training combined with regionally embedded execution.
- Several internal creative, web, specialist, leadership, and account capabilities available to practitioners.

### Resource and maturity assumptions

- Executive sponsorship protects experimentation and cross-functional participation.
- Account teams commit at least onboarding time, recurring context, access, and follow-through.
- Practitioners have listening, commercial judgment, creative adaptability, and the authority to challenge the account team.
- CRM, relationship, pipeline, customer-footprint, and account-team evidence are sufficiently accessible to form a hypothesis.
- The organization can absorb some failed experiments and demote accounts when the economics or situation changes.

### Known unknowns

- Exact 1:1 admission thresholds, regional variations, service levels, staffing ratios, and budget.
- Customer-value, health, delivery-readiness, privacy, and content-reuse controls.
- How influence is weighted, audited, or compared with untreated accounts.
- Whether lift-and-shift reuse consistently improves outcomes across different verticals and business models.

## Enterprise Growth System Mapping

### Growth Motion

- **Primary motion:** Customer Expansion because the reported full 1:1 portfolio generally requires an existing footprint and growth/share-of-wallet opportunity.
- **Conditional motion:** 1:1 Strategic ABM for a material new-logo or deal-acceleration situation without an established customer-value base.
- **Scaling branch:** 1:few ABM only when several accounts share a concrete change, buying situation, value hypothesis, and sales commitment.
- **Fallback:** Account-based Demand when evidence, access, relationship depth, customer readiness, or account-team commitment is insufficient.
- **Routing exclusion:** “White glove” and “1:1” describe service depth; they do not override the customer or commercial state.

## Transferable Mechanism: Embedded Account Learning Loop

### Decision job

Embed an ABM practitioner deeply enough to convert account-team knowledge into coordinated customer progress, while preserving independent judgment and safely reusing only the learning that survives beyond one account.

### Minimum prerequisites

- Explicit customer/commercial state, account economics, and value hypothesis.
- Account-team commitment to time, context, access, decisions, and follow-through.
- Sponsor, practitioner capacity, specialist access, clear rights, and a recurring cadence.
- Evidence on footprint, customer value/health, pipeline, relationships, buying group, delivery readiness, and risk.
- Reuse, confidentiality, measurement, demotion, and exit rules.

### Operating sequence

1. Route the case by customer/commercial state before assigning 1:1 service depth.
2. Qualify account economics, footprint, relationship path, delivery readiness, team commitment, and opportunity cost.
3. Create an embedding charter covering objective, roles, cadence, rights, contribution, boundaries, non-actions, measures, and exit.
4. Run listening-led onboarding; label customer evidence, account-team claims, interpretations, hypotheses, and unknowns separately.
5. Build one living value and relationship hypothesis, including what the customer might dispute and why the status quo could win.
6. Coordinate the smallest credible experience through the account owner, specialists, executives, partners, and customer-facing teams.
7. Run bounded experiments with guardrails, an observable response, and a decision rule.
8. Measure reputation, relationships, customer value, opportunity progress, execution, and influence with baseline and alternatives.
9. Extract the reusable kernel: situation pattern, questions, value logic, proof structure, objections, and modular components; remove account-exclusive facts.
10. Validate a 1:few cluster independently. Continue, adapt, demote, monitor, or exit through a portfolio decision.

The standalone NTT DATA playbook remains assigned to `wiki/ntt-data-embedded-account-management-playbook.md` and is scheduled for recovery wave 11. This sequence is a project adaptation, not NTT DATA’s official SOP.

### Decision and stop rules

- Do not embed when the account team will not invest time, context, access, or follow-through.
- Do not let empathy become uncritical advocacy, unlimited service, or undocumented personal dependence.
- Do not treat industry similarity or reusable content format as a valid 1:few cluster.
- Do not expand when customer health, adoption, realized value, or delivery confidence is weak.
- Reopen the hypothesis when the account team, customer leadership, opportunity, relationship, or delivery state changes.
- Demote, monitor, or stop when expected progress does not justify scarce 1:1 capacity.

### Measures

- Customer value, health, adoption, retention, and expansion readiness.
- Reputation shift, relationship strength, access, multithreading, and role coverage.
- Opportunity/portfolio progression and influence with mechanism, baseline, alternatives, and confidence.
- Account-team contribution, response time, experiment learning, capacity, demotion, and exit quality.
- Reuse quality: time to adaptation, exclusive-context leakage, cluster fit, adoption, and incremental effect.

## Applicability Boundaries

### Strong fit

- Complex enterprise or upper-mid-market accounts with material existing relationships and growth potential.
- Services, technology, industrial, or solution environments where account-team knowledge and trust materially affect growth.
- Mature or developing CoE capable of supporting regionally embedded practitioners.

### Adapt before use

- **Immature enterprise:** begin with one sponsored account and a minimal embedding charter; borrow expertise but retain evidence and exit rules.
- **Resource-constrained mid-market:** combine strategist and operator roles, reduce cadence, and restrict 1:1 to high-economics cases.
- **New-logo context:** use 1:1 Strategic ABM and replace footprint evidence with a stronger access and delivery hypothesis.
- **Channel-led environment:** include partner roles, incentives, ownership, and customer access in the embedding contract.

### Do not use

- Low-value transactional, self-serve, or shallow-relationship buying situations.
- Accounts with weak customer value, no delivery confidence, or no plausible buying-group path.
- Teams seeking content production without shared decisions or follow-through.
- Reuse that could disclose confidential, personal, contractual, or recognizable account context.

## Risks And Failure Modes

- Role capture, groupthink, boundary erosion, single-threading, bespoke inflation, and emotional portfolio lock-in.
- Customer evidence being replaced by confident internal account narratives.
- Influence inflation and award recognition being mistaken for causal proof.
- 1:1 content being generalized without a shared buyer change or value hypothesis.
- Common training becoming rigid central doctrine rather than minimum governance.
- Agency pedigree or personal charisma becoming a substitute for documented capability and process.
