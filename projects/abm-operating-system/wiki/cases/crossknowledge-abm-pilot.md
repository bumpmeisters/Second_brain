# CrossKnowledge ABM Pilot

## Decision Status

- **Record purpose:** Challenge the Enterprise Growth System with a concrete six-month pilot that shifts from MQL volume to selected accounts, weekly portfolio decisions, and account-level progression.
- **Current review gate:** Gate G5 approved by Rolf on 2026-07-23.
- **Allowed use:** Private research, pilot design, comparative pattern learning, and framework challenge.
- **Prohibited use:** Publication, CrossKnowledge or Fullfunnel endorsement claims, audited-result claims, or canonical framework edits without explicit approval.

## Executive Case Capsule

- **Business problem:** CrossKnowledge sold enterprise digital-learning solutions into Global 1000 organizations with multiple decision makers, long buying cycles, mature competitors, and budgets sometimes set years ahead. Its MQL-heavy model produced volume that sales did not trust and insufficient pipeline from target accounts. (source: The ABM Pilot Program With 60% Marketing-Sourced Enterprise Pipeline.md)
- **Operating context:** Large deals, multi-year contracts, distributed HR/L&D, IT, finance, and executive roles, limited buying windows, and a need for sustained relationship building.
- **Mechanism:** Use historical revenue and win/loss analysis to define target accounts and use cases; recruit change-ready leaders and sellers; run a controlled pilot; coordinate a small cross-functional team; review accounts weekly; add, remove, or reduce priority; and report leading indicators plus pipeline outcomes.
- **Reported consequence:** The source reports that marketing pipeline contribution rose from roughly 30-40% or 35% to 60%, and that 35 of 130 target accounts entered multiple meetings or project discussions during six months. These are publisher/client-reported figures without independent attribution evidence.
- **Why it matters to the Enterprise Growth System:** The case tests whether the Growth Execution Loop can operate as a real weekly portfolio cadence and whether pilot evidence is sufficient to scale an operating model.

## Review Question Engine

1. What problem was the pilot solving: pipeline volume, target-account access, sales trust, marketing operating model, or all four?
2. Which 130 accounts received Account-based Demand, 1:few, 1:1, or only seller-led engagement?
3. What made the pilot small: accounts, people, region, service depth, investment, or organizational scope?
4. Which account-selection evidence came from revenue analysis, win/loss interviews, current signals, or seller judgment?
5. What weekly evidence caused promotion, demotion, pause, replacement, or a changed next action?
6. Did pipeline contribution measure source, influence, acceleration, or administrative ownership?
7. What comparison or baseline would be needed to infer causal lift?
8. Did the pilot create customer value and buying progress, or only more vendor activity?
9. Which practices scaled through a playbook, and which depended on one champion AE or consultant?
10. What evidence should be required before organization-wide rollout?

Counter-questions: Is replacing MQLs with account engagement merely replacing one proxy with another? Does a 130-account list conceal insufficient service capacity? Are early wins selectively reported? Would non-champion sellers reproduce the behavior?

Stop rules: Stop causal claims without definitions and comparison. Stop scaling if role ownership, service depth, evidence quality, capacity, or adoption outside champions is unknown. Demote accounts when current evidence no longer justifies scarce attention.

## Enterprise Growth System Mapping

### Growth Motion

- **Primary motion:** Account-based Demand across the 130-account portfolio, because the program builds awareness, relationships, buying-group coverage, and readiness before active opportunities.
- **Conditional motion:** 1:few ABM for accounts sharing a proven industry/use-case change hypothesis; 1:1 Strategic ABM for the few accounts receiving deep, account-exclusive planning and seller commitment; Customer Expansion for existing customers assigned to the later expansion role.
- **Why:** A pilot, target list, podcast, LinkedIn program, or weekly meeting is not a Growth Motion. Motion depends on managed state, unit, and service depth.
- **Routing exclusions:** Do not label all 130 accounts as 1:1 ABM merely because they are named and selected.

### Growth Execution Loop

| Phase | Case evidence | Readiness implication |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial Priority | Revenue analysis, target-account criteria, leadership problem, pilot scope | Define value, unit, owner, service depth, capacity, and learning goals |
| Intelligence & Hypothesis | Win/loss interviews, use cases, triggers, journey questions, stakeholder map | Record fact, seller view, hypothesis, unknown, and disqualifier |
| Orchestrate | Change agents, core/support roles, shared KPIs, weekly working session | Set commitments, decision rights, workload, handoffs, and exit |
| Content & Experience | Thought leadership, research, podcast, LinkedIn, content hubs, seller introduction | Design customer progression; avoid activity-only orchestration |
| Execute & Optimize | Weekly next actions, bottleneck fixes, account addition/removal, gradual AE adoption | Make portfolio and operating decisions from evidence |
| Measure & Learn | Leading indicators, account-to-pipeline, pipeline, sales feedback, playbook | Separate activity, progression, opportunity, revenue, attribution, and reuse |

## Transferable Mechanism

### Decision job

Run a bounded ABM pilot that changes account allocation and cross-functional behavior, produces decision evidence, and earns or rejects the case for wider rollout.

### Minimum prerequisites

- Shared evidence that the current model underperforms.
- Narrow problem, pilot scope, target-account criteria, and learning questions.
- Named business owner, sales champion, cross-functional core team, and RevOps support.
- Defined service levels, capacity, weekly decisions, progression states, and stop rules.
- Outcome definitions that distinguish sourced, influenced, accelerated, and created.

### Operating sequence

1. Diagnose the existing model with historical conversion, pipeline, account, and seller evidence.
2. Recruit change-ready leaders and operators; resolve objections before launch.
3. Define pilot scope across accounts, motion, service depth, team, time, investment, and region.
4. Analyze revenue, wins, losses, use cases, triggers, and disqualifiers.
5. Select accounts and map buying roles, relationships, unknowns, and next-decision hypotheses.
6. Establish the cross-functional team, RACI, service contract, and weekly working session.
7. Execute customer-relevant journeys and record evidence at account and buying-group level.
8. Each week decide next action, blocker response, promotion, demotion, replacement, pause, or exit.
9. Measure activity, progression, opportunity, pipeline, revenue, customer evidence, capacity, and learning separately.
10. Scale only the practices that survived review; test adoption beyond the initial champions.

### Decision and stop rules

- Do not launch the pilot without a falsifiable learning agenda and resource boundary.
- Do not equate selected accounts with deep 1:1 service.
- Do not keep inactive accounts in scarce-service tiers without current evidence.
- Do not scale from champion behavior until other sellers can reproduce the operating contract.
- Do not claim causal pipeline lift from sourced/influenced reporting alone.

### Measures

- Account and role coverage, two-way conversations, executive access, and next-decision evidence.
- Promotion, demotion, replacement, pause, and time in state.
- Opportunity creation, progression, velocity, pipeline, revenue, and customer outcomes.
- Seller adoption, follow-through, capacity, playbook reuse, and exceptions.
- Baseline, comparison, definition changes, and attribution boundaries.

## Applicability Boundaries

### Strong fit

- Enterprise sellers with long cycles, named-account economics, weak MQL conversion, and leadership willing to test a shared revenue operating model.

### Adapt before use

- Smaller teams, short cycles, product-led motions, weak historical data, constrained content capacity, or entrenched compensation/reporting dependencies.

### Do not use

- As a universal claim that six months, 130 accounts, 26% engagement, or 60% marketing pipeline contribution is the correct target or expected result.

## Risks And Failure Modes

- Pilot scope exceeds the team's real service capacity.
- A new engagement proxy replaces MQL volume without better customer evidence.
- Weekly reviews become reporting meetings rather than decision sessions.
- Consultant or champion dependence prevents replication.
- Sellers participate only when immediate opportunities appear.
- Pipeline definitions change during the pilot, invalidating comparison.
- Success stories are shared more often than failures and opportunity costs.
