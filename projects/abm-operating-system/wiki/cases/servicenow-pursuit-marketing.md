# ServiceNow Pursuit Marketing

## Decision Status

- **Purpose:** Test ServiceNow's Pursuit Marketing practice against the Enterprise Growth System while preserving an immediately usable mechanism.
- **Gate:** G3a and the comparative G3b model review were approved on 2026-07-22.
- **Allowed:** Private research, framework challenge, non-sensitive consulting-method development, and use as the evidence base for separately governed public drafting.
- **Prohibited:** Publication, canonical framework edits, ServiceNow endorsement claims, or unqualified reuse of reported figures.

## Executive Case Capsule

- **Problem:** A mature ABM team could give deep 1:1 support to only a few strategic accounts, while sales repeatedly needed similar depth for urgent deals, relationship problems, renewals, downsell risks, leadership changes, and new-account reach. Ad hoc support created hidden evening/weekend work and weak measurement. (source: From ABM to PBM How ServiceNow Runs Deal-Based Marketing at Sprint Speed (Episode 66) 2.md)
- **Context:** ServiceNow describes an established enterprise ABM organization with executive sponsorship, experienced practitioners, sales alignment, broader GTM support, CRM tagging, dashboards, and manual evidence capture. These are preconditions, not incidental details. (source: From ABM to PBM How ServiceNow Runs Deal-Based Marketing at Sprint Speed (Episode 66) 2.md)
- **Mechanism:** Pursuit Marketing applies 1:1 ABM principles to one qualified opportunity or account challenge for a bounded period, then returns commercial ownership to the account team.
- **Reported consequence:** A practitioner could reportedly support about 20 pursuit opportunities/accounts per year versus about five under durable 1:1 coverage. This is an unaudited illustration, not a benchmark. (source: From ABM to PBM How ServiceNow Runs Deal-Based Marketing at Sprint Speed (Episode 66) 2.md)
- **Blueprint relevance:** The case tests whether strategic depth can be time-bounded without inventing a sixth Growth Motion or weakening readiness, relationship, evidence, and human-decision requirements.

- Enterprise SaaS provider serving large accounts and complex buying groups.
- Established ABM function, senior sponsorship, and broader demand, field, industry, customer, and sales capabilities.

- CRM, dashboards, tagging, qualitative notes, and manual/semi-manual evidence capture are available.

- Cohort construction and detailed KPIs.
- Transferability to immature, traditional, regulated, channel-led, and resource-constrained organizations.

## Enterprise Growth System Mapping

### Growth Motion

- **Primary:** 1:1 Strategic ABM: one account, deep context, scarce capacity, account-team leadership, relationship paths, and buying-group progress.
- **Conditional:** Customer Expansion for renewal, retention, downsell, health, and credible white space; weak health routes to recovery first.
- **Fallback:** Account-based Demand when hypothesis, access, owner, evidence, or capacity is insufficient.
- **Boundary:** Pursuit is a service/initiative format inside the selected motion, not a sixth motion.
- **Exclusion:** Broad industry or one-to-many work remains Market Demand or Account-based Demand unless a real 1:few hypothesis and sales leadership exist.

### Growth Execution Loop

| Phase | Case evidence | Readiness implication |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial Priority | Material opportunity/challenge plus opportunity, achievability, collaboration | Name state, window, value, owner, and opportunity cost |
| Intelligence & Hypothesis | Distinguishes relationship, education, reach, deal, retention situations | Separate facts, interpretations, hypotheses, unknowns, alternatives |
| Orchestrate | Sales partnership, sponsorship, cross-functional inputs, permission to stop | Confirm commitment, capacity, rights, risk, follow-through |
| Content & Experience | Customize to actual challenge; reject surface personalization | Define customer progression and context difference |
| Execute & Optimize | Time-bounded response to decision/risk window | Predefine response rules, cadence, non-actions, and exit |
| Measure & Learn | 4Rs, objective evidence, dashboards, tags, notes, cohorts, feedback | Preserve outcome, capacity, evidence limits, alternatives, reusable learning |

## Transferable Mechanism: Qualified Pursuit Sprint

### Decision job

Concentrate scarce 1:1 ABM capability on one material account state for a bounded window, create observable progression, and exit without taking commercial ownership from the account team.

### Minimum prerequisites

- Material account/opportunity/customer-state problem and one observable change.
- Real decision/risk window and plausible marketing influence.
- Account-team co-ownership and experienced ABM leadership.
- Usable commercial, buying-group, relationship, customer, and delivery evidence.
- Named contributors, capacity, rights, measures, review date, and exit rule.

### Operating sequence

1. Translate the asset request into a deal, relationship, reach, education, recovery, or expansion state.
2. Select the durable Growth Motion from the account state; urgency does not decide the motion.
3. Test opportunity, achievability, collaboration, value, hypothesis, access, delivery, capacity, and opportunity cost.
4. Contract the state change, window, owners, contributions, rights, non-actions, cadence, and exit.
5. Diagnose business, commercial, relationship, perception, buying-group, delivery, and health constraints; label evidence states.
6. Write one hypothesis specifying roles, value, intervention, expected progression, evidence, and disconfirmation.
7. Map buying roles, influence, stance, relationship owner/evidence, access route, concern, next step, and single-thread risk.
8. Design the smallest useful perspective, peer interaction, workshop, validation, briefing, direct mail, or listening moment.
9. Continue, adapt, escalate, pause, recover, demote, or stop from observed response and commitments.
10. Record reputation, relationship, revenue, retention/customer value, capacity, evidence limits, learning, handoff, and next portfolio decision.

The standalone ServiceNow playbook remains assigned to `wiki/servicenow-pursuit-marketing-sprint-playbook.md` and is scheduled for recovery wave 11. This sequence is a project adaptation, not ServiceNow's official SOP.

### Decision and stop rules

- Reject asset-only work without a state, hypothesis, owner, or follow-through.
- Defer to Account-based Demand when trigger, access, evidence, collaboration, or capacity is weak.
- Route weak customer health to recovery before expansion.
- Reset account-team commitments once; stop/demote when collaboration remains absent.
- Reopen intelligence when leadership, risk, scope, evidence, or customer state changes.
- Stop when the window passes, hypothesis fails, state changes, influence is implausible, or opportunity cost wins.
- Promote to durable 1:1 only through an explicit portfolio decision.

### Measures

- Objective progression: executive access, relationship, buying-group coverage, workshop, confidence, decision, renewal, or expansion.
- Reputation, relationships, revenue, and retention/customer value.
- Execution effectiveness: time, commitments, capacity, reuse, and exit quality.
- Evidence quality: baseline, source, confidence, alternatives, comparison context, and unknowns.

## Applicability Boundaries

### Strong fit

- Complex enterprise/upper mid-market B2B accounts with material deal, relationship, renewal, or expansion stakes.
- Established GTM, sponsorship, ABM skill, sales commitment, and ability to act.
- Real decision/risk window and one progression target.

### Adapt before use

- **Immature enterprise:** pilot one pursuit with a sponsor and borrowed/external expertise; simplify tools, not evidence or exits.
- **Resource-constrained mid-market:** reserve small capacity, shorten intake, reuse modules, admit only high-materiality/influenceability cases.
- **Traditional enterprise:** expect manual research, fragmented data, regional rights, and account-team dependence.
- **Services/industrial:** redefine progression around trust, diagnostic access, technical proof, sites/networks, or channel relationships.

### Do not use

- Transactional, low-value, self-serve purchases.
- Production requests, broad demand generation, negotiation, or customer-success ownership disguised as pursuit.
- No committed owner, usable evidence, access path, or follow-through capacity.
- Expansion pressure during weak health, adoption, or realized value.

## Risks And Failure Modes

- Reactive sales-support drift; motion confusion; portfolio inflation; single-thread risk.
- Over-customization without decision value; sprint attention without durable handoff.
- Anecdotal, selected-account, manual-note, or cohort measurement bias.
- AI-generated claims or executive language bypassing human review.
- One mature SaaS case becoming a universal operating law.

## Pilot Model Evaluation

### Architecture tournament

| Candidate | Strength | Risk | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source-first essay | Narrative fidelity | Weak comparability/control | Keep as standalone playbook, not case record |
| Blueprint-first scorecard | Fast comparison | Forces ambiguous evidence into model | Reject |

### First-use findings

- Layering exposes context, resources, claim states, motion routing, mechanism, and governance while preserving practical value.
- It resolves durable-motion versus bounded-initiative ambiguity and separates source practice from adaptation.
- Evidence burden is justified for a recognizable company case.
- Operational overlap is controlled by keeping the detailed SOP as a linked derivative.
- Version 1.0.0 is frozen for the first six-case cycle; structural change now requires a severe failure or repeated evidence.
- Cross-case comparison against NTT DATA and the enterprise scale-system record confirms that the layered structure works across practitioner interviews and vendor guidance.
- The first recurring cross-case decision created the candidate pattern matrix. The publication-review template remains deferred until the first actual public draft exists.
